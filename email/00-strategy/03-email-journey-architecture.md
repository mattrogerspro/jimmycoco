# Email Journey Architecture

## System overview

The email programme is organised around customer state, not calendar volume.

## Core journeys

1. **Welcome** — brand promise, Jimmy's authority, shade guidance and first purchase.
2. **Shade-match continuation** — preserve the result, explain fit and return directly to the recommended product.
3. **Browse abandonment** — resolve uncertainty around the viewed category or product.
4. **Cart abandonment** — restore selected items, delivery confidence and checkout path.
5. **Post-purchase** — confirmation, preparation, application, development and result care.
6. **Replenishment** — predict likely need and make repeat purchase effortless.
7. **Win-back** — diagnose lapse and reintroduce the most relevant route.
8. **VIP** — access, recognition, service and relevant launches.
9. **Campaign** — launches, seasonal education, editorial stories and carefully governed offers.

## Journey design rule

Each sequence should answer one unresolved question per message. Do not repeat the same proposition across five differently designed emails.

## Message progression

A strong sequence typically moves through:

> Context → Relevance → Proof → Guidance → Action

## Trigger governance

Every automation must define:

- Entry trigger
- Eligibility rules
- Suppressions
- Delay logic
- Exit conditions
- Priority against other flows
- Personalisation fallback
- Measurement plan

## Cross-flow priority

Transactional messages override marketing. Post-purchase education overrides abandonment. Cart abandonment overrides browse abandonment. Shade-match follow-up overrides generic welcome content when recommendation data exists.

## Continuity

Preserve product, shade, recommendation and routine context between website and email. A customer should never be asked to repeat information already supplied unless it may have changed.
