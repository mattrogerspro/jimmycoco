# Scene 06 — Curated Best Sellers

## Role in the experience

This chapter serves visitors who are ready to shop while preserving the guided, premium character of the homepage.

The page has already created desire, established authority, reduced uncertainty, explained the method and shown credible results. Best Sellers should now convert that confidence into product consideration without reopening the entire catalogue.

This is a curated buying moment, not an inventory grid.

## Commercial objective

Increase qualified product-page visits and add-to-cart intent by presenting a deliberately limited set of clearly differentiated products.

The scene should:

- Serve high-intent visitors efficiently
- Reduce choice overload
- Make product differences understandable at a glance
- Reinforce product suitability and expected result
- Preserve premium positioning
- Support both direct purchase and shade-selection routes

## User questions answered

- Which products are the most popular?
- Which one is most suitable for me?
- What result will each product create?
- How long does each product develop?
- Is it beginner-friendly?
- Do I need to choose a shade before adding it?

## Core message

> The most-loved formulas, clearly matched to the result you want.

Alternative headline:

> Four formulas. Four clearly different results.

## Product count

Show a maximum of four products.

The homepage must not display the full catalogue. Four products are sufficient to create a meaningful range while avoiding decision overload.

A recommended assortment should cover distinct needs, for example:

1. Best for first-time users
2. Best for natural everyday colour
3. Best for deeper bronze
4. Best for fast or professional results

Use actual product names and claims only after they have been verified against the catalogue.

## Product differentiation framework

Every card must answer the same core questions:

- **Best for:** the user or use case
- **Result:** the visible finish
- **Development:** approximate time
- **Skin tones:** suitable range
- **Why choose it:** one specific differentiator

Use consistent metadata so products can be compared without reading long descriptions.

## Architectural silhouette

This scene should feel calm, ordered and product-led after the image-heavy Real Results chapter.

Approved silhouette:

- Clean white field
- Four generous product positions
- Large isolated bottle renders
- Minimal card boundaries or no visible boxes
- One or two products allowed to break the baseline subtly
- Clear editorial heading offset from the product row
- Generous negative space between products

The scene should resemble a curated luxury product gallery, not a Shopify collection grid.

## Layout direction

Use an invisible 12-column grid.

Recommended desktop arrangement:

- Eyebrow and headline: columns 2–7
- Short supporting copy or selector link: columns 8–11
- Four product modules: distributed across columns 2–11
- Maintain approximately 40–72px visual space between product modules

A subtle stagger in image scale or baseline is permitted to create editorial rhythm, but the information structure must remain consistent.

## Product module anatomy

Each product module should include:

1. Product image
2. Optional factual badge
3. Product name
4. Result statement
5. Rating and review count
6. Price
7. Best-for line
8. Development time
9. Skin-tone suitability
10. Primary action

Do not include long paragraphs.

## Badge discipline

Use at most one badge per product.

Approved badge types:

- Most Loved
- Best for Beginners
- Deepest Result
- Fastest Developing
- Jimmy's Pick

Badges must be factual and meaningful. Avoid generic labels such as New, Trending or Must-Have unless operationally accurate.

## CTA hierarchy

### Primary product action

**CHOOSE SHADE**

Use this when a shade, finish or variant must be selected.

### Alternative product action

**VIEW PRODUCT**

Use this when the product requires additional explanation before purchase.

### Section-level secondary action

**See All Tanning Products →**

This should be visually quiet.

Do not use **Quick Add** for products where variant or shade selection materially affects the result.

**CHOOSE SHADE**

Use verified product facts in production.

## Product rendering direction

- Use exact approved packaging references
- Maintain correct bottle proportions, cap, pump, label hierarchy and finish
- Show premium material response without artificial gloss
- Use soft grounded shadows
- Preserve accurate scale relationships between products
- Keep labels visually coherent even if production text is rebuilt later
- Avoid impossible floating angles

The product should feel tangible and collectible.

## Colour environment

- Clean white or very soft warm white background
- Deep charcoal typography
- Matte black primary actions
- Pale stone metadata
- Restrained muted-bronze badges
- Product colours remain accurate and dominant

This scene should be cleaner and lighter than Real Results and warmer than a clinical product catalogue.

## Typography

- Eyebrow: 12–13px uppercase
- Section headline: 44–52px serif
- Product name: 20–24px
- Result line: 16–18px
- Metadata: 14–16px
- Price: 16–18px with sufficient weight
- CTA: clear sans-serif, at least 44px height

Avoid tiny price, rating or suitability copy.

## Eye-tracking path

1. Section headline
2. First or recommended product image
3. Badge or result statement
4. Product name
5. Best-for and development metadata
6. CTA
7. Remaining products
8. Quiet all-products link

The recommended or most popular product may receive slightly greater visual scale, but not an oversized treatment that makes the others appear irrelevant.

## Conversion psychology

This chapter uses:

- **Hick's Law:** four curated choices rather than a full catalogue
- **Recognition over recall:** suitability details appear on every product
- **Social proof:** ratings and review counts near the product
- **Choice architecture:** products are differentiated by outcome and user need
- **Von Restorff effect:** one factual badge helps each product stand out
- **Reduced risk:** development time and skin-tone compatibility are visible before click-through

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Product images require descriptive alt text including product type and variant
- Ratings must include accessible text, not stars alone
- Buttons must be genuine buttons or links with descriptive labels
- On mobile, use a horizontal rail only if pagination and swipe position are clear; otherwise stack three featured products and reveal the fourth deliberately
- Ensure text remains readable when product names wrap
- Do not rely on badge colour alone to convey meaning

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Creates a generic four-card Shopify grid
- Makes all bottles identical
- Distorts labels or packaging
- Uses tiny metadata
- Adds excessive badges
- Uses Quick Add indiscriminately
- Places products on glossy marble pedestals without brand relevance
- Adds strong drop shadows or glassmorphism
- Fills every card with long copy
- Makes product differences unclear
- Uses one huge bestseller and three insignificant products
- Introduces bright colours outside the palette

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- Four products can be meaningfully compared in under fifteen seconds
- Each product has a distinct purpose
- The product renders feel accurate and premium
- The scene provides a calm visual reset after Real Results
- The section feels curated rather than catalogue-like
- The primary actions are clear and commercially useful
- The visitor does not need to remember information from previous scenes to choose a product
