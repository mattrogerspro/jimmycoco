# 03 — Colour System

## Purpose

This document defines how colour should create warmth, luxury, hierarchy and chapter differentiation across the Sunless website.

Colour must support the product category without allowing the page to collapse into one uninterrupted beige field.

## Core principle

The Sunless palette is warm, restrained and skin-conscious. It should feel flattering, editorial and tactile.

The system must balance two requirements:

1. Preserve the recognisable warm neutral identity.
2. Create enough tonal contrast for clear hierarchy and chapter separation.

## Primary palette roles

### Warm ivory

Use for:

- Hero environment
- Soft editorial backgrounds
- Premium product surfaces
- Calm transitional areas

Emotional effect:

- Warmth
- Ease
- Sophistication
- Approachability

Avoid using warm ivory for more than two major consecutive chapters.

### Bright white

Use for:

- Method explanation
- Product comparison
- Clean product presentation
- High-legibility information

Emotional effect:

- Clarity
- Precision
- Relief
- Modernity

White should create a deliberate reset after warmer chapters.

### Soft mineral stone

Use for:

- Recommendation engine
- Guided decision workspaces
- Structured interactive moments

Emotional effect:

- Calm expertise
- Stability
- Premium utility

Stone must remain warm enough to belong to Sunless and distinct enough from ivory to register at distance.

### Natural linen

Use for:

- Jimmy Coco editorial story
- Founder authority
- Human, tactile storytelling

Emotional effect:

- Heritage
- Craft
- Intimacy
- Editorial warmth

### Warm taupe

Use for:

- Customer stories
- Testimonial environments
- More intimate social proof

Emotional effect:

- Belonging
- Reassurance
- Human warmth

Taupe should be visibly deeper than the standard page background without becoming muddy.

### Deep charcoal

Use for:

- Primary CTA buttons
- Strong text
- Final conversion scene
- High-value contrast moments

Emotional effect:

- Authority
- Confidence
- Closure
- Luxury

The final CTA should feel deep and cinematic, not pure black or aggressive.

### Muted bronze and champagne

Use sparingly for:

- Fine rules
- Small accents
- Product details
- Star ratings
- Controlled highlight moments

These accents must never become the dominant interface colour.

## Suggested tonal reference values

These values are directional starting points and must be calibrated against approved brand assets:

- Warm ivory: `#F8F4EE`
- Soft cream: `#F4EEE6`
- Bright white: `#FFFDF9`
- Mineral stone: `#EDE6DD`
- Natural linen: `#E9DED1`
- Warm taupe: `#CDBAAA`
- Champagne accent: `#C8A878`
- Muted bronze: `#A77952`
- Deep charcoal: `#26231F`
- Primary text: `#24211E`
- Secondary text: `#615A53`
- Hairline rule: `#D8CEC3`

Do not treat these as final engineering tokens until verified against the current site and accessibility requirements.

## Background cadence

Recommended homepage sequence:

1. Hero — warm ivory
2. Authority transition — integrated ivory/white
3. Recommendation — mineral stone
4. Hollywood method — bright white
5. Real results — photography-dominant tonal field
6. Best sellers — clean white
7. Jimmy story — natural linen
8. Comparison — bright white
9. Reviews — warm taupe
10. Routine — soft cream or white
11. Final CTA — deep charcoal
12. Footer — warm ivory or pale stone

The sequence should remain recognisably Sunless while producing visible chapter changes.

## Text colour hierarchy

### Primary text

Use deep charcoal rather than pure black for most editorial text.

### Secondary text

Use a warm mid-grey with sufficient contrast. Do not allow secondary copy to become faint or decorative.

### Inverse text

Use warm white on dark surfaces. Avoid cold optical white where it creates harsh contrast.

### Links

Links should be distinguishable by weight, underline, icon or context—not colour alone.

## CTA colour

Primary CTAs:

- Matte deep charcoal background
- Warm white text
- Strong contrast
- Minimal or no shadow

Secondary CTAs:

- Transparent or pale background
- Charcoal border or text
- Clearly quieter than the primary action

Avoid bronze-filled primary buttons. They often weaken contrast and make the interface feel cosmetic rather than authoritative.

## Skin and photography colour

Skin must remain:

- Warm but believable
- Neutral enough to avoid orange cast
- Textured and dimensional
- Consistent across scenes

Do not colour grade every image into the same beige tone. Photography should preserve differences in skin, fabric, architecture and light.

## Contrast requirements

All production UI must meet WCAG AA contrast as a minimum.

Pay particular attention to:

- Secondary text on ivory
- Gold or bronze text
- Fine rules
- Labels inside stone surfaces
- White text over photography
- Disabled and selected states

Decorative subtlety must never override legibility.

## State colours

Functional states should remain restrained but unmistakable:

- Success: muted olive or deep sage
- Warning: warm amber-brown
- Error: restrained brick or deep terracotta
- Information: cool stone-blue only if required

Do not introduce vivid system colours that clash with the luxury palette.

## Common colour failures

Reject designs that:

- Render the entire page in one cream tone
- Use pale grey text that cannot be read
- Create orange skin through warm grading
- Overuse gold accents
- Use pure black everywhere
- Introduce saturated pink, purple or neon beauty colours
- Depend on colour alone to indicate selection
- Use decorative gradients to simulate luxury

## 10% zoom test

At 10% zoom, background shifts should reveal the chapter cadence clearly.

If the entire page still reads as one pale vertical block, increase tonal separation between selected chapters rather than adding borders.

## Success criteria

The colour system succeeds when:

- The brand remains warm and recognisable
- Chapter boundaries are visible at distance
- Text remains highly legible
- Skin looks natural
- Dark moments feel rare and powerful
- Colour supports hierarchy without becoming decoration
