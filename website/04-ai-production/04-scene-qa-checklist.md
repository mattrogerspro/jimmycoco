# Scene QA Checklist

## Purpose

Use this checklist to review every generated website scene before it is approved, refined or assembled into the final homepage.

A scene must pass both creative and commercial review. Visual polish alone is not sufficient.

## Approval states

Assign one status:

- `exploratory`
- `revision required`
- `conditionally approved`
- `approved`
- `superseded`
- `rejected`

Only approved scenes may be used in the final homepage assembly.

## 1. Brand-recognition test

- [ ] The scene is immediately recognisable as Sunless by Jimmy Coco.
- [ ] It feels like an evolution of the current homepage, not a different brand.
- [ ] The warm editorial beauty character is preserved.
- [ ] The scene does not imitate another luxury brand too literally.
- [ ] The visual tone is premium through restraint rather than decoration.

Fail if the logo could be replaced with another beauty brand without the scene feeling different.

## 2. Scene-purpose test

- [ ] The scene resolves one specific customer question.
- [ ] Its role in the homepage narrative is obvious.
- [ ] It does not repeat the job of an earlier scene.
- [ ] Removing it would materially reduce clarity, trust, desire or conversion.
- [ ] The scene’s emotional objective is visible in the composition.

## 3. Five-second comprehension test

Within five seconds, can a first-time visitor understand:

- [ ] What the scene is about?
- [ ] Why it matters?
- [ ] What they should look at first?
- [ ] What action is available, if an action is required?

## 4. Commercial-hierarchy test

- [ ] The most commercially important element is visually dominant.
- [ ] The primary CTA is clear and specific.
- [ ] Secondary actions are visibly quieter.
- [ ] The scene provides enough motivation before asking for action.
- [ ] The CTA wording matches the actual next step.
- [ ] Product or proof information supports the requested action.

## 5. Eye-tracking test

Confirm the intended fixation order from the scene specification.

- [ ] First fixation is correct.
- [ ] Second fixation is correct.
- [ ] The eye reaches the main message without confusion.
- [ ] The eye reaches the CTA or proof in the intended order.
- [ ] Decorative elements do not create false focal points.
- [ ] There are no large accidental dead zones.

## 6. Architectural-silhouette test

Review the scene at 10% zoom and with blur applied.

- [ ] The silhouette is distinctive.
- [ ] The scene does not resemble a generic card stack.
- [ ] Image scale, density and alignment are intentional.
- [ ] The scene remains legible as a chapter before copy can be read.
- [ ] The scene is visibly different from the previous and next chapters.

Fail if it merges into the continuous cream-page problem.

## 7. Adjacent-scene test

Place the scene between thumbnails of the preceding and following chapters.

- [ ] Background cadence works.
- [ ] Density changes deliberately.
- [ ] Consecutive headings are not positioned identically.
- [ ] Card structures are not repeated unnecessarily.
- [ ] The entry and exit feel intentional.
- [ ] The complete storyboard becomes stronger.

## 8. Composition test

- [ ] The 12-column logic is credible even when the grid is broken.
- [ ] Alignment feels deliberate.
- [ ] Whitespace supports hierarchy.
- [ ] Content is not centred by default without reason.
- [ ] Images are used as architecture, not decoration.
- [ ] The scene is neither overcrowded nor artificially empty.
- [ ] No important content is trapped in awkward narrow columns.

## 9. Typography test

- [ ] Serif and sans-serif character matches the approved system.
- [ ] Heading scale reflects scene importance.
- [ ] Body text is visually readable.
- [ ] Line lengths are controlled.
- [ ] Metadata is not too small.
- [ ] Button typography is firm and legible.
- [ ] The image model has not filled the scene with meaningless pseudo-text.

Final production copy must be rebuilt as live text.

## 10. Colour and contrast test

- [ ] Background tone matches the planned cadence.
- [ ] The scene remains within the approved Sunless palette.
- [ ] Skin does not appear orange or artificial.
- [ ] Text remains readable over imagery.
- [ ] Product and background are sufficiently separated.
- [ ] The scene does not become beige-on-beige.
- [ ] No new unapproved accent colour has been introduced.

## 11. Photography test

- [ ] Lighting is natural, controlled and editorial.
- [ ] Skin texture remains believable.
- [ ] There is no harsh HDR or plastic retouching.
- [ ] Hands and anatomy are credible.
- [ ] Poses feel natural and purposeful.
- [ ] The environment feels premium but not like generic spa stock photography.
- [ ] Before-and-after proof uses matched conditions where relevant.

## 12. Jimmy Coco likeness test

Where Jimmy appears:

- [ ] Facial structure is recognisable.
- [ ] Age and grooming are consistent.
- [ ] Hair is consistent.
- [ ] Wardrobe supports his professional role.
- [ ] Body language shows expertise.
- [ ] He is not reduced to a posed celebrity endorsement.

## 13. Product-accuracy test

- [ ] Bottle geometry matches references.
- [ ] Cap or pump is correct.
- [ ] Label architecture is consistent.
- [ ] Product finish and colour are plausible.
- [ ] Relative scale is believable.
- [ ] Shadows and reflections are physically credible.
- [ ] Repeated products match previously approved scenes.

## 14. Component-consistency test

- [ ] Buttons follow the approved component language.
- [ ] Selection controls have clear states.
- [ ] Product cards answer who, why and result.
- [ ] Trust labels are integrated rather than decorative.
- [ ] Form labels are persistent.
- [ ] No new radius, shadow or card family has appeared without justification.

## 15. Copy and claims test

- [ ] All visible claims are approved or clearly marked placeholders.
- [ ] Review counts and press references are accurate.
- [ ] Product benefits are not exaggerated.
- [ ] CTA reassurance is operationally true.
- [ ] Copy uses customer language rather than empty beauty clichés.
- [ ] Terminology matches earlier scenes.

## 16. Cognitive-load test

- [ ] One dominant idea is presented.
- [ ] Options are limited and logically grouped.
- [ ] Users do not need to remember information from an earlier scene.
- [ ] Recognition is favoured over recall.
- [ ] Metadata supports the decision rather than adding noise.
- [ ] The scene avoids redundant icons, badges and copy.

## 17. Accessibility-risk review

A static concept cannot prove full accessibility, but it must not embed avoidable failures.

- [ ] Text contrast appears capable of meeting WCAG AA.
- [ ] Interactive targets can support at least 44px.
- [ ] Text is not baked over complex imagery without protection.
- [ ] Selected states are not indicated by colour alone.
- [ ] Body and metadata sizes are realistic.
- [ ] Motion-dependent meaning is not assumed.
- [ ] Production implementation notes are recorded.

## 18. Responsive-feasibility test

- [ ] The layout can plausibly reflow to tablet and mobile.
- [ ] Essential content has a clear priority order.
- [ ] Full-bleed imagery has viable mobile crops.
- [ ] Controls can stack without becoming overly long.
- [ ] The primary CTA remains reachable.
- [ ] The scene does not depend on tiny desktop-only detail.

## 19. AI-artifact test

Reject or repair:

- [ ] Malformed hands
- [ ] Extra fingers or limbs
- [ ] Warped bottles
- [ ] Impossible shadows
- [ ] Broken reflections
- [ ] Repeated faces
- [ ] Pseudo-logos
- [ ] Inconsistent jewellery or clothing
- [ ] Illegible text blocks
- [ ] Accidental objects
- [ ] Cropped heads, hands or products without compositional intent

## 20. Production-usability test

- [ ] The composition is clear enough to rebuild in Figma or code.
- [ ] Text and controls can be separated from imagery.
- [ ] Images can be exported or recreated at usable resolution.
- [ ] Known defects are documented.
- [ ] The scene has an approved filename and version.
- [ ] Source prompt and references are recorded.

## Scoring

Score each category from 1–10:

- Brand recognition
- Scene purpose
- Commercial hierarchy
- Composition
- Silhouette differentiation
- Typography
- Colour
- Photography
- Product accuracy
- Continuity
- Accessibility readiness
- Production usability

## Approval threshold

A scene should not be approved when:

- Any critical factual or identity test fails
- Product accuracy scores below 8
- Jimmy likeness scores below 8 where relevant
- Commercial hierarchy scores below 8
- Silhouette differentiation scores below 8
- Overall average is below 8.5

## Final approval question

Does this scene make the full Sunless homepage clearer, more desirable, more trustworthy and easier to buy from?

If not, it is not finished.
