# Personalisation and Segmentation

## Principle

Personalisation must make the message more useful. A first name alone is not a strategy.

## Highest-value personalisation

1. Shade-match recommendation
2. Desired depth and undertone
3. Product viewed or added to cart
4. Purchased product and expected usage stage
5. Replenishment timing
6. Routine gaps
7. Experience level
8. Engagement and purchase recency

## Recommendation continuity

When a customer has completed shade match, preserve:

- Recommended product
- Recommended variant
- Why it fits
- Desired result
- Selected development preference
- Alternative recommendation where appropriate

The email CTA should return to a preconfigured product or saved result whenever technically possible.

## Dynamic content rules

Dynamic modules may change product, proof, guidance or CTA destination. They must not create contradictory claims or break the visual hierarchy.

Every dynamic block requires:

- A valid data source
- A safe fallback
- Stock handling
- Pricing validation
- Link validation
- QA for long and missing values

## Segmentation hierarchy

Prefer behavioural and lifecycle segmentation over broad demographics:

- Current intent
- Product fit
- Purchase stage
- Usage stage
- Reorder likelihood
- Engagement state

## Restraint

Do not expose sensitive inference or overstate precision. Use language such as “Based on your shade-match answers” rather than pretending the system has analysed skin medically or with certainty.

## Fallback standard

When personal data is unavailable, default to a useful universal route such as shade matching, best sellers by desired result, or application education. Never display blank fields, generic token errors or irrelevant products.
