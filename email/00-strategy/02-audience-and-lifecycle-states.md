# Audience and Lifecycle States

## Principle

Segmentation should reflect what the customer needs next. Demographic labels alone are too weak to determine useful content.

## Core lifecycle states

### Subscriber, no shopping signal

Needs a clear introduction to the brand, Jimmy's authority and the simplest route to a suitable product.

### Engaged browser

Has viewed categories, products, results or educational content. Needs relevant proof, product differentiation or shade guidance.

### Shade-match participant

Has supplied high-intent preference data. Messages should preserve the recommendation, explain why it fits and provide a direct return path.

### Cart creator

Has selected a specific product or routine. Needs restored context, practical reassurance and a low-friction route back—not a generic brand campaign.

### First-time purchaser

Needs order confidence, application preparation, timing guidance and a successful first result.

### Active customer

Has purchased recently and may be learning the routine, replenishing or expanding into complementary products.

### Repeat customer

Has demonstrated product fit. Prioritise convenience, replenishment, relevant launches and loyalty recognition.

### Business-tool user

Has run a business calculator or taken a template pack on the professional site. A high-intent trade signal, and one that arrives before any purchase. Needs the article that answers the question the tool raised, not a product pitch. See `07-content-triggered-campaigns.md`.

### Lapsed customer

Has exceeded the expected reorder window. Diagnose probable barriers before offering an incentive.

### VIP / high-value customer

Recognise loyalty through access, relevance and service rather than constant discounts.

## Behavioural attributes

Use when available:

- Recommended product and shade
- Desired colour depth
- Undertone preference
- Product format preference
- Development-time preference
- First-time or experienced self-tanner
- Product views and category interest
- Purchase history
- Predicted replenishment window
- Engagement recency
- Discount sensitivity
- Return or support history
- Business tool used, and the result band it produced
- Content pillar engaged with
- Trade or consumer relationship

## Suppression rules

Suppress or adapt messages when:

- A purchase makes the trigger obsolete
- The item is unavailable
- A customer is already receiving a higher-priority service message
- Frequency pressure is too high
- Consent or channel status does not permit the message
- The recommendation is no longer supported by current catalogue data

## Priority order

1. Transactional and service communication
2. Time-sensitive post-purchase guidance
3. Cart recovery
4. Shade-match continuation
5. Browse recovery
6. Replenishment
7. Lifecycle education
8. Campaign promotion

## Segmentation standard

A segment is valid only when it materially changes the message, product selection, timing or call to action. Do not create decorative segments that receive effectively identical content.
