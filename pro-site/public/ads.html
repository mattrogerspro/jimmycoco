The five things that matter

  • Your ad volume and your revenue move in opposite directions. Correlation between average live ads and monthly revenue is −0.50. Your four heaviest advertising months averaged £22,374. Your five lightest averaged £34,381 — 54% more. Ad instances are not spend, so read this as "no evidence that more ads produced more revenue", not as proof that ads hurt.
  • Discounting is what actually moves your numbers. Discount spend correlates with orders at +0.86. That is the strongest relationship in the entire dataset, and it is not a flattering one.
  • You are buying volume with margin. Orders up 4.8× from Jul 2025 to May 2026, but AOV fell from £29.49 to £19.95 — down 32%.
  • Ferne McCann is your single most valuable measurable asset. 971 orders and £27,798 through her codes — 7.2% of all revenue — off only 22 paid ad instances.
  • Ten ads on your page have nothing to do with your brand. Six for Admiral Business insurance, three for Ascot, one for a podcast. Worth knowing who has access.

Three caveats that belong right next to those numbers Ad instances are not spend. One Advantage+ campaign at £10k appears as a handful of ads; forty small tests appear as forty. Until Meta spend is joined to this, −0.50 means "no evidence that more ads produced more revenue", not "ads reduced revenue".

Flight length is per-ad, not per-ad-set. A 7-day ad flight tells you nothing certain about how long the underlying ad set or budget ran. The learning-phase argument later in this report rests on an assumption the Ad Library cannot confirm.

The April 2026 anomaly

27 of 30 days with zero live ads — and your fourth-best month of the year April 2026: 0.2 average live ads, £36,772 revenue, 1,371 orders, 20,963 social sessions — the highest social traffic of any month in the dataset. And 398 Ferne McCann code orders worth £10,202, more than double any other month. 10 April alone did 119 orders and £3,491 — your single biggest revenue day in 13 months.

What was actually running: one Ferne McCann video ad (4 instances, 2 Apr – 19 Jun) carrying the FERNE15 code — and essentially nothing else. Meta-attributed revenue that month was £7,981, about 22% of the total. So this is not proof that organic creator activity works without paid support; a small paid creator flight was live throughout, carrying the exact code that spiked.

What it does show is that one creator ad outperformed months running twenty times the ad volume. September and October 2025 averaged 19.5 and 20.5 live ads and produced £11,687 and £12,936. April ran essentially one creator flight and produced £36,772. Whether the lift came from the paid flight, Ferne's own organic posting alongside it, or both, cannot be separated from this data — and separating it is worth doing, because whichever it is, it is the most efficient thing in the dataset.

Where you win

  • You own the customer. 95% own-site traffic. St. Moriz and Tan-Luxe send 100% of their Meta traffic to retailers, so those buyers become Boots' and Sephora's customers, not theirs. Both brands run their own sites and lists too — but none of their paid media is feeding them.
  • You can measure. This report exists because you have a Shopify store behind your ads. A brand routing every ad to a retailer cannot join media to revenue at all.

Where you lose

  • Creative volume. 29 unique creatives against Coco & Eve's 87 — and 79% of your ad instances are a single catalogue template. Effectively you are running about 28 real creative ideas in 13 months.
  • Creator bench. Four creators, three unmeasurable. Bondi Sands and Coco & Eve run 15 each.
  • Flight discipline. 7-day median. Meta's learning phase typically needs ~50 conversions per ad set per week; week-long flights often kill an ad before it exits learning.

The uncomfortable mirror

Your format profile is closest to Bondi Sands (11% video, 87% catalogue) — a mass-market volume brand competing on price and distribution. Your positioning is closest to Tan-Luxe (premium, celebrity, prestige). You are buying media like a discounter while charging like a luxury brand. The AOV decline in the Effectiveness tab is what that contradiction looks like on a P&L.

What to fix

Seven moves, ordered by how fast they pay back. Every one is grounded in a number from this report.

Fix 01 · This week

Export Meta spend and re-run this join

Everything here rests on ad instances as a proxy for investment, which is the weakest link in the analysis. A spend export by campaign and day turns a −0.50 correlation from a red flag into a decision.

Do: Pull daily spend, impressions and reported purchases by campaign from Ads Manager for Jul 2025 onward. Join to the Shopify daily revenue series. If spend genuinely was flat while instances spiked, the negative correlation dissolves. If it wasn't, you have found a large leak.
Fix 02 · This week

Audit who can run ads on your page

Ten ads for Admiral Business, Ascot and a podcast ran under the Jimmy Coco name. They are in the public Ad Library permanently, attached to your brand.

Do: Check Business Manager page roles and ad-account partner access. Remove anything you don't recognise. If it's a deliberate arrangement, fine — but it should be a decision, not a discovery.
Fix 03 · Highest expected value

Give every creator a code, and build the bench to eight

Ferne delivered 971 orders and £27,798 — 7.2% of revenue — from 22 ad instances, and April showed one creator flight outperforming months with twenty times the ad volume. Chloe Sims, Faye Winter and Phoebe Torrance have no codes, so their contribution is literally unmeasurable.

Do: Unique discount code plus a unique UTM landing page per creator, non-negotiable, before any content goes live. Then double the roster — Bondi Sands and Coco & Eve run 15 creators each; you run four. Track cost per acquired customer per creator and cut the bottom half after 60 days.
Fix 04 · Structural

Stop the seven-day flights

Your median flight is 7 days against comparator medians of 18–39. Meta's published guidance is that an ad set generally needs around 50 optimisation events per week to exit the learning phase; week-long flights make that hard to reach, which typically means repeatedly paying learning-phase delivery costs. Caveat: the Ad Library reports per-ad flights, not ad-set duration — an ad set may well have run longer with creative rotating inside it. Confirm in Ads Manager before acting.

Do: If ad-set durations really are this short, move to a minimum 21-day flight, consolidate budget into fewer longer-running ad sets, and judge creative at day 14 rather than day 3.
Fix 05 · Margin

Wean the business off the discount reflex

Discounts correlate with orders at +0.86 — by far the strongest signal in the data. But AOV has fallen 32%, £46,529 has gone out in discounts, and returning-customer rate collapsed from 46% to 11% during the discounting period. You are renting volume.

Do: Test bundle-led offers instead of sitewide percentage-off. The Malibu Beach Duo already averages £28.58 per order against a 13-month blended AOV of £27.26 — a modest premium, but it holds price where a sitewide 20% off does not, and May 2026's AOV was down at £19.95. Cap sitewide discounting to two events a year. Track contribution margin per order, not order count.
Fix 06 · Untapped

Build the PRO campaign that doesn't exist

The 1 Ltr Professional Spray did 23 orders and £1,484 in 13 months. Zero of your 255 ads address salons or professionals. Sienna X Professional runs 132 ads with LEARN_MORE and SIGN_UP CTAs at exactly this audience, 39-day flights, 28 live right now.

Do: A separate lead-gen campaign to your pro page, with its own creative language — booth economics, treatment time, margin per client, Jimmy's professional credentials. Not glow. This is the one part of your range where your celebrity-artist story is a genuine, defensible advantage and nobody is competing for the attention.
Fix 07 · Creative

Put creative back in front of the catalogue

84% of your ad instances are a product feed. St. Moriz runs 79% video and Coco & Eve produce 87 unique creatives to your 29. Catalogue ads harvest demand that already exists; they do not create it. A reasonable hypothesis — untested by this data — is that thin video volume leaves you with little demand creation, which is why promotions end up doing the heavy lifting. Worth testing rather than assuming.

Do: Target 6–8 new video concepts a month. You already have the AI generation pipeline in the repo and a professional-method story competitors cannot tell. Keep the catalogue layer running underneath — it's cheap and it converts warm traffic — but stop letting it be the whole strategy.

Method & caveats

How this was built

  • Meta Ad Library queried directly for page 93634194988, UK, active and inactive — 255 ad records with dates, platforms, format, CTA, destination, copy and creator attribution.
  • Daily ad coverage computed by expanding every flight window across all 406 days from 1 Jul 2025 to 10 Aug 2026.
  • Shopify Admin API via ShopifyQL: daily and monthly sales, orders, AOV, discounts, referrer attribution, sessions, conversion rate, returning-customer rate, product-level sales and discount-code performance.
  • Correlations are Pearson's r across the 13 complete months (Jul 2025 – Jul 2026). August 2026 is partial and excluded from every average and correlation.

Observed vs inferred

Observed fact — every ad count, date, format, CTA, destination and creator; every Shopify figure including revenue, orders, AOV, discounts, codes, sessions and attribution. All directly queried.

Calculated — daily ad coverage, dark days, the correlations, heavy-vs-light month averages.

Inferred, flagged as such — that low ad-instance counts imply low spend; that short per-ad flights imply short ad-set durations and therefore learning-phase inefficiency; the counterfactual cost of the Dec–Mar Ferne gap; and the reading of the Admiral/Ascot/podcast ads as third-party page access. April 2026's performance is not attributed to organic activity — a paid Ferne flight was live throughout, and the two cannot be separated from this data.

Limitations you should hold in mind

  • Instances are not spend. The single biggest caveat. One Advantage+ campaign at £10k appears as a handful of ads; forty small tests appear as forty. Until spend is joined, the −0.50 correlation is a question, not a verdict.
  • Correlation is not causation, and n = 13. Thirteen monthly data points is a small sample with heavy seasonality (Black Friday, spring/summer tanning). The discount-to-orders relationship is strong and consistent; the ads-to-revenue one is best treated as "no evidence of benefit" rather than "ads reduce revenue".
  • Shopify attribution understates paid social. Last-click referrer misses view-through conversions and anything landing via direct after seeing an ad. The true Meta contribution is higher than 28.2% — though the 44% unattributed bucket cuts both ways, since Google, email and creator traffic sit in there too.
  • Ads with no impressions are omitted by Meta, so brief tests may be invisible.
  • Organic creator posts are not in the Ad Library. April 2026 had both a live paid Ferne flight and, very likely, organic posting. Nothing in this data can apportion the £36,772 between them. Ferne's posting calendar plus Meta spend for that flight would settle it.
  • Google, TikTok and email are outside this analysis, and Google alone is 17% of your revenue.
  • Two different date bases are in play. Revenue totals, orders, AOV, discounts and every correlation use the 13 complete months to 31 Jul 2026 (£370,633). Product sales, referrer attribution, discount-code totals and the creator roster use the full window including partial August 2026 (£388,130). Each table states its basis; the difference is why "£104,590 from Meta" and "£57,291 + £52,588" do not match.
  • Creative-group labels are approximations. Meta collates ad instances that share copy, so a group's stated format and page reflect the dominant value, not every instance. Grouped counts in the Creative library will not always reconcile exactly with the instance-level tables in the Ad profile tab.
