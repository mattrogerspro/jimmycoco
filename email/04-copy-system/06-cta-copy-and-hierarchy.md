# CTA Copy and Hierarchy

## Purpose

Define how calls to action guide customers clearly without creating competing choices or vague destinations.

## Primary rule

Every CTA must tell the customer what will happen next.

## CTA hierarchy

Each email should normally contain:

- one primary CTA;
- zero to two secondary links;
- utility links only where necessary.

The primary CTA carries the principal commercial or service action. Secondary links support consideration or help, but must not compete visually or verbally.

## Preferred CTA patterns

Use direct verb-led language:

- View your recommendation
- Shop your shade
- Continue to checkout
- Track your order
- Read the application guide
- Replenish your routine
- Access your VIP benefits
- Confirm your address
- Review your order
- Compare the shades

## Avoid vague CTAs

Avoid:

- Learn more
- Discover now
- Explore
- Click here
- Get started
- Shop now

These may be used only when the surrounding context makes the destination unambiguous. More specific wording is normally stronger.

## Destination integrity

The CTA destination must match the wording exactly.

Examples:

- “Continue to checkout” must open checkout, not a product page.
- “Track your order” must open valid tracking or an order-status page.
- “Shop your recommended shade” must preserve the recommended variant.
- “Access your benefits” must open the benefit area or relevant account state.

## Dynamic CTAs

Dynamic links must preserve:

- customer identity where required;
- basket or session state;
- recommended product and variant;
- market and currency;
- offer or benefit eligibility;
- source attribution;
- safe expiry behaviour.

Expired or invalid destinations require a useful fallback rather than an error page.

## Button versus text link

Use a button for the primary action.

Use text links for:

- supporting information;
- alternative recommendations;
- preferences;
- help and policy pages;
- lower-priority navigation.

Do not render three equal-weight buttons in one section.

## Repetition

The primary CTA may appear near the opening and once again after meaningful supporting content in longer emails.

Repeated CTA labels should usually remain identical. Do not introduce different labels for the same destination unless the customer context has changed.

## Pressure and consent

Do not use coercive button copy such as:

- Yes, I want perfect skin
- No, I prefer to miss out
- Claim what is yours before it disappears
- Complete your purchase now or lose it

A decline or preference action must remain neutral and respectful.

## Accessibility

CTA copy should:

- make sense out of context;
- avoid multiple identical “Learn more” links;
- remain concise enough for buttons;
- describe the destination for screen-reader users;
- avoid relying on colour or position alone.

## QA checklist

Confirm:

- there is one dominant action;
- the label describes the next step;
- the destination matches the label;
- product and shade state are preserved;
- expired links have fallbacks;
- secondary links do not compete;
- the CTA remains understandable without the image;
- the language is direct without pressure.
