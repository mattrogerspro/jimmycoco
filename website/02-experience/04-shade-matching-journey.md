# Shade-Matching Journey

## Purpose

The shade matcher is the website’s principal guided-selling tool. It must reduce uncertainty and produce a recommendation that feels specific, explainable and commercially useful.

It must not behave like a novelty quiz.

## Customer outcome

At completion, the customer should receive:

- One primary product recommendation
- One credible alternative
- Recommended depth or shade
- Suggested number of coats
- Development time
- Application area
- Preparation and application guidance
- Relevant routine additions
- A clear route to the product page or Add to Bag

## Questionnaire length

Target four to six questions. Every question must materially affect the result.

Recommended sequence:

1. Where will you use the product?
2. What result depth do you want?
3. How would you describe your natural skin depth?
4. Which undertone description feels closest?
5. How quickly do you need the result?
6. How experienced are you with self-tan?

Only include questions that are supported by real recommendation logic.

## Question design

- Ask one question per screen or clear panel.
- Use plain language before technical terminology.
- Pair undertone language with visual guidance.
- Allow “I’m not sure” where appropriate.
- Show progress honestly.
- Preserve previous answers.
- Allow backward navigation without restarting.

## Recommendation logic

The recommendation should account for:

- Face, body or both
- Desired result depth
- Natural skin depth
- Undertone
- Development window
- Product-format preference, when relevant
- Experience level
- Event or maintenance use case

Do not recommend a product solely because it is a bestseller.

## Result hierarchy

### Primary recommendation

Display:

- Product image
- Product name
- Why it matches the answers
- Expected result
- Suggested application plan
- Price
- Review summary
- Add to Bag
- View Full Product Details

### Alternative

Offer one alternative only, explaining the trade-off clearly.

Example:

> Choose this instead for a faster development time and slightly lighter finish.

### Routine

Recommend no more than two genuinely relevant supporting products.

## Explainability

Every result must state why it was selected.

Preferred pattern:

> Recommended because you selected a natural medium result, neutral-warm undertone and an overnight development window.

Avoid opaque language such as “Our algorithm found your perfect glow.”

## Confidence and correction

Allow customers to adjust:

- Desired depth
- Development speed
- Face/body use

The result should update without requiring a complete restart.

## Data and privacy

- Do not require email before showing the result.
- Ask for email only as an optional save or follow-up action.
- Explain how saved results will be used.
- Do not imply biometric or diagnostic accuracy.

## Mobile behaviour

- Use large, thumb-friendly options.
- Keep progress visible but quiet.
- Avoid horizontal option carousels.
- Keep the result CTA visible without obscuring content.
- Ensure back navigation does not lose answers.

## Analytics

Track:

- Start rate
- Completion rate
- Drop-off by question
- Answer distribution
- Recommendation distribution
- Product-detail click-through
- Add-to-bag rate
- Purchase rate by recommendation
- Alternative selection rate

## Failure conditions

The journey fails if:

- It asks questions that do not change the result.
- Every path recommends the same bestseller.
- The result appears before explaining why.
- Email is required to reveal the answer.
- Undertone choices are confusing or visually inaccurate.
- The recommendation conflicts with the product page.

## Success criteria

The shade matcher succeeds when customers feel less uncertain, understand the logic of the recommendation and can move directly into a product page that preserves their selected result and application plan.
