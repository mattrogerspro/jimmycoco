# Brand Voice for Email

## Purpose

Define how Sunless sounds in the inbox across lifecycle, service and campaign communications.

The voice must feel recognisably premium, expert and human without becoming distant, over-written or promotional.

## Core voice

Sunless email speaks as a calm authority in self-tanning.

It should feel:

- expert without sounding clinical;
- luxurious without sounding ornamental;
- warm without becoming over-familiar;
- confident without exaggeration;
- concise without feeling abrupt;
- helpful before persuasive;
- visually aware without describing design for its own sake.

## Brand promise in language

The central verbal idea is:

**Red-carpet colour. Made effortless at home.**

Email copy should repeatedly support three beliefs:

1. The result can look natural and professionally considered.
2. The customer can achieve it without unnecessary complexity.
3. Sunless will guide the decision and application process with clarity.

## Voice pillars

### 1. Calm expertise

State what matters, explain why and remove uncertainty.

Use:

- “For a deeper result, apply a second light layer.”
- “Your recommended shade is designed to develop without an orange cast.”
- “Exfoliate the evening before for a more even finish.”

Avoid:

- “This game-changing formula will transform everything.”
- “You NEED this tanning hack.”
- “The ultimate secret celebrities do not want you to know.”

### 2. Editorial restraint

Prefer one precise idea over several decorative claims.

Use short paragraphs, controlled rhythm and deliberate emphasis. Do not fill space with generic luxury language.

Avoid chains such as:

“Indulge in the ultimate, transformative, radiant tanning ritual.”

Prefer:

“A considered tan, built around your skin and the finish you want.”

### 3. Human reassurance

Acknowledge likely concerns without amplifying them.

Useful concerns include:

- choosing the wrong depth;
- orange or unnatural colour;
- streaking;
- difficult application;
- patchy fading;
- uncertainty about face versus body products;
- not knowing when to reapply.

Reassure with practical control, not vague promises.

### 4. Professional credibility

Jimmy Coco’s authority should be communicated through method, experience and specificity rather than celebrity name-dropping alone.

Use celebrity or professional context only when approved and relevant. Never imply endorsement, current use or exclusivity without evidence and usage rights.

### 5. Effortless direction

Every message should make the next step obvious.

Do not make customers decode:

- which product is being discussed;
- which shade or variant applies;
- what action to take;
- when to use the product;
- whether a benefit or offer has conditions.

## Sentence style

Prefer:

- active voice;
- direct verbs;
- concrete nouns;
- natural contractions where appropriate;
- short and medium-length sentences;
- one principal idea per paragraph.

Use fragments sparingly for editorial emphasis:

“Even colour. No guesswork.”

Do not rely on fragments throughout an entire message.

## Pronouns

Use “you” to make guidance direct and useful.

Use “we” when Sunless is genuinely acting, recommending or supporting.

Use “I” only when a message is explicitly and truthfully authored by Jimmy Coco or another named person.

Do not simulate a personal message from Jimmy when it is an automated brand communication.

## Vocabulary

Preferred language:

- colour;
- glow;
- finish;
- depth;
- undertone;
- development;
- application;
- routine;
- natural-looking;
- even;
- considered;
- tailored;
- recommended;
- professional;
- effortless;
- refined.

Use carefully:

- flawless;
- perfect;
- transformative;
- iconic;
- exclusive;
- celebrity;
- red carpet;
- instant;
- guaranteed.

Avoid unless factually and legally approved:

- risk-free;
- miracle;
- clinically proven;
- toxin-free;
- chemical-free;
- universally suitable;
- zero streaks;
- no transfer;
- lasts X days;
- works for everyone.

## Humour and informality

Light warmth is acceptable. Forced jokes, slang and internet trends are not the default brand mode.

Avoid:

- “Hey babe”;
- “Glow bestie”;
- “Obsessed!”;
- excessive exclamation marks;
- meme language;
- manufactured urgency.

## Punctuation

- Use one exclamation mark only when genuine energy is appropriate.
- Avoid repeated punctuation.
- Avoid title case in full sentences.
- Use em dashes selectively, not in every paragraph.
- Do not use ellipses to manufacture suspense.
- Keep emoji out of core lifecycle and service messages unless a future campaign standard explicitly permits them.

## Voice by message type

### Service and transactional

Clear, factual and reassuring. Information comes before brand expression.

### Guidance and education

Expert, practical and generous. Explain enough to create confidence.

### Product recommendation

Specific and evidence-led. Connect customer inputs to the recommendation.

### Promotional

Controlled and commercially clear. State the value without hype or pressure.

### Recovery and problem resolution

Direct, accountable and empathetic. Do not bury the remedy beneath brand language.

### VIP

Recognising and service-led. Never imply social superiority or shame lower tiers.

## Voice QA

Before approval, ask:

- Does this sound like calm expert guidance?
- Is every claim supportable?
- Is the customer’s next step obvious?
- Is the language more precise than generic beauty marketing?
- Does the message reduce uncertainty?
- Would the copy still feel premium without exaggerated adjectives?
- Is personal language truthful rather than simulated?

If not, revise before production.
