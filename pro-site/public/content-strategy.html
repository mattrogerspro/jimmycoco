Content strategy: owning the UK spray tan business conversation

A plan for an education system that serves salon owners, feeds the email programme and gives the pro site something worth ranking. 8 August 2026.

01The finding that shapes everything

I expected a crowded field and a fight for differentiation. That is not the situation. The category is empty.

Across roughly 50 searches covering every operational question a UK salon owner asks about spray tanning, one pattern repeated: SalonGeek — an unstructured forum whose relevant threads date from 2008 to 2018 — holds a top-three position on about half of them. It ranks on tans per litre, compressor choice, COSHH, solution shelf life, patch test policy, bridal pricing and mobile profitability. Not because it is good. Because there is nothing else.

Where the forum does not rank, US brands do. Sjolie, Norvell, ArtesianTan and SprayTanClass occupy the results for UK business-economics, compliance and product-science queries, in dollars, under US regulation. An assistant asked "how much should a UK salon charge for a spray tan" currently has no British source to cite.

The one UK brand that attempts professional content is Sienna X, with 170–190 posts. Most of it is consumer tan-tips relabelled for therapists. Its flagship pricing article — the single most commercially valuable page in the category — was published in October 2017, runs about 450 words, contains one number, and ends by asking you to phone them.

The trade bodies are absent entirely. BABTAC's most recent surfacing asset on this subject is a PDF from 2016. The NHBF has nothing spray-tan-specific at all. Nobody occupies the role of official guidance, which means the role is available.

02Why the audience is receptive right now

This is not a good year to sell salons a product. It is an exceptional year to help them run a business.

The UK hair and beauty sector is under the heaviest cost pressure in a decade, and the specific shape of that pressure tells you what to write about. Employer National Insurance thresholds fell to £5,000 and are frozen to 2031. The National Living Wage rose to £12.71 in April. Hair and beauty now has the highest minimum-wage coverage of any occupation group in the country at 43.3%, which means wage floors hit this sector harder than almost any other. Business rates were overhauled in April 2026 and salons were pointedly excluded from the 15% discount extended to pubs and music venues.

The result: 20% of hair and beauty businesses were operating at a loss in March 2026, around three quarters describe margins as razor-thin, and 72.6% expected to raise prices within three months. Average salon profit margin sits at 8.2%.

Three further numbers define the editorial opportunity precisely, because each one is a business problem with a content answer:

29%of new salon clients ever rebook — 71% never returnPhorest, 2025, n=716 UK/IE
3.14%no-show rate for tanning — the highest of any treatment category, peaking Jun–AugTreatwell, 2025
4%average UK salon retail as a share of revenue, against a 15–25% benchmarkSalonIQ, 2026
70%of clients who don't buy retail say they would like toPhorest, 2025

Read those together and the proposition writes itself. Tanning has the worst no-show problem in the salon. Retail attach is a quarter of what it should be, and the clients are willing. Seven in ten new clients never come back. A supplier that helps fix those three things earns the right to sell solution — and each of them routes naturally to the litre, the retail range and the certification programme without a word of pitch.

03Positioning

The strategic claim

Sunless by Jimmy Coco becomes the business authority for UK spray tanning — the source a salon owner reaches for when deciding what to charge, whether they need a licence, how to stop no-shows, which solution suits a client's skin, and how to make the treatment actually profitable. Not the brand with the best tan tips. The brand that knows the numbers.

Three things make that defensible rather than aspirational.

Nobody else is doing it, as section 01 establishes. This is not a differentiation play against strong incumbents; it is occupation of vacant ground.

Jimmy is a genuine authority with press behind him — Vogue, The Times, Bloomberg, NBC News — which is exactly the E-E-A-T signal Google and AI assistants weight for advice content. We built that entity graph into the site last week, so the structured data already connects the person to the publication.

The commercial model aligns with the content. A supplier writing honestly about salon economics eventually has to talk about cost per tan, and £2.14 a tan on a £60 litre is a strong number that survives scrutiny. We are not writing around a weak product.

The tone follows from the audience. These are people running businesses on 8% margins who have been marketed at relentlessly. The content should read like a supplier who has seen inside a lot of salons and is telling you what actually works, including when the answer is unglamorous. Specific numbers, named sources, honest uncertainty. Where the evidence is thin — and section 07 shows it often is — we say so. That candour is the differentiation, because every competitor in the category states trade folklore as fact.

05The five pillars

Each pillar is a body of work we intend to own outright, not a category label. Priority reflects gap size multiplied by commercial value.

1. The Economics of Tanning

Priority 1

What a spray tan actually costs to deliver, what to charge, and how to make the treatment carry its own room. Total white space — there is no UK page anywhere that computes cost per tan in sterling, and the paid alternatives are US spreadsheets on Etsy.

Cost per tan and gross margin · pricing a treatment and a menu · regional price benchmarks · putting prices up without losing clients · retail attach economics · bridal, party and group pricing · membership and subscription models · capacity and utilisation · is tanning worth the room, against other treatments.

2. Compliance and Legitimacy

Priority 1

The questions people are afraid to ask out loud. Highest authority, lowest competition on the entire list — searches for UK licensing return US content and a 2008 forum thread, and insurance queries return nothing but quote forms.

Do you need a licence — local authority special treatments licensing, which genuinely varies by council · insurance: what cover, what qualification insurers accept, what a missed patch test does to a claim · COSHH assessment for DHA aerosol · risk assessment · patch testing protocol · contraindications and when to decline · consultation and record-keeping under GDPR · Consumer Rights Act and handling complaints · DHA concentration limits under UK and EU cosmetics regulation.

3. The Craft

Priority 2

Technique and troubleshooting at professional depth, grounded in actual science rather than trade folklore. This is where Jimmy's authority is most credible and where we can be genuinely better than everyone, because the competition repeats unsourced claims.

How DHA actually works · why tans go orange, with the four distinct causes separated · reading skin and choosing solution across the Fitzpatrick range · inclusive consultation and deeper skin tones · troubleshooting patchiness, green tones, failed development · hands, knees and the high-failure zones · prep and aftercare that clients will actually follow · equipment, cleaning and ventilation.

4. Filling the Diary

Priority 2

Demand, retention and the UK tanning year. Nothing UK-specific exists on seasonality anywhere — all results are US or Australian, which is useless when your peak is prom season and Christmas parties.

The UK spray tan year, month by month · surviving January · prom, wedding, holiday and party season · no-shows and deposits, where tanning is the worst category in the salon · rebooking, given 71% of new clients never return · Google Business Profile and local search for mobile therapists · Treatwell, Fresha and Booksy listings · social content that works for tanning · ASA rules on tanning claims.

5. Building the Business

Priority 3

Everything beyond the treatment — the broadest pillar, and the one that earns readers who do not yet buy solution.

Starting a tanning business or adding it to an existing salon · training: a neutral buyer's guide, since every current result is a course seller · hiring and keeping staff in a 16-week-to-fill market · employed vs self-employed vs rent-a-chair, and what the NIC changes did to that maths · the VAT threshold problem · stock control and ordering · salon software · what the 2026 business rates overhaul means.

07The evidence standard

This section exists because the research turned up a specific hazard, and because we have already been bitten once by a claim nobody could substantiate.

The technical research came back with an unusually long list of things the industry states as fact that are not established. "DHA resistance" as a physiological phenomenon: no evidence. Hormonal effects from pregnancy, PCOS or HRT on tan development: no peer-reviewed evidence, every claim traced to salon blogs. The "15% FDA limit": does not exist — 21 CFR 73.2150 contains no numeric cap. Erythrulose making tans last longer: contradicted by sources on both sides. Green cast on olive skin: asserted everywhere, verified nowhere. Two major suppliers give directly opposite advice on which skin tone violet base suits.

Meanwhile the genuinely strong material is barely used by anyone: the ACS Omega kinetics work on the Maillard mechanism, the UK SAG-CS 2024 opinion with actual modelled margins of safety for three booth architectures, Draelos on stratum corneum thickness governing colour depth, Jung et al. on free radical generation under UV.

The rule

Every factual claim carries a source, and the article renders its citations — the pro-site article schema already supports a citations array, so this is a discipline, not a build. Where the profession disagrees, we present the disagreement rather than picking a side and sounding confident. Where evidence does not exist, we say "this is practitioner experience, not established science."

This is not caution for its own sake. It is the entire competitive moat. Every rival in this category writes trade folklore in a confident voice. Being the one source that distinguishes what is known from what is assumed is what makes us the citable one — for salon owners and for the assistants answering their questions.

09How it connects to email and the site

The point of the system is that one piece of research produces three assets. This is the part that has to be designed rather than improvised, because it is where content programmes usually decay into a blog nobody reads.

1 · ResearchVerified sources and a claims register, held in 03-research/ and reusable across pieces
2 · ArticlePublished to the pro site through the existing articles system, with citations and FAQ schema
3 · EmailA campaign in email/campaigns/ that teaches the same thing and links to the article
4 · ToolWhere relevant, a calculator that makes the lesson usable and captures the address

Content into the email programme

The email strategy already organises around customer state rather than calendar, and already has a Campaign journey defined for "launches, seasonal education, editorial stories". Educational content slots into that journey rather than requiring a new one. Concretely:

A monthly Salon Business Brief to the trade list, built from that month's articles — this is the recurring reason to email people who have not yet bought anything, which the programme currently lacks. Article-derived nurture inserts into the existing salon onboarding and reseller lifecycle sequences, so a new account receives the pricing guide and the compliance pack in their first month. Seasonal triggers from the UK tanning year: the January survival piece lands in the first week of January, prom pricing in April, party season in October. And tool-triggered follow-ups — someone who runs the cost calculator gets the pricing article next, which is a genuine lifecycle state we can add to 02-audience-and-lifecycle-states.md.

Every one of those is an existing campaign folder pattern. Nothing new needs building in the email system — 06-distribution/ simply specifies which article becomes which campaign, and the campaign is authored with the skill that already exists.

Content into the website

The articles hub is live, indexed and empty, which the SEO audit flagged as actively harmful — it teaches Google the section is thin. Content fixes that directly. Beyond publishing, three structural links matter: articles cross-link to the product and the trial through the commercial route named in each brief; the calculators live on the site as tools rather than gated downloads, because gated content is invisible to both search and AI; and every article's citations and FAQ items strengthen the entity graph we built around Jimmy, since article authorship resolves to his @id.

The loop back

Email tells us which subjects the trade list actually opens. Search tells us which questions get asked. Both feed the next quarter's queue, held in 02-pillars/. The system should get better at choosing topics over time rather than working through a fixed list.
