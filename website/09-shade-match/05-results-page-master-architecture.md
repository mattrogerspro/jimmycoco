# 05 — Results Page Master Architecture

## Role

The results page converts the consultation into confidence and purchase. It must answer, in order:

1. What is my match?
2. Why does it suit me?
3. What result should I expect?
4. How do I use it?
5. What should I buy now?

## Recommended page sequence

### 1. Result confirmation

Use a calm label such as **“Your best match”** and a concise result statement tied to the customer’s goal.

### 2. Primary purchase zone

Show:

- accurate product image;
- product name and selected variant;
- price;
- result summary;
- review summary where verified;
- quantity;
- Add to Bag;
- delivery and returns reassurance;
- full product details link.

The selected variant must already be active.

### 3. Why this matches

Translate answer logic into three to four plain-language reasons, such as desired depth, control, application area and timing.

### 4. Expected result

State:

- expected visible depth;
- recommended coats;
- development time;
- finish or fading characteristics only where approved;
- what to adjust for a lighter or deeper result.

### 5. Matching real results

Show a small, relevant set filtered by product, depth, coats and skin-depth range where data permits. Clearly label lighting and development context.

### 6. Alternative option

Present one alternative with a meaningful trade-off, not a competing grid.

### 7. Complete the routine

Offer only genuinely useful Prepare, Apply and Perfect products.

### 8. Review answers

Allow customers to inspect and change their answers without losing the current result until recalculation is confirmed.

### 9. Save result

After the complete result is visible, offer optional email, account save or copy-link actions. Never obscure the result behind capture.

## Visual hierarchy

The primary product, selected variant and Add to Bag must dominate. Editorial explanation should increase confidence without pushing the buying controls below an unnecessarily large hero.

## Personalisation language

Use answer-based language carefully:

> Recommended for your preference for natural, buildable colour and greater application control.

Avoid invasive or deterministic language:

> We analysed your skin and know exactly how it will react.

## Persistent recommendation context

When the customer opens the full product page, show a quiet marker:

> Your Shade Match: Medium — 1 coat recommended

This context must not become a promotional badge or alter factual product information.

## Results-page states

- Standard high-confidence result
- Moderate-confidence result
- Low-confidence result
- Recommended product unavailable
- Returning saved result
- Result recalculated after changed answer
- Product or logic version updated

## Success criteria

The customer sees one clear recommendation, understands the reasoning within seconds and can add the correct variant without repeating decisions.
