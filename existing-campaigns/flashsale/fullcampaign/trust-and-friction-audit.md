# Trust, Risk & Friction Audit — Flash Sale Email

**Date:** 30 July 2026
**Verdict:** the email sells the *discount* well but does almost nothing to remove the *hesitation* that stops people acting on it. For a flash sale — which lives or dies on fast decisions — this is the biggest conversion gap in the whole campaign. Everything below uses your **real store terms** (pulled from the live product page), not assumptions.

---

## The principle

A discount creates desire. Trust signals remove the friction between desire and clicking buy. Every unanswered question is a reason to close the tab and "think about it" — and in a time-limited sale, "think about it" means lost.

Right now the email leaves all of these unanswered:

- *"When will it arrive?"* — no delivery time
- *"How much is shipping?"* — no shipping info (you offer **free over £30** — invisible here)
- *"What if it's the wrong shade / goes orange?"* — no reassurance (you have a **money-back guarantee** — invisible here)
- *"Can I spread the cost?"* — no payment options (you offer **Klarna** — invisible here)
- *"Is this a real, safe shop?"* — no payment badges, no reviews

You already hold the answers to every one of these. They're just not in the email.

## The high-impact fixes (with real copy + placement + the data)

### 1. A trust bar — highest ROI, ~20 minutes' work
A thin strip directly under the hero, four items, live text (no images needed):

> ✓ **Free UK delivery over £30**  ·  ✓ **14-day money-back guarantee**  ·  ✓ **Pay in 3 with Klarna**  ·  ✓ **Dispatched in 1–3 days**

This single row answers shipping, risk, payment and speed in one glance — the four questions that cause hesitation. It's the biggest return for the least effort in this whole email.

### 2. Risk reversal — the single most important one for self-tan
Self-tan has an unusually high *perceived* risk: "what if it's streaky, too dark, orange, wrong for me?" That fear is the number-one thing stopping a first-time or hesitant buyer — and a discount doesn't remove it, it can even amplify it ("cheap = maybe it's not great"). You have the antidote and aren't using it:

> **Love it or your money back.** Not your shade? Our 14-day 100% money-back guarantee means there's no risk in trying.

Place it near the bundles and again by the final CTA. Money-back guarantees measurably lift completion rates — and for self-tan specifically this is *the* lever.

### 3. Klarna on the £59 Glow Kit — dissolves the sticker shock
BNPL is the highest-impact checkout addition there is: **+78% checkout conversion, −10% abandonment, +20% average order value** ([Baymard/industry data 2026](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/checkout-conversion-statistics)). You offer Klarna. Put it on the highest-priced bundle where price friction is greatest:

> A-List Glow Kit — **£59**  *(or 3 interest-free payments of £19.67 with Klarna)*

£19.67 reads far softer than £59.

### 4. Delivery deadline — the honest urgency the client wouldn't give
The client declined a *sale* deadline. A *shipping* deadline is different — it's a fact, not a scarcity tactic, and it's perfect for self-tan because the purchase is event-driven (a night out, a holiday, the weekend):

> **Order by [Thu 4pm] for delivery in time for the weekend** — dispatched in 1–3 working days.

This creates real urgency with zero compliance risk, which resolves the tension we flagged earlier.

### 5. Free-shipping threshold as an AOV nudge
Free UK delivery kicks in at **£30**. Three of your four bundles already clear it — but the **Malibu Beach Duo at £28 sits £2 short.** That's a textbook nudge:

> Just £2 from free UK delivery — most bundles qualify already.

Shipping cost is the **#1 cause of checkout abandonment (48%)**, so surfacing "free over £30" is pure upside, and the £28-vs-£30 gap gently pushes people toward a bigger basket.

### 6. Payment badges in the footer
A row of accepted-payment icons (Visa · Mastercard · Amex · PayPal · Apple Pay · Klarna) is a universally-read "this is a real, secure shop" signal. **19–25% of shoppers abandon over security concerns**, and **13% leave when their payment method isn't shown** — you accept everything, so show it.

### 7. Reviews — the gap behind the gap
Star ratings are the highest-ROI trust element in ecommerce email — and **you have none on your site to show.** I won't put ratings in the email that don't exist. But this is the strategic miss: **start collecting reviews** (a post-purchase review-request flow in Klaviyo), because until you have them, every product card is missing its most persuasive trust signal. In the meantime, the Kylie section is your borrowed credibility — but it's currently just an image. Give it a line:

> *"[Kylie Jenner's] go-to glow"* — or a short real quote/credit you're cleared to use.

## Bottom line

The email is a strong *offer* with almost no *reassurance*. You're asking people to buy on impulse in a sale while leaving unanswered the four questions that make them hesitate — when you already have a good answer to every one (free delivery over £30, 14-day money-back guarantee, Klarna, 1–3 day dispatch). Adding them is low-effort and hits the highest-abandonment friction points directly. This is the most valuable change left on the table, and I under-weighted it first time round — you were right to push.
