# Email 01 — Continue Exploring

## Role

Reconnect the customer with the strongest genuine browse signal while the session is still recent.

This message must feel like a useful continuation, not a statement that the brand has been monitoring them.

## Default timing

Send approximately 2–4 hours after the qualifying session, subject to local-time, quiet-hour, consent and contact-pressure rules.

Do not send when a cart, checkout or purchase event has occurred.

## Primary objective

Return the customer to the most relevant product, collection or result path with as little friction as possible.

## Message hierarchy

1. Calm continuation headline
2. The strongest browsed product or category
3. One concise suitability or result statement
4. One primary CTA
5. Optional low-emphasis shade-match or guidance link when uncertainty is evident

## Recommended subject-line territories

Use recognition without surveillance.

Suitable directions:

- Continue finding your Sunless result
- A closer look at your glow options
- Your next step to a more natural tan
- Still deciding which finish feels right?

Avoid:

- We saw you looking
- You left this behind
- Caught your eye
- We noticed you browsing
- Your browser history says

## Preview-text function

Preview text should add the reason to return, such as suitability guidance, finish, development time or shade support. It should not repeat the subject line.

## Dynamic content priority

Select one primary context using this order:

1. Repeated product-detail interest
2. Engaged product-detail interest
3. Product linked from a shade-match or result-gallery journey
4. Narrow collection intent
5. Meaningful filtered collection state

Do not display a large grid of everything viewed. One strong signal is more persuasive and less intrusive.

## Product-led structure

When one product is clearly dominant:

- approved product image;
- product name;
- verified short descriptor;
- current price only when validated immediately before send;
- selected or inferred variant only when the evidence is reliable;
- one concise line explaining who it suits;
- CTA: `View the product` or `Continue exploring`.

Do not say a variant is “your shade” unless it came from a valid shade-match result.

## Collection-led structure

When intent is broader than one product:

- editorial collection image or approved product grouping;
- collection title;
- short explanation of the choice the collection helps solve;
- no more than three representative products;
- CTA: `Explore the collection`.

Products must be in stock or safely purchasable at render time.

## Guidance fallback

When browse behaviour suggests uncertainty rather than clear preference, the primary route may be:

- `Find my match`
- `Compare the options`
- `See how each finish works`

The message should reduce choice anxiety rather than force a product recommendation.

## Copy tone

- calm;
- editorial;
- expert but not clinical;
- no fake scarcity;
- no guilt;
- no discount by default;
- no claim that the customer personally selected or loved something unless that action was explicit.

## Visual structure

Recommended module order:

1. Email header
2. Compact editorial introduction
3. Dominant product or collection module
4. One reassurance or guidance module
5. Primary CTA
6. Standard footer

Keep the first useful CTA visible early on mobile.

## Asset rules

- Use only approved source product photography.
- Do not AI-redraw packaging, labels, colour, skin or product texture.
- Do not use celebrity or customer imagery unless rights, wording and context are approved for this specific message.
- Crops may adapt to the email frame, but source subjects must not be generatively altered.

## Required fallbacks

If the primary product becomes unavailable:

1. use an approved same-intent alternative when the merchandising relationship is explicit;
2. otherwise route to the relevant collection;
3. otherwise suppress the email.

Never display a broken image, invalid price or unavailable selected variant.

## Success event

Primary success is a qualified return to the relevant product, collection or guidance path. Add-to-cart and purchase are downstream outcomes, but click quality matters more than raw click volume.

## Exit and handoff

After click:

- a cart event hands ownership to cart abandonment;
- a purchase exits recovery and enters post-purchase logic;
- a completed shade match may hand ownership to shade-match follow-up;
- continued browsing may update the active browse state, but must not create a duplicate sequence.
