# Product Page — Application Method

## Purpose

The application chapter should make professional technique feel achievable at home. It must reduce fear of streaking, uneven colour, transfer and poor fading without overwhelming the visitor.

## Method architecture

Use four clear stages:

1. Prepare
2. Apply
3. Develop
4. Maintain

Each stage should state the action, timing and critical professional tip.

## Prepare

Include only relevant guidance:

- Exfoliation timing
- Hair-removal timing
- Skin dryness considerations
- Areas that may need a light barrier product
- Requirement for clean, dry skin

Avoid universal instructions that conflict with the specific formula.

## Apply

Explain:

- Required amount per body area
- Tool or mitt
- Working sequence
- Circular or sweeping technique where verified
- Treatment of hands, feet, knees, elbows and face
- Whether a second coat is appropriate

Jimmy’s professional tip should be concise and useful, not decorative endorsement copy.

## Develop

State:

- Minimum and maximum development window
- Whether colour guide transfer is possible
- Clothing advice
- Water, perspiration and activity restrictions
- Rinse instructions
- When the final colour becomes visible

All timings must match approved product data.

## Maintain

Cover:

- Moisturising
- Shower and exfoliation habits
- Expected fading pattern
- Reapplication timing
- How to correct uneven wear where appropriate

## Visual direction

Use an elegant linear ritual rather than four identical cards. Alternate close-up application photography, concise numbered instructions and product/tool details.

The chapter should feel instructional and calm:

- Warm neutral setting
- Real hands and skin texture
- Correct product and mitt
- Accurate amount of formula
- No theatrical splashes or impossible product motion

## Progressive disclosure

Show essential steps first. Place extended details, ingredients-specific cautions and FAQs below or inside accessible disclosures. Do not hide development time or rinse instructions.

## Video

A short method video may support the instructions but cannot replace them. Provide captions, controls and a transcript or equivalent text guidance.

## Error prevention

Proactively answer high-risk mistakes:

- Applying too much product to dry areas
- Missing blend points
- Dressing too quickly
- Exceeding development guidance
- Layering before the first coat is even
- Using the wrong accessory

## Copy style

Use direct, calm verbs:

- Exfoliate
- Apply
- Blend
- Allow to develop
- Rinse
- Moisturise

Avoid vague phrases such as “activate your glow ritual.”

## Accessibility

- Number steps semantically.
- Do not place instructions only inside photography.
- Give video controls accessible names.
- Keep timing and cautions visually distinct.
- Preserve the sequence on mobile.

## Failure modes

Reject sections that:

- use generic instructions unrelated to the product;
- omit timing;
- show incorrect product application;
- rely on tiny captions;
- create a four-card template with equal visual weight;
- use long lifestyle copy instead of actions;
- promise that mistakes are impossible.

## Success criteria

The customer should feel capable of reproducing the intended result and understand the few actions that most strongly affect the outcome.
