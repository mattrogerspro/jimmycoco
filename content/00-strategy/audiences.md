# Audiences

Four trade readers, in priority order, plus the constrained consumer case.
Every brief names exactly one primary reader.

The sector shape behind this: 51,821 UK hairdressing and beauty businesses
(IBISWorld 2025); 80%+ employ fewer than five people; 64% turn over under £99k;
**61% of the sector is self-employed**, up from 58% in 2024; 86% female-owned.
The modal reader of this programme is a woman running a business with fewer than
five people in it, on a margin around 8%.

---

## 1. The owner-operator — primary

Runs a salon, works in it, and does the books at 9pm. One to four staff, or
none. Turnover under £99k. Makes buying decisions alone and quickly.

**What she's actually asking**

- What does a tan cost me to put through the door?
- Am I charging enough? What's everyone else charging?
- Do I need a licence for this? Is my insurance actually covering it?
- Why don't my clients come back?

**How she reads.** On a phone, between clients or after close. Skims for the
number, then decides whether to read the reasoning. This is why every article
leads with the answer — see the structure patterns.

**What loses her.** A costing article with no cost in it. Anything that reads as
a product page wearing an article's clothes. American figures.

**Commercial route.** The litre, the trade account, the retail range. She is the
buyer for essentially everything we sell.

---

## 2. The mobile or home-based therapist

Roughly 40% of self-employed beauty professionals work mobile or from home. Very
often spray tanning is the *whole* business rather than one service on a menu,
which makes her the most engaged reader in the programme and the most
underserved by everyone else's content.

**What she's asking**

- Is mobile spray tanning actually profitable once I count the driving?
- What do I need in place to work from home legally — planning, licensing,
  insurance, the tent?
- How do I handle no-shows when I've already driven there?
- What do I charge versus a salon?

**Why she matters commercially out of proportion to her size.** She buys smaller
but she buys constantly, she switches brands on evidence rather than
relationship, and she is the most likely of any reader to share a link in a
Facebook group of 4,000 other mobile therapists.

**Nobody is writing for her.** Mobile-specific operational content in the UK is
close to non-existent.

---

## 3. The employed therapist

Works in someone else's salon. Doesn't buy. Reads for competence, for the
patch-test and consultation questions she's unsure about, and — often — because
she's costing out going independent.

**Why we write for her anyway.** She becomes reader 1 or reader 2 within a
couple of years, and she arrives already knowing the brand. She's also the
person who forwards a compliance piece to the owner.

**What she's asking.** Why did that tan go patchy? How long between waxing and
tanning? What am I legally required to ask before I spray? What should I be
earning?

**Route.** Craft pillar and compliance pillar. Newsletter, not product.

---

## 4. The multi-site operator

Two or more sites, or a salon with a team of six-plus. A minority of the market
but disproportionate order value.

**What he or she is asking.** Cost per treatment across sites. Consistency of
result between therapists. Stock control and shrinkage. Whether the tanning room
earns its floor space against the alternatives.

**Route.** Volume terms, trade account, multi-site pricing. Content that
supports this reader is mostly pillar 5.

---

## The consumer reader — constrained

Per [decision 1](decisions.md#1-audience-trade-led-with-some-consumer),
consumer topics appear only as **assets a therapist can hand to a client**.

The rules, restated because this is the one that will drift:

1. Category is **"For your clients"**.
2. The article opens addressed to the therapist — what this is for, when to send
   it — then delivers the client-facing content.
3. It carries a *Share this with your client* block with a copyable summary.
4. No more than roughly one in five published pieces.
5. If it can't be justified as a therapist-to-client asset, it belongs on
   jimmycoco.co.uk, not here.

Candidates: why tans go orange · how to make a spray tan last · what to do
before your appointment · what a patch test is and why you're being asked.
