# 07 — Proof Verification and Disclosures

## Purpose

This document defines the minimum evidence standards required before a result may appear in the Sunless Results Gallery.

The goal is not to make every image look clinical. It is to make every claim traceable, proportionate and honest.

## Provenance categories

Every result must be assigned one provenance type:

### Verified customer result

Submitted by a confirmed purchaser or captured through an approved customer programme, with product and method details verified where possible.

### Sunless studio result

Captured by the Sunless team under documented application and photography conditions.

### Campaign result

Created for editorial or advertising use with professional production. Styling and lighting must be disclosed.

### Customer-submitted result

Provided by a customer but not fully verified against purchase or application records.

## Minimum record

Before publication, store:

- result ID;
- consent status;
- source and provenance;
- original image files;
- capture date where available;
- product and variant used;
- number of coats;
- development time;
- application tool;
- preparation steps where known;
- lighting description;
- editing or retouching record;
- approval status;
- expiry or review date for consent where applicable.

## Claims rule

The gallery may describe what happened in a documented result. It must not generalise one result into a guaranteed universal outcome.

Prefer:

> One coat developed for six hours on medium neutral skin.

Avoid:

> Guaranteed deep bronze in six hours.

## Disclosure placement

Display essential context beside the result. Longer provenance and image-integrity guidance may live in a clearly linked disclosure page.

Do not hide material differences in small-print footnotes.

## Required result disclosure

Use a concise statement such as:

> Individual results vary by starting skin tone, preparation, application, development time and lighting.

Where lighting or framing differs materially, use a more specific note:

> Images were captured in different locations and are shown for directional comparison only.

## Retouching and editing

Permitted only when it preserves the factual appearance of the source:

- crop and straightening;
- consistent exposure correction;
- removal of dust or sensor artefacts;
- global colour correction applied consistently.

Not permitted for proof imagery:

- changing skin colour;
- selective warming or saturation;
- skin smoothing;
- reshaping the body or face;
- removing streaks, patches or texture;
- replacing backgrounds in a way that changes perceived colour;
- AI reconstruction or generative fill over relevant skin areas.

## Consent

Consent must cover:

- publication on the website;
- marketing reuse where applicable;
- name or anonymised attribution;
- duration and withdrawal process;
- any paid, gifted or incentivised participation.

A withdrawal process must exist even where the original submission was voluntary.

## Incentivised content

Where a product, payment or benefit was provided in return for content, retain that fact in the record and disclose it where legally or ethically required.

## Celebrity and public-figure imagery

Use only imagery with documented rights and approved usage. Never imply product use or endorsement solely because a person appears in a photograph.

Celebrity photographs must not be altered to create synthetic evidence, invented product use or false before-and-after comparisons.

## Review governance

Each result requires named approval from the responsible brand or content owner before publication. Results should be re-reviewed when:

- product names or formulas change;
- the linked SKU is discontinued;
- consent changes;
- new evidence shows the metadata is inaccurate;
- the image no longer meets current integrity standards.

## Success criteria

A result is publishable only when its provenance, method, rights and disclosures are strong enough that the brand could explain them confidently to a customer, regulator or platform reviewer.
