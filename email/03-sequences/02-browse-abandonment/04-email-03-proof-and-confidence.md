# Email 03 — Proof and Confidence

## Role

Replace uncertainty with credible evidence connected to the customer’s strongest browse intent.

The message should show what the product or category can realistically achieve, how it is used and why the result is trustworthy.

## Default timing

Send approximately 48 hours after Email 02 when the contact remains eligible and no higher-priority state has taken ownership.

## Primary objective

Increase confidence through relevant verified proof rather than repetition, urgency or discounting.

## Proof hierarchy

Use the strongest approved evidence available in this order:

1. verified before-and-after result using the relevant product or routine;
2. verified customer review tied to the relevant product or result;
3. approved professional-method explanation;
4. verified application demonstration;
5. relevant aggregate review data when current and substantiated.

Do not include weak or unrelated proof merely to fill the email.

## Relevance rules

Proof should match the browse context wherever data permits:

- same product;
- same product family;
- same intended result depth;
- same face or body use case;
- same application format;
- same routine stage.

Do not infer protected, sensitive or personal characteristics from browse behaviour. Do not claim that a customer result is “like yours” unless the comparison is supported by explicit, consented shade-match data and approved wording.

## Before-and-after requirements

Any before-and-after module must state or link to the relevant context, including where available:

- product and variant used;
- number of applications or coats;
- development time;
- preparation method;
- lighting or image-standard disclosure;
- whether the result is customer-submitted or brand-produced.

The source photographs must remain authentic. Do not use generative editing, skin retouching, colour manipulation, body reshaping or selective relighting.

## Review requirements

Reviews must be:

- traceable to an approved review source;
- current enough for use;
- reproduced accurately;
- free from invented names, scores or quotations;
- clearly connected to the reviewed product when presented as product proof.

Do not shorten a quotation in a way that changes its meaning.

## Message hierarchy

1. Confidence-led headline
2. One dominant proof story
3. Concise explanation of the method or product connection
4. One primary CTA back to the relevant product or result page
5. Optional secondary link to more real results

## Subject-line territories

- See the result in real life
- Real results, explained clearly
- The proof behind the finish
- A closer look at the Sunless result

Avoid exaggerated language such as “guaranteed,” “perfect on everyone” or “flawless every time.”

## Visual structure

1. Header
2. Short editorial introduction
3. Dominant proof image or review module
4. Result-context caption
5. Product connection
6. Primary CTA
7. Optional results-gallery link
8. Footer

The proof must remain understandable when images are blocked. Use descriptive alt text and meaningful live text.

## CTA guidance

Use one primary action based on the context:

- `See the product`
- `View the full result`
- `Explore real results`
- `Find my match`

Do not split attention between multiple products unless the browse intent is genuinely category-level.

## Required validation

Immediately before send, validate:

- consent and suppression;
- source and usage rights for every image;
- review provenance and wording;
- product association;
- disclosure copy;
- destination URLs;
- product, price and stock state;
- no cart, checkout or purchase event;
- no newer recommendation or browse state supersedes the selected proof.

## Suppression and handoff

Exit or transfer when the customer:

- purchases;
- adds the relevant item to cart;
- begins checkout;
- completes a more specific shade match;
- withdraws consent;
- enters a higher-priority service state.

## Success criteria

Measure qualified product return, full-result views, shade-match starts and completions, add-to-cart and purchase. Review complaint, unsubscribe and negative-engagement signals to ensure proof is building trust rather than feeling intrusive.
