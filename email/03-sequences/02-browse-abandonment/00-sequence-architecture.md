# Browse Abandonment — Sequence Architecture

## Purpose

Recover genuine product interest by reducing uncertainty and restoring the easiest relevant path back to the website.

Browse abandonment sits between general lifecycle marketing and cart abandonment. It should never carry the urgency or commercial pressure of a cart flow because the visitor has not yet made a purchase commitment.

## Eligible intent hierarchy

Rank behaviour using the strongest recent signal:

1. Repeated views of one product detail page
2. Long or engaged product-detail view
3. Product detail plus shade-match interaction
4. Repeated views within one collection
5. Meaningful collection filtering or sorting
6. Results-gallery interaction connected to a product

A single shallow page view is not sufficient.

## Default cadence

- **Email 1:** approximately 2–4 hours after the qualifying session
- **Email 2:** approximately 24 hours later if no cart or purchase
- **Email 3:** approximately 48 hours later
- **Email 4:** approximately 5 days after entry

Apply local-time and quiet-hour rules. Do not send more than one behavioural marketing email in a day.

## Message progression

1. **Continuation:** make it easy to return to the strongest interest.
2. **Guidance:** explain who the product or category suits and how options differ.
3. **Proof:** show relevant verified results or reviews.
4. **Closure:** offer one final useful return path without fake urgency.

## Priority and flow ownership

Higher-priority states suppress browse abandonment:

1. Transactional and service communications
2. Active cart abandonment
3. Active checkout recovery
4. Post-purchase messaging
5. Shade-match follow-up where a recommendation is more specific than browse intent
6. Browse abandonment
7. General welcome or campaign messaging

## Immediate exit conditions

Exit when:

- any purchase is completed;
- the browsed item is added to cart and cart abandonment becomes eligible;
- marketing consent is withdrawn;
- the address becomes suppressed or undeliverable;
- the customer begins a more specific shade-match flow;
- the relevant item and all safe alternatives become unavailable;
- the sequence reaches its final send.

## Frequency controls

- Maximum one browse sequence within a rolling 14-day period unless the customer demonstrates clearly different high-intent behaviour.
- Maximum one active browse sequence at a time.
- Repeated views should update the existing state rather than create duplicate enrolments.
- Campaign pressure and other automated flows must be considered before each send.

## Commercial restraint

Discounts are not a default browse-abandonment device. Guidance, suitability, proof and clarity should recover intent first. Any incentive must be governed by an approved commercial policy and must not train customers to browse solely to trigger offers.
