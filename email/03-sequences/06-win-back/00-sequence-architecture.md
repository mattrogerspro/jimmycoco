# Win-Back — Sequence Architecture

## Purpose

Re-engage customers whose relationship with Sunless has meaningfully lapsed, while protecting trust, margin, sender reputation and customer choice.

Win-back is not a generic promotional blast. It is a controlled lifecycle intervention for customers who were previously active and now show a credible change in behaviour.

## Default cadence

- **Email 1:** when the contact first crosses the approved lapse threshold
- **Email 2:** approximately 4–5 days later
- **Email 3:** approximately 5–7 days later
- **Email 4:** approximately 7 days later

Apply local time, quiet hours, global contact-pressure rules and behaviour-based suppression before every send.

## Message progression

1. **Recognition without guilt:** reintroduce the brand and provide a relevant path back.
2. **Current value:** explain what has changed, improved or become easier.
3. **Reason to return:** provide a stronger commercial or experiential reason, with incentives only when policy permits.
4. **Preference reset or closure:** let the customer update frequency, pause, unsubscribe or continue the relationship on their terms.

## Eligible customer types

Potentially eligible contacts include:

- previous purchasers whose normal repurchase cycle has been exceeded;
- repeat purchasers who have stopped purchasing for materially longer than their established pattern;
- formerly engaged subscribers with meaningful historical browse, shade-match or purchase activity;
- lapsed high-value customers without unresolved service concerns;
- first-time purchasers who completed delivery and education flows but did not return inside an evidence-based window.

A subscriber who merely stopped opening email is not automatically eligible. Opens are incomplete and increasingly unreliable as a primary engagement signal.

## Flow priority

Higher-priority states suppress win-back:

1. Transactional and service communication
2. Active support, complaint, return, refund or safety case
3. Checkout or cart recovery
4. Post-purchase education
5. Replenishment based on a credible product need
6. Active shade-match follow-up
7. Win-back
8. General campaigns

## Immediate exit conditions

Exit when:

- a purchase is completed;
- a meaningful cart or checkout state begins and the relevant recovery flow takes ownership;
- a new replenishment need becomes more specific;
- marketing consent is withdrawn;
- the address hard-bounces, complains or becomes suppressed;
- a support, refund, return, replacement or safety case becomes active;
- the customer requests reduced frequency, a pause or no further marketing;
- the sequence reaches its final send.

## Re-entry rules

- A completed purchase resets lapse state.
- Do not re-enter a customer immediately after an unsuccessful win-back cycle.
- Default minimum cooldown: 90 days after sequence completion, subject to product cycle and jurisdiction.
- Re-entry should require new evidence of prior reactivation followed by a fresh lapse.
- Repeated non-engagement should progressively reduce contact frequency rather than trigger endless win-back attempts.

## Commercial restraint

Discounting must not be the default mechanism. The sequence should first use:

- better guidance;
- relevant newness;
- a more suitable product path;
- shade-match support;
- routine simplification;
- verified proof;
- improved convenience.

Any incentive must be approved, margin-aware, time-bounded and validated at send and redemption.
