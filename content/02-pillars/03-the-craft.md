## Why this is third, despite being where our expertise is

Two reasons, and they pull in opposite directions.

Against going first: it is the most crowded space in the category. Every tanning
brand publishes technique content. It is also the space where the industry's
folklore is thickest, so it takes the most research per published word.

For going first: it is the only pillar with genuinely strong primary literature
behind it, and almost nobody uses it. The ACS Omega kinetics paper, Draelos on
stratum corneum thickness, Jung et al. on free radical generation, the SAG-CS
opinion — these exist, they're rigorous, and the trade writes as though they
don't. An article that explains *why* knees go dark using the actual mechanism
is a categorically different object from the fifty pages that say "exfoliate
well!".

So: third, but done properly. Craft content carries Jimmy's byline and is the
pillar most likely to be cited by another professional.

## The four sources that carry this pillar

Use these. They're in the [source library](../03-research/source-library.md).

| Source | What it gives us |
|---|---|
| **ACS Omega kinetics paper** (PMC9753197) | The Maillard mechanism, and the influence of pH and temperature. **Temperature is the single most influential variable on browning intensity** — a fact with enormous practical consequence that essentially nobody in the trade knows. |
| **Draelos** | Colour depth is proportional to stratum corneum thickness. This one fact explains dark knees, dark elbows, light faces, and *why* exfoliation works — it replaces an entire genre of folklore. |
| **Jung et al.** | DHA-treated skin generates >180% more free radicals under UV. A DHA tan gives roughly SPF 3–4 and is not sun protection. |
| **UK SAG-CS 2024 opinion** | Regulator modelling of booth exposure, with margins of safety by booth type. UK limits: leave-on 14%, rinse-off 22.5%. EU 10%. |

## The folklore problem

More claims-register entries touch this pillar than any other. Before writing
anything here, read
[`03-research/claims-register.md`](../03-research/claims-register.md) in full.
The ones that will come up constantly:

- **"DHA resistance"** — no evidence it exists. Draelos explains the observation
  better.
- **Hormonal effects** (pregnancy, PCOS, HRT) on development — no peer-reviewed
  evidence; every claim traces back to salon blogs.
- **Erythrulose "makes tans last longer"** — sources contradict each other.
- **Green or grey cast on olive skin** — asserted everywhere, verified nowhere.
  Green *underarms* from aluminium in deodorant is a different thing and is
  verified.
- **Violet base for cool skin (goGLOW) vs warm skin (Sjolie)** — directly
  opposite advice from two credible trainers, no study either way.
- **Higher DHA = darker vs faster** — unsettled.
- **Shaving and waxing intervals** — the numbers everyone quotes have no
  evidenced basis.
- **Application sequence** — no standard exists.
- **Acetone as a gun cleaner** — one source recommends it; it will destroy
  Teflon seals. Do not repeat.

Addressing these head-on is the pillar's differentiator, not an obstacle to it.

## Articles

### 1. Why tans go patchy, orange or grey — `why-tans-go-wrong`

The diagnostic hub. Symptom → mechanism → cause in the room → prevention → fix.
Structured so a therapist can find her specific problem in thirty seconds.

Built on Draelos and the kinetics paper. Where the trade explanation is
folklore, say so and give the real one.

**Pattern:** C · **CTA:** prep article

### 2. What DHA actually does to your skin — `what-dha-does`

The foundational explainer. Maillard reaction, stratum corneum, why it's not a
dye and not a stain, why temperature and pH matter, why it fades the way it
does. The piece that makes every other craft article make sense, and the one
most likely to be cited.

**Pattern:** C · **Sources:** ACS Omega, Draelos, 21 CFR 73.2150

### 3. Prepping skin: what matters and what doesn't — `skin-prep-that-matters`

Exfoliation (and why it works — thickness, not "removing dead skin" as a vague
good), moisturiser barriers, deodorant, oils, makeup. Honest about the intervals
nobody can evidence: waxing, shaving, and benzoyl peroxide all get the
"unevidenced" treatment rather than an invented number.

**Pattern:** C · **Register:** heavy — read it first

### 4. Why a tan develops differently on different parts of the body — `uneven-development`

Draelos, applied. Knees, elbows, ankles, hands, face, back. The single most
useful mechanism in the pillar and it explains most of what therapists ask about.

**Pattern:** C

### 5. Is a spray tan sun protection? — `is-a-spray-tan-sun-protection`

No — roughly SPF 3–4 — and Jung et al. found DHA-treated skin generates >180%
more free radicals under UV. Important, well-evidenced, and a genuine duty-of-care
message a therapist can pass to a client.

Strong candidate to also run as a **"For your clients"** asset.

**Pattern:** C · **Sources:** Jung et al.

### 6. Fixing a tan that's gone wrong — `fixing-a-tan`

Practical remediation. What can be corrected, what has to fade, what to say to
the client. Routes to the mitt and the range where it's the honest answer.

**Pattern:** C · **CTA:** the range

## Standing rules

- Jimmy's byline, so it must sound like a practitioner: specific, calm, from the
  room.

  without a source is exactly the folklore we're displacing.
- Where the trade disagrees, name who holds which position.
- No PSI, CFM or ACH figures — see the claims register.
