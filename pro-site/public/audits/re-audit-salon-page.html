Conversion re-audit: the salon page, after the build-out.

The same thirty-phase standard, re-run against the salon page now live at www.jimmycoco.pro. The trajectory reads 4.6 → 8.0 → 8.2 → 8.8. The finding that capped all three previous passes is gone: the forms no longer submit by mailto: — they write to a database, and there is a reviewed pipeline behind them. Accessibility and performance are now measured rather than inferred, and both are at or near 100. What has not moved is the other capping finding: there is still not one salon owner's voice on this page. That is now the highest-value fix on the site.

Three bars per category: the original entry page (pale), the 16 July pass (bronze) and now (green). The delta shown is 16 Jul → now. The gains cluster exactly where the work went: Form UX +2.5 and Accessibility +2.6 are the two largest movements in the history of this audit, because each was held down by a single blocking defect rather than a spread of small ones. Trust & proof +1.0 reflects real press and credentials arriving, but it is still the second-lowest bar and will not clear 9 without a named salon. Copywriting falls 0.2 — the only regression in this pass, and a self-inflicted one, explained below.

Remaining findings, ranked

High1 · No salon owner's voice — unchanged across three audits

Problem: the page sells to salon owners and quotes exactly one customer — a consumer, on the tan itself. Alongside it sits an aggregate survey stat. Neither is a trade buyer saying what a trade buyer needs to hear: that it sold, that clients rebooked, that the margin was real. Every other trust signal here is the brand asserting its own credibility — Hollywood, red carpet, accreditation, press. Those establish glamour. They do not de-risk a £60 first order for a salon owner in Warrington.

Fix: one named salon, one photograph, one number. "We put 40 clients through the first litre in six weeks and 12 rebooked inside a month." That single block lifts Trust & proof, Conversion psychology and Competitive strength together. It is the cheapest remaining point on the board, and has been for three passes.

High2 · Trial product naming must remain consistent

Current state: the primary conversion point now names Malibu Professional Spray (10% DHA), matching the product card, CTAs and schema. A prospect sees one clear product name from the first page view through to the trial request.

Guardrail: keep the same Malibu name in all future trial, calculator and product copy.

High3 · Lead capture works; the confirmation email does not exist

Problem: new this pass, and a direct consequence of fixing the mailto: wiring. An applicant now submits successfully, sees an on-screen thank-you — and receives nothing. The uk-reseller-lifecycle campaign is wired but disabled, with no templates built. Under the old mailto: the applicant at least had a sent item in their own outbox as proof. Now they have neither. Silence after submission is the worst possible state for a business asking salons to hand over details and wait.

Fix: build the four templates and enable the campaign. Until then, the on-screen confirmation should state plainly when a human will reply.

Medium4 · The trial box heading no longer matches its contents

Problem: the heading reads "Become a Jimmy Coco Certified Salon." The two items beneath are the trial solution and the shade guide — the contents of a sample box, not a certification programme. Certification is a strong offer and now has its own section at the foot of the page; borrowing its headline here muddies both. This is the copywriting regression noted in the scorecard.

Medium5 · Two different numbers for the same claim

Problem: the credentials block says over 15 years. The story paragraph says more than 15 years. The trial box says 20+ years of red-carpet colour. Three claims, two numbers, one page. Whichever is right, a reader who notices trusts the rest slightly less — and this page's proposition rests on Jimmy's track record.

Medium6 · No FAQ at the point of decision

Problem: the salon FAQ — equipment compatibility, yield, mobile pros, training, trade terms — lives on the product page. Those are precisely the objections raised immediately before requesting a trial, and the trial form is on this page. The answers exist; they are in the wrong place.

Medium7 · Trade terms still deferred to a call

Problem: margin, minimum order and lead time are all "confirmed on your setup call". To a cautious buyer that reads as pricing that varies by how hard you push. A published indicative margin band removes the objection without conceding negotiating room.

Low8 · Audit files ship publicly

Problem: this document sits under public/audits/ and is fetchable by anyone who guesses the path. It is noindex, but an internal commercial review naming weaknesses should not be one URL away from a prospect.

Fix: move it out of public/ and serve it behind the admin login.

4.6 → 8.0 → 8.2 → 8.8. For the first time the page is not being held back by its plumbing.

Three audits ago this page could not capture a lead. Two audits ago it captured them into an email client most visitors do not have. It now captures them into a database, routes them through an approval queue, and issues a portal account with trade pricing at the other end. That is a different class of asset, and the technical scores — 100 accessibility, 100 desktop performance, zero layout shift — say it is built properly.

What remains is not technical. Of the eight open findings, six are copy or content, and one of them is a single word. The ceiling is now set by the fact that the page asks salon owners to trust it without letting a single salon owner speak. Fix that, fix the product-name mismatch and turn on the confirmation email, and the next pass clears 9.2 without a line of new code.

Scoring note: Form UX and Accessibility moved further in one pass than in the previous two combined, because each was capped by one blocking defect rather than a spread of small ones. Mobile is now measured rather than inferred. Copywriting is the only decline.
