# Alt Text and Accessibility Assistance

## Purpose

Define how AI may assist with alt text and accessibility review without replacing human judgement.

## Alt-text principles

Alt text should communicate the image’s function in the email, not describe every visible detail.

Use:

- concise product identification when the image supports purchase;
- result context when documentary evidence is essential;
- action or destination context for linked images;
- empty alt text for purely decorative imagery;
- surrounding copy rather than duplicating long visual descriptions.

## AI-assisted alt-text workflow

Provide the model with:

- final approved image;
- email purpose;
- surrounding headline and body copy;
- image link destination;
- whether the asset is decorative, informative, functional or documentary;
- approved product and person names where permitted.

Request:

- one concise recommendation;
- one shorter fallback;
- explanation of what information should remain in visible copy instead.

## Protected-person rules

Do not use AI to identify an unnamed person from an image. Names must come from approved metadata. Do not infer age, ethnicity, health, identity, endorsement or relationship.

## Product rules

Use approved catalogue names and variants. Do not infer shade, ingredients, size or performance from appearance.

## Accessibility audit assistance

AI may flag:

- vague link labels;
- repeated alt text;
- image-dependent instructions;
- missing headings;
- confusing reading order;
- unexplained abbreviations;
- inaccessible date or price formatting;
- excessive text embedded in imagery.

## Human review

A human reviewer must verify that alt text:

- matches the final crop;
- does not repeat adjacent copy unnecessarily;
- uses approved names and claims;
- remains concise;
- communicates function;
- works when the image is blocked;
- does not expose private or inferred information.
