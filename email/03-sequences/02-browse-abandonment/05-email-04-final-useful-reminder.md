# Email 04 — Final Useful Reminder

## Role

Close the browse-abandonment sequence with one final, genuinely useful route back to the customer’s strongest interest.

This is not a countdown, last-chance message or artificial scarcity device. It should feel like a respectful final nudge before the brand stops following up on that browse session.

## Default timing

Send approximately five days after sequence entry, subject to eligibility, local time, contact pressure and flow ownership.

## Primary objective

Offer one low-friction next step that resolves the most likely remaining uncertainty.

## Message strategy

Choose one route only:

- return to the strongest product;
- revisit the relevant collection;
- compare two closely related options;
- complete shade match;
- view real results;
- read concise application guidance.

The route should be selected from the latest valid behavioural state, not frozen from the original session when newer eligible evidence exists.

## Message hierarchy

1. Calm closing headline
2. One sentence acknowledging the decision may still be open
3. One useful product, comparison or guidance module
4. One primary CTA
5. Optional preference-management link in the footer

## Subject-line territories

- One last guide to choosing your result
- Still deciding? Start here
- Your clearest next step
- A final look at the options

Avoid:

- Last chance
- Don’t miss out
- Your item is waiting
- Final hours
- We won’t remind you again

unless a real, independently valid promotion or stock deadline exists and has been approved for this customer state.

## Incentive policy

A discount is not part of the default flow.

An incentive may appear only when:

- it is authorised by the commercial policy;
- the contact is eligible;
- it does not conflict with another active offer;
- the expiry and terms are real;
- the sequence can accurately validate the code immediately before send.

Do not invent urgency around the incentive or imply exclusivity unless it is genuine.

## Product route

When one product remains clearly dominant, include:

- approved product image;
- current product name;
- one concise suitability statement;
- validated price only when useful;
- one CTA: `View the product`.

## Guidance route

When uncertainty remains stronger than product intent, use a decision-support CTA such as:

- `Find my match`
- `Compare the finishes`
- `See real results`
- `Learn the application method`

This route should be preferred over weak or speculative product personalisation.

## Visual structure

Keep the email intentionally compact:

1. Header
2. Closing editorial message
3. One product or guidance module
4. Primary CTA
5. Footer

Do not add a large recommendation grid, unrelated best sellers or multiple promotional banners.

## Required validation

Before send, confirm:

- current marketing consent;
- no suppression, complaint or hard bounce;
- no cart, checkout or purchase event;
- no higher-priority flow owns the contact;
- target product, collection or tool is valid;
- price, stock, variant and URL are current;
- all imagery and claims are approved;
- global and sequence frequency limits are respected;
- the final-send idempotency key has not been used.

## Sequence completion

After this email:

- mark the browse sequence complete;
- preserve outcome and engagement data;
- clear active flow ownership;
- do not re-enrol the contact for the same or substantially similar intent inside the rolling cooldown window;
- allow future lifecycle or campaign eligibility according to contact-pressure policy.

## Success criteria

Evaluate qualified return visits, decision-support interactions, shade-match completion, add-to-cart and purchase. Also monitor unsubscribes, complaints and inactivity to confirm that the final reminder remains restrained and useful.
