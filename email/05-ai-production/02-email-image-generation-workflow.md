# Email Image Generation Workflow

## Purpose

Define the controlled workflow for creating AI-assisted email imagery that remains faithful to Sunless brand, product truth and approved source assets.

## Workflow stages

1. Brief
2. Source validation
3. Prompt assembly
4. Low-risk concept generation
5. Art-direction review
6. Asset-faithfulness review
7. Email crop and responsive review
8. Accessibility and performance review
9. Approval and export
10. Archive and traceability

## Stage 1 — Brief

Record:

- message ID and sequence;
- image role;
- required size and aspect ratio;
- target module;
- customer state;
- visual objective;
- source assets;
- copy-safe area;
- prohibited changes;
- owner and approver.

## Stage 2 — Source validation

Confirm every supplied image is approved and classified as:

- immutable documentary source;
- immutable product source;
- immutable celebrity source;
- immutable customer-result source;
- brand reference;
- generative reference;
- decorative generation input.

Immutable assets may be positioned or cropped only within the approved rules. They must not be regenerated.

## Stage 3 — Prompt assembly

The prompt must separate:

- what must remain exact;
- what may be generated;
- composition;
- lighting;
- surfaces;
- scale;
- negative space;
- email-safe crop requirements;
- exclusions and failure conditions.

## Stage 4 — Concept generation

Generate only the elements approved for synthesis. For mixed compositions, create the generative environment separately from protected product or person assets whenever possible.

## Stage 5 — Art-direction review

Evaluate:

- brand consistency;
- visual hierarchy;
- emotional tone;
- composition at email width;
- copy-safe area;
- uniqueness versus recent sends;
- absence of generic AI artefacts.

## Stage 6 — Asset-faithfulness review

Compare protected assets against their source files at high magnification.

Reject when there is any unapproved change to:

- faces;
- body shape;
- skin tone;
- product colour;
- packaging;
- logos;
- label typography;
- cap, pump or bottle geometry;
- customer-result evidence.

## Stage 7 — Email crop review

Test desktop, mobile and high-density exports.

Confirm:

- focal subject remains visible;
- crop does not remove product identity;
- text remains outside critical detail;
- mobile crop is intentionally art-directed;
- image remains understandable when partially clipped by clients;
- no essential information depends on imagery.

## Stage 8 — Accessibility and performance

Prepare:

- concise functional alt text;
- optional empty alt for purely decorative assets;
- width and height attributes;
- compressed WebP, PNG or JPEG as appropriate;
- fallback format when required;
- file-size target;
- dark-mode review.

## Stage 9 — Approval and export

The approver must review the image inside the final email template with real copy and production data.

Store:

- approved master;
- desktop export;
- mobile export;
- source links;
- prompt version;
- generation tool and model;
- approval record.

## Stage 10 — Archive

Archive rejected and approved generations separately. Never allow an unapproved draft to be selected through an ambiguous filename.

## Release blockers

Do not release when:

- a protected asset has been altered;
- product presentation is inaccurate;
- image rights are unclear;
- crop fails on mobile;
- file size is unreasonable;
- the image makes an unsupported result or endorsement claim;
- traceability is incomplete.
