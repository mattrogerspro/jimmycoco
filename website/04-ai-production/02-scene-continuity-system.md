# Scene Continuity System

## Purpose

This document ensures that independently generated homepage chapters assemble into one coherent Sunless experience rather than a collection of unrelated concepts.

The production method intentionally creates scenes separately. Continuity therefore has to be designed, documented and checked.

## Governing principle

Consistency and variety are not opposites.

The brand language must remain constant while the architecture, density and mood change deliberately from chapter to chapter.

## Elements that must remain constant

Across every scene, preserve:

- Logo proportions and clear space
- Serif and sans-serif character
- Matte-black primary CTA language
- Product packaging geometry
- Warm neutral colour family
- Natural, editorial skin photography
- Fine-rule and icon language
- Corner-radius family
- Maximum content-width logic
- Copy tone
- Product naming conventions
- Trust language
- Level of visual restraint

## Elements that must vary

Deliberately vary:

- Background tone
- Chapter height
- Image scale
- Content density
- Alignment
- Text-to-image ratio
- Use of the grid
- Emotional atmosphere
- Number and type of controls
- Amount of whitespace
- Full-bleed versus contained behaviour

## Adjacent-scene rule

No two consecutive scenes may share the same combination of:

- Background family
- Horizontal alignment
- Card structure
- Image scale
- Heading placement
- Density
- Vertical padding

If adjacent scenes look interchangeable at thumbnail size, at least one must be redesigned.

## Approved visual cadence

Recommended sequence:

1. Dense cinematic hero — warm ivory
2. Integrated authority transition — quiet and compact
3. Contained recommendation workspace — soft stone
4. Airy method spread — bright white
5. Photography-dominant proof — image-led tonal field
6. Curated product gallery — clean white
7. Asymmetrical founder editorial — natural linen
8. Minimal comparison — bright white
9. Human testimonial environment — warm taupe
10. Linear ritual scene — clean white
11. Dark cinematic conversion — deep charcoal
12. Quiet community and footer utility — reduced visual weight

## Transition design

Every scene should consider both its entry and exit.

### Entry requirements

Ask:

- What visual energy is arriving from the previous scene?
- Does the new scene need contrast or continuity?
- Where should the eye land first?
- Is the background transition deliberate?

### Exit requirements

Ask:

- What question remains unresolved?
- What should motivate the next scroll?
- Is there a visual or semantic handoff?
- Does the lower edge prepare the next background and density change?

Avoid hard divider lines unless structurally necessary.

## Colour continuity

The palette may shift in tone, but it must remain within the approved Sunless world.

Continuity anchors:

- Deep charcoal text
- Warm ivory and stone neutrals
- Champagne and linen warmth
- Natural bronze skin
- Matte-black CTAs
- Muted gold only as a restrained detail

Do not introduce a new accent colour simply to differentiate a scene.

## Typography continuity

Maintain:

- One editorial serif family
- One refined sans-serif family
- Consistent button and metadata typography
- Stable title, body and microcopy relationships

Vary hierarchy through scale, measure, alignment and whitespace rather than switching fonts.

## Product continuity

For repeated products:

- Use the same approved bottle geometry
- Keep label architecture consistent
- Keep relative bottle proportions stable
- Preserve finish, cap and pump details
- Use plausible light response for the scene

A product may be photographed differently, but it must remain unmistakably the same object.

## Human continuity

Jimmy Coco must retain:

- Recognisable facial structure
- Consistent age
- Consistent hair and grooming
- Professional wardrobe logic
- Credible expert posture

Models may vary, but skin treatment must remain natural, premium and unexaggerated.

## Component continuity

Use the component-language document as the canonical source for:

- Buttons
- Links
- Selection controls
- Product cards
- Trust labels
- Review metadata
- Form fields

Do not allow each image generation to invent a new button or card family.

## Copy continuity

Use consistent terms across the page.

Preferred language:

- Match My Shade
- Desired result
- Skin tone
- Undertone-aware
- Buildable colour
- Development time
- Product used
- Verified purchase
- Natural daylight

Avoid changing terminology between scenes for superficial variety.

## Scene anchor method

After the hero is approved, create a compact anchor sheet containing:

- Logo and navigation sample
- Primary and secondary button sample
- Headline and body typography sample
- Core neutral palette
- Approved product bottle
- Approved Jimmy likeness
- Skin-colour reference
- Fine-rule and radius examples

Attach the anchor sheet to every subsequent scene generation.

## Thumbnail storyboard

Maintain a storyboard showing all scenes at small scale.

For every new scene:

1. Place it between the preceding and following scene placeholders.
2. Blur or zoom out.
3. Check silhouette differentiation.
4. Check palette continuity.
5. Check progression in density and emotion.
6. Revise before investing in fine detail.

## Continuity defects

Common defects include:

- New logo styling in one scene
- Different bottle shape between product and CTA scenes
- Repeated pale background for too many chapters
- Sudden cold-grey or pink colour shift
- Inconsistent serif character
- Different button radius and height
- Jimmy appearing as different people
- Product photography becoming glossy and synthetic
- Every scene using identical centred headings
- Abrupt transitions with no visual handoff

## Approval checklist

A scene passes continuity when:

- It belongs unmistakably to Sunless
- It is visibly different from adjacent scenes
- Its product and human references are consistent
- Its controls use the approved component language
- Its background fits the planned cadence
- Its exit naturally prepares the next chapter
- It strengthens the complete storyboard

## Final rule

Never approve a scene only in isolation.

A beautiful scene that damages the rhythm, consistency or narrative of the full homepage is not an approved scene.
