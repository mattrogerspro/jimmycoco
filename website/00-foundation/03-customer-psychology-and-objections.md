# 03 — Customer Psychology and Objections

## Purpose

This document defines the customer’s emotional state, decision barriers and reassurance needs across the website.

The customer is not simply choosing a beauty product. They are deciding whether to risk a visible result on their own skin.

## Core emotional truth

The financial risk of self-tan is usually moderate. The appearance risk is high.

Customers may fear:

- Looking orange
- Choosing the wrong depth
- Streaking or patchiness
- Product gathering around dry areas
- Uneven hands, knees, elbows or ankles
- Transfer onto clothing or bedding
- An unpleasant smell
- A result that looks artificial in daylight
- Poor fading
- Being unable to correct a mistake
- Looking inappropriate for an event

The website should create relief, not simply excitement.

## Primary customer jobs

The visitor is trying to accomplish one or more of the following:

- Look healthier or more polished
- Prepare for a holiday, event or photography
- Avoid sun exposure
- Recreate a professional finish at home
- Find a product suitable for their skin tone
- Replace a product that looked unnatural
- Build a repeatable beauty routine
- Feel more confident in their appearance

## Awareness levels

### Problem aware

The visitor wants colour but is afraid of getting self-tan wrong.

Website response:

- Name the common failure modes
- Explain why they happen
- Show that Sunless has a clear method
- Lead with guidance rather than catalogue depth

### Solution aware

The visitor is comparing self-tan brands.

Website response:

- Explain undertone matching and buildable colour
- Show specific proof
- Clarify product differences
- Connect Jimmy’s expertise to the result

### Product aware

The visitor knows Sunless or a particular product.

Website response:

- Provide immediate product access
- Show rating, suitability, development time and result
- Make variant choice simple

### Most aware

The visitor is returning to repurchase.

Website response:

- Keep navigation and search efficient
- Avoid forcing the shade quiz
- Support quick access to previous or bestselling products

## Primary objections

### “Will it look orange?”

Resolve with:

- Undertone explanation
- Daylight photography
- Skin-tone-specific results
- Specific customer reviews
- Product suitability guidance

### “Which shade should I buy?”

Resolve with:

- A short recommendation flow
- One primary recommendation
- One lighter or deeper alternative
- Clear explanation of why

### “Can I apply it properly?”

Resolve with:

- Three-step preparation and application method
- Beginner-specific reassurance
- Visual instructions
- Clear number-of-coats guidance
- Correction and maintenance advice

### “Will it streak or cling to dry areas?”

Resolve with:

- Preparation guidance
- Formula-specific benefits where supported
- Reviews from users with similar concerns
- Close-up, realistic results

### “Will it smell or transfer?”

Resolve with factual product information and verified reviews. Do not bury these concerns in FAQs if they materially affect purchase confidence.

### “How long will it take?”

Resolve with:

- Development time
- Rinse instructions
- Event planning guidance
- Delivery timing
- Clear application duration

### “Will it suit very fair or deeper skin?”

Resolve through relatable proof and inclusive shade logic, not generic claims.

### “Why is it worth the price?”

Resolve with:

- Jimmy’s expertise
- Formula and method differentiation
- Guided selection
- Product longevity or usage information where verified
- Reduced risk of buying incorrectly

### “What happens if I choose incorrectly?”

Resolve with visible shade support, customer service and any genuine return, exchange or satisfaction policy.

## Decision modes

### Emotional buyer

Responds to:

- Beautiful skin
- Aspirational photography
- Identity
- Confidence
- Event readiness
- Founder story

### Analytical buyer

Responds to:

- Product comparison
- Development time
- Skin compatibility
- Formula details
- Proof methodology
- Review specificity

### Sceptical buyer

Responds to:

- Verified reviews
- Standardised before-and-after evidence
- Transparent claims
- Clear policies
- Natural skin texture
- Absence of exaggerated language

### Impatient buyer

Responds to:

- Immediate understanding
- Strong product labels
- Clear CTA hierarchy
- Quick recommendation
- Minimal unnecessary copy

The homepage must support all four without becoming dense.

## Behavioural principles

### Hick’s Law

Reduce unnecessary choices and provide a recommended path.

### Recognition over recall

Place skin suitability, result depth and development time directly on product cards. Do not make users remember information from earlier chapters.

### Social proof

Use proof from customers who share the visitor’s concern or skin context.

### Authority

Demonstrate Jimmy’s expertise through method and decisions, not fame alone.

### Loss aversion

Explain the cost of choosing a generic undertone or unclear product carefully. Avoid fear-based exaggeration.

### Commitment and consistency

A short quiz creates small commitments that increase confidence in the final recommendation.

### Processing fluency

Use consistent labels, concise copy and obvious hierarchy. Difficulty understanding the product is often interpreted as risk.

## Emotional progression by chapter

1. **Hero:** desire and relevance
2. **Authority:** credibility
3. **Recommendation:** relief
4. **Method:** understanding
5. **Results:** belief
6. **Products:** confidence
7. **Founder:** aspiration and authority
8. **Comparison:** rational validation
9. **Reviews:** belonging and reassurance
10. **Routine:** control
11. **Final CTA:** commitment

## Anxiety-reduction microcopy

Use short reassurance near moments of action:

- Takes about 60 seconds
- No email required
- See why this shade suits you
- Suitable for first-time tanners
- Develops in 6–8 hours
- Need help choosing? Ask our team
- Verified purchase
- Photographed in natural daylight

Only use statements that are factually and operationally accurate.

## Signs of psychological failure

The experience is failing when:

- Users repeatedly move between similar products
- Visitors cannot explain the differences
- Product browsing begins before confidence is created
- Reviews are generic rather than objection-specific
- The shade tool feels like marketing rather than guidance
- Aspirational imagery increases desire but also makes results feel unattainable
- The page creates more questions than it answers

## Success test

A successful experience leaves the customer thinking:

> I understand which product is right for me, why it should look natural and how to use it without getting the result wrong.
