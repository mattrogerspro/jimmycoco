# Subject Line System

## Purpose

Create subject lines that earn attention through relevance, clarity and restraint rather than tricks, false urgency or inflated claims.

## Primary rule

The subject line must accurately represent the message that follows.

A subject line is successful only when it attracts the right open and leads to a useful customer action without damaging trust, deliverability or long-term engagement.

## Recommended structure

Most subject lines should use one of these patterns:

1. **Direct value** — “Your shade recommendation is ready”
2. **Useful guidance** — “How to prepare for an even tan”
3. **Relevant reminder** — “Time to refresh your colour?”
4. **Specific product context** — “A closer look at Malibu Beach”
5. **Service update** — “Your order has been dispatched”
6. **Recognition** — “Welcome to Sunless VIP”
7. **Resolution** — “We’ve updated your delivery”

## Length

Aim for approximately 25–50 visible characters where practical.

Longer lines are acceptable when clarity requires them, but the most important words should appear early because mobile and inbox clients truncate unpredictably.

Do not force every subject into an arbitrary character count at the expense of meaning.

## Front-loading

Place the main customer value or state first.

Prefer:

- “Your shade match is ready”
- “Your order is on its way”
- “A better way to apply face tan”

Avoid:

- “A little note to let you know your shade match is ready”
- “Something exciting has happened with your order”

## Personalisation

Use first-name personalisation only when:

- the value is reliable;
- a natural fallback exists;
- it materially improves the line;
- repeated use will not feel mechanical.

Good:

- “Matt, your shade match is ready”

Fallback:

- “Your shade match is ready”

Do not insert names into every message.

## Subject line categories

### Welcome

Purpose: set expectation and establish value.

Examples:

- “Welcome to Sunless”
- “Your glow starts here”
- “A more considered way to tan”

### Shade match

Purpose: signal progress, result or useful next step.

Examples:

- “Find your Sunless shade”
- “Your recommended colour is ready”
- “The right depth, without the guesswork”

### Browse abandonment

Purpose: reconnect to genuine interest without surveillance language.

Examples:

- “Still considering Malibu Beach?”
- “A closer look at the tan you viewed”
- “Your questions, answered”

Avoid:

- “We saw you looking”
- “You forgot this”
- “Caught you browsing”

### Cart abandonment

Purpose: clarify retained cart and remove barriers.

Examples:

- “Your Sunless selection is still saved”
- “Ready when you are”
- “A final look before checkout”

Use urgency only when a real expiry, stock constraint or cart rule is verified.

### Post-purchase

Purpose: support confidence, application and anticipation.

Examples:

- “Your Sunless order is confirmed”
- “Before your first application”
- “How to get the best from your new routine”

### Replenishment

Purpose: prompt a useful stock check, not assume depletion.

Examples:

- “Running low on your Sunless favourite?”
- “Is it time to replenish?”
- “Keep your routine ready”

### Win-back

Purpose: reintroduce relevance respectfully.

Examples:

- “A considered return to colour”
- “Your Sunless routine, when you’re ready”
- “Has your tanning routine changed?”

### VIP and loyalty

Purpose: recognise status, service or benefit clearly.

Examples:

- “Welcome to Sunless VIP”
- “Your VIP benefit is now available”
- “An update to your Sunless VIP status”

### Promotional campaign

Purpose: state the actual value and terms.

Examples:

- “20% off selected Sunless routines”
- “Early access begins today”
- “A limited edit for summer colour”

Do not hide a discount behind vague clickbait.

### Transactional and service

Purpose: immediate recognition and retrieval.

Examples:

- “Order JC-1042 confirmed”
- “Your refund has been processed”
- “Action needed: confirm your delivery address”

Clarity overrides editorial tone.

## Curiosity

Curiosity is acceptable only when the message still gives the customer enough context to make an informed choice.

Acceptable:

- “The step most tanning routines miss”

Not acceptable:

- “You won’t believe this”
- “We need to talk”
- “Bad news…” when no bad news exists

## Urgency and scarcity

Use only when all relevant facts are verified at send time.

Approved forms include:

- a real offer expiry;
- a genuine event closing time;
- verified limited inventory;
- a benefit expiration;
- a required account or delivery action.

Avoid:

- “Last chance” when another extension is planned;
- “Selling fast” without inventory evidence;
- “Only a few left” from stale data;
- countdown language disconnected from a real deadline.

## Capitalisation and punctuation

- Use sentence case by default.
- Avoid ALL CAPS.
- Avoid repeated punctuation.
- Avoid decorative symbols and excessive emoji.
- Question marks are acceptable when the email genuinely helps answer the question.
- Do not add “RE:” or “FWD:” to simulate a conversation.

## Subject and preview pairing

The preview text must extend the subject, not repeat it.

Example:

Subject: “Your shade recommendation is ready”
Preview: “See the depth, undertone and routine selected from your answers.”

## Testing priorities

Test in this order:

1. proposition or customer value;
2. specificity;
3. direct versus guidance-led framing;
4. customer-state relevance;
5. length and front-loading;
6. personalisation;
7. punctuation or stylistic details.

Do not celebrate open-rate improvements if clicks, conversions, complaints or long-term engagement worsen.

## Approval checklist

Confirm:

- the line matches the email content;
- important words appear early;
- any urgency is real and current;
- personalisation has a fallback;
- the line avoids surveillance language;
- there is no false reply or forward convention;
- the line is understandable when truncated;
- the paired preview adds new information;
- service messages prioritise clarity;
- the line can be defended as honest after the campaign ends.
