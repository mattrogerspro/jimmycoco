# Email Measurement and Success Framework

## Purpose

Measure whether email improves customer confidence, conversion, retention and lifetime value—not merely whether messages were opened.

## Primary business outcomes

- Revenue per recipient
- Conversion rate
- Repeat-purchase rate
- Time to second purchase
- Average order value
- Replenishment conversion
- Customer lifetime value
- Unsubscribe and complaint rate

## Journey metrics

### Welcome
- Signup-to-first-purchase conversion
- Shade-match starts and completions
- First-order revenue per subscriber

### Browse and cart recovery
- Recovery conversion
- Recovered revenue
- Incremental lift against holdout groups

### Post-purchase
- Education engagement
- Product-use confidence signals
- Review submission
- Cross-sell conversion
- Return/refund rate

### Replenishment
- Reorder rate
- Time-to-reorder
- Subscription or repeat-order uptake where applicable

### Win-back
- Reactivation rate
- Revenue per reactivated customer
- Subsequent 90-day retention

## Diagnostic metrics

Use opens cautiously because privacy features can inflate them. Prioritise:

- unique click rate
- click-to-conversion rate
- landing-page engagement
- add-to-cart rate
- purchase rate
- revenue per delivered message
- unsubscribe and spam complaint rate
- bounce and deliverability trends

## Attribution principles

- Separate flow revenue from campaign revenue.
- Use consistent attribution windows.
- Report both attributed and incremental performance where holdouts are possible.
- Avoid claiming email caused every purchase that occurred after an open.
- Review performance by customer segment and lifecycle state.

## Experimentation standard

Every meaningful test should define:

1. Hypothesis
2. Primary metric
3. Guardrail metric
4. Audience and exclusions
5. Test duration or sample requirement
6. Decision rule
7. Learning captured

Test one material variable at a time when possible: proposition, hierarchy, offer, proof, CTA or timing.

## Quality scorecard

Review each email or flow across:

- relevance
- clarity
- brand consistency
- accessibility
- commercial hierarchy
- mobile usability
- deliverability risk
- measurement readiness

## Reporting cadence

- Weekly: operational issues, campaign results and flow anomalies
- Monthly: lifecycle performance, segment trends and testing learnings
- Quarterly: retention, contact policy, deliverability and strategic roadmap

## Definition of success

A successful email programme creates measurable incremental revenue while making customers more informed, more confident and more likely to return.
