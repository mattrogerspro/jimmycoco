# Scene 12 — Community, Newsletter and Footer

## Role in the experience

This final utility chapter supports secondary engagement after the main conversion journey has already concluded. It should provide community proof, email capture, navigation, policies and reassurance without weakening the emotional impact of the final conversion scene above it.

The page must not appear to end on Instagram content or a newsletter form. The commercial ending is Scene 11; this scene is the quiet afterword.

## Commercial objective

- Capture visitors who are not ready to purchase
- Encourage ongoing brand engagement
- Support account, policy and customer-service navigation
- Provide community reassurance
- Preserve trust at the end of the page

## User questions answered

- Can I keep in touch without buying now?
- What will I receive if I subscribe?
- Where can I find delivery, returns and contact information?
- Is the business legitimate and easy to reach?
- Can I see more real customer content?

## Architectural silhouette

This scene should be low-density, calm and clearly subordinate to the dark conversion scene above it.

Suggested sequence:

1. A restrained community image ribbon or editorial mosaic
2. A spacious newsletter invitation
3. A structured multi-column footer
4. Payment, policy and legal details at the lowest level

The chapter may return to warm ivory or pale linen, creating a gentle release from the charcoal CTA above.

## Community module

The community element should feel curated rather than like an automatically embedded social feed.

Use:

- Four to six high-quality customer or behind-the-scenes images
- Varied crop ratios rather than identical squares
- Real skin, real routines and believable environments
- Minimal interface chrome
- One concise editorial introduction

Suggested heading:

> The Sunless community

Supporting line:

> Real routines, real events and natural-looking results.

Possible action:

**Explore the Community**

Do not make this module more visually dominant than the final CTA above it.

## Community photography direction

- Natural, flattering daylight
- Consistent warm-neutral colour grade
- Real customers and backstage moments
- Diverse skin tones and ages
- Believable bathrooms, dressing rooms, events and home routines
- No low-quality social screenshots
- No visible platform interface inside the images
- No generic influencer collage
- No duplicate crops

## Newsletter module

The email capture must offer tangible value rather than simply asking visitors to “join the club.”

Recommended heading:

> Get your personalised shade guide.

Supporting copy:

> Receive your recommended shade, application advice, expert tanning tips and early access to new launches.

Use only benefits the email programme can genuinely deliver.

### Form structure

- Persistent label: **Email address**
- Optional first name only if operationally useful
- One clear submit action
- Concise privacy reassurance
- Link to the privacy policy

Recommended CTA:

**Send My Shade Guide**

Alternative:

**Join the Glow Club**

The benefit-led option is preferred.

## Trust and utility details

The lower footer may include:

- Accepted payment methods
- Delivery geography
- Customer-service contact details
- Company or trading information
- Social links
- Copyright
- Cookie preferences

These details must be readable but visually tertiary.

## Layout direction

- Use warm ivory, linen or a subtle two-tone pale field.
- Let the community imagery occupy a wide, shallow band rather than a large grid.
- Give the newsletter substantial breathing room and a strong single-column focus.
- Use a fine rule or tonal transition into the footer.
- Footer columns should align to the 12-column grid.
- Preserve generous lower padding.
- Avoid a visually crowded “mega-footer.”

## Colour environment

- Warm ivory or natural linen base
- Soft stone alternate field for newsletter or footer
- Deep charcoal text
- Matte black form action
- Muted bronze accent used sparingly
- Fine warm-grey rules

## Typography

- Newsletter heading at approximately 36–44px serif
- Community heading at approximately 32–40px serif
- Footer headings at approximately 13–15px with clear weight
- Footer links at approximately 14–16px
- Legal copy no smaller than approximately 12–13px
- Form label and privacy copy must remain readable

## Eye-tracking path

1. Curated community imagery
2. Newsletter value proposition
3. Email field and CTA
4. Main footer navigation
5. Trust, payment and legal details

## Conversion psychology

- **Reciprocity:** the shade guide offers useful value in exchange for an email address.
- **Commitment:** a low-friction email action supports visitors not ready to purchase.
- **Social proof:** community imagery demonstrates continued real-world use.
- **Trust and transparency:** clear contact, delivery and policy routes reduce legitimacy concerns.
- **Peak-End protection:** quiet styling prevents utility content from replacing the dark CTA as the remembered ending.

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Every form field must have a persistent programmatic label.
- Include clear validation and error recovery.
- Do not rely on placeholder text as the label.
- Ensure keyboard access and visible focus for every footer link.
- Community images need appropriate alt text or empty alt attributes when decorative.
- Social links require descriptive accessible names.
- Legal and privacy copy must remain readable.
- Footer link groups should use semantic headings and navigation landmarks.

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Creates a large generic Instagram square grid
- Ends the page visually on social imagery instead of the final CTA
- Uses tiny unreadable footer links
- Adds a newsletter popup
- Gives the newsletter no specific benefit
- Creates a crowded mega-footer
- Adds unsupported payment, delivery or policy claims
- Uses low-quality social screenshots
- Makes every community image the same size
- Uses excessive decorative icons
- Repeats the deep charcoal CTA background across the entire footer

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- It feels like a quiet editorial afterword rather than another major sales chapter
- The newsletter proposition provides clear value
- Community content feels curated and authentic
- Footer navigation is easy to scan
- Trust and policy routes are easy to locate
- The dark final CTA remains the emotional end of the homepage
- The page closes calmly, professionally and accessibly
