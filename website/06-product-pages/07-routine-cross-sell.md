# Product Page — Routine Cross-Sell

## Purpose

The routine section should increase average order value by helping the customer achieve a better result, not by displaying generic related products.

## Routine model

Use the shared three-stage logic:

1. Prepare
2. Apply
3. Perfect

The current product should occupy its correct stage. Recommend only the missing products that materially improve preparation, application, finish or maintenance.

## Recommendation rules

Each recommendation must explain:

- Why it is relevant to this product
- What problem it prevents or result it improves
- When it is used
- Price
- Whether it is already included in the selected bundle

Avoid recommendations based solely on bestseller status.

## Architecture

Preferred layout:

- One horizontal or stepped ritual on desktop
- Current product clearly marked
- One or two supporting products, not a large carousel
- Combined total shown when bundle purchase is available
- Individual and combined Add to Bag actions clearly distinguished

The section should feel like a professional kit recommendation rather than “You may also like.”

## Copy pattern

### Prepare

> Smooth dry areas before application for a more even-looking fade.

### Apply

> Distribute mousse evenly and protect hands with the approved tanning mitt.

### Perfect

> Refine hands, feet and high points, then maintain moisture as the colour develops and fades.

Claims and products must be adjusted to factual product data.

## Bundle logic

When a bundle exists, state:

- Exact included products
- Standard combined price
- Bundle price
- Saving
- Whether substitutions are allowed
- Stock status of each item

Do not show misleading savings or hide that the customer is adding multiple items.

## Add-to-bag behaviour

The customer must understand whether the control adds:

- The single supporting product
- All recommended routine items
- A predefined bundle SKU

Use explicit labels such as:

- `ADD MITT — £X`
- `ADD COMPLETE ROUTINE — £X`

## Personalisation

Recommendations may change based on:

- Selected product
- Product type
- Customer concern
- Shade-match result
- Items already in cart

Do not recommend a duplicate accessory or incompatible formula.

## Visual direction

Use premium product renders with restrained connectors or stage labels. Avoid three identical ecommerce cards floating on a cream background. The ritual should have clear directional flow and generous breathing room.

## Accessibility

- Preserve logical reading order.
- State product names and prices in text.
- Do not rely on the stage illustration alone.
- Announce cart updates clearly.
- Make combined-purchase terms understandable before activation.

## Failure modes

Reject sections that:

- show unrelated products;
- include more than three or four choices;
- obscure the current product;
- use “complete the look” fashion language;
- hide bundle contents;
- add products without explicit consent;
- repeat the homepage bestseller grid.

## Success criteria

The customer understands why each addition matters and can build a practical routine without losing confidence in the original purchase.
