# Headline and Opening Copy

## Purpose

Define how the first visible words inside an email establish relevance, confidence and momentum.

## Core rule

The opening must confirm why the customer should care within the first screen.

It should not merely repeat the subject line or delay the main point with generic brand language.

## Opening hierarchy

A typical opening uses:

1. optional eyebrow or state label;
2. one clear headline;
3. one short supporting paragraph;
4. primary CTA when action is appropriate.

Not every email needs all four elements.

## Headline principles

Headlines should:

- communicate one idea;
- be understandable without the hero image;
- connect to the customer’s current state;
- avoid inflated claims;
- remain visually concise;
- support the primary CTA.

Preferred patterns:

- “Your colour, considered”
- “Meet your recommended shade”
- “A smoother first application”
- “Your routine is ready when you are”
- “An update to your order”

Avoid:

- “Unlock your ultimate glow journey”
- “The wait is over!!!”
- “Everything you need to know about everything”
- headlines that depend on unexplained puns.

## Eyebrows

Use eyebrows only when they add useful orientation.

Examples:

- “Your shade match”
- “Application guide”
- “Order update”
- “VIP access”

Do not use an eyebrow and headline that say the same thing.

## Opening paragraph

The first paragraph should answer at least one of these questions:

- Why am I receiving this?
- What has happened?
- What is being recommended?
- What should I do next?
- How does this help me?

Keep it to one or two short paragraphs before the primary action.

## Openings by lifecycle state

### Welcome

Establish the brand’s role and immediate value.

Example:

“Sunless brings Jimmy Coco’s professional approach to colour into a routine designed for home. We’ll help you choose the right depth, apply it evenly and maintain a natural-looking finish.”

### Shade match

Connect the recommendation directly to the customer’s answers.

Example:

“Based on the depth, undertone and finish you selected, Malibu Beach Medium is your strongest match.”

### Browse abandonment

Acknowledge interest without sounding invasive.

Example:

“Malibu Beach caught your attention. Here is what to know about the finish, development time and who it suits best.”

### Cart abandonment

State that the selection remains available and reduce the main barrier.

Example:

“Your selected shade and routine are still saved. Review the details below or return to checkout when you’re ready.”

### Post-purchase

Lead with confirmation or preparation.

Example:

“Your order is confirmed. Before it arrives, here is the preparation routine we recommend for a smoother, more even result.”

### Replenishment

Use a check-in, not an assumption.

Example:

“If your current bottle is running low, you can replenish the same shade and keep your routine consistent.”

### Win-back

Respect distance and offer relevance.

Example:

“Your routine may have changed since we last saw you. Here is a simple way to return to colour without starting from scratch.”

### VIP

Recognise status through service and access.

Example:

“You now have access to Sunless VIP benefits, including priority guidance and selected programme privileges.”

### Service and recovery

State the issue and remedy immediately.

Example:

“Your delivery has been delayed by one working day. We have updated the tracking information below and no action is required.”

## Image relationship

The headline must remain accurate if the image fails to load.

Do not place essential details only inside a hero image. Product name, offer terms, status and primary action should exist as live text.

## CTA placement

Place the primary CTA near the opening when:

- the customer already understands the action;
- the message is transactional;
- the recommendation is ready;
- the basket can be resumed;
- a benefit can be accessed.

Delay the CTA briefly when explanation is necessary for informed action.

## Opening length

Aim for fast comprehension on mobile. The headline, short supporting copy and CTA should usually fit within the first meaningful viewport without requiring excessive scrolling.

## QA checklist

Confirm:

- the opening explains the message purpose;
- the headline is not a duplicate of the subject;
- the first paragraph reduces uncertainty;
- the main action is visible at the right moment;
- essential information is live text;
- the copy is clear with images blocked;
- the tone matches the lifecycle state;
- there is no generic luxury filler before the customer value.
