# Research

The evidence base for the whole programme. **Do not re-run the work recorded
here** — it took three parallel research programmes and roughly 340k tokens to
produce, and it is expensive to reproduce.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| [`uk-market-data.md`](uk-market-data.md) | The citable UK numbers — market, sector pressure, retention, pricing benchmarks. Sourced. |
| [`competitive-landscape.md`](competitive-landscape.md) | Who ranks for what, and why the category is empty. The finding the strategy rests on. |
| [`claims-register.md`](claims-register.md) | What the industry states as fact that is not established. **Read before writing anything.** |
| [`source-library.md`](source-library.md) | The primary sources worth building on, and how to cite them. |

## How to use this folder

1. Before writing any article, read the claims register in full. It is short and
   it will save you publishing something the whole industry believes and nobody
   has evidenced.
2. Pull the figures you need from `uk-market-data.md` — they come with their
   source, year and sample size attached, and those travel into the article's
   `citations` array unchanged.
3. Anything not in here needs verifying yourself, into the article's own
   `research.md`. Then promote it back up here if it's reusable.

## What still needs doing — where new research effort should go

Ranked. These are gaps, not repeats.

1. **Local authority licensing.** The single highest-value unresolved area.
   Needs the enabling legislation for each UK nation plus a real sample of
   council positions to show the spread honestly. Blocks the compliance hub.
2. **The 18% DHA question.** Professional rapid solutions are sold at 18% DHA,
   above every published regulator figure for leave-on self-tan (UK 14%, EU
   10%). Rinse-off is 22.5%. Whether booth application is correctly categorised
   as rinse-off is the crux. Commercially significant. Blocks all DHA-percentage
   compliance content.
3. **Insurance policy wording.** Actual wording from 3+ UK treatment liability
   providers, quoted. Nobody has done this and it converts straight into both an
   article and the template pack.
4. **The regulator PDFs.** SAG-CS 2024, HSE RR721, the BABTAC 2016 asset —
   documents `WebFetch` could not parse. Firecrawl is the right tool.
5. **Sienna X's full post list.** ~170–190 posts. Mapping them tells us every
   topic the only UK competitor covers badly, which is a ready-made commission
   list.
6. **UK seasonality data.** For `uk-spray-tan-year`, the highest-share-potential
   article in the programme. Needs search seasonality, our own order data, and
   therapist input — none of it exists in written form.
7. **Regional pricing, refreshed.** The benchmarks in `uk-market-data.md` are
   good but a 2026 sweep of real published salon prices by region would make the
   pricing article unarguable.

Firecrawl is well suited to 3, 4 and 5. Items 1 and 2 need judgement, not
crawling. Item 6 needs data we partly own.
