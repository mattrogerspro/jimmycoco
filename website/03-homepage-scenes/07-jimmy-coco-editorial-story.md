# Scene 07 — The Man Behind Hollywood's Glow

## Role in the experience

This chapter humanises the authority behind the products and turns Jimmy Coco from a credential into a meaningful reason to believe.

It follows the curated product gallery, so the visitor has already seen what to buy. This scene now explains why the products and method carry unusual credibility.

The chapter is not a biography for its own sake. It must connect Jimmy's professional experience directly to how Sunless formulas, shade guidance and application logic were created.

## Commercial objective

Increase trust, perceived expertise and premium value by showing that Sunless originates from real professional practice rather than celebrity licensing or generic white-label product development.

The scene should:

- Demonstrate Jimmy's authentic involvement
- Connect backstage experience to product decisions
- Make the brand feel personal and culturally credible
- Justify premium positioning
- Deepen emotional connection before comparison and reviews
- Provide a memorable editorial pause in the sales journey

## User questions answered

- Who is Jimmy Coco?
- Why should his experience matter to me?
- Is he genuinely involved in the products?
- What did he learn from working with photographed skin?
- How does that expertise translate into an at-home result?
- Is this a serious founder-led brand or a celebrity endorsement project?

## Core message

> Decades behind the world's most photographed skin shaped every Sunless formula.

Alternative headline:

> The man behind Hollywood's glow.

## Story principle

The chapter must follow this logic:

1. Jimmy worked closely with highly photographed skin in demanding professional settings.
2. He learned that believable colour depends on undertone, controlled depth, preparation and finish.
3. Sunless translates that judgement into formulas and guidance customers can use at home.

Do not present a chronological résumé. Present a clear causal relationship between experience and product value.

## Architectural silhouette

This should be an asymmetrical magazine feature on a distinct warm-linen field.

Approved silhouette:

- Oversized cinematic portrait or backstage application image occupying 55–65% of the scene
- Narrow editorial text column offset against the image
- Large pull quote breaking the grid
- Jimmy's handwritten signature used sparingly
- Two or three supporting images in an irregular mini-gallery
- No card boxes around biography copy

The composition must feel unmistakably different from the orderly four-product gallery above and the minimal comparison scene below.

## Layout direction

Use an invisible 12-column grid, then deliberately break it.

Recommended desktop arrangement:

- Main image: columns 1–7 or 5–12, extending close to one canvas edge
- Eyebrow and headline: narrow opposing column, approximately 4 columns wide
- Pull quote: spans across image and negative space without obstructing the subject
- Supporting gallery: two smaller images offset at different baselines
- Signature: positioned near the closing thought, not used as decoration over photography

Avoid a predictable 50/50 split.

## Image narrative

The scene should show Jimmy working, observing or refining—not simply posing.

Approved moments:

- Applying product professionally backstage
- Assessing skin tone in soft natural light
- Preparing a client for a photographed event
- Selecting or comparing formula tones
- Working in a refined private suite, studio or backstage environment
- A close detail of hands, tools, product and real skin

At least one image should establish Jimmy's face clearly. Supporting images may focus on process.

## Photography direction

- Cinematic editorial documentary style
- Soft directional daylight or carefully controlled warm studio light
- Authentic backstage or private-suite atmosphere
- Natural skin texture and realistic colour
- Tactile fabrics and refined surfaces
- Calm, concentrated expressions
- Subtle filmic depth without heavy grain
- Warm linen, stone, champagne and charcoal tones

Jimmy must appear actively engaged in professional work, not presented as a generic celebrity portrait.

Avoid:

- Red-carpet step-and-repeat imagery
- Paparazzi aesthetics
- Nightclub lighting
- Overly staged handshakes or posed founder portraits
- Artificial orange colour casts
- Excessive black-and-white nostalgia

## Editorial gallery

Use two or three small supporting images with intentionally different crops:

1. Close application detail
2. Product and tools in a professional setting
3. Quiet portrait or observational moment

The gallery should look curated, not like a social-media grid.

## Colour environment

- Background: natural linen, clearly warmer and deeper than the product-gallery white
- Main typography: deep charcoal
- Secondary typography: warm grey-brown
- Fine rules: muted stone
- Signature or small accent: restrained bronze or ink-like charcoal
- Photography: warm-neutral, never orange

The linen field should visibly separate this chapter at 10% zoom.

## Typography

- Eyebrow: 12–13px uppercase tracking
- Headline: 48–60px editorial serif
- Pull quote: 34–46px serif, depending on composition
- Body copy: 17–19px refined sans-serif or readable editorial serif if consistent with the system
- Caption text: 13–15px
- CTA: clear, restrained and visually secondary to the story

Use a narrow text measure to preserve editorial character.

## Eye-tracking path

1. Jimmy's face or professional action
2. Large headline
3. Pull quote
4. Introductory story
5. Supporting process images
6. Product connection
7. Signature
8. CTA

The visitor should encounter the human proof before the biography text.

## Emotional objective

The user should feel:

- This expertise is real
- Jimmy understands what makes skin look natural on camera and in person
- The products come from a credible method
- The brand is personal, not anonymous
- The premium price reflects judgement and experience

## Conversion psychology

This chapter uses:

- **Authority:** professional experience shown through real practice
- **Authenticity:** observational imagery rather than endorsement poses
- **Narrative transportation:** the visitor enters Jimmy's working world
- **Causal credibility:** expertise is connected directly to product decisions
- **Human trust:** a visible founder reduces anonymity
- **Premium framing:** craftsmanship and judgement justify value

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Do not place essential copy over visually complex photography
- Maintain strong text contrast on the linen field
- Provide descriptive alt text for Jimmy's working images
- Quote styling must remain readable and should not rely on decorative quotation marks alone
- On mobile, lead with one strong image, then the headline and story, followed by the supporting gallery
- Signature should not replace an accessible text name

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Produces a generic celebrity headshot
- Makes Jimmy unrecognisable or changes his appearance between images
- Creates a conventional 50/50 About section
- Places all content inside cards
- Uses a fake red-carpet backdrop
- Adds invented awards, client names or quotations
- Makes the scene too dark or nightclub-like
- Uses orange skin colour grading
- Adds a huge decorative signature across the image
- Creates a uniform three-image grid
- Makes the founder story disconnected from the product
- Uses tiny body copy or a long unreadable biography

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- Jimmy's authority is apparent before the copy is fully read
- The founder story clearly explains the product's origin and value
- The composition feels like a Vogue magazine feature
- The warm-linen environment creates an obvious visual chapter change
- The imagery feels observed and authentic rather than posed
- The user understands that Sunless offers access to Jimmy's judgement, not merely his name
- The scene provides an emotional pause without losing commercial relevance
