# Product Grid and Card System

## Purpose

The product grid is the primary commercial surface of every collection page. It must make the range feel curated rather than crowded and make the difference between products understandable before the customer opens a product page.

## Grid behaviour

### Desktop

- Use four columns when cards remain readable and product imagery is the dominant visual.
- Use three columns when cards require more educational content, multiple variants or richer result guidance.
- Maintain consistent card widths and image ratios.
- Avoid masonry layouts; alignment supports comparison.

### Tablet

- Use two or three columns depending on width.
- Keep essential product differences visible without hover.

### Mobile

- Default to two columns only when names, prices and controls remain legible.
- Use one column for richer comparison-led cards or where product photography needs greater scale.
- Never compress complex product information into an unreadable two-column layout.

## Product-card hierarchy

Each card should include, in order:

1. Product image
2. Optional restrained badge
3. Product category or format
4. Product name
5. One-line result descriptor
6. Suitability cue
7. Review score and count
8. Price
9. Variant or shade cue where relevant
10. Availability or dispatch information where useful
11. Primary interaction

## Result descriptor

Every product must state the outcome it creates.

Examples:

- Buildable medium-to-deep colour
- Subtle everyday glow
- Express colour in 1–3 hours
- Lightweight facial tanning drops
- Even application for hard-to-reach areas

Avoid repeating generic brand claims across every card.

## Suitability cue

Use one concise line such as:

- Best for first-time tanners
- Ideal for fair to medium skin tones
- Best for deeper, event-ready colour
- Designed for face and skincare mixing
- Best for controlled gradual development

These cues must be based on approved product data.

## Image system

- Use one consistent primary image ratio across the grid.
- Product scale should be optically consistent even when packaging dimensions differ.
- Keep backgrounds within the approved warm neutral family.
- Do not place every product on an identical floating pedestal.
- Secondary hover media may show texture, application or result, but the primary image must identify the product clearly.
- Do not hide product identity behind elaborate campaign styling.

## Badges

Approved examples:

- Best Seller
- New
- Jimmy’s Pick
- Award Winner
- Limited Edition

Rules:

- Use only factually supportable badges.
- Limit to one badge per card in most cases.
- Do not use urgency badges as decoration.
- Sold-out status must not resemble a promotional badge.

## Price presentation

- Show the current selling price clearly.
- Show unit pricing where required or commercially useful.
- Present sale pricing transparently with the previous price and current price.
- Avoid visual tricks that make discounts appear larger than they are.

## Variants and shades

Where a product has meaningful variants:

- Show labelled swatches or concise variant names.
- Indicate the number of additional options.
- Update the product image only when doing so is accurate and stable.
- Do not use colour circles without text labels.
- Do not force variant selection directly in the grid when it creates error risk.

## Quick add

Quick add is appropriate only when the product has no unresolved required choice.

Use:

- **Add to Bag** for a single-SKU product;
- **Choose Options** when a required shade, size or format must be selected;
- **View Product** when education is essential before purchase.

Do not allow an ambiguous default variant to be added accidentally.

## Hover behaviour

Desktop hover may reveal:

- secondary image;
- concise quick-add control;
- variant preview;
- one supporting product fact.

All essential information must remain visible without hover.

## Comparison discipline

Cards should align the same types of information vertically wherever possible. Customers should be able to scan across a row and compare:

- depth;
- development time;
- format;
- suitability;
- price.

## Accessibility

- Entire card link behaviour must not create invalid nested controls.
- Images require descriptive alt text focused on product identification.
- Badges and swatches need readable text equivalents.
- Review scores must include accessible wording.
- Focus states must be obvious.
- Quick-add confirmation must be announced to assistive technology.

## Failure modes

Reject cards that:

- rely on product names alone;
- use tiny grey metadata;
- hide price until hover;
- display five or more badges;
- place multiple competing buttons on every card;
- use inconsistent image crops;
- overfill the card with paragraph-length copy;
- use generic lifestyle images as the primary identification image;
- make all products look functionally identical.

## Success criteria

A customer should understand the basic difference between adjacent products without opening each one in a new tab.
