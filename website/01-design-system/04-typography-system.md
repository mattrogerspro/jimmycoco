# 04 — Typography System

## Purpose

This document defines how typography should communicate luxury, clarity, authority and editorial rhythm across the Sunless website.

Typography is one of the strongest continuity devices in the brand. It should remain recognisable even as chapter composition changes.

## Core pairing

Use:

- An elegant editorial serif for major headlines, quotes and selected product storytelling
- A refined modern sans-serif for navigation, body copy, controls, labels and commerce information

The exact production fonts should follow the approved current brand implementation or the closest licensed equivalents.

## Typography character

### Serif

The serif should feel:

- Editorial
- Sophisticated
- Warm
- High contrast but readable
- Contemporary rather than antique
- Confident without becoming theatrical

Avoid overly decorative fashion serifs, extreme Didone contrast at small sizes or fonts that feel bridal, historical or ornamental.

### Sans-serif

The sans-serif should feel:

- Clean
- Precise
- Human
- Neutral enough to support the serif
- Highly readable in product and interface contexts

Avoid geometric fonts that feel cold or technology-led, and avoid rounded fonts that feel casual.

## Desktop type scale

Directional reference:

- Display hero: 64–72px
- Major editorial chapter title: 44–52px
- Standard section heading: 36–44px
- Large quote: 36–52px depending on composition
- Product title: 20–24px
- Card heading: 18–22px
- Lead paragraph: 19–22px
- Standard body: 16–18px
- Navigation: 14–16px
- Button label: 14–16px
- Product metadata: 14–16px
- Eyebrow or overline: 12–14px
- Legal or auxiliary text: minimum 12–13px where necessary

Production values should be responsive and tested rather than copied mechanically.

## Hierarchy rules

Every chapter should have one unmistakable typographic entry point.

Do not allow:

- Eyebrow, heading and body to share similar visual weight
- Multiple headlines to compete
- Product names to disappear beneath badges
- Button labels to feel smaller than surrounding metadata

The heading should answer what the scene is about. Supporting copy should explain why it matters. Interface labels should explain what to do.

## Line length

Recommended desktop measures:

- Hero supporting copy: 38–52 characters per line
- Standard body: 50–72 characters per line
- Editorial founder copy: 48–65 characters per line
- Product descriptions: 32–48 characters per line

Avoid broad paragraphs stretching across large screens.

## Line height

Directional guidance:

- Display serif: 0.98–1.08
- Section serif: 1.05–1.16
- Large quotes: 1.06–1.18
- Body sans: 1.5–1.7
- Product metadata: 1.35–1.5

Serif headlines need optical spacing, not simply tight numeric line height.

## Letter spacing

- Large serif: neutral or slightly negative where the font allows
- Sans body: neutral
- Uppercase eyebrow: modest positive tracking
- CTA labels: slight tracking only if it improves authority

Avoid excessive letter spacing as a substitute for luxury.

## Alignment rhythm

Do not centre all chapter headings.

Approved variation:

- Left-aligned hero
- Centred recommendation introduction
- Left-aligned method title with offset supporting copy
- Image-overlaid or edge-aligned results title
- Centred product gallery heading
- Asymmetrical founder typography
- Centred minimal comparison heading
- Left-aligned testimonial quote
- Directional routine labels
- Large left- or centre-aligned final CTA depending on photography

Typography alignment should reinforce the chapter silhouette.

## Editorial quotes

Quotes should be used sparingly.

A premium quote treatment may include:

- Large serif text
- Narrow measure
- Minimal attribution
- Signature or editorial caption
- Significant whitespace

Do not place quotation marks at an exaggerated decorative scale unless the approved design language supports it.

## Product typography

Every product card should present information in a consistent sequence:

1. Optional concise badge
2. Product name
3. Result or suitability statement
4. Rating and price
5. Development time or key attribute
6. CTA

The product name should not be visually overpowered by discount messages or review counts.

## Button typography

Buttons should feel direct and confident.

Preferred labels:

- Match My Shade
- Choose Shade
- Shop Best Sellers
- See My Recommendation
- Buy Now
- Learn More

Avoid vague labels such as:

- Explore
- Discover More
- Begin Journey
- Unlock Glow

Use title case or uppercase consistently according to the approved component system. Do not mix both arbitrarily.

## Numerals and data

Ratings, development time and result depth should be highly scannable.

Use tabular numerals where useful for comparison. Keep units clear:

- 6–8 hours
- 1–2 coats
- 4.8/5

Do not hide important product data inside prose.

## Accessibility

- Standard body should generally not fall below 16px
- Critical metadata must not rely on tiny text
- Text over images requires sufficient tonal protection
- Links must remain identifiable without relying only on colour
- Line height and paragraph spacing should support cognitive accessibility
- Avoid long uppercase sentences

## Image-generation text protocol

AI image models frequently distort text.

For generated concepts:

- Keep visible copy concise
- Prioritise hierarchy and approximate line length
- Use no more than one main headline, one short paragraph and a small number of labels per scene
- Rebuild final typography as live text in Figma or production

Do not judge a generated scene only by spelling accuracy. Judge whether the typographic architecture is correct.

## Common typography failures

Reject designs that:

- Use tiny body copy to create false elegance
- Use the serif for every label and control
- Introduce a third unrelated font
- Centre every heading
- Use oversized headings without enough surrounding space
- Use vague luxury copy instead of clear language
- Apply excessive tracking
- Allow low-contrast text to disappear into cream backgrounds

## Success criteria

The typography system succeeds when:

- The page feels editorial and premium
- Product information remains easy to compare
- The eye understands hierarchy before reading
- Typography provides continuity across different chapter architectures
- Every important action is legible and specific
- The design feels confident rather than delicate
