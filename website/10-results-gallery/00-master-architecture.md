# 00 — Results Gallery Master Architecture

## Purpose

The Results Gallery is the website’s evidence system. It converts visual transformations into structured proof that supports shade selection, product confidence and purchase.

It must feel more rigorous than an Instagram feed and more human than a clinical case-study archive.

## Commercial role

The gallery should:

- reduce fear of choosing the wrong product;
- demonstrate results across varied skin tones and desired depths;
- help visitors identify people with a similar starting point;
- connect outcomes directly to products, shades and routines;
- support the shade-match recommendation with visible evidence;
- create a credible route from curiosity to Add to Bag.

## Primary user questions

- Will this work on skin like mine?
- Which product created this result?
- Is the colour natural or orange?
- How deep is the result after one or two coats?
- How long did it develop?
- Is the comparison trustworthy?
- What should I buy to recreate it?

## Approved page sequence

1. Navigation and breadcrumb
2. Editorial introduction
3. Guided filter bar
4. Featured transformation
5. Results grid
6. Load-more or pagination control
7. Shade-match invitation
8. Education and disclosure note
9. Final commercial CTA
10. Footer

## Information hierarchy

### Level 1 — visual outcome
The skin result must be the first thing the visitor understands.

### Level 2 — relevance
Starting skin tone, desired depth and product used must be visible without opening the detail page.

### Level 3 — method
Coats, development time and application method provide credibility.

### Level 4 — purchase
The recommended product and routine become available after relevance and proof are established.

## Architectural silhouette

The gallery should open with a spacious editorial introduction, then move into a dense but calm image grid. It must not resemble a social-media masonry wall.

Use:

- a wide, restrained introduction;
- a contained filter system;
- one large featured result;
- a regular, aligned grid;
- deliberate pauses between commercial modules.

Avoid:

- endless irregular masonry;
- overlapping cards;
- tiny captions;
- influencer-style decorative badges;
- excessive promotional interruptions.

## Desktop grid

- 12-column page grid
- 3 result cards per row at standard desktop width
- occasional featured result spanning 6–8 columns
- consistent image ratios within each card family
- maximum content width aligned with the rest of the website

## Conversion pathways

Every result should provide:

- View Result
- View Product
- Match My Shade

Only one of these should be visually dominant in each context. Within the gallery, `View Result` is primary. On the detail page, `Add to Bag` becomes primary.

## Personalisation

When the visitor has completed shade matching, the gallery may prioritise results with similar attributes and display a restrained cue such as:

> Closest to your shade profile

Do not hide other results or imply mathematical certainty.

## Success criteria

The system succeeds when visitors can:

- find a relevant transformation quickly;
- understand exactly how it was achieved;
- trust the image comparison;
- reach the correct product without confusion;
- see meaningful variety without feeling overwhelmed.
