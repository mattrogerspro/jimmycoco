# Email 02 — Guidance and Differentiation

## Role

Help the customer understand which product, format or finish best fits the result they appear to be considering.

This email should answer the question behind the browse behaviour rather than simply repeat the product image.

## Default timing

Send approximately 24 hours after Email 01 when no cart, checkout, purchase or higher-priority flow has taken ownership.

## Primary objective

Reduce product-choice uncertainty and move the customer toward one clear next step.

## Content strategy

Choose the most relevant guidance route from the observed intent:

- product suitability;
- format comparison;
- result depth;
- development time;
- face versus body use;
- preparation and application complexity;
- professional versus at-home use;
- routine compatibility.

Do not infer skin tone, undertone, sensitivity or personal characteristics from browse behaviour alone.

## Message hierarchy

1. A decision-oriented headline
2. The customer’s strongest apparent choice
3. Two or three meaningful differentiators
4. One recommended next action
5. Optional link to the shade-match experience when the choice remains uncertain

## Product comparison rules

A comparison should include no more than three options and only dimensions that genuinely help the decision.

Suitable dimensions may include:

- finish;
- colour depth;
- application format;
- development time;
- face or body suitability;
- buildability;
- routine position.

Do not invent superiority claims. Avoid generic comparison rows such as “premium quality” unless substantiated and useful.

## Product-specific route

When one product is dominant, explain:

- who it is designed for;
- the visible result it is intended to create;
- how it is applied;
- how quickly it develops;
- how it differs from the closest relevant alternative.

Use one primary CTA: `See if it suits your routine` or `View the product`.

## Category-specific route

When browsing shows category rather than product intent, frame the email around the customer’s decision.

Examples:

- mousse versus soufflé;
- face contour product versus all-over face tan;
- gradual colour versus a deeper developed result;
- at-home product versus professional solution.

The email should direct to a filtered or editorial landing state, not a generic shop page.

## Shade-match route

When the evidence does not support a confident product route, use the shade-match experience as the primary CTA.

Recommended framing:

- a quick expert-guided choice;
- no obligation;
- no email gate before results;
- a clear explanation of why the recommendation was made.

Do not describe deterministic rules as artificial intelligence unless an approved AI capability genuinely exists.

## Subject-line territories

- Which Sunless finish fits your routine?
- A simple way to choose your result
- Mousse, soufflé or contour?
- Find the format that feels right

Avoid false personalisation such as “We chose this for you” when no valid recommendation exists.

## Visual structure

1. Email header
2. Decision headline and concise introduction
3. Comparison or suitability module
4. One expert guidance block
5. Primary CTA
6. Optional secondary shade-match text link
7. Footer

Keep tables responsive and readable on mobile. Where email-client support is weak, stack comparison content vertically.

## Data requirements

Before send, validate:

- product names and handles;
- approved descriptors;
- current price and currency when shown;
- stock and variant state;
- comparison attributes;
- destination URL;
- image asset;
- any claims or time statements.

## Suppression and handoff

Exit or transfer when:

- item added to cart;
- checkout started;
- purchase completed;
- shade match completed and its follow-up is more specific;
- consent withdrawn;
- target choice is no longer valid.

## Success criteria

The email succeeds when it creates a higher-quality return session, a comparison interaction, a shade-match completion, an add-to-cart event or a purchase without relying on pressure or an unearned incentive.
