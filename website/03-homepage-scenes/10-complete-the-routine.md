# Scene 10 — Complete the Routine

## Role in the experience

This scene increases average order value only after the visitor has gained confidence in the core product. It reframes accessories and complimentary products as a simple professional ritual rather than another catalogue row.

It follows the warm, human testimonial scene and must return the experience to a lighter, calmer and more ordered visual environment.

## Commercial objective

Increase basket size by helping customers understand the minimum complete routine required to achieve a more even, controlled and polished result.

## User questions answered

- What else do I actually need?
- In what order should I use the products?
- Which steps are essential and which are optional?
- How do I improve the final result?
- Can I buy the routine together?

## Core message

> Three steps to a more professional finish.

Supporting statement:

> Prepare the skin, apply with control and perfect the areas that reveal a self-tan first.

## Architectural silhouette

This scene should feel linear, spacious and ritualistic rather than card-based.

Suggested silhouette:

- Clean white or soft ivory background
- One long horizontal visual path
- Three clearly separated stages: Prepare, Apply, Perfect
- Products floating or resting on a continuous tactile surface
- Subtle directional movement from left to right
- Minimal supporting copy
- One compact routine summary and CTA at the end

The layout should resemble an editorial beauty ritual or luxury instruction spread, not a cross-sell carousel.

## The three stages

### 01 — Prepare

Purpose:

- Smooth dry areas
- Create a more even application surface
- Reduce patchiness around elbows, knees and ankles

Potential product role:

- Exfoliating or preparation product
- Moisturiser or barrier product where appropriate

### 02 — Apply

Purpose:

- Distribute product evenly
- Protect hands
- Control pressure and blending

Potential product role:

- Tanning mitt
- Body brush or approved application tool
- Recommended core tanning formula

### 03 — Perfect

Purpose:

- Refine hands, feet, wrists and edges
- Correct excess product
- Support a polished final finish

Potential product role:

- Detail brush
- Face or finishing formula
- Approved correcting or blending accessory

Use only products that actually exist and are commercially approved.

## Product hierarchy

The customer’s recommended tanning formula remains the primary product. Accessories must support it, not compete with it.

Each step should show:

- Step number
- Step name
- One concise benefit
- Product name or role
- Optional price where appropriate
- Essential or optional indicator

Do not present more than three primary steps or more than one main accessory per step in the default view.

## Layout direction

- Use a 12-column grid with a continuous visual path across columns 2–11.
- Give each step a different scale or alignment while preserving flow.
- Use elegant arrows, fine rules or implied product direction rather than thick connectors.
- Allow products to break the baseline slightly to create movement.
- Keep copy compact and aligned consistently.
- Include one routine bundle summary toward the right or lower edge.

## Visual rhythm

The sequence should read:

Small preparation object → dominant application moment → refined finishing object.

The core tanning product may be largest in the centre to reinforce commercial priority.

## Colour environment

- Clean white or very soft ivory background
- Natural linen or pale stone surface
- Warm neutral product shadows
- Deep charcoal typography
- Muted bronze step numerals
- Matte black CTA

The scene should feel brighter and calmer than the testimonial scene before it.

## Product rendering

- Packaging must match approved references
- Correct scale relationships
- Realistic contact shadows
- Tactile mitt and brush materials
- No impossible levitation
- No excessive glossy reflections
- No duplicate or invented products
- No decorative petals, water splashes or generic beauty props unless they serve the application story

## Typography

- Section heading at approximately 42–48px serif
- Step numerals large but restrained
- Step names at approximately 20–24px
- Supporting copy at approximately 16–18px
- Product metadata at approximately 14–16px
- Use short sentences and generous line height

## CTA strategy

Primary CTA:

**Build My Routine**

Alternative where a predefined set exists:

**Add the Complete Routine**

Secondary action:

**Shop Application Tools**

Do not make every product carry a competing black button.

## Bundle behaviour

Where operationally supported, show a compact summary:

- Recommended tan
- Essential application tool
- Optional finishing product
- Combined price
- Any genuine bundle saving

Do not invent discounts or imply a bundle saving unless one exists.

## Eye-tracking path

1. Scene heading
2. Prepare
3. Core application product and tool
4. Perfect
5. Routine summary
6. Primary CTA

## Conversion psychology

- **Goal gradient:** a three-step path makes completion feel achievable.
- **Chunking:** reduces several products into one memorable ritual.
- **Commitment consistency:** customers who choose a tan are more likely to complete the recommended method.
- **Authority:** Jimmy’s method justifies the sequence.
- **Choice reduction:** clearly separates essential from optional products.

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Do not use arrows as the only indication of sequence; include explicit step numbers.
- Ensure all product names and step labels are live text.
- On mobile, stack steps vertically in the same order.
- Use descriptive alt text for product and tool imagery.
- Ensure bundle controls communicate additions and removals clearly.
- Do not auto-select optional products without transparent indication.

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Creates another four-card product grid
- Shows more than three main steps
- Invents products or packaging
- Uses huge arrows or infographic graphics
- Adds botanical props unrelated to self-tan
- Makes all products equal size and importance
- Uses tiny unreadable product labels
- Makes the scene look like a generic skincare routine
- Adds unsupported discount badges
- Loses the left-to-right ritual flow

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- The routine is understood in seconds
- The core product remains visually dominant
- Essential and optional items are distinguishable
- The scene increases perceived ease rather than complexity
- The layout is visibly lighter and more linear than the review scene
- The primary CTA offers one simple next step
