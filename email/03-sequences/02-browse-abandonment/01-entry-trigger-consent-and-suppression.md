# Browse Abandonment — Entry Trigger, Consent and Suppression

## Purpose

Define exactly when a visitor is eligible to enter the browse-abandonment sequence, when they must be excluded and how the system prevents accidental or intrusive messaging.

## Core eligibility

A contact may enter only when all of the following are true:

- the visitor can be linked to a known contact through an authenticated account, a consented email session, a first-party identifier or another approved identity method;
- valid marketing consent exists for the destination address and jurisdiction;
- the contact is not globally suppressed, unsubscribed, bounced or complaint-blocked;
- the session contains a qualifying high-intent browse signal;
- no cart, checkout or purchase event has superseded browse intent;
- no higher-priority lifecycle flow currently owns the contact;
- the strongest browsed product, collection or result path is still valid and commercially available;
- the contact has not entered another browse-abandonment sequence inside the rolling frequency window.

## Qualifying browse signals

At least one meaningful signal is required. Suitable examples include:

- two or more views of the same product-detail page within seven days;
- one engaged product-detail view meeting the approved dwell, scroll or interaction threshold;
- use of product-specific accordions, shade controls, application guidance or media;
- repeated views within one tightly related collection;
- meaningful filter or sort use that narrows product intent;
- a results-gallery interaction that leads to a product page;
- a shade-match recommendation view where the shade-match follow-up flow is not active or eligible.

The implementation team must define numeric thresholds from real behavioural data. The documentation does not assume that dwell time alone proves intent.

## Signals that do not qualify alone

Do not enter a contact because of:

- a single shallow product or collection view;
- homepage browsing only;
- accidental navigation or immediate back-button behaviour;
- bot, crawler or internal-team traffic;
- customer-service visits;
- order-status, returns or account-management activity;
- visits generated by broken links or redirects;
- tracking events missing reliable product or session identity.

## Identity standards

Browse behaviour may be attached only when the identity link is lawful, technically reliable and documented.

Do not merge anonymous and known behaviour merely because two sessions share an IP address, device family or approximate location. Identity confidence must meet the approved first-party data standard.

When a visitor identifies later in the session, only eligible first-party events inside the approved lookback window may be associated with the contact.

## Consent rules

Marketing consent must be checked at sequence entry and again immediately before each send.

The contact record should preserve:

- consent status;
- consent source;
- consent timestamp;
- policy or form version;
- jurisdiction when relevant;
- unsubscribe and suppression state.

Service or transactional permission must never be treated as marketing permission.

## Immediate suppression conditions

Suppress or exit the sequence when any of the following occurs:

- purchase completed;
- relevant item added to cart and cart abandonment becomes eligible;
- checkout started;
- marketing consent withdrawn;
- hard bounce, complaint or provider suppression;
- the customer enters an active post-purchase flow;
- the customer begins a newer, more specific shade-match journey;
- the target product is discontinued or unavailable without a safe replacement;
- the destination URL fails validation;
- the active sequence reaches its final message;
- the contact exceeds the global contact-pressure limit.

## Temporary pause conditions

Pause rather than permanently exit when:

- a higher-priority campaign or behavioural message is scheduled within the contact-pressure window;
- stock is temporarily unavailable but replenishment is expected and the customer has not entered a back-in-stock flow;
- required product or recommendation data fails validation;
- the contact enters quiet hours;
- the send would violate local-time or frequency policy.

A paused message must be revalidated before release. Never send an old browse reminder simply because the delay later expires.

## Cross-flow ownership

Use this default ownership order:

1. Service and transactional communication
2. Checkout recovery
3. Cart abandonment
4. Post-purchase education and service
5. Active shade-match follow-up with a valid personal recommendation
6. Browse abandonment
7. Welcome and general campaign messaging

Only one behavioural recovery flow should own the contact at a time.

## Frequency limits

Default safeguards:

- one active browse-abandonment sequence per contact;
- no more than one new browse sequence in a rolling 14-day period unless the new intent is materially different and stronger;
- no more than one behavioural marketing email in a calendar day;
- every send counts toward the global marketing contact-pressure policy;
- repeated eligible events update the active sequence rather than creating duplicate enrolments.

## Pre-send validation checklist

Immediately before rendering and dispatch, confirm:

- current consent and suppression status;
- current flow ownership;
- no cart, checkout or purchase event has occurred;
- target product or collection still exists;
- current price and currency are correct;
- relevant variant and stock state are valid;
- destination URL resolves correctly;
- approved product imagery exists;
- no unverified claims or stale review figures are present;
- idempotency key has not already been used.

If any essential check fails, suppress or pause. Do not construct missing commercial facts.
