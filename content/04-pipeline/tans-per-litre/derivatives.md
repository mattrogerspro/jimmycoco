## The email

**Mechanism:** tool-triggered follow-up, and a Salon Business Brief issue.

**The one number:** **£46** — what one extra tan per litre is worth over a year.

**Subject lines**
1. What one extra tan per litre is actually worth
2. 36ml. That's the whole answer.
3. You're probably not measuring this

**Body angle** — 120–200 words. Open on the fact that everyone quotes a number
and nobody says what it rests on. Give the 28, give the 36ml, then the £46. Close
on the one-week measurement method — the email's value is that it asks her to do
something small and concrete rather than read something.

Do not sell the litre. The article's conclusion is that solution cost barely
moves her margin.

## The tool

**Links to:** the tans-per-litre yield tool — **not built.**

The article *is* the specification: mode 1 of
[`../../05-tools/tans-per-litre-tool.md`](../../05-tools/tans-per-litre-tool.md)
implements exactly the measurement method here, and the reorder formula is the
second output. Ship mode 1 only; mode 2 needs yield-by-equipment data that does
not exist.

When the tool ships, the article's secondary CTA points at it.
