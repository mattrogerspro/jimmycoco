# Body Copy and Message Structure

## Purpose

Define how Sunless email copy explains, persuades and guides without becoming dense, repetitive or promotional.

## Core structure

Most emails should follow:

1. customer state or reason for contact;
2. principal value or recommendation;
3. concise supporting evidence or guidance;
4. one primary action;
5. optional secondary detail;
6. service, preference and legal footer.

## One-message rule

Each email should have one dominant job. Supporting content may help complete that job, but must not create competing narratives.

Examples:

- a shade-result email recommends and explains the match;
- an application email helps the customer use the product correctly;
- a replenishment email helps assess whether it is time to reorder;
- a service email communicates the operational state and remedy.

## Paragraph design

- Keep paragraphs short, particularly on mobile.
- Place the most important sentence first.
- Use one main idea per paragraph.
- Prefer headings and bullets when they improve scanning.
- Do not use bullets for every sentence.
- Avoid long introductions and repeated conclusions.

## Explanation framework

For product, shade and routine guidance, use:

**Recommendation → reason → expected result → next step**

Example:

“Malibu Beach Medium is your strongest match. Its balanced undertone suits the natural-looking finish you selected, while the buildable depth gives you control over the final result. Review the application method before adding it to your routine.”

## Persuasion framework

Persuasion should reduce uncertainty through:

- relevance to the customer’s stated need;
- product or routine specificity;
- clear use instructions;
- credible proof;
- transparent conditions;
- a reversible or low-friction next step where genuinely available.

Do not rely on adjective volume, fear of missing out or unsupported social proof.

## Educational content

Education should answer practical questions:

- what the product does;
- who it is suitable for;
- how deep the colour develops;
- how long development takes;
- how to prepare;
- how to apply;
- how to maintain and remove;
- what to do when results are uneven.

Separate verified product instructions from general guidance. Do not invent timings or performance claims.

## Product modules

Each product module should prioritise:

1. product name;
2. shade or variant;
3. one relevant benefit;
4. price and availability when current;
5. direct CTA.

Do not paste full product-page copy into an email.

## Proof modules

Proof must be traceable and presented in context.

Use:

- verified reviews;
- approved before-and-after results;
- documented professional method;
- factual product attributes;
- transparent customer context.

Do not:

- rewrite reviews beyond light approved editing;
- imply typical results from exceptional examples;
- use celebrity imagery as proof of current endorsement without rights and substantiation;
- omit material result conditions.

## Repetition

Intentional repetition may reinforce one central idea across subject, headline and CTA, but each element should contribute something new.

Remove repeated:

- benefit lists;
- product descriptions;
- urgency;
- introductory phrases;
- multiple versions of the same CTA.

## Links

Link text should describe the destination.

Prefer:

- “View your shade recommendation”
- “Read the application guide”
- “Track your order”

Avoid repeated “Click here” links.

## Closing copy

A closing should either:

- reinforce the next step;
- offer relevant support;
- provide necessary operational information;
- close naturally without another sales pitch.

Example:

“Not certain about the depth? Reply to this email and our team can help you compare the options.”

Do not promise reply support unless the inbox is monitored and routed correctly.

## Length by message type

### Transactional

As short as clarity permits.

### Abandonment

Short to moderate; focus on the main barrier.

### Guidance

Moderate; enough detail to be useful, with links to deeper content.

### Post-purchase education

Can be longer when structured into practical steps.

### Campaign

Short by default; one editorial proposition and a direct route to shop or learn.

## QA checklist

Confirm:

- the email has one dominant job;
- the main point appears early;
- copy is scannable on mobile;
- claims are verified;
- proof is traceable;
- product details are current;
- links describe their destinations;
- support promises are operationally real;
- no section exists only to fill space;
- the final action matches the message purpose.
