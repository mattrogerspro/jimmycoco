How subject lines are generated: the flow, guardrails and gates.

Every subject line that leaves this system is the product of a governed pipeline, not a moment of inspiration. This report documents that pipeline end to end — where subjects are written, the rules that constrain them, the automated checks they pass through, and the human gates that stand between a draft and a send. The repository is the single source of truth; every rule cited here is traceable to a file in it.

Part one

The production flow: where a subject line comes from

A subject line is never written in isolation. It emerges at step five of a nine-step pipeline, after the strategy, audience and message architecture are already fixed — and it cannot reach a recipient until every later step has passed. The QA doctrine is explicit on the ordering: “Do not polish a subject line before confirming that the customer should receive the message.”

1

A plain-language request

Anyone can ask for a campaign in ordinary words — “build a five-email salon recruitment campaign for Sydney.” No intake form, no schema knowledge. The request is expanded into an internal brief: market, audience, primary commercial outcome, offer, cadence, exclusions and handoff.

2

Mandatory repository preflight

Before any copy exists, the canonical generator prompt requires the producer to read the governing sources: brand truth, email strategy, the design and template system, the copy system, lifecycle sequences (collision check), and the closest comparable campaign. The rule is absolute: “Do not claim compliance with any document you have not actually read.” A concise Compliance Preflight is issued and production proceeds only on a READY TO PRODUCE status.

3

One message, one purpose

Strategy rules fix what each email is allowed to ask before its subject is drafted. Every campaign defines one primary commercial outcome; every email makes one clear point with one primary action. The subject line will later be tested against exactly this purpose.

4

Facts resolved or tokenised

Unknown prices, terms, availability or claims never reach copy. They become explicit approval tokens — {{approved_price}}, [SOURCE REQUIRED] — so no subject or preview can smuggle an unverified fact into the inbox. “Do not replace unknown facts with plausible-sounding copy.”

5

Subjects drafted as options, in sequence.md

This is where subject lines are actually written. The campaign folder contract requires, for every email: Subject option A, Subject option B where useful, and an optional option C only when materially distinct — plus paired preview text and the complete plain-text body. Subjects are written against the Subject Line System (Part two, below) and in the approved Sunless voice.

6

Mirrored into structured data

The chosen subject becomes the title field (with preview) in the campaign's email-data.json — the single content source the renderer consumes. A standing consistency rule requires sequence.md and email-data.json to remain semantically equivalent, so the human-readable copy and the machine-rendered copy can never drift apart.

7

Generated, never hand-built

Branded HTML is produced exclusively by the shared master template via node email/campaigns/_shared/build-all.js, which also validates every message record (title, preview, headline, blocks) and enforces asset governance. Generated HTML is never edited by hand; the subject's paired preview is embedded as hidden preheader text at render time.

8

Automated verification

The campaign then passes the skill validator (folder contract, schema, message fields, studio timeline agreement), the repository test suite (npm test), the Studio build, and — for Resend work — npm run templates:check, which compares repository HTML byte-for-byte against published templates and verifies that every declared personalisation variable actually appears in the rendered output. Any failure is a release blocker: “do not weaken or bypass validation merely to obtain a green result.”

9

Human gates before any send

The nine QA gates are reviewed (Part four), a Production Report is issued listing sources actually read and approval tokens outstanding, and the campaign lands in one of four statuses — none of which authorise sending. Publishing templates, enabling a campaign and going live each require fresh, explicit human approval, and the send path itself is double-locked in code.

Part two

The Subject Line System: the rules subjects are written under

“The subject line must accurately represent the message that follows.”

Primary rule — 02-subject-line-system.md

The system's stated purpose is to earn attention “through relevance, clarity and restraint rather than tricks, false urgency or inflated claims.” A subject line succeeds only when it attracts the right open and leads to a useful action without damaging trust, deliverability or long-term engagement.

The seven approved structures

Most subject lines must use one of seven patterns, each anchored to a real customer state or value:

PatternRepository example
Direct value“Your shade recommendation is ready”
Useful guidance“How to prepare for an even tan”
Relevant reminder“Time to refresh your colour?”
Specific product context“A closer look at Malibu Beach”
Service update“Your order has been dispatched”
Recognition“Welcome to Sunless VIP”
Resolution“We've updated your delivery”

Length and front-loading

Aim for roughly 25–50 visible characters where practical, with the most important words first — inbox clients truncate unpredictably, so the line must survive being cut short. Meaning is never sacrificed to hit an arbitrary count.

Front-loaded“Your shade match is ready”“Your order is on its way”
Buried value“A little note to let you know your shade match is ready”“Something exciting has happened with your order”

Personalisation — earned, never mechanical

A first name may appear only when the data is reliable, a natural fallback exists, it materially improves the line, and repetition won't feel mechanical. Every personalised subject ships with its fallback: “Matt, your shade match is ready” degrades to “Your shade match is ready.” Names are not inserted into every message. In cold outreach the same discipline applies through tokens — the Sydney campaign's “A sun-safe glow for {{salon_name}}?” — and the sync tooling verifies each declared variable actually exists in the rendered template.

Curiosity, capitalisation and conventions

Curiosity is permitted only when the recipient still has enough context to make an informed choice — “The step most tanning routines miss” passes; “You won't believe this” and manufactured “Bad news…” do not. Sentence case is the default; ALL CAPS, repeated punctuation, decorative symbols and excessive emoji are out. A question mark is fine when the email genuinely answers the question. And a hard convention rule: never fake “RE:” or “FWD:” to simulate a conversation — which is why the one legitimate-looking “re:” in the live campaigns (“re: colour that survives a Sydney summer”) sits within a real multi-touch thread of prior outreach rather than simulating one from nothing.

The subject never travels alone

Preview text is governed as “the second half of the inbox proposition.” It must extend the subject with new information — never repeat it — and its first 35–60 characters must stay meaningful when truncated. Dynamic preview fields each require a validated source, a fallback, length handling, and suppression when stale.

Pairs correctlySubject: “Your shade recommendation is ready”Preview: “See the depth, undertone and routine selected from your answers.”
Repeats itselfSubject: “Your shade match is ready”Preview: “Your shade match is ready to view.”

Part three

Guardrails: what a subject line may never do

Three layers of guardrails constrain every line before it reaches QA: the voice system (how Sunless sounds), the truth system (what may be claimed), and the urgency standards (when pressure is legitimate).

Voice — calm authority, editorial restraint

Sunless speaks as “a calm authority in self-tanning”: expert without clinical, luxurious without ornamental, warm without over-familiarity, helpful before persuasive. The brand's central verbal idea — “Red-carpet colour. Made effortless at home.” — is supported through specificity, not hype. Vocabulary is explicitly tiered:

TierLanguage
Preferredcolour · glow · finish · depth · undertone · development · routine · natural-looking · even · considered · professional · effortless · refined
Use carefullyflawless · perfect · transformative · iconic · exclusive · celebrity · red carpet · instant · guaranteed
Avoid unless legally approvedrisk-free · miracle · clinically proven · chemical-free · zero streaks · no transfer · lasts X days · works for everyone

Banned registers include “Hey babe”, “Glow bestie”, meme language, excessive exclamation and manufactured urgency. “I” may be used only when a message is truthfully authored by a named person — a personal note from Jimmy is never simulated.

Truth — every claim sourced or tokenised

No subject, preview or body may invent customer counts, awards, results, pricing, delivery times, stock, exclusivity or celebrity association. Celebrity and authority references require approval for the exact use, context, market and duration. Copy may not use “selected”, “exclusive” or “invited” unless the underlying selection process is real and documented. Unknown facts stay tokens; the campaign ships with its approval gaps listed rather than papered over.

Urgency — factual, proportionate, recorded

Urgency is legitimate only when backed by a real operational condition — a documented promotion end time, verified inventory state, confirmed cutoff or benefit expiry — and “the underlying condition must be recorded and reproducible.”

Approved deadline language“Ends Sunday at 11:59 pm GMT.”“Order by 1 pm for the stated dispatch window.”
Prohibited pressure“Last chance” when an extension is planned“Only a few left” from stale dataEvergreen countdowns that restart · “Hurry.” · “Don't wait.”
Surveillance language is banned outright. Browse-abandonment subjects reconnect to genuine interest — “Still considering Malibu Beach?” — never “We saw you looking”, “You forgot this” or “Caught you browsing”. And even truthful urgency becomes coercive when repeated: the orchestration layer caps reminders, deadline proximity and sequence overlap.

Part four

Gates and requirements: what stands between a draft and a send

The review order

Copy QA runs in a fixed ten-step sequence — customer state and necessity first, then data accuracy, proposition, structure, claims and legal, CTA, tone, personalisation and fallbacks, and only at step nine the subject and preview, before final proof. Subjects are reviewed late by design: a beautiful line on a message that shouldn't exist is still a failure.

The subject-line approval checklist

Before approval, every line must confirm all ten of these, verbatim from the system:

  • The line matches the email content
  • Important words appear early
  • Any urgency is real and current
  • Personalisation has a fallback
  • The line avoids surveillance language
  • There is no false reply or forward convention
  • The line is understandable when truncated
  • The paired preview adds new information
  • Service messages prioritise clarity
  • The line can be defended as honest after the campaign ends

The nine campaign QA gates

Subjects then travel inside the campaign through the canonical prompt's gates. Gate 4 addresses copy directly; the others ensure the subject's claims, rendering and consistency hold everywhere it appears.

Gate 1

Repository compliance. Required documents actually read; comparable campaigns reviewed; no rule silently overridden; campaign registered.

Gate 2

Strategy. Explicit audience and single outcome; entry, exclusions, cadence, stop rules, handoff; lifecycle-collision check complete.

Gate 3

Truth and claims. Every commercial statement sourced or tokenised; no unsupported superlatives or guarantees.

Gate 4

Copy. Subject and preview align with the body; approved voice; safe personalisation fallbacks; plain text exists; no false urgency, coercion or clickbait.

Gate 5

Template and rendering. All branded emails generated by the master template from structured data; build completes clean; mobile, Outlook and blocked-image behaviour reviewed.

Gate 6

Accessibility. Logical headings, adequate contrast, text-not-image essentials, sensible reading order, real alt text.

Gate 7

Assets. Every asset approved and traceable; rights, market, channel and expiry valid; protected people unmodified.

Gate 8

Delivery and compliance. Correct ESP token syntax; approved sender and monitored reply path; working unsubscribe and address; suppression defined; legal review stated.

Gate 9

Consistency. README, sequence.md, email-data.json, studio.json, generated HTML and the registry all agree — the subject in the human file and the rendered file can never differ.

Permitted final statuses

A completed campaign may carry exactly one status, and none of them send email:

Draft — not approved for send Ready for human review Changes required Blocked

“Never use READY TO SEND unless a named human approver has explicitly approved current copy, current assets, current commercial facts, current recipient basis and current rendered output.” Beyond the statuses, the send path is double-locked in code: a campaign must be enabled: true in shared/campaign-registry.js and the environment must have EMAIL_LIVE_MODE on. New campaigns are registered disabled by default.

Measured on outcomes, not opens

“Do not celebrate open-rate improvements if clicks, conversions, complaints or long-term engagement worsen.”

Testing priorities — 02-subject-line-system.md

When subjects are tested, the hierarchy runs proposition first, then specificity, framing, customer-state relevance, length and front-loading, personalisation — and stylistic detail last. Open rate is treated as diagnostic and potentially unreliable; the measures that count are replies, qualified requests, conversions, complaints and unsubscribes.
