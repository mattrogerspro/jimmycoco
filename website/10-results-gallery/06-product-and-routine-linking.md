# 06 — Product and Routine Linking

## Purpose

The proof system should move naturally from `I believe this result` to `I understand what to buy` without turning the gallery into a product carousel.

## Conversion hierarchy

### Gallery card

Primary: **View Result**

Secondary: product name as a quiet text link.

### Result detail

Primary: **Recreate This Result** or **Add to Bag**

Secondary: **View Product Details**

Tertiary: **Match My Shade**

The shade-match route should become more prominent when the visitor’s starting profile differs materially from the featured result.

## Exact product linking

Every result must link to the exact:

- product;
- shade or variant;
- size where materially relevant;
- current sellable SKU.

Do not link a discontinued or unavailable variant to a generic category without explaining the substitution.

## Preselection behaviour

When a visitor follows a result to the product page:

- preselect the shade or variant used;
- preserve a small context cue such as `Used in the result you viewed`;
- retain the originating result ID for analytics;
- do not override an existing shade-match recommendation without explanation.

## Routine composition

The routine should be generated from the recorded method, not a universal upsell bundle.

Potential stages:

1. Prepare
2. Apply
3. Perfect or maintain

Only show products that materially contributed to the demonstrated result or are genuinely required to reproduce the method.

## Cross-sell limit

- one primary tanning product;
- up to two supporting products;
- one application tool where relevant.

Avoid long accessory rows, unrelated bestsellers and discount-led bundles inside the proof experience.

## Routine module

Each item should state its role:

- `Used to create the colour`
- `Used for even application`
- `Used to prepare dry areas`
- `Recommended for maintaining the result`

Do not imply a supporting product was used in the original result unless it was recorded.

## Stock handling

When the exact product is unavailable:

1. retain the original product information for evidence integrity;
2. clearly label it unavailable;
3. offer the closest approved alternative with an explanation;
4. allow the user to join a restock notification where supported;
5. avoid silently replacing the product.

## Shade-match conflict handling

When the result uses a shade different from the visitor’s recommendation, display:

> This result used [shade]. Based on your shade match, we recommend [shade] for a similar depth on your starting tone.

The recommendation should not erase the evidence record.

## Analytics

Track:

- result viewed;
- product link selected;
- routine item selected;
- Add to Bag from result;
- shade match started from result;
- result-to-purchase attribution;
- substitution shown and selected.

## Success criteria

Product linking succeeds when the customer can recreate the method confidently while the evidence remains honest, specific and free from indiscriminate upselling.
