# 06 — Primary Recommendation and Explanation

## Objective

Make the primary recommendation feel decisive, transparent and commercially complete.

## Purchase-zone hierarchy

1. **Your best match** label
2. Product name
3. Selected shade or variant
4. Concise suitability statement
5. Price and verified review summary
6. Quantity and Add to Bag
7. Delivery, returns and payment reassurance
8. Full product details link

## Recommendation statement

Use one sentence that combines desired result and control:

> Best suited to your preference for a natural medium bronze with buildable control.

Do not overload this line with every answer.

## Why it matches

Show three or four reasons with specific labels:

- **Depth:** aligns with the customer’s desired visible result.
- **Control:** matches their experience and confidence level.
- **Timing:** fits the selected development preference.
- **Application:** appropriate for face, body or both.

Concern-based reassurance may be included only when supported by approved product evidence.

## Result guidance

Provide a compact prescription:

- Recommended starting depth
- Number of coats
- Development time
- When to rinse or finish
- How to make the result lighter or deeper

This guidance must be generated from structured product instructions, not improvised copy.

## Product image system

Use:

- one accurate hero pack shot;
- one texture or application detail where relevant;
- one result reference when verified;
- restrained thumbnails or controls.

Do not turn the recommendation into a generic product gallery. The customer should remain focused on the match.

## Add-to-bag behaviour

- Add the selected variant without asking the customer to choose again.
- Preserve recommendation metadata in cart state.
- Confirm the product, variant and quantity clearly.
- Offer View Bag and Continue Shopping.
- Do not automatically add routine items.

## Full product-page handoff

Opening the product page must preserve:

- selected variant;
- recommended number of coats;
- result context;
- the customer’s ability to return to results.

## Trust details

Use concise, verified reassurance near purchase:

- dispatch estimate;
- returns summary;
- payment security;
- verified review rating;
- stock state.

Avoid unverified scarcity, countdowns or repetitive trust badges.

## Success criteria

The customer can explain why the product was chosen and add the correct variant in one action.
