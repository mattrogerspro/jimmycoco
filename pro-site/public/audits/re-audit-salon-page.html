Internal commercial review · Re-audit, third pass

Conversion re-audit: the salon page, after the build-out.

The same thirty-phase standard, re-run against the salon page now live at www.jimmycoco.pro. The trajectory reads 4.6 → 8.0 → 8.2 → 8.8. The finding that capped every previous pass is gone: the forms no longer submit by mailto: — they write to a database, and there is a reviewed pipeline behind them. Accessibility and performance are now measured rather than inferred, and both are at or near 100. What has not moved is the other capping finding: there is still not one salon owner's voice anywhere on this page. That is now the single highest-value fix on the site.

The gains cluster exactly where the work went: Form UX +2.5 and Accessibility +2.6 are the two largest movements in the history of this audit, because both were held down by a single blocking defect each. Part three · Open findings

HighNo salon owner's voice. Unchanged across three audits.

The page sells to salon owners and quotes exactly one customer — a consumer, on the tan itself: "The tan looks so natural, I thought you'd just been on holiday." Alongside it sits an aggregate survey stat. Neither is a trade buyer saying the thing a trade buyer needs to hear: that it sold, that clients rebooked, that the margin was real. Every other trust signal on this page is the brand asserting its own credibility — Hollywood, red carpet, accreditation, press. Those establish glamour. They do not de-risk a £60 first order for a salon owner in Warrington.

Fix: one named salon, one photograph, one number. "We put 40 clients through the first litre in six weeks and 12 rebooked inside a month." That single block would lift Trust & proof, Conversion psychology and Competitive strength together. It is the cheapest remaining point on the board and it has been the cheapest for three audits running.

HighThe trial box offers a product that does not exist on this page.

At the primary conversion point, the trial box lists "The Sunset professional solution". The product is called Malibu everywhere else — six times on this page, in the product card, the CTAs and the schema. A prospect reading carefully at the moment of committing sees two different product names and has to decide whether the free trial is even the thing they just read about.

Fix: one word. Change it to Malibu, or, if Sunset is a genuinely different trial formulation, say so explicitly.

HighLead capture works. The confirmation email does not exist yet.

This is new, and it is a direct consequence of fixing the mailto: problem. An applicant now submits successfully and sees an on-screen thank-you — and then receives nothing. The uk-reseller-lifecycle campaign is wired but disabled, with no templates built. Under the old mailto: the applicant at least had a sent item in their own outbox as proof. Right now they have neither. For a business asking salons to hand over their details and wait, silence after submission is the worst possible state — worse than before the fix.

Fix: build the four templates and enable the campaign. Until then the on-screen confirmation should say plainly when a human will reply.

MediumThe trial box heading no longer matches its contents.

The heading reads "Become a Jimmy Coco Certified Salon." The two items beneath it are the trial solution and the shade guide — the contents of a sample box, not a certification programme. Certification is a genuinely strong offer and now has its own section at the foot of the page; borrowing its headline here muddies both. This is the copywriting regression noted in the scorecard.

MediumTwo different numbers for the same claim.

The credentials block says over 15 years. The story paragraph says more than 15 years. The trial box says 20+ years of red-carpet colour. Three claims, two numbers, one page. Whichever is right, a reader who notices trusts the rest of the page slightly less — and this page's entire proposition rests on Jimmy's track record.

MediumNo FAQ at the point of decision.

The salon FAQ — equipment compatibility, yield, mobile pros, training, trade terms — lives on the product page. Those are precisely the objections a salon owner raises immediately before requesting a trial, and the trial form is on this page. The answers exist; they are just in the wrong place.

MediumTrade terms are still deferred to a call.

Margin, minimum order and lead time are all "confirmed on your setup call". For a cautious buyer this reads as pricing that varies by how hard you push. A published indicative margin band would remove a real objection without conceding negotiating room.

LowAudit files ship publicly.

This document sits under public/audits/ and is fetchable by anyone who guesses the path. It is now noindex, but internal commercial reviews naming competitors and weaknesses should not be one URL away from a prospect. Move it out of public/.

Part four · The verdict

8.8 — strong, and for the first time the page is not being held back by its plumbing.

Three audits ago this page could not capture a lead. Two audits ago it captured them into an email client that most visitors do not have. It now captures them into a database, routes them through an approval queue, and issues a portal account with trade pricing at the other end. That is a different class of asset, and the technical scores — 100 accessibility, 100 desktop performance, zero layout shift — say it is built properly.

What remains is not technical. Of the eight open findings, six are copy or content, and one of them is a single word. The page's ceiling is now set by the fact that it asks salon owners to trust it without letting a single salon owner speak. Fix that, fix the Sunset/Malibu slip, and turn on the confirmation email, and the next pass clears 9.2 without a line of new code.

Scoring note: Form UX and Accessibility moved further in one pass than in the previous two combined, because each was capped by one blocking defect rather than by a spread of small ones. Mobile is now measured rather than inferred. Copywriting is the only decline.
