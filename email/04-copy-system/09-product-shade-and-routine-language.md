# Product, Shade and Routine Language

## Purpose

Define how Sunless describes products, shades, outcomes and routines in email so that copy remains commercially clear, aesthetically refined and technically honest.

## Product naming

Use the approved canonical product name on first mention. Shortened names may follow only when there is no ambiguity.

Preserve:

- official capitalisation;
- variant and shade names;
- size or format where purchase decisions depend on them;
- approved professional or consumer designation.

Do not invent luxury-sounding aliases that make products harder to identify.

## Product description hierarchy

Describe products in this order:

1. What it is
2. Who or what result it is for
3. The most useful differentiator
4. How it fits into the application routine
5. One clear action

Example structure:

> A tinted tanning soufflé for controlled, buildable colour. Designed for an even guide colour and a polished finish, it works best when applied to prepared skin with the Sunless Brush.

## Outcome language

Preferred language includes:

- natural-looking colour;
- even, controlled application;
- buildable depth;
- polished finish;
- believable warmth;
- tailored result;
- red-carpet colour made practical at home.

Avoid:

- flawless;
- perfect on everyone;
- guaranteed streak-free;
- instant transformation;
- universally flattering;
- age-defying or body-shaming language.

Claims must match approved product evidence and market requirements.

## Shade language

Shade copy must help the customer choose without creating a hierarchy of skin tones.

Describe:

- starting tone;
- undertone where relevant;
- desired depth;
- finish;
- buildability;
- application variables.

Prefer:

- `A deeper result for customers seeking richer warmth.`
- `A balanced starting point for a medium-depth result.`
- `Build with a second application when more depth is desired.`

Avoid terms that imply paler or deeper skin is a defect to correct.

## Recommendation language

The recommendation must distinguish product match from guaranteed final outcome.

Use:

- `Based on your answers, this is your strongest match.`
- `This shade is the best starting point for the depth you selected.`
- `Choose the alternative when you prefer a softer result.`

Do not use pseudo-clinical confidence scores unless they are meaningful, validated and intentionally customer-facing.

## Application language

Instructions should be concise, chronological and action-led.

Use verbs such as:

- prepare;
- exfoliate;
- moisturise;
- blend;
- apply;
- buff;
- develop;
- rinse;
- maintain.

State timing only when sourced from approved product instructions. Do not simplify in a way that changes safe or correct usage.

## Routine language

A routine is a purposeful sequence, not a bundle of unrelated products.

Explain each role:

- preparation improves the application surface;
- application tools support control and blending;
- colour product creates the selected depth;
- maintenance supports an even fade and future applications.

Cross-sell copy must explain compatibility and usefulness rather than merely increasing basket size.

## Professional authority

Jimmy Coco’s professional experience may support method and credibility where approved, but must not become repetitive celebrity name-dropping.

Use authority to explain:

- why a technique matters;
- how a result is controlled;
- what professionals do differently;
- how the customer can reproduce the method at home.

## Ingredient and formulation language

Use only approved ingredient, formulation and benefit statements. Avoid translating ingredient presence into unsupported performance, health or safety claims.

## Product comparisons

Comparisons should clarify customer choice using consistent dimensions:

- result depth;
- format;
- application method;
- development behaviour;
- intended use;
- experience level;
- routine compatibility.

Do not create artificial winners. Every option shown should have a clear reason to exist.

## Availability and price

Product, price, currency, stock and variant data must be validated at render or send time.

Do not write:

- `selling fast` without verified evidence and approved policy;
- `last chance` without a real final opportunity;
- `exclusive` when broadly available;
- `your shade is waiting` when the exact variant is unavailable.

## QA checklist

Confirm:

- product and variant names are canonical;
- the description identifies what the product does;
- shade language is inclusive and practical;
- results are not guaranteed;
- instructions match approved usage;
- routine products have a explained role;
- claims are substantiated;
- price and availability are current;
- the CTA matches the product state.
