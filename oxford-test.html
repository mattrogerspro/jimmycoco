Culture & history / Oxford

The Ashmolean Museum

Britain's first public museum — a world-class, free collection from antiquities to European painting and Asian and Islamic art.

01 / Why it matters

More than a stop on a list.

A world-class free museum ranging across antiquities, European painting and Asian and Islamic art — and one of Oxford's best rainy-day plans.

Founded in 1683, the Ashmolean is the University of Oxford's museum of art and archaeology and Britain's first public museum. Its present Greek Revival building on Beaumont Street, designed by Charles Cockerell and built 1841–1845, holds collections spanning ancient Egypt, Greece and the Near East through to European painting and Asian and Islamic art. It is one of Oxford's great free days out and a reliable rainy-day plan — large enough to reward a focused visit to two or three departments rather than a dash through everything.

02 / The essentials

What makes it remarkable.

  1. 01

    Britain's first public museum, founded in 1683; the present Greek Revival building on Beaumont Street was designed by Charles Cockerell and built 1841–1845.

  2. 02

    Free general admission; some special exhibitions are ticketed.

  3. 03

    Collections span ancient Egypt, Greece and the Near East through European painting to Asian and Islamic art.

  4. 04

    Signature objects include the Alfred Jewel, the plastered Jericho Skull, the Sumerian King List and Old Master drawings by Michelangelo and Raphael.

  5. 05

    A rooftop restaurant tops the museum; allow 1.5–3 hours.

05 / Good to know

Questions,
answered.

01Is the Ashmolean Museum free?

Yes — general admission is free and no booking is needed for the permanent collections. Some special exhibitions are ticketed.

02How long do you need at the Ashmolean?

Allow 1.5–3 hours. It is large, so it rewards focusing on two or three departments rather than rushing everything.

03What are the must-see objects at the Ashmolean?

The Alfred Jewel, the Jericho Skull, the Sumerian King List, ancient Egyptian collections, and Old Master drawings by Michelangelo and Raphael.
