# Product Image Library

## Purpose

Define how product imagery is selected, transformed and maintained for email without changing product truth.

## Product-image principles

1. Product identity must remain exact.
2. Variant mapping must be explicit.
3. Packaging version must be current.
4. Background treatments must not alter product colour.
5. Email derivatives must remain traceable to the source packshot or approved photograph.
6. Product groups must represent combinations that can actually be purchased or used together.

## Required product asset types

Where available, maintain:

- front packshot;
- angled packshot;
- side or rear view when operationally useful;
- texture or formula image;
- applicator image;
- scale or in-hand image;
- lifestyle image;
- routine grouping;
- shade or variant comparison;
- mobile crop;
- transparent-background master;
- neutral-background fallback.

Not every product requires every type. The manifest must show which approved roles exist.

## Product identity protections

Do not alter:

- bottle, jar, cap, pump or brush geometry;
- label text or typography;
- logo position;
- packaging colour;
- product colour;
- shade identifier;
- quantity or pack size;
- included accessories;
- reflective or material characteristics in a misleading way.

## Allowed operations

Subject to review:

- crop;
- scale;
- background removal;
- neutral shadow creation;
- compression;
- colour-profile conversion;
- placement on an approved generated or photographed surface;
- grouping with other approved product sources.

These operations must not distort or obscure product identity.

## Variant mapping

Every variant-specific asset must include:

- product ID;
- variant ID;
- approved variant name;
- packaging version;
- colour or shade reference;
- market availability;
- replacement or retirement state.

Never select a variant by visual resemblance alone.

## Routine groupings

A grouped image must be approved against:

- actual product compatibility;
- current catalogue availability;
- correct relative scale;
- accurate packaging;
- no duplicated or invented product;
- no implication that all items are included when sold separately;
- copy and CTA destination.

## Product-card assets

Product-card exports should:

- use consistent scale within a grid;
- preserve readable packaging where practical;
- avoid arbitrary crop differences;
- use a stable background treatment;
- remain clear at mobile width;
- include a fallback when the preferred image is unavailable.

## Hero product assets

Hero imagery may use more art direction, but must still:

- show the correct product and variant;
- preserve packaging and colour;
- provide copy-safe space;
- support an intentional mobile crop;
- avoid unrealistic product scale;
- avoid false liquid, texture or application claims.

## Shade and colour accuracy

For shade-dependent products:

- use colour-managed sources;
- preserve approved tone references;
- review exports on representative displays;
- avoid warm filters that change shade meaning;
- do not use generated swatches as product evidence;
- distinguish illustrative mood imagery from colour-accurate product imagery.

## Packaging changes

When packaging changes:

1. mark previous assets as superseded;
2. create a new major version;
3. identify affected templates and sequences;
4. update routine groupings;
5. review any embedded copy or alt text;
6. preserve previous assets only for historical send records.

## Availability and catalogue state

The asset manifest should distinguish:

- active;
- temporarily unavailable;
- market-limited;
- discontinued;
- replaced;
- professional-only;
- consumer-only.

Asset approval does not override catalogue state.

## Release blockers

Do not use when:

- variant mapping is unknown;
- packaging is outdated;
- product colour has shifted;
- source rights are unclear;
- the composite changes product form;
- a grouped image misrepresents bundle contents;
- the image destination points to a different product or variant;
- mobile crop makes the product unrecognisable.
