# Product Discovery and Merchandising

## Purpose

Define how customers discover the right product quickly, without presenting the range as an undifferentiated catalogue.

## Merchandising principle

Organise products around the result the customer wants, then explain the format.

The customer should never need expert product knowledge before the website becomes useful.

## Primary discovery dimensions

Prioritise:

- Desired depth: subtle, medium, deep
- Application area: face, body, both
- Development speed: instant, express, overnight, gradual
- Experience level: first-time, confident, professional result
- Undertone suitability
- Finish: natural, luminous, matte
- Format: mousse, mist, serum, lotion

## Collection-page hierarchy

### Opening

Use a concise editorial introduction that explains the collection and offers a visible route to Find Your Shade.

### Discovery controls

Use a limited, understandable filter set:

- Result
- Area
- Development time
- Format
- Skin or undertone suitability

Do not expose every technical product attribute as a filter.

### Product grid

Each card should communicate:

- Product name
- Product type
- One-line result statement
- Suitable user or use case
- Price
- Review summary, when supported
- Primary product image
- Available variants, where relevant

Avoid long badge stacks.

## Product card language

Preferred examples:

> Natural medium colour for face and body.

> Best for first-time tanners who want controllable depth.

> Develops in 2–4 hours.

Avoid vague phrases such as “iconic glow” or “ultimate radiance” unless supported by useful detail.

## Sorting

Recommended options:

- Recommended
- Best Selling
- Newest
- Price: Low to High
- Price: High to Low

“Recommended” should reflect deliberate merchandising, not an unexplained algorithm.

## Comparison support

Allow comparison only when it helps resolve real ambiguity.

A useful comparison should show:

- Result depth
- Undertone suitability
- Development time
- Application area
- Format
- Experience level
- Finish

Do not create oversized comparison tables for products with obvious differences.

## Best-seller discipline

Best sellers should be curated, not merely ranked.

Each featured product must represent a distinct customer need. Avoid displaying four near-identical mousses without explaining the difference.

## Bundles and routines

Merchandise routines as a practical system:

1. Prepare
2. Apply
3. Perfect

Every bundle should explain:

- Who it is for
- Why the products belong together
- What is included
- The saving, only where genuine
- Whether products can be removed or substituted

## Out-of-stock behaviour

- Keep the product page available where useful.
- Clearly state the stock status.
- Offer back-in-stock notification.
- Suggest only a genuinely equivalent alternative.
- Preserve reviews, instructions and result content.

Do not silently redirect to a collection page.

## Search merchandising

For customer-intent queries such as “face tan,” “first time,” “deep result” or “no transfer,” return:

1. Most relevant products
2. Shade-match route
3. Relevant results
4. Relevant guidance content

## Mobile discovery

- Keep filters in a clear modal or drawer.
- Show the active filter count.
- Preserve filter state when visiting and returning from a product page.
- Avoid tiny horizontal filter chips as the only control.
- Keep product-card copy concise and readable.

## Commercial guardrails

- Never let a sponsored or campaign product override obvious customer suitability.
- Do not use false scarcity.
- Do not overpopulate the first viewport.
- Do not repeat the same products in multiple consecutive modules.
- Every recommendation must explain why it is relevant.

## Success criteria

Product discovery succeeds when customers can identify a plausible best-fit product within one collection view, understand why products differ and move into the product page without unresolved basic questions.
