# 01 — Entry Points and Pre-Quiz Framing

## Role

Entry points must present Shade Match as useful expert guidance, not as a promotional interruption or lead-capture gimmick.

## Approved entry points

- Homepage hero primary CTA
- Homepage recommendation chapter
- Collection-page guidance panel
- Product-page “Not sure which shade?” link
- Search and no-results recovery
- Navigation utility link
- Cart reassurance for customers with an unresolved variant

## Entry-point message hierarchy

1. **Outcome:** find the product and depth most likely to suit the customer.
2. **Effort:** approximately one minute or a small number of questions.
3. **Privacy:** no email required before the recommendation.
4. **Authority:** guided by Jimmy Coco’s professional method.

## Preferred copy

### Primary label

**Find My Shade**

### Supporting line

> Answer a few quick questions for a personalised product and depth recommendation.

### Reassurance

> Takes about 60 seconds. No email required.

Only use time and privacy statements when operationally accurate.

## Pre-quiz introduction screen

The introduction should contain:

- concise headline;
- one-sentence benefit;
- three brief outcomes;
- clear start button;
- quiet skip route back to shopping;
- optional visual showing natural skin depth variation rather than artificial swatches.

Suggested outcomes:

- your best-fit product;
- your recommended depth and number of coats;
- a simple application routine.

## Visual direction

Use a restrained editorial composition with one human skin image or a small set of accurate result references. Avoid quiz-game graphics, loud gradients, confetti, oversized progress rings and cartoon illustrations.

## Contextual entry behaviour

Entry source should influence framing but not secretly pre-answer questions.

Examples:

- From a face collection: acknowledge interest in facial tanning, then confirm within the questionnaire.
- From a dark-depth product: mention that the customer can check whether that depth fits, but do not bias the result.
- From cart: preserve existing cart contents and return the customer after the recommendation.

## Exit behaviour

Customers must be able to leave without losing the underlying page state. Use calm copy such as **“Return to shopping”** rather than “Abandon quiz.”

## Failure modes

Reject entry designs that:

- demand email before starting;
- exaggerate scientific accuracy;
- appear as intrusive pop-ups immediately on arrival;
- use discounts as the primary reason to participate;
- hide the expected effort;
- or route every entry point to a different version of the questionnaire.

## Success criteria

The visitor understands what they will receive, how long it takes and that they remain in control before pressing Start.
