# Copy Testing and QA

## Purpose

Define how Sunless email copy is reviewed, tested and approved before production use.

## Principle

Copy quality is not measured only by clicks. It must also protect clarity, trust, deliverability, accessibility, margin and service accuracy.

## Review order

Review in this sequence:

1. Customer state and message necessity
2. Data and operational accuracy
3. Core proposition
4. Message structure
5. Claim and legal compliance
6. CTA clarity
7. Tone and brand consistency
8. Personalisation and fallbacks
9. Subject and preview text
10. Grammar, formatting and final proof

Do not polish a subject line before confirming that the customer should receive the message.

## Copy brief requirements

Every email must define:

- lifecycle or service owner;
- eligible audience;
- trigger;
- primary customer need;
- primary business objective;
- one main message;
- required data;
- primary CTA;
- suppressions and conflicts;
- claim or legal dependencies;
- success measure;
- guardrails.

## Structural QA

Confirm:

- the opening establishes relevance quickly;
- one dominant idea leads the email;
- paragraphs are short enough for inbox reading;
- headings add navigation rather than decoration;
- the body explains why the CTA is useful;
- secondary content does not compete with the main action;
- the ending resolves naturally;
- plain-text order matches intended hierarchy.

## Subject and preview QA

Check that:

- they work together rather than repeat;
- the subject accurately represents the email;
- the preview adds context or value;
- personalisation has a fallback;
- urgency is factual;
- no unsupported claim is introduced;
- truncation does not create misleading meaning;
- service messages remain explicit.

## Dynamic-copy QA

Test every meaningful variant, including:

- missing first name;
- long and short product names;
- multiple basket items;
- single basket item;
- missing image;
- out-of-stock product;
- changed price;
- alternative shade recommendation;
- different currencies and markets;
- VIP transition states;
- unknown or delayed delivery date.

The rendered sentence must remain grammatical and complete in every state.

## Claim QA

For every performance, ingredient, comparison, scarcity, authority or celebrity claim, record:

- exact approved wording;
- evidence or approval source;
- applicable products;
- market limitations;
- expiry or review date;
- required qualification;
- owner.

Remove claims that cannot be traced.

## Tone QA

Ask:

- Does this sound calm and expert?
- Is it useful before it is promotional?
- Does it respect the customer’s choice?
- Is the level of familiarity earned by the relationship?
- Does the tone match the active lifecycle state?
- Has luxury become empty ornament?
- Has brevity removed necessary clarity?

## Accessibility and comprehension

Review for:

- plain language;
- descriptive link and button labels;
- headings that make sense out of visual context;
- no essential information expressed only through imagery;
- understandable dates, times and units;
- acronyms explained when necessary;
- reading order and screen-reader coherence;
- suitable alt text supplied with the copy brief.

## Test hierarchy

Prioritise tests in this order:

1. Message relevance
2. Proposition and customer value
3. Lifecycle timing
4. CTA meaning
5. Content order
6. Proof or guidance type
7. Subject and preview approach
8. Length
9. Personalisation
10. Decorative phrasing

Do not optimise punctuation while the offer, timing or audience remains uncertain.

## Suitable hypotheses

Examples:

- guidance-led browse reminders create stronger qualified return than discount-led reminders;
- explaining why a shade fits increases recommendation acceptance;
- explicit service subject lines reduce support contacts;
- one clear routine recommendation outperforms a broad product grid;
- benefit-led VIP copy creates better sustained use than status-led flattery;
- specific deadline language reduces complaints compared with vague urgency.

## Test design requirements

Every test must define:

- hypothesis;
- control and treatment;
- eligible audience;
- primary outcome;
- guardrails;
- sample and duration assumptions;
- stopping rule;
- decision rule;
- implementation owner.

Do not run tests that withhold essential service, consent, safety, account or programme-change information.

## Outcome measures

Depending on purpose, review:

- qualified site return;
- shade-result acceptance;
- basket recovery;
- purchase and contribution margin;
- product returns;
- support contacts;
- benefit usage;
- unsubscribe and complaint rates;
- repeat purchase;
- time to resolution;
- customer-service satisfaction.

Open rate is diagnostic and may be unreliable. It is not sufficient proof of copy effectiveness.

## Approval checklist

Before release, confirm:

- audience and trigger are correct;
- message has one clear job;
- all data and claims are validated;
- subject, preview, headline and CTA align;
- fallbacks have been rendered;
- legal and service language is approved;
- links and destination states are correct;
- plain text is complete;
- spelling and punctuation are checked;
- no internal labels or placeholders remain;
- final copy is stored as the approved source of truth.
