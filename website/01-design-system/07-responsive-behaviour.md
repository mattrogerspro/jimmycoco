# Responsive Behaviour Standards

## Purpose

This document defines how the Sunless by Jimmy Coco website adapts across desktop, tablet and mobile without losing hierarchy, editorial intent, product clarity or conversion strength.

Responsive design is not a mechanical reduction of the desktop composition. Each breakpoint must preserve the role of the scene while changing its architecture to suit the available space.

## Governing principle

Preserve the **narrative, hierarchy and emotional sequence**, not the exact desktop arrangement.

The responsive system must protect:

- The primary message
- The primary CTA
- Product recognition
- Jimmy Coco’s authority
- Skin-result credibility
- Clear shade guidance
- Readable proof
- Deliberate chapter separation
- Luxury restraint

A mobile layout that merely stacks every desktop element is not acceptable.

## Breakpoint model

Use content-led breakpoints rather than device-brand breakpoints.

Recommended ranges:

- **Large desktop:** 1440px and above
- **Desktop:** 1180–1439px
- **Tablet landscape:** 900–1179px
- **Tablet portrait:** 700–899px
- **Mobile:** 390–699px
- **Small mobile:** below 390px

These ranges are directional. Components should adapt when their composition breaks, not only when a fixed breakpoint is crossed.

## Universal responsive rules

### 1. Preserve one dominant action

Every scene must retain one visually dominant action. Secondary actions may collapse into text links, accordions or later placement.

### 2. Protect the first five seconds

On mobile, the first viewport must still communicate:

- What Sunless is
- The desired result
- Why it is credible
- What the visitor should do next

### 3. Prioritise editorial image crops

Do not centre-crop every image automatically. Each responsive crop must preserve:

- Face
- Skin result
- Product
- Jimmy’s working gesture where relevant
- Negative space required for copy

### 4. Avoid excessive vertical inflation

Mobile should not become an endless sequence of oversized headings, padded cards and duplicate copy. Compress secondary content while preserving breathing room around primary moments.

### 5. Maintain chapter distinction

The 10% zoom principle becomes a scroll-rhythm principle on mobile. Consecutive scenes must still differ through:

- Background tone
- Image proportion
- Alignment
- Density
- Type scale
- Card usage
- Edge treatment

## Typography scaling

Use fluid type with defined minimum and maximum values.

Recommended ranges:

- Hero headline: 42–72px
- Chapter heading: 34–56px
- Editorial pull quote: 28–48px
- Product title: 20–28px
- Body copy: 16–19px
- Metadata: 13–15px
- Buttons: 14–16px

Headlines must not be reduced until they lose editorial authority. Prefer fewer words, improved line breaks and narrower measures before making type too small.

## Horizontal margins

Recommended outer gutters:

- Large desktop: 64–96px
- Desktop: 48–72px
- Tablet: 32–48px
- Mobile: 20–24px
- Small mobile: 16–20px

Full-bleed photography may ignore these gutters, but live text and controls should return to a safe content margin.

## Scene-specific responsive behaviour

### Navigation and utility

Desktop:

- Full primary navigation
- Visible shade-match route
- Utility icons

Tablet:

- Reduced top-level links
- Shade match remains visible
- Secondary links move into menu

Mobile:

- Logo, menu, cart and one clear shade-match action
- No crowded icon row
- Sticky navigation only if it remains visually quiet

### Hero

Desktop:

- Layered cinematic composition with copy, model, Jimmy and product visible together

Tablet:

- Preserve model and product as dominant
- Jimmy may move to a secondary background crop
- Copy width reduces

Mobile:

- Use one decisive image crop
- Place headline and CTA either above or below the image, not over a visually complex area
- Jimmy’s authority may appear as an integrated caption or lower image moment
- Product must remain visible in the first or second viewport

Do not attempt to fit all desktop hero layers into one narrow frame.

### Authority strip

Desktop:

- Horizontal editorial colophon

Mobile:

- Two-row or horizontally scrollable proof rail
- No carousel requiring precision interaction
- Never reduce proof text below readable size

### Shade recommendation engine

Desktop:

- Contained guided workspace with progress, question and result area

Tablet:

- Single-column flow with result preview below question

Mobile:

- One question per view
- Large touch targets
- Persistent progress cue
- Sticky next action only when it does not obscure content
- Recommendation result becomes a focused product card with one CTA

### Hollywood Method

Desktop:

- Three-part editorial explanation with controlled directional flow

Mobile:

- Convert to a numbered vertical sequence
- Use one supporting image or diagram at a time
- Keep each step concise
- Do not create three identical stacked cards

### Real Results

Desktop:

- Large proof image with forensic metadata

Mobile:

- Image first
- Metadata in a compact structured block
- Before/after pairs must remain comparable without tiny side-by-side images
- Prefer swipe or stacked sequence where necessary

### Curated Best Sellers

Desktop:

- Four-product gallery

Tablet:

- Two-column gallery

Mobile:

- One product per row or controlled two-column arrangement only when product labels remain readable
- Product suitability and result must remain visible without opening a modal

### Jimmy Coco editorial story

Desktop:

- Asymmetrical portrait-led editorial spread

Mobile:

- Portrait image first or second, followed by concise story and quote
- Avoid turning the story into biography cards
- Preserve one large emotional image

### Professional comparison

Desktop:

- Two-column or side-by-side comparison

Mobile:

- Stack by comparison criterion, not by brand
- Each row should read: criterion → Sunless → generic alternative
- Avoid requiring horizontal table scrolling where possible

### Customer stories

Desktop:

- Varied portrait-led testimonials

Mobile:

- One story at a time
- Concern label, customer image, result and quote remain together
- Avoid tiny multi-card carousels

### Complete the routine

Desktop:

- Prepare → Apply → Perfect linear flow

Mobile:

- Vertical numbered ritual
- Product, role and outcome grouped together
- Bundle CTA follows the full sequence

### Final conversion scene

Desktop:

- Dark cinematic closing scene

Mobile:

- Maintain strong tonal contrast
- Keep one product route and one shade-match route
- Avoid excessive copy

### Community, newsletter and footer

Mobile:

- Newsletter first
- Community second
- Utility links in accordions or grouped lists
- Legal and support content remains accessible without dominating the closing experience

## Interaction adaptation

Desktop hover states must have mobile equivalents.

Any information revealed on hover must also be available through:

- Tap
- Focus
- Persistent label
- Expandable disclosure

Do not rely on hover for product suitability, comparison details or trust proof.

## Touch targets

Minimum target size:

- 44px by 44px for essential controls
- 48px preferred for primary actions and answer selectors

Maintain at least 8px separation between adjacent controls.

## Image loading priorities

Prioritise:

1. Hero visual
2. Hero product image
3. Recommendation-engine assets
4. First proof image

Defer lower-page lifestyle imagery where appropriate.

Use responsive image sources and modern formats. Do not serve desktop-scale imagery to mobile unnecessarily.

## Responsive quality checks

Every scene must be tested at:

- 1440px
- 1280px
- 1024px
- 768px
- 430px
- 390px
- 360px

Review at each width for:

- Overlapping copy
- Orphaned words
- Broken image crops
- Lost CTA hierarchy
- Excessive empty space
- Tiny metadata
- Repetitive card stacking
- Abrupt chapter transitions
- Hidden product information

## Success criteria

Responsive implementation succeeds when:

- The homepage still feels art-directed on mobile
- The primary journey remains obvious
- Product choice becomes easier, not harder
- Typography retains authority
- Photography remains emotionally credible
- No critical information is desktop-only
- Mobile length feels deliberate rather than bloated
- Every scene retains its original narrative purpose
