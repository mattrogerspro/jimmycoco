# VIP Benefits, Recognition and Service Design

## Purpose

Define benefits that feel genuinely valuable, can be delivered consistently and reinforce the premium Sunless relationship.

## Benefit hierarchy

Prioritise benefits in this order:

1. Better service
2. Better access
3. Better guidance
4. Better convenience
5. Thoughtful recognition
6. Financial incentives

## Recommended benefit categories

### Service

- priority routing for genuine support requests;
- expert product or shade guidance;
- faster escalation where operationally possible;
- proactive help after a documented service issue;
- clear named ownership for private-client support when available.

Do not promise instant or 24/7 support unless it exists.

### Access

- early access to approved launches;
- limited preview windows;
- invitation to product education or brand events;
- first notice of restocks relevant to prior purchases;
- access to selected product testing when consent and operations permit.

“Exclusive” must mean genuinely restricted access.

### Guidance

- personalised routine review based on consented purchase and shade-match data;
- product-transition advice when formulas or ranges change;
- professional application education;
- relevant seasonal or occasion planning.

Do not fabricate personal recommendations from spend alone.

### Convenience

- saved, validated routine or reorder path;
- simplified product comparison;
- optional replenishment preferences;
- consolidated account and benefit view;
- support for changing variants or formats.

### Recognition

- qualification welcome;
- anniversary or tenure recognition;
- meaningful order or routine milestones;
- handwritten or human-reviewed notes for genuinely high-touch segments;
- unexpected value that does not require another purchase.

### Financial benefits

Optional examples:

- policy-approved reward;
- shipping benefit;
- gift with qualifying purchase;
- private bundle;
- limited event credit.

Every financial benefit requires current terms, exclusions, expiry, market availability and margin approval.

## Benefit quality test

Before publishing a benefit, confirm:

- it exists operationally;
- capacity is sufficient;
- customer eligibility can be calculated accurately;
- fulfilment ownership is assigned;
- terms are clear;
- expiry and exclusions are known;
- support can resolve failure;
- the benefit is meaningful enough to mention.

## Recognition tone

Recognition should be warm, restrained and specific.

Use:

- “Thank you for continuing to choose Sunless.”
- “Your continued support has qualified you for…”
- “We’re pleased to offer you earlier access…”

Avoid:

- “You’re one of our most obsessed customers.”
- “You spent enough to unlock…”
- “Prove your VIP status.”
- language that exposes spend thresholds unnecessarily.

## Benefit ledger

Maintain a customer-level record containing:

- benefit ID and version;
- eligibility date;
- issued date;
- redemption state;
- expiry;
- market and product exclusions;
- fulfilment status;
- failure or support state;
- source campaign or lifecycle event.

## Failure recovery

When a promised benefit fails:

1. pause related promotional messaging;
2. route the case to support;
3. explain the issue plainly;
4. restore, replace or honour the benefit according to policy;
5. record the failure for programme QA.

## Design principle

VIP value should remain evident even when no discount is active. If the programme has nothing meaningful to offer without a promotion, the service design is incomplete.
