Shipping Configuration Review

Sunless by Jimmy Coco · Prepared 5 August 2026 · Data window: 23 July – 4 August 2026 and trailing 12 months

Summary

Shipping on the website is misconfigured in three separate, compounding ways. An undisclosed automatic discount is giving away free UK delivery on roughly a third of orders; the published rates are wrong on every page that quotes them; and the site advertises a free-delivery threshold that does not exist.

None of these is visible from the Shopify shipping settings screen, which is why the behaviour has appeared random. All three were confirmed against live order records and the store's delivery and discount configuration.

Finding 1 — A hidden discount is zeroing the shipping fee

▲ Critical — revenue leak, live now

An automatic app-based discount is removing the £3.99 UK shipping charge whenever a basket contains The A-List Face Tanning Mist. It has been active since 23 July 2026 and does not appear in the store's standard shipping settings.

The rule is stored as a Shopify Function discount titled A-list Face mist, supplied by an app under the discount type shipping-discount-2. On the customer's order it appears simply as "FREE SHIPPING", with no explanation of what triggered it.

Because it is a product-triggered rule rather than a basket-value rule, two customers spending exactly the same amount on the same day can be charged different totals. That is the behaviour that has looked arbitrary.

Commercial impact

The A-List Face Tanning Mist appeared in 162 orders in the 13 days since the rule went live. At £3.99 per order that is £646 of shipping revenue foregone, or approximately £18,100 a year if the rule and the current sales rate both continue. Measured against the £1,358 of shipping actually collected in the same period, the store is losing roughly a third of its potential UK shipping income without having decided to.

If this promotion was deliberate, it is simply not being marketed — no page mentions it, so it is buying no additional sales. If it was not deliberate, it is a straightforward leak.

Finding 2 — Published shipping rates do not match what is charged

▲ High — suppressing add-to-cart

Product pages quote £5.50 and the shipping policy page references £5.99. The store actually charges £3.99. Customers are being quoted up to £2.00 more than they will pay.

Average order value is £26.93. At that basket size, £5.99 reads as 22% of the order while £3.99 reads as 15% — a material difference at the moment a shopper decides whether to proceed.

Critically, this discourages customers before they reach the basket, so it never appears in cart or checkout abandonment reporting. It shows up only as a weaker add-to-cart rate, where the cause is invisible.

The £5.99 figure is not a UK rate at all — it is the Channel Islands express price, which appears to have been copied into UK-facing content.

Finding 3 — The site advertises free delivery that does not exist

▲ Critical — breaks a promise at checkout

Product pages state "Free UK/NI Delivery on orders over £30." No such rate exists in the store. The UK zone contains exactly one delivery method: £3.99, with no conditions and no free tier.

This was verified twice — once against the delivery profile and again against every active and expired discount rule. The only all-countries free-shipping promotion, "Free shipping on all orders over £40", expired on 17 June 2026 after running for less than a day.

The consequence is a cost surprise at the worst possible moment. With an average order value of £26.93 — just below the advertised £30 threshold — customers are likely adding an extra item specifically to qualify for free delivery, then being charged £3.99 regardless.

The store's own funnel data carries the fingerprint of exactly this:

Across all traffic, 6,033 sessions a year reach checkout without completing, representing roughly £162,000 in attempted purchases. Some abandonment is unavoidable; recovering even a modest share of the portion caused by this mismatch is worth several thousand pounds annually.

◆ Compliance note

Advertising free delivery above a stated threshold and then charging for it is a misleading price indication under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations, independent of its commercial effect. This should be corrected promptly.

Three different sets of shipping prices are live simultaneously — one in Shopify, one on the policy page, one on product pages — and no two agree. This pattern indicates the rates are hard-coded into the theme in at least two places rather than drawn from Shopify, which means they will drift again the next time rates change.

Recommended actions

  1. Decide whether the free-shipping rule is intended — today Locate the app-generated discount A-list Face mist and either remove it or promote it. It is currently costing roughly £50 a day and generating no marketing benefit, because no customer is told it exists.
  2. Remove the "Free UK/NI Delivery over £30" claim, or make it real Either delete the claim from product pages, or add a genuine UK free-shipping tier at £30. The second option costs £3.99 on qualifying baskets but honours what customers have already been promised — and with a £26.93 average order value, a £30 threshold is well placed to lift basket sizes. This should be a deliberate commercial decision rather than an accident of the theme.
  3. Correct every published rate to £3.99 Update product pages and the shipping policy page. The headline rate falls from £5.50 to £3.99, which should improve add-to-cart rates at no cost.
  4. Fix the international claims Correct the Ireland threshold to €40, remove the EU free-delivery claim entirely, correct EU rates to the actual per-zone pricing, and remove the phantom UK express service from the policy page.
  5. Make rates dynamic, not hard-coded Pull shipping rates from Shopify rather than typing them into the theme. Until this is done, every future rate change will reintroduce the same class of error.

✓ Expected outcome

Recovering the shipping leak is worth approximately £18,100 a year. Correcting the quoted rates should lift add-to-cart rates, and removing the false free-delivery promise addresses a checkout drop-off currently sitting within a £162,000 annual pool of abandoned baskets. None of the five actions requires development work beyond content edits and one discount setting.

Method & scope. Findings were verified against Shopify delivery profiles, all active and expired discount rules, individual order records with line-item and discount-allocation detail, and the live published content of the shipping policy and product pages. Funnel figures are drawn from Shopify session analytics for the trailing twelve months to 4 August 2026. The annualised impact figure extrapolates from a 13-day observation window and assumes current sales mix continues; it should be treated as an indicative run rate rather than a forecast. No changes were made to the store during this review.
