# Campaign and Scene Continuity

## Purpose

Define how AI-assisted email assets remain recognisably part of one Sunless brand world across a sequence, campaign and wider customer lifecycle.

Continuity does not mean repeating the same composition. It means preserving the same visual truth, product truth and emotional language while allowing each message to perform a distinct role.

## Continuity layers

Maintain continuity across:

1. brand palette and surfaces;
2. typography and module hierarchy;
3. product scale, colour and packaging;
4. lighting direction and photographic character;
5. skin presentation and result realism;
6. campaign architecture and narrative progression;
7. image treatment and crop behaviour;
8. CTA and copy hierarchy;
9. protected people, product and logo assets;
10. mobile and desktop adaptations.

## Campaign continuity brief

Before generating campaign assets, define:

- campaign ID and version;
- central proposition;
- customer state;
- visual world;
- approved palette;
- lighting direction;
- surface and material language;
- product hero rules;
- human-presence rules;
- documentary versus generative boundaries;
- sequence order;
- required differences between messages;
- prohibited repetition;
- desktop and mobile crop strategy.

## Sequence progression

Each email should advance the story.

Example progression:

1. introduce the customer need;
2. clarify the recommendation or method;
3. provide proof or guidance;
4. reduce a remaining objection;
5. support action or completion.

Do not create five visually interchangeable emails with different copy placed over the same scene.

## Visual constants

Possible constants include:

- warm ivory, linen, stone and champagne surfaces;
- restrained matte-black accents;
- natural directional light;
- editorial negative space;
- accurate product photography;
- realistic, unretouched skin texture;
- refined serif headline and clear sans-serif support copy;
- one dominant focal point per module.

Only use constants that are approved by the active design system and campaign brief.

## Controlled variation

Vary deliberately:

- camera distance;
- composition density;
- product grouping;
- surface height;
- image orientation;
- subject placement;
- amount of negative space;
- editorial versus instructional emphasis;
- human presence;
- crop and module scale.

Variation must not alter product truth or imply a different result.

## Product continuity

Across every campaign asset:

- use the same approved source image or verified product master;
- preserve exact packaging, labels, cap, pump, colour and proportions;
- keep variant identity consistent;
- do not allow AI to redraw labels;
- do not change fill level or product shade unless the source asset does;
- record every crop, mask and compositing operation.

## Human and celebrity continuity

Protected people must remain identical to their approved source images.

Permitted operations are limited to approved placement, scale, masking and non-destructive crop. Do not regenerate, retouch, recolour or harmonise faces, bodies or skin through generative tools.

A campaign must not imply that a person used, endorsed or achieved a result from a product unless that exact claim and usage are approved.

## Result continuity

Before-and-after or customer-result imagery is documentary evidence.

Preserve:

- source crop relationship;
- lighting disclosure;
- development timing;
- product and application context;
- skin tone and texture;
- required qualification or disclaimer.

Never use a generated result as proof.

## Copy and image relationship

The image must support the message without carrying facts that disappear when images are blocked.

Check:

- subject, preview, headline and image express the same proposition;
- image does not introduce unsupported scarcity, celebrity use or results;
- CTA remains understandable without the image;
- image-safe text areas remain consistent between desktop and mobile;
- campaign imagery does not overpower lifecycle relevance.

## Continuity board

For each campaign, maintain a review board containing:

- all approved email renders in order;
- desktop and mobile versions;
- source assets;
- prompt IDs;
- palette and lighting reference;
- product scale comparison;
- rejected repetition or inconsistency examples;
- approval notes.

Review the entire board, not only individual assets.

## Continuity failure modes

Reject or revise when:

- the brand world changes unpredictably between emails;
- product scale or packaging changes;
- skin tone or texture is inconsistent;
- lighting creates incompatible product colour;
- every message uses the same composition;
- a mobile crop tells a different story from desktop;
- a celebrity or customer image appears altered;
- a generated scene creates unsupported usage context;
- the campaign looks like unrelated AI outputs rather than one directed system.

## Approval standard

A campaign is continuous when every message is individually useful, visually distinct and unmistakably part of the same approved Sunless experience.
