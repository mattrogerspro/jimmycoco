Why seed now

Winter is the window — because getting ready takes weeks.

It's mid-July. Tanning feels a season away, which is exactly the point: the salons that are ready in October win the whole summer. The ones that start looking in October are already behind, because trialling a solution, training staff on the shade method and placing a first stock order all need lead time.

31 Oct – 7 Nov

Spring Racing Carnival — Derby Day, the Melbourne Cup (Tue 3 Nov 2026), Oaks Day & Stakes Day. The first major tan-demand spike of the season.

Nov – Dec

School formals, graduations and the spring–summer wedding run.

December

Christmas parties and New Year's Eve — a month of booked-out weekends.

Dec – Feb

The Australian summer holidays — sustained peak demand through February.

The lead-time argument does the selling. Reaching out in winter isn't early — it's precisely timed. A July first-touch leaves ~10–14 weeks to sample, train and stock before the carnival opens the season.

Who to reach

Build the list around decision-makers.

Ideal targets

  • Dedicated spray-tan studios and mobile tanning businesses
  • Beauty & skin salons with an existing tan booth
  • Hair-and-beauty salons adding tanning as a summer service
  • Higher-end / boutique salons that fit the premium positioning

List hygiene

  • Get the owner or manager's first name — never send to info@
  • Verify addresses (bounce-check) before sending
  • Capture city/suburb for light personalisation
  • Segment by state if you want to localise the season calendar
A note on channel tension. Sunless's consumer story positions the at-home product as "as good as a salon tan." To salons, never lead with that. The B2B pitch is the opposite and complimentary: Jimmy's professional solution makes their service better, and the take-home range earns them margin. Keep the two audiences' messaging cleanly separate.

Plain text
Why 1, 2 & 5 stay plain text. True cold email lands best when it looks like a person typed it. Reserve the branded HTML (Emails 3 & 4) for once there's a flicker of engagement — or A/B it against the plain versions. Every email carries a single ask, and for cold, that ask is a reply.

Set-up & sending

Get the plumbing right, or none of it lands.

Personalisation tokens

  • {{first_name}} — owner / manager first name
  • {{salon_name}} — the salon's business name
  • {{city}} — city or suburb (optional colour)
  • {{sender_name}} · {{sender_title}}
  • {{calendar_link}} — 15-min booking link
  • {{shade_guide_link}} — Jimmy's shade guide PDF
  • {{business_address}} · {{unsubscribe_link}} — required in every send

Deliverability checklist

  • Send from a dedicated domain/inbox, warmed 2–3 weeks first
  • Authenticate: SPF, DKIM, DMARC
  • Start under ~40 sends/inbox/day; ramp slowly
  • Emails 1, 2 & 5 as plain text for best inbox placement
  • One ask per email — for cold, make it a reply
  • Send Tue–Thu, 8–10am salon-local; stop on reply
Australian compliance (Spam Act 2003). Every send needs accurate sender identification and a working unsubscribe, and you should only email business roles the message is genuinely relevant to (B2B outreach relevant to the recipient's function can rely on inferred consent). This is practical guidance, not legal advice — confirm your set-up with your own adviser.

Measuring it

Watch replies, not opens.

For cold B2B, reply rate and sample requests are the truth; opens are increasingly unreliable. Directional benchmarks for a well-targeted, well-warmed list:
