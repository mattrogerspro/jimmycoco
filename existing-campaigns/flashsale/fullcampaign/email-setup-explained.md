# Why the emails land in Promotions (and how to improve it)

**Good news first:** the technical setup is correct. We verified the actual delivered email — it passes every security check Gmail runs (the email equivalent of valid ID and letterhead), and it reaches the Gmail inbox. So this is **not** a "broken setup" problem.

## So why are opens low on Gmail?

Because Gmail files promotional emails into the **Promotions tab** by default — a separate tab people check far less often than their main inbox. It's not spam and it's not an error; it's just where Gmail puts marketing. That's the main reason the open rate looks low.

Three things influence whether you climb out of Promotions and get opened more:

1. **How engaged the list is.** Gmail watches whether *your* subscribers tend to open and click. Regularly removing people who never open actually lifts delivery for everyone else.
2. **How image-heavy the emails are.** Very image-led designs read as "advertising" to Gmail. A better balance of live text to images helps.
3. **The numbers can mislead.** Apple's Mail app auto-"opens" every email for privacy reasons, so its ~58% figure isn't real people. Judge success on **clicks and sales**, not opens.

## A couple of small housekeeping items

- **Tighten the domain's anti-spoofing setting** (currently "monitor only") so no one can impersonate the brand — and it unlocks the brand logo showing next to emails in Gmail.
- **Lock an old, unused domain** that still has leftover sending permissions.

**In short:** nothing is broken. The opportunity is getting out of the Promotions tab through better list health and content — and reading success by clicks and sales rather than opens.
