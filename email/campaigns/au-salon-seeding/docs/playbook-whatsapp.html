Before you send a single message

WhatsApp isn't email.

It actively polices business messaging, so this track only works where you can legitimately reach a business number — a salon's publicly listed WhatsApp Business number, or someone who's opted in. Don't import a cold list and blast it; that gets numbers banned.

Two ways to run it

Business app (manual, low volume) — send personally, one at a time, personalised. Best for a curated shortlist of high-value salons. Never use broadcast lists for cold contacts.

Business Platform / API (via a BSP) — the first business-initiated message must be a pre-approved template with an opt-out line. Message 1 has a template-formatted version for this.

The 24-hour window: once a salon replies, you have 24 hours of free-form conversation before you're back to approved templates. AU Spam Act 2003: identify yourself, contact only where relevant, always offer "Reply STOP". (Practical guidance, not legal advice.)

How it fits

One rhythm, two channels.

WhatsApp lands a day or two after each email, so every beat gets a second, softer knock. Ten touches across three weeks — or drop Email 2 for a lighter run.

Two specifics worth getting right

The API template & the voice note.

Message 1, formatted as an API template

If you send via the WhatsApp Business Platform, your first business-initiated message must be a pre-approved template. This version keeps the promotional restraint low and includes the required opt-out:

Hi {{first_name}}, it's {{sender_first}} from Sunless by Jimmy Coco — the professional tanning line from artist Jimmy Coco. We'd love to post {{salon_name}} a complimentary sample of our Sunset solution and shade guide before summer. Would you like us to send it? Reply STOP to opt out.

10:24 ✓✓

Optional voice-note script (~25 sec)

Voice notes convert once there's a flicker of interest — they feel unmistakably human. Read it naturally, don't perform it:

“Hey {{first_name}}, {{sender_first}} here from Sunless by Jimmy Coco — just a quick one. The thing that makes Jimmy Coco work for a salon is it pays off twice: you can charge a premium for the tan itself because the result and the name carry weight, and then there's the take-home range clients grab on the way out, which is margin you're probably leaving on the table right now. I'd genuinely love to send you a free sample so you can see the colour for yourself before the summer rush. Just reply here and I'll sort it. Cheers!”

Media tip: attach a single product shot (the Sunset 1 Ltr bottle) or one clean before/after with Message 1 or 3 — WhatsApp renders it inline and lifts replies. One image only; more reads as spam.

Sending notes

Keep it low, personal, human.

Do

  • Message business hours, salon-local — Tue–Thu, late AM / early arvo
  • Send one to one for cold — never broadcast lists
  • Personalise every message with a real first name
  • Keep volume low to protect number health
  • Use a voice note once there's any interest

Don't

  • Import a bought list and blast it — fast route to a ban
  • Attach more than one image
  • Send evenings or weekends
  • Keep messaging after a reply — switch to a real chat
  • Lengthen the copy; WhatsApp rewards brevity

Email + WhatsApp, together

Two knocks, one warm door.

Email carries the detail and the branded moments; WhatsApp carries the warmth and the quick yes. Run them on the one rhythm, stop the moment a salon replies, and let the free sample do the rest. Seed it now — the season looks after itself.
