# Scene 09 — Customer Stories and Reviews

## Role in the experience

This scene turns abstract social proof into relatable human evidence. It must resolve the visitor’s remaining concerns by showing customers with recognisable skin tones, experience levels and desired outcomes.

It follows the minimal comparison scene, so it should deliberately shift into a warmer, more intimate and emotionally human environment.

## Commercial objective

Increase conversion confidence by helping visitors find someone like themselves who achieved a believable result with a clearly identified Sunless product.

## User questions answered

- Does this work on skin like mine?
- Is it suitable for beginners?
- Will the colour look natural rather than orange?
- Will it fade evenly?
- Which product did this customer use?
- Can I trust these reviews?

## Core message

> Real people. Real skin. Results they would choose again.

Supporting statement:

> Explore verified customer stories by skin tone, experience and desired result.

## Architectural silhouette

This chapter must not become another row of equal review cards.

Use a warm, portrait-led editorial composition with deliberate asymmetry.

Suggested silhouette:

- Full-width warm taupe or soft cocoa environment
- One dominant testimonial occupying approximately half the scene
- One large customer portrait or before-and-after image
- Two or three smaller supporting stories arranged asymmetrically
- Filter controls kept quiet and compact
- A generous block of negative space around the principal quotation

The scene should feel like a luxury magazine feature about customers, not a review widget.

## Review hierarchy

### Featured story

The principal testimonial should include:

- Customer portrait or result image
- Large quotation
- Verified purchase marker
- Natural skin tone
- Experience level
- Product used
- Number of coats where known
- Development time where known
- Specific result or objection resolved

### Supporting stories

Use two or three concise stories addressing different concerns:

- Fair or cool-toned skin
- First-time self-tanner
- Medium or olive skin seeking natural depth
- Deep bronze result
- Even fading
- Event readiness

Do not display more than four full testimonials at once.

## Review copy standard

Prefer specific, believable statements such as:

> “I’m very fair and usually turn orange. One coat gave me a natural golden colour and faded evenly through the week.”

Avoid vague praise such as:

> “Amazing product. I love it.”

Generated concepts must not invent customer claims for production use. Use placeholder-style copy only until verified review content is supplied.

## Filter system

Possible filters:

- Fair skin
- Light–medium
- Olive
- Deep
- First time
- Natural glow
- Deep bronze

Filters should appear as refined compact controls, not a large cluster of bright pills.

## Layout direction

- Use one dominant 7-column story and a secondary 4-column story field.
- Alternate portrait orientation and text alignment.
- Let the primary quotation breathe.
- Use image cropping that prioritises skin result and authentic expression.
- Avoid putting every review inside a bordered white box.
- Use subtle tonal panels only where needed for legibility.

## Photography direction

- Realistic natural daylight
- Genuine skin texture retained
- Diverse ages and skin tones
- Calm, believable expressions
- No exaggerated influencer posing
- No orange cast
- No over-smoothed skin
- No artificial HDR
- No nightclub, pool-party or generic holiday imagery
- Consistent warm-neutral colour grade

The scene must feel inclusive without looking mechanically representative.

## Colour environment

- Warm taupe, cocoa or muted mushroom background
- Cream or ivory typography panels where necessary
- Deep charcoal text
- Muted gold star detail
- Soft bronze accents
- Natural skin tones with no oversaturation

This is one of the homepage’s clearest tonal departures from white.

## Typography

- Large serif quotation at approximately 32–44px
- Compact sans-serif metadata at approximately 14–16px
- Section heading at approximately 42–48px
- High line-height and generous quotation spacing
- Review metadata must remain readable rather than decorative

## Trust signals

Use only verifiable markers in production:

- Verified purchase
- Product used
- Skin tone
- Date or recency
- Review platform or source where relevant
- Unretouched or natural-light context where accurate

Avoid fabricated reviewer names, ratings or review counts.

## CTA strategy

Primary action:

**See Results on My Skin Tone**

Secondary action:

**Read All Verified Reviews**

The first action should route to filtered proof or the shade-matching journey.

## Eye-tracking path

1. Dominant customer portrait
2. Large quotation
3. Verified purchase and skin-tone metadata
4. Product used
5. Supporting customer stories
6. CTA

## Conversion psychology

- **Similarity bias:** visitors trust evidence from people who resemble them.
- **Social proof:** verified customer results reduce uncertainty.
- **Specificity effect:** precise detail feels more credible than generic praise.
- **Risk reduction:** objection-led stories answer practical fears.
- **Identity and belonging:** broad representation helps customers imagine themselves using the brand.

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Do not embed essential review copy inside images in production.
- Ensure filters work by keyboard and expose selected state to assistive technology.
- Add descriptive alt text for customer and result photography.
- Avoid carousel-only access to reviews; provide a linear fallback.
- Star rating must include a text equivalent.
- Preserve readable contrast over taupe backgrounds.

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Creates six identical white review cards
- Uses generic stock-photo headshots
- Produces waxy or heavily retouched skin
- Adds fabricated press logos or review platforms
- Makes every customer the same age, tone or visual type
- Uses tiny quotation text
- Adds an oversized cluster of filter chips
- Repeats the same rating and layout in every panel
- Makes the chapter feel like a SaaS testimonial grid
- Fails to create a distinct warm, intimate mood

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- Visitors can quickly find a customer story relevant to them
- The featured review feels specific, human and credible
- The layout is editorial rather than repetitive
- The chapter is visibly different from the white comparison scene before it
- Review evidence is connected to a product and result
- The CTA helps the visitor continue toward a confident choice
