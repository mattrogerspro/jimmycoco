# Editorial Marketing and Repurposing Playbook

## Principle

Lead with a useful answer, demonstrate the Jimmy Coco method, then offer the relevant product as the next logical step. The marketing should feel like expert service rather than an advert disguised as advice.

## One-source system

The approved article is the canonical source. Every email, social post, video and retail module must inherit its facts, product links and claim status. Derivatives may shorten; they may not strengthen claims.

## Conversion modules

Choose one per article:

### Product decision

- Product name and accurate image
- One-sentence suitability explanation
- Development time or format where verified
- CTA: `Choose Your Shade` or `Shop [Product]`

### Routine

- Prepare, apply, perfect sequence
- Essential product/tool separated from optional addition
- CTA: `Build Your Jimmy Coco Routine`

### Professional

- Solution name, intended depth and verified yield
- Trade-only context
- CTA: `Explore Professional Solutions`

Do not use a discount as the default article CTA.

## Repurposing matrix

| Source section | Email | Short video | Carousel | Product page | PR/editorial pitch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct answer | Subject/preheader | Opening hook | Frame 1 | FAQ | Timely expert angle |
| Numbered method | Main body | Demonstration | Frames 2–5 | How-to module | Supporting expertise |
| Mistakes | PS block | “Avoid this” cut | Saveable checklist | FAQ | Consumer-behaviour insight |
| Product role | CTA module | Product close-up | Final frame | Cross-link | Product context |
| Expert principle | Founder note | Voiceover | Pull quote only if approved | Authority module | Attributable comment |

## Email deployment

For each article create one educational email:

- subject: useful outcome, not clickbait;
- preheader: clarify the product or situation;
- 60–120 words of self-contained value;
- one image;
- one article CTA;
- one product CTA only when the reader has enough context.

Email creation remains governed by the repository’s separate campaign workflow and does not authorise sending.

## Social deployment

### Demonstration video

- 30–45 seconds;
- show product in the first three seconds;
- show the decisive technique at normal speed;
- use on-screen steps that work without audio;
- close on the article answer and one next step.

### Carousel

1. Reader problem
2. Why it happens
3. First technique
4. Second technique
5. Product/tool role
6. Mistake to avoid
7. Save/read/shop action

### Caption

Use one useful paragraph, one product-specific line and one action. Avoid long hashtag blocks and unsupported before-and-after promises.

## Founder and PR deployment

Use Jimmy’s authority when there is a genuine expert contribution:

- natural versus over-dark tanning trends;
- red-carpet and photography timing;
- matching face and body colour;
- professional layering and contour logic;
- what clients misunderstand about development;
- salon consultation and shade selection.

All direct quotes require an interview transcript or written approval. Celebrity names require rights and relevance review.

## Seasonal hooks

- January: healthy-looking winter colour and face routines
- February–March: occasion trials and spring reset
- April–June: wedding, races, holidays and festival preparation
- July–August: face/body top-ups, travel and sun-protection clarification
- September: even fade and routine reset
- October–December: party, gifting, professional education and event timing

Evergreen articles should not be rewritten as disposable seasonal pieces. Add a campaign wrapper and preserve the canonical guide.

## Testing priorities

Test one variable at a time:

1. CTA label;
2. placement of the product module;
3. instructional image versus product still life;
4. concise versus expanded opening answer;
5. article-to-shade-selector versus article-to-product route.

Do not test claims, safety language or factual directions as marketing variants.

## Reporting

Report weekly on distribution activity and monthly on commercial behaviour. Attribute assisted journeys, not only last-click purchases. Compare:

- article readers versus non-readers;
- product click-through by topic;
- tool/bundle attachment after technique articles;
- face/body routine attachment;
- professional solution enquiries by article;
- email and social traffic quality;
- search impressions and rankings by cluster.

## Guardrails

- No competitor-bashing or comparative superiority claims.
- No invented testimonials or transformations.
- No unapproved celebrity endorsements.
- No product recommendation outside the current Jimmy Coco range.
- No price, stock or offer copied into evergreen creative without a dynamic source.
- No sending, boosting or publishing without the appropriate channel approval.
