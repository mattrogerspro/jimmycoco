# Email Copy Strategy and Principles

## Purpose

Define how Sunless email copy creates value across acquisition, conversion, product use, retention and service.

## Strategic role of copy

Email copy should perform one primary job at a time:

1. Clarify what has happened or what the customer should know.
2. Reduce uncertainty about shade, product, routine or next step.
3. Help the customer make or complete a decision.
4. Support successful product use.
5. Recognise an established customer relationship.
6. Resolve or route a service need.

A message may support several outcomes, but it must have one dominant purpose.

## Message hierarchy

Every email should answer these questions in order:

1. Why am I receiving this?
2. What is the most important thing to understand?
3. Why is it relevant to me?
4. What should I do next?
5. What happens if I do nothing?

Do not make the reader infer the purpose from decorative copy.

## Core copy principles

### Lead with relevance

Begin with the customer state, event or benefit—not a generic brand introduction.

### Explain before persuading

Where uncertainty exists, explain the recommendation, product, timing or process before asking for purchase.

### Prefer concrete language

Use observable descriptions such as finish, depth, development time, application area and routine step. Avoid vague luxury filler.

### One message, one centre of gravity

The primary headline, body and CTA must point to the same action or outcome.

### Earn urgency

Urgency must come from a real deadline, stock state, event or customer need. Never manufacture pressure.

### Protect customer confidence

Do not imply that the customer chose incorrectly, failed to maintain status or should feel embarrassed about their skin, shade, spend or lapse.

### Be concise, not abrupt

Remove repetition and filler while retaining enough context for a confident decision.

### Make service language human

Operational information must be clear and direct, but never cold, robotic or evasive.

## Commercial principles

Copy should improve commercial performance by increasing clarity and confidence, not by increasing pressure.

Preferred value levers:

- better shade confidence;
- clearer product suitability;
- easier routine completion;
- relevant education;
- accurate social proof;
- useful access or service;
- genuine recognition;
- real availability or timing.

Discounting should not be the default answer to uncertainty.

## Content priority

Use this order when deciding what to include:

1. Essential service or event information
2. Customer-specific relevance
3. Primary benefit or recommendation
4. Supporting proof or explanation
5. Primary CTA
6. Secondary guidance
7. Terms, preferences and legal content

## Prohibited patterns

Do not use:

- fake countdown language;
- false scarcity;
- unsupported celebrity, clinical or performance claims;
- shame-based body or skin language;
- manipulative guilt;
- exaggerated intimacy;
- invented personalisation;
- repeated exclamation marks;
- all-caps pressure copy;
- generic phrases such as “you won’t want to miss this” without substance;
- multiple competing primary CTAs;
- unexplained jargon.

## Quality test

Before approval, the copy should pass five questions:

- Is it true?
- Is it useful?
- Is it clear?
- Is it appropriate to this customer state?
- Does the CTA logically follow from the message?

If any answer is uncertain, the email is not ready.
