# AI Failure Modes

## Purpose

This document catalogues the most common ways image-generation models weaken the Sunless website concept. Use it during prompting, selection, revision and approval.

A generation can look polished and still fail commercially, structurally or visually.

## 1. The continuous ivory block

### Symptom

The full page or multiple scenes use nearly identical cream backgrounds, spacing and widths.

### Why it happens

Image models average luxury-beauty references into one pale visual field.

### Business impact

- Weak chapter separation
- Reduced scroll momentum
- Lower memorability
- Important conversion moments fail to stand out

### Correction

- Generate one scene at a time
- Specify unique architectural silhouette
- Use the approved background cadence
- Evaluate at 10% zoom
- Increase contrast through scale, photography and density—not arbitrary decoration

## 2. Card-grid default

### Symptom

Every concept becomes a row of three or four rounded cards.

### Why it happens

Card grids are overrepresented in modern website training examples.

### Business impact

- Template feel
- Visual repetition
- Luxury positioning weakens
- Editorial scenes become interface-heavy

### Correction

State explicitly where cards are prohibited. Use full-bleed photography, narrow editorial columns, asymmetry, floating products and typography-led spreads.

## 3. Generic Shopify transformation

### Symptom

The design gains conventional product tiles, promotional badges, discount labels and repeated “Shop Now” buttons.

### Business impact

The brand becomes interchangeable with mass-market beauty stores.

### Correction

Preserve Sunless identity, limit products, prioritise guidance and proof, and use specific CTA language.

## 4. Luxury through decoration

### Symptom

Excess gradients, glass panels, gold lines, glow effects, heavy shadows or ornamental flourishes.

### Business impact

The page feels trend-led, artificial or inexpensive.

### Correction

Define luxury as restraint, proportion, photography, typography and negative space.

## 5. Flat beige-on-beige contrast

### Symptom

Model, product, background and copy all occupy similar tonal values.

### Business impact

- Poor readability
- Weak focal hierarchy
- Product disappears
- Accessibility risk

### Correction

Use tonal separation, deep-charcoal typography, controlled shadows and distinct materials. Warm does not mean monochrome.

## 6. Distorted product packaging

### Symptom

Bottle shape, cap, pump, label, brand name or finish changes.

### Business impact

- Product credibility loss
- Inconsistent catalogue
- Concept cannot be used as a reliable production reference

### Correction

Attach dedicated packaging references. Prioritise geometry before label text. Rebuild final text in the design tool.

## 7. Jimmy Coco identity drift

### Symptom

Jimmy looks like a different person, a generic stylist or an implausibly young model.

### Business impact

Founder authority and authenticity collapse.

### Correction

Use multiple approved likeness references and define age, grooming, posture and working role. Reject near-matches.

## 8. Celebrity endorsement pose

### Symptom

Jimmy looks toward the camera holding a bottle instead of working.

### Business impact

The brand appears like a vanity project rather than professional methodology.

### Correction

Show genuine application, judgement, preparation or backstage interaction.

## 9. Plastic or orange skin

### Symptom

Skin is heavily airbrushed, oversaturated, metallic or unnaturally orange.

### Business impact

Directly reinforces the category’s largest consumer fear.

### Correction

Specify natural undertones, retained texture, neutral warmth, realistic pores and natural daylight. Reject aggressively retouched outputs.

## 10. Before-and-after fraud cues

### Symptom

Before and after images use different poses, lighting, clothing, crop or body proportions.

### Business impact

Proof feels manipulated.

### Correction

Lock camera, crop, posture and lighting. Add product, development time and no-retouching metadata.

## 11. Tiny illegible interface copy

### Symptom

The model fills cards with pseudo-text or extremely small labels.

### Business impact

- Weak hierarchy
- Accessibility failure
- False impression of complexity

### Correction

Use short high-value copy only. Rebuild final typography as live text after image generation.

## 12. CTA hierarchy collapse

### Symptom

Multiple equal buttons or a hidden primary action.

### Business impact

Visitors do not know what to do next.

### Correction

Use one dominant matte-black CTA and one visibly quieter alternative.

## 13. Unclear recommendation logic

### Symptom

The recommendation engine looks attractive but does not visibly connect user choices to one product.

### Business impact

The most important commercial innovation becomes decorative UI.

### Correction

Show the three inputs, a clear selected state and one large personalised recommendation with rationale.

## 14. Mixed classification dimensions

### Symptom

Options combine result depth, expertise and user identity in one choice set.

### Business impact

Decision confidence falls.

### Correction

Ask one dimension at a time: desired result, skin tone, experience.

## 15. Excessive product quantity

### Symptom

The model displays six to twelve similar products.

### Business impact

Choice overload and catalogue fatigue.

### Correction

Limit homepage best sellers to four and recommendation outputs to one primary plus one alternative.

## 16. Founder story as biography slab

### Symptom

Large paragraph beside a conventional portrait card.

### Business impact

Momentum drops and the story feels self-indulgent.

### Correction

Use an editorial spread connecting Jimmy’s experience directly to product and method decisions.

## 17. Reviews as repeated widgets

### Symptom

Six identical quotation cards with tiny faces and generic praise.

### Business impact

Proof becomes skimmed and forgettable.

### Correction

Use concern-led customer stories, larger portraits, relevant metadata and varied editorial composition.

## 18. Comparison-table theatre

### Symptom

Aggressive green ticks, red crosses and unsupported claims.

### Business impact

Premium trust is replaced by sales pressure.

### Correction

Use quiet factual rows, minimal rules and claims that can be substantiated.

## 19. Routine as another product rail

### Symptom

Prepare, Apply and Perfect become three standard catalogue cards.

### Business impact

Cross-sell feels arbitrary rather than useful.

### Correction

Show one directional ritual with a clear relationship between steps.

## 20. Weak ending

### Symptom

The page ends with Instagram tiles, utility links or a generic newsletter field.

### Business impact

The Peak-End Rule is wasted.

### Correction

Create the dark cinematic final conversion scene before quieter community and footer content.

## 21. Overly uniform spacing

### Symptom

Every chapter has identical vertical padding.

### Business impact

The experience feels mechanical and generated.

### Correction

Follow the varied spacing and density cadence. Use optical balance rather than one token everywhere.

## 22. Abrupt style drift

### Symptom

One scene introduces a cold palette, glossy surfaces, different typography or futuristic components.

### Business impact

The homepage feels assembled from unrelated concepts.

### Correction

Use the anchor sheet and scene-continuity system for every generation.

## 23. Inspiration becomes imitation

### Symptom

The result looks like an Apple, Aesop or Rhode page with the Sunless logo applied.

### Business impact

Originality and brand recognition weaken.

### Correction

Use reference brands for quality principles only. Sunless identity and current homepage remain the primary anchor.

## 24. Impossible UI

### Symptom

Controls overlap, labels float without state, buttons are too small or layouts cannot responsively reflow.

### Business impact

The concept cannot be implemented faithfully.

### Correction

Review every generated scene as a real interface. Rebuild production UI with semantic components.

## 25. False factual claims

### Symptom

The model invents awards, review counts, press names, guarantees or product benefits.

### Business impact

Legal and trust risk.

### Correction

Only use approved claims and operationally accurate reassurance. Mark placeholders clearly.

## Rejection rule

Reject a generation immediately when it materially fails any of the following:

- Brand recognition
- Product accuracy
- Jimmy likeness
- Natural skin rendering
- Primary CTA clarity
- Scene silhouette
- Adjacent-scene differentiation
- Factual integrity

Do not attempt to rescue a fundamentally incorrect scene through minor text or colour edits.

## Success criterion

The failure-mode system succeeds when reviewers can explain precisely why an attractive image is unsuitable and what must change in the next generation.
