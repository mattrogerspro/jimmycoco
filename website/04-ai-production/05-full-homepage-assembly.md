# Full Homepage Assembly

## Purpose

This document defines how approved scene concepts are assembled into one coherent desktop homepage and prepared for responsive design, production UI and implementation.

The final homepage must not be treated as a simple vertical collage of generated images. It is a controlled editorial composition built from approved scene architecture, real interface components and production-ready assets.

## Governing principle

Generate scenes separately. Assemble the experience deliberately.

The final design tool—not the image model—is responsible for exact typography, spacing, accessibility, responsive behaviour, component consistency and factual copy.

## Required inputs

Before assembly begins, confirm that the following exist:

- Approved scene image or composition reference for every homepage chapter
- Approved hero anchor sheet
- Current Sunless logo assets
- Approved product renders
- Approved Jimmy Coco photography or likeness references
- Approved model and result imagery
- Final or working copy deck
- Website colour and typography specifications
- Component-language document
- Scene QA records
- Asset manifest and filenames

Do not assemble exploratory or rejected scene versions.

## Approved chapter order

1. Navigation and utility
2. Hero
3. Integrated authority strip
4. Shade recommendation engine
5. Hollywood method
6. Real results
7. Curated best sellers
8. Jimmy Coco editorial story
9. Professional comparison
10. Customer stories and reviews
11. Complete the routine
12. Final conversion scene
13. Community, newsletter and footer

This order may change only when user research, analytics or commercial testing supports the change.

## Stage 1 — Storyboard assembly

Create a low-resolution full-page storyboard before rebuilding detailed UI.

### Method

- Place every approved scene at approximately 10% scale
- Preserve intended relative chapter heights
- Remove most readable copy
- View the page as colour, silhouette, density and image rhythm

### Questions

- Are 10–12 distinct chapters immediately visible?
- Does the page still become one cream block?
- Are there too many consecutive card-led scenes?
- Does the eye receive alternating information and emotional relief?
- Are the hero and final CTA the two strongest emotional peaks?
- Is the recommendation engine clearly the commercial centrepiece?

Revise chapter proportions before proceeding.

## Stage 2 — Define the master grid

Use the approved desktop grid system.

Recommended working frame:

- Reference viewport width: 1700px
- Invisible 12-column grid
- Shared maximum content-width logic
- Scene-specific full-bleed permissions
- Consistent left and right optical margins

Do not force full-bleed scenes into the central content container.

## Stage 3 — Rebuild typography as live text

Generated text must not be treated as production typography.

Rebuild:

- Navigation
- Headings
- Body copy
- Buttons
- Product metadata
- Review quotations
- Comparison labels
- Form labels
- Footer links

Check:

- Approved type families
- Correct hierarchy
- Line length
- Line height
- Contrast
- Responsive feasibility

## Stage 4 — Rebuild controls as components

Replace image-generated interface elements with live components.

Includes:

- Primary and secondary buttons
- Shade-selection controls
- Recommendation states
- Product-card actions
- Review filters
- Comparison rows
- Before/after controls
- Email form
- Navigation and account controls

Use the component-language document as canonical guidance.

## Stage 5 — Replace or refine imagery

Generated images may function as:

- Composition concepts
- Photography briefs
- Temporary high-fidelity placeholders
- Retouching bases, where rights and production method allow

Before launch, verify:

- Usage rights
- Product accuracy
- Human consent and likeness requirements
- Resolution
- Mobile crops
- Colour consistency
- Retouching quality
- File size and format

## Scene boundaries

Each chapter must have a defined start and end.

Document for every scene:

- Desktop height range
- Top and bottom padding
- Background value
- Full-bleed or contained behaviour
- Entry transition
- Exit transition
- Mobile reflow logic

Avoid arbitrary horizontal rules between every chapter.

## Transition system

Use transitions through:

- Background-tone change
- Image scale
- Negative space
- Density shift
- Asymmetrical overlap
- Change from contained to full bleed
- Typography scale

Transitions should be obvious at distance and subtle at full size.

## Vertical rhythm review

Do not use one uniform section-padding token throughout the page.

Check that:

- Decision-heavy chapters have enough processing space
- Photography chapters are not crowded by nearby headings
- Sparse chapters create genuine relief
- Product galleries remain efficient
- Founder and review chapters feel human and editorial
- The final CTA has enough height to feel cinematic

## Page-length discipline

Every chapter must earn its vertical space.

Use the delete test:

- Does the scene answer a new question?
- Does it increase desire, clarity, trust or confidence?
- Does it support conversion or average order value?
- Is the same job already handled elsewhere?

Merge or remove weak repetition.

## Commercial-path review

Verify the principal pathways:

### Guided shopper

Hero → Match My Shade → Recommendation → Product detail or purchase

### High-intent shopper

Hero → Best Sellers → Choose Shade → Purchase

### Sceptical shopper

Authority → Method → Results → Reviews → Recommendation

### Returning customer

Navigation or best sellers → Known product → Purchase

No pathway should be trapped by editorial content.

## CTA distribution

Required major conversion opportunities:

- Hero primary shade-match CTA
- Recommendation purchase CTA
- Real-results contextual CTA
- Best-seller product actions
- Final conversion CTA

Do not place a large CTA in every scene. Excessive repetition reduces hierarchy.

## Trust distribution

Trust should be placed near moments of uncertainty:

- Authority below the hero
- Recommendation rationale near the recommended product
- Result verification with before/after proof
- Customer metadata with reviews
- Shade-support or policy reassurance near purchase actions

Do not isolate all trust proof in one distant section.

## Responsive translation

Desktop architecture must not simply shrink.

For each scene, define:

- Mobile content order
- Hero crop
- Heading length
- CTA position
- Card stacking or horizontal rail behaviour
- Image aspect ratio
- Metadata reduction
- Sticky-action requirements
- Elements removed or progressively disclosed

The mobile homepage may be shorter than desktop while retaining the same narrative.

## Accessibility pass

Before visual approval, check:

- Heading hierarchy
- Colour contrast
- Body and metadata size
- Interactive target size
- Keyboard order
- Focus states
- Form labels
- Error messaging
- Carousel controls
- Reduced-motion behaviour
- Alt-text plan
- Semantic landmarks

The static image concepts do not determine final accessibility.

## Performance plan

Document:

- Above-the-fold image priority
- Responsive image sizes
- Modern formats
- Lazy-loading boundaries
- Video poster and playback strategy
- Font loading
- Third-party script limits
- Carousel impact
- Cumulative-layout-shift prevention

Luxury perception is damaged by slow, unstable rendering.

## Full-page QA tests

### 10% zoom test

The page resolves into distinct chapters.

### Blur test

The main focal points remain clear without readable text.

### Greyscale test

Hierarchy survives without colour.

### Squint test

Primary CTAs and emotional peaks remain visible.

### Scroll test

Interest rises and falls intentionally rather than flattening.

### Delete test

No chapter exists only because ecommerce pages normally contain it.

### Peak-End test

The hero and final CTA are memorable.

### Brand-removal test

Even with the logo hidden, the page still feels specifically Sunless.

- `homepage-desktop-v01`
- `homepage-desktop-v01-review`

- `scene-05-real-results-v03-approved`

Never overwrite an approved version without preserving history.

## Definition of completion

The homepage assembly is complete when:

- Every scene has passed its QA checklist
- The full page passes the silhouette and narrative tests
- Product and human references are consistent
- All interface text and components are live
- Responsive layouts are defined
- Accessibility risks are addressed
- Performance requirements are documented
- Commercial pathways are clear
- The page feels like one art-directed Sunless experience rather than a collage of AI generations
