# Commercial Role of Email

## Objective

Email should grow revenue by increasing confidence, conversion, product success, repeat purchase and customer lifetime value. Revenue is the outcome of relevance and successful product use—not the organising principle visible to the customer.

## Commercial jobs

### Convert considered interest

Support visitors who have browsed, completed shade match or viewed products but have not yet purchased. Messages should resolve the most likely remaining objection rather than repeat generic product claims.

### Recover interrupted intent

Browse and cart recovery should restore context: product, shade, desired result, delivery confidence and application reassurance.

### Improve first-order quality

Guide customers towards the right product and routine. A smaller, well-matched first order is more valuable than a larger poor-fit order that causes disappointment.

### Increase first-use success

Post-purchase education must arrive before the likely application moment and explain preparation, amount, technique, development time and finishing.

### Build the routine

Cross-sell only when it makes the outcome easier or more reliable: Prepare, Apply, Perfect.

### Drive replenishment

Replenishment timing should reflect product type, quantity, frequency and previous behaviour. Do not treat every customer as having the same usage cycle.

### Retain and reactivate

Win-back should identify likely reasons for lapse—seasonality, product mismatch, uncertainty, or simple replenishment delay—and choose the message accordingly.

## Revenue hierarchy

1. Correct recommendation
2. Successful application
3. Repeat purchase
4. Routine expansion
5. VIP retention
6. Selective promotional acceleration

Discounting should not sit above product fit or success.

## Offer principles

Use offers when they have a clear strategic purpose:

- Reduce first-purchase friction
- Reward loyalty
- Encourage a complete routine
- Reactivate a genuinely lapsed customer
- Support a defined seasonal event

Avoid permanent discounting, fake countdowns and escalating incentives that train customers to wait.

## Primary commercial metrics

- Revenue per recipient
- Conversion rate by flow and lifecycle state
- First-to-second order rate
- Time to second order
- Repeat purchase rate
- Replenishment conversion
- Average order value
- Routine attachment rate
- Unsubscribe and complaint rate
- Incremental lift versus holdout where possible

## Guardrail metrics

Revenue gains are unacceptable if accompanied by rising complaints, unusually high returns, poor first-use sentiment, declining engagement or excessive discount dependency.
