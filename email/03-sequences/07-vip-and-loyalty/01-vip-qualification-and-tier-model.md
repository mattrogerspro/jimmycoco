# VIP Qualification and Tier Model

## Purpose

Define a fair, auditable and commercially responsible method for identifying VIP customers.

Qualification must never rely on one opaque score alone. The model should combine customer value, purchase behaviour, engagement quality and service state.

## Recommended qualification dimensions

Use a weighted model built from approved first-party data:

- recency of qualifying purchase;
- purchase frequency;
- net revenue after discounts, refunds and returns;
- contribution margin where available;
- breadth or depth of product adoption;
- repeat purchase consistency;
- account tenure;
- advocacy or referral activity when verified;
- professional account status where relevant;
- service and complaint state.

## Exclusions from value calculations

Do not inflate qualification with:

- cancelled orders;
- refunded or fully returned orders;
- fraudulent or disputed payments;
- test orders;
- employee or internal orders unless policy explicitly permits;
- gross revenue that ignores extreme discounting;
- email opens as a proxy for loyalty.

## Suggested tier structure

Keep tiers limited and meaningful.

### Member

A recognised repeat customer who qualifies for improved relevance and occasional access.

### VIP

A sustained high-value customer eligible for enhanced recognition, early access and service benefits.

### Private Client

Optional invitation-only tier for exceptionally valuable or strategically important customers, provided the business can genuinely deliver a higher-touch service.

Do not launch a tier merely because it sounds premium.

## Qualification methods

A customer may qualify through one of three approved routes:

1. **Behavioural qualification** — thresholds met through purchase history.
2. **Professional qualification** — verified professional or trade relationship.
3. **Manual invitation** — documented business decision with an owner and review date.

Manual overrides must record the reason and expiry.

## Review windows

Use rolling review periods rather than lifetime spend alone.

Recommended approach:

- evaluate monthly;
- use a rolling 12-month primary window;
- consider longer-term tenure as a secondary factor;
- allow a grace period before downgrade;
- prevent rapid movement caused by one refund, one missed purchase or temporary inactivity.

## Qualification confidence

Store a confidence label:

- **High** — complete order and margin history, stable identity and no unresolved service issues.
- **Medium** — sufficient purchase evidence but incomplete margin, identity or product data.
- **Low** — fragmented records, manual imports or uncertain account merging.

Low-confidence records should not trigger expensive or capacity-limited benefits automatically.

## Tier entry rules

Before assigning a tier, confirm:

- customer identity is reliable;
- qualifying orders are valid;
- returns and refunds are accounted for;
- no active complaint, safety or fraud state exists;
- market and benefit eligibility are valid;
- the programme version and threshold set are recorded.

## Tier renewal

Renew when the customer remains within the approved value range or grace policy.

Renewal should not require the customer to “earn back” recognition through urgency-driven purchasing.

## Tier transition

When a customer no longer qualifies:

- apply the grace period;
- preserve earned benefits until their stated expiry;
- avoid language such as “you lost your status”;
- explain any material change plainly when customer-facing communication is necessary;
- retain a lower-frequency relationship when consent remains valid.

## Status data contract

Each record should include:

- customer ID;
- tier;
- qualification route;
- qualification date;
- review date;
- programme version;
- threshold version;
- confidence level;
- qualifying value metrics;
- benefit eligibility;
- manual override reason and owner when applicable;
- current service-risk flag;
- transition reason.

## Governance

Threshold changes must be versioned and reviewed for:

- customer impact;
- margin impact;
- regional fairness;
- operational capacity;
- accidental overqualification;
- accidental exclusion of valuable repeat customers.
