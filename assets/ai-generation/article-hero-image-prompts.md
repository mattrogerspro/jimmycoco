# Article hero images — copy-paste prompts

Six complete prompts. **Each block below is self-contained** — style, lighting, palette, camera and
constraints are already inside it. Copy one block, paste, run. Nothing to assemble.

Written to the guardrails in `assets/ai-generation/_shared/creative-rules.md`. All six are
people-free by design: no tan lines to police, no hands with the wrong number of fingers, no
wardrobe compliance — and a still life reads as more considered than another stock model. It also
lets the set hang together as one publication.

---

# 1 · What a spray tan actually costs you to deliver

*The article argues the cost isn't the bottle, it's the room and the chair time. So the picture is
the room — empty, between clients. An empty room is money going out whether anyone's in it or not.*

```
A small professional spray tanning room in a UK salon, empty between clients, late afternoon. A
pop-up spray tanning tent stands open and unoccupied on the left, its fabric soft ivory, one panel
catching low window light. Beside it a compact stainless treatment trolley holds a spray tan gun
resting in its cradle with the hose coiled loosely, an unbranded amber-tinted one-litre solution
bottle standing behind it with its label turned fully away from camera, and a neat stack of folded
cream towels. A pair of disposable slippers sits on the pale wood floor. Warm low sun rakes across
the floor through a window just out of frame, throwing a long soft shadow. Walls in warm off-white
with one mineral-stone accent. The room is clean and cared for but genuinely used — a slight scuff
on the skirting, a towel not quite square.

Editorial still-life photography, warm and quietly premium. Natural directional daylight from a
low window, soft falloff, gentle shadows with warm bounce, no hard flash and no studio strobe
look. Palette restricted to warm neutrals — ivory, linen, warm off-white, mineral stone, pale
sand, champagne, muted bronze — with charcoal for the darkest values, never pure black and never
cool grey or blue. Shot full-frame on a 35mm lens at f/2.8, eye level, camera set back so the room
reads as small. Realistic surface texture: towelling grain, matte plastic, brushed metal, real
dust. Colour-graded warm and slightly desaturated, film-like. No HDR, no digital over-sharpening,
no heavy vignette. Deep warm shadow in the right third as negative space for a headline.

No text, letters, numerals, logos, labels, branding or watermarks anywhere in frame. No visible
product label on the bottle. No people, hands or faces. No money, coins, charts or calculators.

--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 150 --quality 2
```

**Watch for:** any part of the bottle label facing camera — the model will invent branding. Reroll
rather than retouch. If a render puts a person in the room, discard it: it argues the opposite of
the article.

# 2 · How many spray tans do you get from a litre?

*The article's one instruction is mark the bottle, count the tans, divide. So show exactly that —
the method as a photograph. Nobody shoots a bottle this way, which is why it stops the scroll.*

```
A single unbranded one-litre professional spray tan solution bottle standing on a pale travertine
counter, photographed in profile so its printed label faces entirely away from camera. The bottle
is amber-bronze tinted and roughly one third full, the liquid line sitting low and clearly
readable, backlit by a window so the solution glows warm amber where light passes through it. Down
the visible side of the bottle runs a narrow strip of matte masking tape bearing a hand-drawn
tally in blue ballpoint — simple vertical pen strokes in groups of five, each fifth stroke crossed
diagonally through the previous four, about twenty-five strokes in total, slightly uneven and
clearly drawn by hand at different moments. Only tally strokes: no numbers, no letters, no words,
no writing of any kind. In the soft background, thrown well out of focus, the pale suggestion of a
treatment room.

Editorial still-life photography, warm and quietly premium. Backlight is the key light, window
daylight, soft falloff and gentle warm-bounced shadow, no hard flash and no studio strobe look.
Palette restricted to warm neutrals — ivory, linen, warm off-white, mineral stone, pale sand,
champagne, muted bronze — with charcoal for the darkest values, never pure black and never cool
grey or blue. Shot full-frame on an 85mm lens at f/2.8, camera slightly below the liquid line so
the bottle has presence. Realistic surface texture: matte plastic, paper tape, ballpoint ink,
travertine grain. Colour-graded warm and slightly desaturated, film-like. No HDR, no digital
over-sharpening. Bottle placed left of centre with wide clean negative space to the right.

No text, letters, numerals, logos, labels, branding or watermarks anywhere in frame. The tally is
plain pen strokes only. No people, hands or faces.

--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 150 --quality 2
```

**Watch for:** the tally drifting into digits or letters. Reject any render with a character in it.
If it keeps failing, drop the tape entirely — the low fill line alone carries the idea.

# 3 · The journal index page

*Sits above "Advice for better tans and stronger salons", so it has to cover the whole publication.
The subject is the half-hour after the last client, when the business actually gets thought about.*

```
The back counter of a small UK beauty salon at the end of the working day, just after closing. A
closed hardback appointment diary lies flat with a pen resting in the gutter, its cover plain
linen-textured card, completely blank with no writing or printing on it. Beside it a used mug of
tea, a small brass desk lamp switched on and pooling warm light, and at the edge of frame an
unbranded amber one-litre solution bottle and a folded cream towel. Through a window behind, a
soft blue-hour street outside, thrown out of focus, a few warm lights beginning to show. The
interior is lit warm against the cool dusk outside and that contrast is the whole mood. Surfaces
are pale wood and warm off-white, lived-in and tidy.

Editorial still-life photography, warm and quietly premium. Key light is the brass lamp with
window dusk behind, soft falloff, gentle warm shadow, no hard flash and no studio strobe look.
Palette restricted to warm neutrals — ivory, linen, warm off-white, mineral stone, pale sand,
champagne, muted bronze — with charcoal for the darkest values and never pure black. The only cool
tone permitted anywhere in the frame is the blue hour seen through the window; it must not spill
into the room. Shot full-frame on a 50mm lens at f/2.0, low three-quarter angle across the
counter, foreground objects slightly soft. Realistic surface texture: cloth-bound board, glazed
ceramic, aged brass, pale wood grain. Colour-graded warm and slightly desaturated, film-like. No
HDR, no digital over-sharpening. Composition weighted right, generous empty counter to the left
for a masthead.

No text, letters, numerals, logos, labels, branding or watermarks anywhere in frame. The diary
cover is blank. No people, hands or faces.

--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 150 --quality 2
```

**Watch for:** blue bleeding out of the window and into the room. Reroll if it does.

# 4 · What to charge for a spray tan in 2026

*The article's whole point is that no published benchmark exists, so the number is yours to decide.
A blank price card in a holder is that question as an object — and being blank solves the no-text
constraint by design rather than fighting it.*

```
A small blank price card standing in a slim brushed-brass card holder on a pale limestone salon
counter, photographed slightly from the side. The card is thick warm-white cotton stock with a
soft deckled edge and it is completely blank — no writing, no printing, no marks of any kind. A
fine black fountain pen lies uncapped beside it, angled as though just set down. Further back and
well out of focus, the warm suggestion of a treatment room: a folded cream towel, the soft curve
of an unbranded amber bottle, a brass lamp throwing a pool of light. Late afternoon window light
rakes across the counter from the left, catching the texture of the card stock and throwing a
long soft shadow to the right. Everything is calm, considered and slightly expectant, as though a
decision is about to be written down.

Editorial still-life photography, warm and quietly premium. Natural directional daylight from a
low side window, soft falloff, gentle warm-bounced shadows, no hard flash and no studio strobe
look. Palette restricted to warm neutrals — ivory, linen, warm off-white, mineral stone, pale
sand, champagne, muted bronze — with charcoal for the darkest values, never pure black and never
cool grey or blue. Shot full-frame on an 85mm lens at f/2.8, close and low so the card dominates
the frame. Realistic surface texture: cotton paper fibre, brushed brass, limestone grain, lacquer
on the pen. Colour-graded warm and slightly desaturated, film-like. No HDR, no digital
over-sharpening. Card placed right of centre with clean empty counter to the left for a headline.

No text, letters, numerals, logos, labels, branding, prices or watermarks anywhere in frame — the
card is entirely blank and that is the point. No people, hands or faces. No coins, banknotes,
calculators or charts.

--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 150 --quality 2
```

**Watch for:** the model writing a price on the card. It will try. The blankness is the whole
image — reject anything with a mark on it.

# 5 · Is mobile spray tanning actually profitable?

*Nobody photographs the actual job. It isn't the treatment, it's the loading and unloading — the
boot of a car on somebody else's street, twice per client, in the dark half the year.*

```
The open boot of a small estate car on a quiet British residential street at dusk, tailgate raised,
packed for a mobile spray tanning job. Inside, neatly stowed: a long padded tent bag, a hard case
for the spray gun, a stack of folded cream towels, a slim collapsible step stool and an unbranded
amber one-litre solution bottle wedged upright with its label turned away from camera. The boot
light is on, throwing a warm pool over the kit against the cooling blue of the street behind.
Beyond the car, out of focus, the soft outline of terraced houses and a single lit front window.
Damp tarmac holds a faint reflection. The kit is well used and cared for — the tent bag creased
from handling, one towel not quite square.

Editorial still-life photography, warm and quietly premium. Key light is the warm boot lamp
against blue-hour ambient, soft falloff, gentle shadow, no hard flash and no studio strobe look.
Palette restricted to warm neutrals — ivory, linen, warm off-white, mineral stone, pale sand,
champagne, muted bronze — with charcoal for the darkest values, never pure black. The only cool
tone permitted is the dusk beyond the car; the interior of the boot stays warm. Shot full-frame on
a 35mm lens at f/2.5, standing height looking slightly down into the boot. Realistic surface
texture: ballistic nylon, moulded plastic, towelling, wet tarmac. Colour-graded warm and slightly
desaturated, film-like. No HDR, no digital over-sharpening, no lens flare. Kit weighted to the
right, darker street to the left as negative space for a headline.

No text, letters, numerals, logos, branding, labels or watermarks anywhere in frame. No vehicle
number plate visible. No car manufacturer badges. No people, hands or faces.

--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 150 --quality 2
```

**Watch for:** number plates and manufacturer badges — both count as text and both will appear if
you let them. Frame so the plate is out of shot rather than trying to blur it.

# 6 · What the retail shelf is really worth

*The article says stock less: four things that sell beats fifteen that sit. So the image is a sparse,
well-lit shelf with one gap in it. The gap is the sale — and it's the whole argument in one object.*

```
A single narrow retail shelf on a warm off-white salon wall, styled sparsely and lit well. Four
unbranded cosmetic containers stand evenly spaced along it — a frosted glass jar, a matte cream
tube, a soft amber pump bottle and a small ribbed tub — all with their labels turned away from
camera or unprinted. There is one clear gap in the line where a fifth container has obviously been
taken, a faint clean ring left on the pale timber where it stood. A small sprig of dried eucalyptus
lies at one end. Warm late-afternoon window light comes in from the left, raking along the shelf,
picking out the ring in the dust and throwing soft shadows to the right. Below the shelf the wall
falls into warm shadow. The styling is deliberate and uncrowded, closer to a gallery than a shop.

Editorial still-life photography, warm and quietly premium. Natural directional daylight from a
low side window, soft falloff, gentle warm-bounced shadow, no hard flash and no studio strobe
look. Palette restricted to warm neutrals — ivory, linen, warm off-white, mineral stone, pale
sand, champagne, muted bronze — with charcoal for the darkest values, never pure black and never
cool grey or blue. Shot full-frame on an 85mm lens at f/2.8, straight on and slightly below shelf
height. Realistic surface texture: frosted glass, matte laminate, timber grain, a little real
dust. Colour-graded warm and slightly desaturated, film-like. No HDR, no digital over-sharpening.
Products weighted left with the gap and clean shelf to the right as negative space for a headline.

No text, letters, numerals, logos, labels, branding, packaging copy or watermarks anywhere in
frame — every container is unprinted or turned away. No people, hands or faces. No price tags.

--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 150 --quality 2
```

**Watch for:** the model filling the gap. It reads an even line as "correct" and will close it. The
gap is the image — if it's full, reroll.

## Technical

**Size.** Generate **16:9 at 2400 × 1350** or larger. Don't generate square and crop.

**Crop safety.** The same file is used at two shapes — about **2.07:1** on the article page (which
trims roughly the top and bottom 12%) and **16:10** in the cards. Keep everything that matters
inside the central 16:10 region, and put nothing you need to see in the top or bottom eighth. Each
prompt above places its headline negative space to the left or right for exactly this reason.

**Midjourney.** The `--style raw` on every prompt matters — the default aesthetic pushes these
toward glossy commercial product photography, which is the opposite of what the journal should look
like.

**Flux / SDXL in ComfyUI.** Paste the whole block into positive conditioning and the universal
negative into negative conditioning. Keep guidance moderate — around 3.5 for Flux, 6 for SDXL.
High guidance hardens the light and brings back the plastic sheen the brand rules exclude.

**Consistency.** Generate image 1 first. Once you like it, hold that seed and reuse it as the
starting point for the other five, so light and grade carry across. All six should look like the
same photographer shot them over a single afternoon.

## Reject and reroll on any of these

1. Any readable text, numeral, logo or label anywhere in frame
2. A visible product label, even partially
3. A number plate or car badge (image 5)
4. Money, coins, charts or calculators
5. Any person, hand or face
6. Blue or cool grey in the room; pure black in the shadows
7. Glossy, plastic, over-retouched surfaces or an HDR look
8. Subject drifting into the top or bottom eighth, where the page crop removes it
9. A price written on the blank card (image 4) or the shelf gap filled in (image 6)
