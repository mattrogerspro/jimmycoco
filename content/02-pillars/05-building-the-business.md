# Pillar 5 — Building the Business

**Owns:** "How do I grow past myself?"
**Category string:** `Building the Business`
**Author:** Sunless by Jimmy Coco Trade Team
**Primary reader:** the owner-operator scaling up; the multi-site operator
**Status:** last of the five — after 1, 2 and 4 have depth

---

## Why last

It's the pillar with the smallest audience and the least distinctive gap. General
small-business content for salons does exist — the NHBF and the accountancy
world cover a lot of it, and we have no particular authority on employment law
or company structure.

What we *do* have is the tanning-specific version of each question, which nobody
covers: what a therapist's tanning column should produce, what training a new
tanner properly actually costs, how you keep results consistent between two
therapists, what stock control looks like for solution.

So the rule for this pillar is narrower than the others: **only write the
tanning-specific version.** If the article would be equally true for a nail bar,
it isn't ours.

## Scope

**In:** hiring and training a tanning therapist · consistency of result across a
team · stock control and shrinkage on solution · adding tanning to an existing
salon · multi-site operations · buying decisions (equipment, brand, training) ·
what the tanning column should produce per therapist.

**Out:** employment law · company structure · general marketing · anything where
we'd be repeating the NHBF less well.

## The one genuinely open gap

**A neutral training buyer's guide.** Spray tan training in the UK is sold by
the people who benefit from it, and there is no impartial account of what a
course should contain, what accreditation actually means, what it should cost,
or how to tell a good one from a certificate mill.

We sell solution and we do offer training, so a "neutral" guide from us needs
handling honestly: state the interest openly at the top, then write the guide we
would want a therapist to have even if she trained elsewhere. Done properly it's
the most credible thing in the pillar. Done carelessly it's a brochure and it
damages the programme.

## Articles

### 1. What a tanning therapist should produce — `therapist-column-economics`

Column economics for a tanning therapist: hours, utilisation, treatment mix,
retail attach, and what the column contributes after loaded labour cost. Uses
the pillar-1 model, applied to a person rather than a treatment.

Directly useful to anyone deciding whether to hire.

**Pattern:** A · **CTA:** profit calculator

### 2. Training a new tanner: what it actually costs — `training-cost`

Course fee, wage during training, ruined solution, slower column while learning,
time to competence. The honest total, which is always larger than the course
fee — and which is why so many salons under-train.

**Pattern:** A

### 3. Keeping results consistent across a team — `consistency-across-a-team`

The problem that stops most salons scaling tanning: two therapists, two results,
one reputation. Protocol, documentation, checking. Overlaps pillar 3.

**Pattern:** C/A hybrid · **CTA:** the craft hub

### 4. Stock control for solution — `solution-stock-control`

Shelf life, storage, decanting, waste, shrinkage, reorder points, and what to
do with part-litres. Small topic, entirely unwritten, and constantly asked.

**Pattern:** A · **Register:** solution shelf life claims need real sourcing —
manufacturer data, not forum consensus

### 5. Adding spray tanning to an existing salon — `adding-spray-tanning`

For hair salons, nail bars and clinics considering it. Space, extraction,
licensing, kit, training, breakeven. Draws on pillars 1 and 2 and is the natural
top-of-funnel piece for a trade account.

**Pattern:** A · **CTA:** trade account — the one place in the pillar where a
direct commercial CTA is the honest next step

### 6. Choosing spray tan training: a buyer's guide — `choosing-training`

The neutral guide, with our interest stated openly at the top. What a course
should contain, what accreditation means and doesn't, what it should cost, the
questions to ask, the warning signs.

**Pattern:** B/A hybrid · **Careful:** declare the interest; make no claim about
a named competitor's training

## Standing rules

- If it would be equally true for a nail bar, it isn't ours.
- Where we have a commercial interest in the answer, say so in the article.
- Employment, tax and legal points get the standing note and a pointer to the
  proper source, not our interpretation.

## Commercial route

Trade account and volume terms. This is the pillar where the reader is actively
buying, so the CTAs can be more direct — but only after the article has done its
job.
