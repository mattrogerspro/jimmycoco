# Scene 08 — Professional Comparison

## Role in the experience

The professional comparison scene clarifies why Sunless produces a more believable result than a typical one-tone self-tan. It converts Jimmy Coco’s expertise into a concise, legible product advantage.

This scene follows the founder story. The visitor should already trust Jimmy; now the page must make the product difference easy to understand.

## Commercial objective

Increase purchase confidence by helping analytical and comparison-driven visitors understand why Sunless is worth choosing over a generic alternative.

## User questions answered

- What is technically different about Sunless?
- Why should I expect a more natural result?
- Is the premium positioning justified?
- How is this better than the self-tan I already use?

## Core message

> Professional results begin with better colour logic.

Supporting statement:

> Sunless combines undertone-aware shades, buildable depth and Jimmy Coco’s professional application method—rather than relying on one generic brown base.

## Architectural silhouette

This scene must be quiet, minimal and diagrammatic.

It should contrast with the warm, image-heavy founder editorial immediately before it and the human testimonial environment immediately after it.

Suggested silhouette:

- Bright white or near-white full-width chapter
- Narrow editorial heading block offset to the left
- Large comparison field occupying the centre-right
- Generous empty space above and below
- Thin rules and restrained icons
- No photography except, optionally, one small formula or swatch detail

The composition should feel closer to an Apple specification spread than a conventional ecommerce comparison table.

## Comparison structure

Use two columns only:

### Sunless by Jimmy Coco

- Undertone-aware shade system
- Buildable, controllable colour
- Professional layering logic
- Natural-looking fade
- Guided shade recommendation

### Typical one-tone self-tan

- Generic brown base
- Fixed colour depth
- Limited application guidance
- Greater risk of flat or mismatched colour
- Customer must guess the correct product

Keep the comparison factual, calm and non-aggressive. Avoid implying that every competitor product performs badly.

## Layout direction

- Use a 12-column grid.
- Place the heading across approximately columns 2–5.
- Position the comparison across approximately columns 5–11.
- Use one strong vertical divider or a wide field of negative space between columns.
- Use short rows with compact labels and one supporting sentence where necessary.
- Keep the entire comparison visible in one desktop viewport when possible.
- Do not use a dense matrix with many ticks and crosses.

## Visual hierarchy

1. Scene headline
2. Sunless column title
3. Key differentiator rows
4. Quiet competitor reference column
5. Supporting explanation or CTA

Sunless should be visually privileged through hierarchy, not through loud colour.

## Iconography

Use a restrained monoline icon system only where icons improve recognition:

- Undertone / colour wheel
- Layering / stacked translucent planes
- Buildable depth / gradient swatch
- Fade / soft diminishing line
- Guidance / precise pointer or profile

Icons should be small, elegant and secondary to the copy.

## Colour environment

- Bright white or very pale mineral background
- Deep charcoal text
- Soft bronze or muted gold detail for Sunless
- Light warm grey for the comparison column
- Fine neutral rules
- No large beige cards
- No saturated red or green status colours

The scene must create a visual reset after the founder story.

## Typography

- Large editorial serif headline at approximately 42–48px
- Column titles in refined sans-serif or small serif display
- Row labels at approximately 16–18px
- Supporting text at approximately 15–17px
- Avoid tiny legal-style text
- Keep line lengths short and scannable

## CTA strategy

Use one quiet action beneath the comparison:

**Find My Best Match**

A secondary text link may read:

**Explore Jimmy’s Method**

Do not add multiple purchase buttons inside the comparison rows.

## Eye-tracking path

The intended fixation order is:

1. Headline
2. Sunless column title
3. First differentiator row
4. Remaining Sunless benefits
5. Typical self-tan comparison
6. CTA

The eye should understand the advantage before reading every word.

## Conversion psychology

- **Contrast effect:** makes Sunless benefits more legible through side-by-side comparison.
- **Cognitive fluency:** turns abstract expertise into a simple decision framework.
- **Anchoring:** establishes professional colour logic as the correct standard.
- **Risk reduction:** explains why shade matching and layering reduce the chance of a poor result.
- **Authority:** translates Jimmy’s experience into a repeatable product system.

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Do not rely on colour alone to distinguish columns.
- Maintain strong contrast for all text and rules.
- Use semantic table or definition-list structure in production.
- Ensure screen readers encounter each comparison row in a logical order.
- Avoid horizontal scrolling on mobile; stack paired rows vertically.
- Use clear headings for both columns.

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Creates a dense corporate pricing table
- Uses many green ticks and red crosses
- Adds excessive cards, badges or shadows
- Makes the comparison visually louder than the hero
- Uses tiny unreadable labels
- Invents technical claims that are not approved
- Makes the competitor column look deliberately cheap or mocking
- Uses one large cream rectangle with no visual reset
- Adds product photography that competes with the explanation
- Creates more than two primary columns

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- The difference between Sunless and a generic one-tone self-tan is understood in seconds
- The scene feels calm, premium and factual
- It creates a clear visual reset after the founder story
- It supports the product price and premium positioning
- The comparison does not feel hostile or exaggerated
- The CTA naturally returns the visitor to shade matching
