# Product Page — Mobile Experience

## Purpose

The mobile product page must preserve purchase clarity, confidence and premium character within a narrow viewport. It is not a scaled-down desktop canvas.

## First-screen priorities

The first mobile viewport should communicate as much of the following as practical without becoming cramped:

- Product image
- Product name
- Result statement
- Rating
- Price
- Current selection
- Add to Bag path

Do not push all purchase information below an oversized lifestyle image.

## Recommended mobile sequence

1. Quiet breadcrumb or back control
2. Swipeable primary gallery
3. Product identity and result statement
4. Rating and verified-review count
5. Price and size
6. Suitability summary
7. Variant controls
8. Add to Bag
9. Delivery and returns reassurance
10. Result-at-a-glance facts
11. Product story
12. Real results
13. Application method
14. Reviews
15. Routine
16. Final purchase reminder

## Gallery

- Use touch-friendly horizontal swipe.
- Show visible pagination.
- Keep the primary product image compact enough that purchase information follows quickly.
- Do not trap vertical scrolling inside the gallery.
- Video must not autoplay with sound.

## Variant controls

Use large labelled buttons or stacked radio cards. Avoid horizontally clipped selectors unless the affordance is obvious and all options remain accessible.

The selected result must remain visible directly above the primary CTA.

## Sticky purchase bar

After the main purchase controls scroll out of view, show a compact bottom bar containing:

- Product or selected variant name
- Price
- Add to Bag action

The bar must:

- respect safe-area insets;
- avoid covering content;
- hide or adapt when the keyboard opens;
- expose selection errors clearly;
- disappear when a full purchase panel is already visible.

Do not include multiple competing actions in the sticky bar.

## Accordions

Use accordions selectively for detailed formula information, ingredients, delivery and extended FAQs. Keep suitability, result, timing and essential application instructions visible in the main flow.

## Typography and spacing

- Use readable body text, generally no smaller than 16px.
- Preserve editorial serif hierarchy without creating six-line headings.
- Keep touch targets at least 44px high.
- Use clear section spacing rather than decorative dividers everywhere.
- Reduce image and headline scale before removing essential information.

## Performance

Mobile performance is a commercial requirement:

- Prioritise the first product image.
- Lazy-load lower media.
- Avoid multiple autoplaying videos.
- Reserve image dimensions.
- Deliver appropriately sized responsive assets.
- Keep interaction responsive on mid-range devices.

## Cart feedback

After Add to Bag:

- Confirm the exact product and selection.
- Provide a clear route to cart or checkout.
- Allow the customer to continue browsing.
- Do not obscure the entire page with an oversized modal.

## Accessibility

- Maintain visible keyboard focus for external keyboard users.
- Provide meaningful control labels.
- Announce selection and cart changes.
- Respect reduced-motion settings.
- Ensure the sticky bar does not interrupt screen-reader reading order.

## Failure modes

Reject mobile designs that:

- place Add to Bag several screens below the product image;
- shrink desktop two-column layouts until text is unreadable;
- use tiny swatches;
- hide price or selection inside a sticky bar only;
- use a full-screen menu for ordinary product controls;
- cover content with persistent chat, cookie and purchase overlays;
- retain decorative desktop whitespace at the expense of buying clarity.

## Success criteria

A mobile visitor can understand the product, select the right option and add it to bag using one hand, without losing access to the proof and guidance that justify the purchase.
