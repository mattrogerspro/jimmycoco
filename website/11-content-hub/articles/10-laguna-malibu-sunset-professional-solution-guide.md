# Laguna, Malibu or Sunset? Jimmy Coco Professional Solution Guide

Use Laguna for a Light/Medium result, Malibu for Medium/Dark, and Sunset for Dark/Extra Dark. That depth ladder is the starting point—not the whole consultation. A professional should also consider the client’s starting tone, desired finish, previous tanning experience, event, development time and the amount of solution applied.

## Begin with the client’s requested result

Ask the client to describe the finish, not merely select a shade name. Useful prompts include:

- Do you want a subtle lift, a clear bronze or your deepest result?
- Is this for everyday wear, a holiday, an event or photography?
- How did your last spray tan develop and fade?
- Which areas usually become too dark or too light?
- How much time is available for development and the first rinse?

Translate the answer into the Laguna–Malibu–Sunset ladder only after the desired outcome is clear.

## Laguna: Light/Medium

Laguna is the most restrained starting point in the current professional trio. It is the logical route when the consultation calls for light-to-medium depth, when a client wants a subtle first experience or when the artist needs maximum control over a quiet finish.

Do not present Laguna as “only for pale skin”. Depth preference and professional judgement matter alongside starting tone.

## Malibu: Medium/Dark

Malibu sits at the centre of the range. It suits consultations asking for an unmistakable bronze without automatically moving to the deepest option. For salons, it can function as the core depth between a restrained Laguna result and the darker Sunset route.

Keep the application even and resist using extra solution to compensate for uncertain consultation. Choose the correct solution first, then spray consistently.

## Sunset: Dark/Extra Dark

Sunset is the deepest stated option in the current trio. Use it when the client and artist have agreed on a dark-to-extra-dark result and when the development plan, event and previous experience support that choice.

Deeper does not mean heavier spray technique. The current professional pages recommend using solution sparingly—preferably less than 35 ml. Control should come from the selected formula, approved timing and an even application, not saturation.

## Use application quantity consistently

All three current one-litre pages state:

- use sparingly, preferably less than 35 ml;
- approximately 28 full-body tans per litre;
- shake well before use;
- store in a cool, dark environment away from elevated heat and sunlight;
- bring the solution to room temperature before use;
- perform the specified patch test and follow the full safety directions;
- the product does not contain sunscreen and does not protect against sunburn.

A salon should standardise gun setup, spray pattern and usage tracking so the intended formula—not random variation in quantity—drives the result.

## Development and rinse planning

The current professional direction is 6–12 hours before washing off, with additional instructions for pale or hard-to-tan skin and a second layer. Explain the client’s approved plan in writing:

- application time;
- earliest and latest rinse time;
- what to avoid during development;
- first-rinse instructions;
- sun-protection reminder;
- what to do if irritation occurs.

Do not shorten the explanation because the client has tanned elsewhere. The professional service includes clarity.

## Consultation record

Record at minimum:

- selected solution;
- batch and application date where required;
- approximate volume used;
- starting profile and requested depth;
- development instruction given;
- relevant patch-test and safety record;
- result and feedback at follow-up.

This turns future appointments into informed refinements rather than a fresh guess.

## Common professional mistakes

- Selecting solely from the client’s natural tone
- Treating Dark/Extra Dark as permission to oversaturate
- Allowing usage to vary widely between artists
- Giving a vague “rinse later” instruction
- Failing to record the chosen solution and development plan
- Storing solution in heat or direct light
- Suggesting any spray tan provides sun protection

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the three solutions be mixed?

Do not publish or use a mixing system unless Jimmy Coco supplies an approved protocol with supported ratios and record-keeping guidance.

### How much solution should one service use?

The current pages state approximately 28 full-body tans per litre, which works out at about 36 ml per service. Actual use must reflect equipment, technique and coverage while remaining inside approved directions.

### Which solution should a first-time client choose?

First-time status supports a restrained consultation, but it does not replace a complete discussion of starting tone, desired depth and event. Laguna may be the logical choice when the requested result is Light/Medium.
