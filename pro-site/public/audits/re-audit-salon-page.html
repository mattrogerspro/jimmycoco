Conversion re-audit: the salon page, revisited.

The same thirty-phase standard, re-run against the current live salon page at pro.jimmycoco.email and scored against the original funnel entry page from the first audit. The trajectory now reads 4.6 → 8.0 → 8.2: every critical from round one stays resolved, and since the 15 July re-audit the page has added a real salon FAQ, proper labelling on the order form, and an independent client-survey stat. Until those two are fixed, the ceiling is the same. Details below.

Part three · What still caps the score

Remaining findings, ranked

High1 · The forms still submit via mailto:

Problem: unchanged since both prior audits, and now true of both the trial form and the product order form — each is wired to action="mailto:pro@jimmycoco.co.uk". That opens the visitor's desktop email client — jarring on desktop, frequently broken on mobile, invisible to analytics, and every abandoned half-written email is a lead you'll never know existed. On the page whose entire job is to capture salon trials, nothing is actually captured.

Fix: wire both forms to a real endpoint. The natural home already exists: the repo's /api/campaigns/enroll → Supabase pipeline, which would drop every trial and order request straight into the outreach system and its onboarding sequences. A hosted form service is the quick interim. This remains the one change standing between the page and its purpose.

High2 · Still no salon voices anywhere

Problem: the page proves Hollywood credibility (Jimmy's own quote, red-carpet story) and has since added a client-survey stat — but it offers zero peer proof. No stockist quote, no salon name, no "we switched because…". Salon owners trust other salon owners above celebrities and above statistics.

Fix: one authentic line from an existing stockist — salon name and city — placed between the calculator and the application ritual would lift trust a full point. Collect it via the trial follow-up sequence that already exists in the campaign system.

Medium3 · No measurement on the page

Problem: still no analytics. Calculator engagement — the single most telling intent signal available — is unmeasured, as are scroll depth and CTA clicks. You cannot see which salons nearly converted.

Fix: lightweight analytics with events on slider use and form submission. Calculator-users who don't submit are your retargeting audience.

Medium4 · The trial form is still placeholder-labelled

Problem: the home trial form's fields carry placeholders only — no visible or aria labels; screen readers announce empty fields and placeholders vanish on focus (a WCAG failure, and harder for everyone over 45 — a relevant share of salon owners).

Fix: proper top or floating labels, matching the product order form, which has already been corrected this way. Ten-minute change.

Low5 · Celebrity imagery rights for trade use

Problem: reduced but not gone — the story section now leads with Jimmy himself rather than a campaign face, which is the right move. Where any recognisable campaign imagery from the consumer deck still appears, rights that cover consumer marketing don't automatically cover a trade-recruitment context.

Fix: confirm scope for any recognisable faces, or favour Jimmy-at-work imagery, which arguably sells the method better to professionals anyway.

Low6 · Small finishing items

Problem & fix: the Salon FAQ is resolved but lives on the product page — mirror a short three-question version above the home-page close so objections are answered before the ask. Beyond that: move to hosted, lazy-loaded imagery as the build grows, and add custom focus states to complete keyboard polish.

4.6 → 8.0 → 8.2. The design work is done, twice over. The score is now hostage to two fixes — proof and plumbing.

Would this page convert salons today? Yes — and it presents better than it did in July: the FAQ answers objections, the order form is properly labelled, and an independent stat backs the promise. The narrative still runs correctly: recognition → formula → economics → simplicity → second revenue line → free, risk-free next step.

But the two highest-impact changes have not moved in three passes: replace mailto: with the enroll endpoint so leads are actually captured, and add one real stockist quote. Those two alone are realistically worth another point — and, far more important than the score, they are the difference between a page that looks like it converts and a page that does. Until then this stalls around 8.2 and won't clear 8.5.

Forecast: against the original page, expect multiples rather than percentages on trial-request rate — the original had no form and no visible ask at all. But measure honestly: with the forms on mailto:, the true submission rate is currently unknowable. Wiring the endpoint is what turns this audit's optimism into numbers you can see. Calculator engagement above 40% of visitors would confirm the centrepiece is working.

Don't touch: the calculator-echo mechanism, the dark close section, the litre facts strip, the "no lock-in" posture. They're the page's personality now.
