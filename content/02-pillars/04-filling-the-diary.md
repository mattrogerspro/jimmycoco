## The three numbers this pillar is built on

These are the most commercially useful facts we have about this audience, and
each one is the seed of an article.

**29% of new salon clients ever rebook.** 71% never return (Phorest 2025,
n=716). A reader who improves that by five points changes her business more than
any pricing decision she could make.

**Tanning has the highest no-show rate of any treatment category, at 3.14%**,
peaking June–August (Treatwell 2025). UK no-shows cost the sector £1.6bn a year
at roughly £39 per miss. Pre-payment halves them — and only 17% of salons use
it. That is a fully-formed argument with a number at both ends.

**Retail is 4% of UK salon revenue against a 15–25% benchmark** (SalonIQ 2026),
and **70% of clients who don't buy retail say they'd like to** (Phorest). The
largest unclaimed margin in the reader's business, requiring no extra chair
time.

## The white space nobody has touched

**Seasonality — the UK tanning year.** There is zero content anywhere on this,
in the UK or elsewhere, and it is the single most obviously useful thing a
tanning business could read. When the peaks are, what drives them (party season,
wedding season, holiday season, awards and proms, the June–August no-show
spike), what to do in the troughs, how to staff and stock against it.

It's a genuine gap because it requires UK-specific knowledge that a US brand
can't fake and a forum thread never assembled.

## Articles

### 1. Why 71% of your new clients never come back — `client-retention-rebooking`

The hub. What the 29% figure actually measures, what the rebooking moment is,
why it fails, and what changes it. Practical: the rebook happens in the chair or
it doesn't happen.

**Pattern:** A (it has numbers) · **CTA:** the retail attach article

### 2. Stopping no-shows without losing bookings — `spray-tan-no-shows`

Tanning has the worst no-show rate of any category, and it peaks in summer. The
argument for deposits or pre-payment, with the counter-argument taken seriously
— a reader's fear is that a deposit costs her the booking, and that fear is
reasonable and needs answering rather than dismissing.

Mobile section: a no-show costs a mobile therapist the journey as well as the
slot.

**Pattern:** A · **CTA:** template pack (deposit and cancellation policy)

### 3. The UK spray tan year — `uk-spray-tan-year`

The unwritten article. Month by month: what drives demand, when the troughs are,
what to do in them, how to stock and staff against the shape of the year.

Highest share-potential piece in the programme. Also the most research-hungry,
because almost none of it exists in written form — it needs real data (search
seasonality, our own order data, therapist interviews) rather than assertion.

**Pattern:** A · **Needs:** genuine data work before drafting

### 4. Selling retail without selling — `retail-at-the-chair`

The 4%-versus-15–25% gap, and the 70% who'd like to buy but don't. Reframes
retail as answering a question the client already has rather than as a pitch —
which is both true and the only version this audience will act on.

Pairs with `retail-attach-maths` in pillar 1: that one is the money, this one is
the method.

**Pattern:** A · **CTA:** the range · **Uses:** £18 soufflé, £12.50 mitt

### 5. Bridal and event tanning: pricing and process — `bridal-tanning`

High-value, high-anxiety work with its own timings, trials, contracts and
pricing logic. Currently owned by SalonGeek threads. Overlaps pillar 1 on
pricing and pillar 2 on contracts.

**Pattern:** A · **CTA:** template pack

### 6. The consultation as a commercial moment — `consultation-commercial`

The consultation is required for compliance and is also the highest-intent
selling moment in the appointment. Bridges pillars 2 and 4 — and the template
pack is the artefact that serves both.

**Pattern:** A · **CTA:** template pack

## Standing rules

- Every article in this pillar carries at least one of the three headline
  numbers, with its source and sample size.
- No "engagement" or "loyalty" abstractions. Everything reduces to something the
  reader does in the room on Monday.
- Retail content uses the real prices — £59 / £18 / £12.50.
- Take the reader's objection seriously before answering it. She has heard "just
  take deposits" before and has a reason she hasn't.

## Commercial route

Retail range and trade account, via the retail-attach content. The template pack
carries most of the rest.
