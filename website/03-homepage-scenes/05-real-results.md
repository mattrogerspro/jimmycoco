# Scene 05 — Real Skin, Real Results

## Role in the experience

This is the principal proof chapter. The recommendation engine has guided the visitor and the Hollywood Method has explained the mechanism. Real Results must now prove that the promise works on believable human skin.

This chapter should create the moment where scepticism gives way to belief.

## Commercial objective

Increase purchase confidence by showing specific, credible and relatable outcomes rather than aspirational campaign imagery alone.

The scene should:

- Demonstrate results on different skin tones
- Connect each result to a named product and application method
- Reduce fear of orange colour, streaking and unrealistic editing
- Help the visitor picture the result on themselves
- Create a strong conversion moment before the product gallery

## User questions answered

- Does the product work on skin like mine?
- Is the colour natural in ordinary daylight?
- Which product created this result?
- How many coats were used?
- How long did it develop?
- Are the images retouched or manipulated?

## Core message

> Real skin. Real daylight. Real confidence.

Alternative supporting line:

> See the exact product, shade, development time and number of coats behind every result.

## Proof standard

Every result must include enough context to become evidence rather than decoration.

Required metadata:

- Natural skin tone
- Undertone where known
- Product used
- Shade or depth
- Number of coats
- Development time
- Lighting context
- Verified customer or approved model status
- Retouching statement where accurate

Do not show anonymous portraits with vague claims.

## Architectural silhouette

This scene should be photography-dominant and visually expansive.

Approved silhouette:

- Near full-width photographic field
- One dominant hero result taking approximately 55–65% of the visual area
- Two supporting results at different scales
- Minimal interface layered around, not over, important facial or skin detail
- Asymmetrical editorial composition
- Little visible background beyond the imagery

This chapter must feel structurally different from the airy Hollywood Method and the contained product gallery that follows.

## Layout direction

Recommended desktop composition:

- Large left or centre before/after pair spanning 6–7 columns
- Narrow editorial proof column spanning 3–4 columns
- Two smaller supporting result crops offset below or beside
- One clear CTA at the end of the visual path

Avoid a uniform row of four equal before/after cards.

## Hero result

The dominant result should show one relatable customer or model in consistent natural light.

Preferred treatment:

- Before and after images aligned by camera angle and crop
- Same background and lighting
- Natural facial expression
- Real skin texture retained
- Clear tonal difference without exaggerated saturation
- Subtle vertical comparison slider or fixed side-by-side layout

The result must not resemble a dramatic filter transformation.

## Supporting results

Use two additional skin profiles to broaden relevance, for example:

- Fair or light skin seeking subtle golden colour
- Medium or olive skin seeking a deeper bronze result

A deep-skin example should be included when the actual product range and approved evidence support it.

### Trust badges

Use only factual badges:

- Verified purchase
- Natural daylight
- No beauty filter
- Product and method disclosed

Do not overload each result with badges. Two or three concise proof markers are sufficient.

### CTA

**See Results on My Skin Tone**

Alternative:

**Find My Match from These Results**

The CTA should lead back into a personalised route, not simply a generic gallery.

## Photography direction

- Documentary beauty photography
- Realistic skin texture
- Consistent, soft natural light
- Neutral white balance
- No orange colour grading
- Minimal makeup unless explicitly relevant
- No artificial contouring or high-gloss retouching
- Similar pose, crop and lens between before and after
- Backgrounds should be calm and unobtrusive

The result should look professionally documented, not clinically photographed.

## Image ethics and credibility

- Do not fabricate “unretouched” or “verified” claims
- Do not alter body shape or facial structure
- Do not exaggerate the before image by cooling or desaturating it
- Do not warm the after image independently
- Do not change exposure between before and after
- Do not imply medically proven outcomes

## Colour environment

This scene should be dominated by human skin, light and photography rather than a large cream UI field.

Use:

- Natural skin tones
- Soft neutral architectural backgrounds
- Deep charcoal text panels where required
- Warm white captions
- Minimal muted bronze accents

Avoid placing every result inside a pale beige card.

## Typography

- Serif headline: 46–56px
- Proof statement or pull quote: 24–32px
- Caption headings: 13–15px confident sans-serif
- Metadata: 15–17px
- CTA: clear, high-contrast and at least 44px tall

## Eye-tracking path

1. Dominant after image or comparison boundary
2. Customer's face and visible skin result
3. Headline
4. Before image
5. Product and method metadata
6. Supporting results
7. CTA

The after image may lead emotionally, but the before and evidence must be immediately available.

## Conversion psychology

This chapter uses:

- **Specificity:** exact product, coats and development time
- **Social proof:** relatable customers rather than anonymous claims
- **Similarity bias:** users trust evidence from people like themselves
- **Risk reduction:** controlled photography makes the result believable
- **Visual evidence:** proof reduces reliance on brand assertion
- **Commitment momentum:** the next action connects proof to personal choice

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Use explicit Before and After text labels, not colour or position alone
- Comparison sliders must be keyboard accessible
- Provide descriptive alt text explaining visible colour change without subjective exaggeration
- Keep captions outside complex image areas
- Do not use autoplaying transitions that obscure comparison
- On mobile, stack each before/after pair with metadata directly beneath

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Produces mismatched people in the before and after frames
- Changes facial features, pose, lighting or background
- Uses orange or overly saturated skin
- Smooths all skin texture
- Creates glamour campaign photography instead of documentary proof
- Places four equal cards in a generic grid
- Adds unreadable fake metadata
- Covers faces with UI overlays
- Invents unrealistic badges or claims
- Uses dramatic transformation effects
- Makes the before image intentionally grey or unattractive

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- The proof feels credible before the visitor reads all the metadata
- At least two skin profiles are clearly represented
- The exact product and method are easy to identify
- The section is image-dominant and structurally distinct
- The visitor can imagine their own likely outcome
- The CTA naturally moves from evidence to personalised product choice
