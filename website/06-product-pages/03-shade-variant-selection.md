# Product Page — Shade and Variant Selection

## Purpose

Selection controls must reduce uncertainty rather than simply expose SKU options. The visitor should understand the visible result attached to each choice before adding to bag.

## Selection hierarchy

Use only the controls relevant to the product:

1. Result depth or shade
2. Formula type
3. Size
4. Bundle or subscription option
5. Quantity

Do not present irrelevant controls or duplicate the same choice under different labels.

## Variant labelling

Use explicit names and outcome descriptions:

- Light — subtle, sun-kissed colour
- Medium — balanced everyday bronze
- Deep — richer event-ready colour

Where undertone matters, state it:

- Golden
- Olive
- Violet-neutralising
- Neutral bronze

All labels must reflect approved product information.

## Control design

Preferred control types:

- Large labelled segmented buttons for three to five choices
- Radio-card selectors when each choice needs supporting explanation
- Swatches only when colour is genuinely meaningful and always paired with text
- Dropdowns only for long, low-risk lists

Every control must show selected, hover, focus, unavailable and error states.

## Contextual guidance

When a selection changes, update:

- Product image where applicable
- Result description
- Suitability summary
- Price
- Availability
- Development guidance if different
- Add to Bag state

The page must never show one selected label while purchasing another SKU.

## Recommendation state

Visitors arriving from shade matching should see a clear but restrained confirmation:

> Recommended for your desired depth and undertone.

Preselection is acceptable only when the result is visible, editable and accurately tied to the selected SKU.

## Comparison support

Where adjacent choices are easy to confuse, provide a compact comparison layer showing:

- Expected depth
- Undertone
- Number of coats
- Development time
- Best use case

Do not force the customer to open multiple tabs or memorise separate descriptions.

## Unavailable variants

Unavailable choices must remain legible but clearly disabled. Explain the state and offer a relevant action:

- Notify me
- Choose the closest available alternative
- Recheck my match

Do not silently hide variants that users may expect to exist.

## Subscription or replenishment

Only show subscription controls when the commercial offer is real and understandable. Clearly state:

- Frequency
- Price difference
- Ability to pause or cancel
- When the first and recurring charges occur

One-time purchase should remain visible and easy to select.

## Validation

If a required selection is missing, move focus to the relevant control and explain what must be chosen. Do not rely on colour, shaking buttons or generic error banners.

## Failure modes

Reject selectors that:

- use unlabelled circles;
- hide the selected state;
- use vague names such as A, B or Option 3;
- change price without explanation;
- make unavailable items appear selectable;
- force shade matching before any product information is visible;
- present more than one primary recommendation without hierarchy.

## Success criteria

The visitor can distinguish each option, understand the outcome, confirm the current selection and trust that the correct SKU will be added to bag.
