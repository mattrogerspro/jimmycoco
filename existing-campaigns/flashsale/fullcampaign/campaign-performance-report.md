# Summer Flash Sale — Campaign Performance Report

**Campaign:** SUMMER FLASH SALE (email)
**Sent:** Wednesday 29 July 2026, ~6:07pm · via Klaviyo
**Audience:** 34,431 · **Delivered:** 31,408
**Prepared:** 30 July 2026

---

## 1. The headline in one line

**Deliverability is clean and the store converts well — but the email itself didn't earn enough clicks, and that's where nearly all the lost revenue is.** The good news: the fix is entirely in the email, not the plumbing or the checkout.

---

## 2. Results vs industry benchmark

Benchmarks are Klaviyo's 2026 Health & Beauty *campaign* averages ([source](https://www.klaviyo.com/uk/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks-open-click-and-conversion-rates)).

| Metric | This campaign | H&B benchmark | Where you landed |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Open rate | **19.5%** | 30.5% | 64% of benchmark — below |
| Click rate | **0.39%** | 1.24% | **31% of benchmark — well below** |
| Placed-order rate | **0.04%** | 0.19% | 22% of benchmark — below |
| Unsubscribe | 0.18% | ~0.2% | in range |
| Spam complaints | **0.00%** | <0.02% | excellent |
| Bounce | 1.0% | <2% | healthy |

**Revenue: £449.88** from **13 orders** (£34.61 average order value).

Every engagement metric is below benchmark, and **click rate is the worst — a third of where it should be.** That's the single most important number in this report.

---

## 3. The funnel — where it breaks

| Stage | Count | % of delivered | Read |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Delivered | 31,408 | 100% | ✅ clean |
| Opened | 6,118 | 19.5% | below benchmark, and inflated (see §4) |
| **Clicked** | **122** | **0.39%** | **← the collapse happens here** |
| Ordered | 13 | 0.04% | £449.88 |

Read the two steps that matter:

- **Opened → Clicked:** of everyone who opened, only **2% clicked** (122 of 6,118). A healthy figure is nearer 10%. **The email gets opened and then fails to move people to act.**
- **Clicked → Ordered:** of the 122 who clicked, **13 ordered — a 10.7% conversion rate.** That's *healthy*. **Your website turns clicks into sales just fine.**

So the bottleneck is unambiguous: it's not deliverability, and it's not the store. **It's the email's ability to generate a click.** Everything downstream works.

---

## 4. One caveat on the open rate

The 19.5% open rate is *softer than it looks*. Apple's Mail app auto-registers an "open" on every email for privacy reasons — so a chunk of those 6,118 are machines, not people. The true human open rate is lower still. This is why the click and revenue numbers — which can't be faked — are the ones to steer by, not opens.

## 5. What people actually clicked (and what it tells us)

| Link | Unique clicks |
|---|---:|
| The Glow Edit (£32) | 41 |
| Shop all / collections | 39 |
| Homepage | 38 |
| Malibu Beach Duo (£28) | 19 |
| **Refund policy** | **5** |
| Terms of Service | 3 |

Two useful signals in here:

1. **The Glow Edit was the top performer** — the lower-priced entry product pulled the most clicks. Worth leading with it, or giving it more prominence, next time.
2. **People clicked through to find your Refund Policy.** That's a tell: shoppers were *looking for reassurance the email didn't give them* (returns, guarantee). It's real-world proof of the trust gap flagged in the trust-and-friction audit — they had to go hunting for it.

**🐞 One bug:** a Google Fonts stylesheet link is being tracked as a clickable link (5 clicks) — a leftover font reference sitting in the email body. Harmless to recipients but it shouldn't be there; worth cleaning up.

## 6. What's working — keep doing it

- **Deliverability is genuinely healthy:** 0 spam complaints, 1% bounce, and authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) all verified passing. Nothing here is holding you back.
- **The store converts:** 10.7% of clickers bought. The checkout and product pages are doing their job.
- **List hygiene is respected:** 843 suppressed contacts were correctly skipped, and spam complaints are zero.

*(One thing to check: ~1,830 sent emails — about 5% — didn't register as delivered *or* bounced. Worth a look at the Deliverability tab to confirm nothing's being quietly blocked.)*

## 7. The opportunity, in money

If this same campaign had merely hit the **industry-average click rate (1.24%)** — with everything else held constant — the maths is:

> 389 clicks → ~41 orders → **~£1,440 revenue** — roughly **3× what it made.**

And if it reached the benchmark *order* rate, it would be nearer **£2,000 (4.6×)**. These are illustrative, not promises — but they size the prize. **The gap between £450 and ~£1,500 is almost entirely "getting more of the people who opened to click,"** which is squarely fixable.

---

## 8. How to improve it — prioritised

The full detail sits in two companion documents (sales-psychology and trust-and-friction audits); here's the ranked plan.

### Do first — remove the hesitation that's killing clicks (this is the 2%-CTOR problem)
1. **Add a trust bar under the hero** — *Free UK delivery over £30 · 14-day money-back guarantee · Pay in 3 with Klarna · Dispatched in 1–3 days.* You offer all of these and the email shows none. (People were literally clicking to find your refund policy.)
2. **Put the money-back guarantee front and centre.** Self-tan's biggest blocker is "what if it's the wrong shade?" — you neutralise it and don't say so.
3. **Add Klarna to the £59 kit** ("3 payments of £19.67"). BNPL lifts conversion materially and softens the top price.
4. **Lead with The Glow Edit** — the data says it's your click magnet.

### Do next — lift opens and clicks structurally
5. **Malibu unengaged subscribers** and segment engaged-first — raises both open and inbox placement over time.
6. **Start collecting product reviews** (post-purchase flow). Star ratings are the highest-ROI trust signal and you currently have none to show.
7. **Escape the Promotions tab** with a lighter image-to-text balance and a re-engagement ask.

### Housekeeping
8. Fix the tracked-font-link bug; consider a Thursday-evening send (this went Wednesday 6pm — fine, but Thursday tests better for event-led self-tan); add real UTM tags for cleaner attribution.

---

## 9. Bottom line for the client

This campaign was **technically well-delivered and reached a large, clean list with zero spam complaints** — the hard infrastructure is sound. It under-performed because the **email didn't give people enough reason, or enough reassurance, to click** — proven by a 2% click-to-open rate against a store that converts clicks at 11%. The revenue opportunity (roughly 3×) is real and sits entirely in the email's content and trust signals, all of which are low-effort to add. **Next send: same list, same deliverability, but with the trust and clarity fixes above — that's where the growth is.**
