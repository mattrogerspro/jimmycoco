## Why this is second

The biggest open goal in the category. BABTAC's most recent indexed asset on the
subject is a 2016 PDF. NHBF has nothing spray-tan-specific. No free, GDPR-aware,
UK-compliant forms library exists anywhere — the templates market is owned by
Etsy sellers. Meanwhile the questions are genuinely difficult, genuinely
consequential, and asked constantly.

It is also the pillar where being wrong has consequences, which is why
[decision 2](../00-strategy/decisions.md#2-compliance-depth-full-heavily-sourced)
attaches conditions to it.

## The conditions — read before writing anything here

1. **Cite the regulator, not another blog.** legislation.gov.uk, HSE, the local
   authority, the SAG-CS opinion, the insurer's own policy wording. If a claim's
   trail ends at a salon marketing site, it does not go in.
2. **Local authority variation is stated as variation.** Special treatments
   licensing genuinely differs by council. We explain the mechanism — what the
   regime is, why it varies, what to ask — and tell the reader to check her own
   authority. We never say "you need a licence" or "you don't" as a national
   fact.
3. **The standing note appears on every article.** General information, current
   at date of publication, not legal advice, check with your own council and
   your own broker.
4. **Nothing on the [claims register](../03-research/claims-register.md) is
   asserted.** The three that matter most here:
   - There is **no "15% FDA limit"**. 21 CFR 73.2150 contains no numeric cap.
   - There is **no published ventilation ACH or CFM standard** for spray tan
     booths, and no legal requirement expressed as one.
   - There is **no published PSI standard** for spray tan equipment.

   Saying each of those plainly *is* the content. Every competitor page states
   at least one of them as fact.
5. **No fear as a lever.** What the rule is, where it comes from, what to do.
   Not what it will cost you when they catch you.
6. **These articles do not sell.** CTA is a template, another article, or the
   Brief. Never a product — see the
   [CTA policy](../01-editorial-system/cta-policy.md#hard-rules).

## Blocked

**Nothing about DHA percentage compliance publishes until the 18% question is
resolved.** Professional rapid solutions are sold across the industry at 18% DHA,
which exceeds every published regulator figure for leave-on self-tan (UK 14%,
EU 10%). The UK rinse-off figure is 22.5%, and whether a booth application is
correctly categorised as rinse-off is exactly the unresolved point. This is
commercially significant for us as well as editorially, and it needs a real
answer — not an article — first. Tracked in
[`03-research/claims-register.md`](../03-research/claims-register.md).

## Articles

### 1. Do you need a licence to spray tan in the UK? — `spray-tan-licence-uk`

The hub, and the single most valuable unanswered question in the category. The
honest answer is "it depends which council", and the article's job is to make
that useful rather than frustrating: what the special treatments regime is, the
different statutory routes (London boroughs differ from the rest of England;
Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland differ again), how to find out for your
own address, what to ask, and what it typically involves.

**Pattern:** B · **CTA:** template pack · **Sources needed:** the enabling
legislation for each nation, plus 3–4 worked council examples showing the spread

### 2. Insurance for spray tanning, explained — `spray-tan-insurance`

What treatment liability actually covers, what voids it, why patch-test policy
and consultation records appear in policy wording, what changes when you work
mobile or from home, and what a client claim actually looks like.

Nobody has explained this properly. It also converts directly into the template
pack, because half of what insurers require is documentation.

**Pattern:** B · **CTA:** template pack · **Sources:** actual policy wording
from 3+ UK providers, quoted

### 3. Patch testing: what's actually required — `patch-testing-requirements`

Genuinely contested — the training standard says 48 hours, plenty of practice
says 24, and insurers set their own terms. Present the disagreement, then the
practical position: your insurer's wording is the requirement that binds you.

**Pattern:** B · **CTA:** template pack · **Register:** 24h vs 48h is a live
disagreement, not a settled fact

### 4. COSHH for a spray tan room — `coshh-spray-tanning`

What COSHH actually requires of a salon, what a risk assessment for a tanning
room contains, where the safety data sheet fits, and what "adequate control"
means when no numeric standard exists. The SAG-CS 2024 opinion is the strongest
asset available here and nobody in the trade is using it.

**Pattern:** B · **CTA:** template pack · **Sources:** HSE COSHH guidance,
SAG-CS 2024

### 5. Spray tan ventilation: what's real and what isn't — `spray-tan-ventilation`

The article that says the quiet part. There is no ACH standard. There is no CFM
requirement. There is a genuine exposure question, and the SAG-CS opinion is the
only regulator modelling of it, with actual margins of safety by booth type.
That's the substance — and it is far more useful than the invented numbers
everyone else publishes.

**Pattern:** B · **CTA:** COSHH article · **Register:** no ACH/CFM figure may
be given, including as a recommendation

### 6. Spray tanning from home: what you need in place — `home-based-spray-tanning`

Planning, licensing, insurance, household policy implications, extraction,
storage. ~40% of self-employed beauty professionals are mobile or home-based and
this is unwritten anywhere.

**Pattern:** B · **Reader:** mobile/home-based therapist

### 7. Consultation records and GDPR — `consultation-records-gdpr`

What you must record, what lawful basis you're relying on, how long to keep it,
what a client can ask for. Health data in a consultation form is special
category data, which most salons don't know.

**Pattern:** B · **CTA:** template pack · **Careful:** lawful basis and
retention periods are exactly where an unsourced claim would do damage
