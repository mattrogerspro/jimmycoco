# Editorial Article and Blog Playbook

## Purpose

Create premium, useful and commercially responsible articles that sound like Jimmy Coco’s professional judgement translated by an excellent beauty editor.

## Article brief

Every article begins with these fields:

- primary reader;
- problem or decision;
- search intent;
- direct answer;
- one primary Jimmy Coco product route;
- facts and approved sources;
- claim-risk level;
- required image evidence;
- internal links;
- success event.

If the direct answer or product route cannot be stated clearly, the topic is not ready.

## Voice

### Use

- calm authority;
- clear instructions;
- specific product names;
- realistic expectations;
- tactile but restrained beauty language;
- British English for the UK site;
- short reassurance at points of anxiety.

### Avoid

- “unlock your glow journey” and similar clichés;
- unsupported superlatives;
- fake urgency;
- invented customer quotes;
- presenting Jimmy’s advice in quotation marks unless a transcript exists;
- framing paleness as a flaw;
- implying darker is always better;
- shaming mistakes;
- competitor mentions in customer-facing copy.

## Required structure

1. **Title:** natural question or outcome; 45–65 characters where possible.
2. **Opening answer:** resolve the core question immediately.
3. **Why it matters:** one short paragraph connecting technique to visible result.
4. **Method:** numbered, scannable instructions.
5. **Product role:** why the named Jimmy Coco product or tool fits this step.
6. **Mistakes:** three to five concrete failure modes.
7. **FAQ:** only questions that add new information.
8. **Next step:** one primary commercial action.

Use a table only when readers need to compare known choices, timings or product roles.

## Evidence and claims protocol

### Tier A — operational facts

Examples: size, variant, development time, usage quantity, application instructions.  
Required evidence: current product page, packaging or approved product-data record.

### Tier B — product performance claims

Examples: streak-free, long-lasting, even fade, hydrating, fast-drying.  
Required evidence: approved claim file, formulation substantiation or authorised product copy.

### Tier C — ingredient and skin claims

Examples: plumping, firming, antioxidant defence, collagen support, anti-ageing.  
Required evidence: formulation-level substantiation and legal approval for the market. These claims require explicit final review even when they appear on a live product page.

### Tier D — authority, press and celebrity claims

Required evidence: approved press source, client permission and current rights record. Do not turn a historical association into a current endorsement.

## Safety language

- State clearly where relevant that self-tan does not provide sun protection.
- Tell readers to follow the product label and on-pack directions.
- Do not provide medical advice.
- Do not claim suitability for pregnancy, sensitive skin, acne, eczema or another condition without approved evidence.
- Do not invent a patch-test interval. Use the exact approved instruction for the product and market.
- Do not imply that more product, more layers or longer development is always safer or better.

## SEO and answer-engine standard

- One primary intent per article.
- Put the direct answer in the opening paragraph.
- Use question-led H2s when they match genuine reader decisions.
- Define product format, result, development time and application tool in plain language.
- Include concise FAQ answers that stand alone without the rest of the article.
- Add `Article`, `BreadcrumbList` and appropriate `FAQPage` structured data only when the rendered page visibly contains the same information.
- Include author, reviewer, published date and updated date.
- Use a self-referencing canonical URL.
- Write unique meta titles and descriptions; do not truncate the core promise.
- Add descriptive alt text that identifies the product, action and body area without keyword stuffing.

## GEO/citation-readiness

To make content easier for AI answer systems to understand and cite:

- state the answer before brand storytelling;
- use stable names consistently;
- separate consumer Soufflé, facial mist and professional spray solution clearly;
- quantify only verified timings and quantities;
- present comparison tables with explicit column labels;
- show who wrote and reviewed the advice;
- maintain an `Editorial sources` block in repository drafts;
- update visible dates when facts materially change, not for cosmetic freshness.

## Image standard

Every article requires:

- one editorial hero image;
- two to four instructional images or short clips;
- one product-context image;
- one optional proof image only when provenance is recorded.

Images must use current Jimmy Coco packaging and approved people. Do not generate or retouch product labels. Show natural skin texture and believable daylight colour. Instructional imagery must prioritise hand position, amount, blend edge and application sequence over decorative posing.

## Product-link standard

- Link to the exact live product handle.
- Do not list an unavailable product as the primary solution.
- Do not include price in evergreen body copy unless commercial policy requires it.
- If a bundle is region-specific, identify the market.
- Use a fallback route when a named item is unavailable.

## Editorial QA checklist

- [ ] The first paragraph answers the title.
- [ ] The article contains no competitor product or brand references.
- [ ] Every Jimmy Coco name and link is current.
- [ ] Timings and quantities match approved directions.
- [ ] No unsupported formula, skin, celebrity or results claim remains.
- [ ] Advice does not contradict the product label.
- [ ] The article does not imply sun protection.
- [ ] Heading order is logical.
- [ ] Images have rights, provenance and alt text.
- [ ] Internal links form a useful journey.
- [ ] One primary CTA is visually dominant.
- [ ] Metadata, canonical and schema match visible content.
- [ ] A named editor and product/claims reviewer have approved publication.

## Definition of done

An article is ready to publish only when the content, product data, claims, imagery, links, metadata, structured data and mobile rendering have all passed review. A polished draft without evidence is not production-ready.
