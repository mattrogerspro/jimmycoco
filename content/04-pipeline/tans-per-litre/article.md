# How many spray tans do you get from a litre?

> **Twenty-eight** from a litre of the professional solution. At £60 a litre that
> is **£2.14 of solution per tan**, and about **36ml** on the client.
>
> Your own number will sit either side of that. Here is how to measure it, and
> what each tan of difference is actually worth — which is less than almost
> everyone assumes.

Ask this question anywhere in the UK trade and you get a number back within
seconds. Ask what the number is based on and the conversation stops.

That is the honest state of the answer. Most of what ranks for this question is
forum threads written between 2008 and 2018, and most of the rest is US brands
quoting US pricing. So here is the figure, what it rests on, and how to work out
yours — because yours is the only one that matters for your ordering.

## The figure

**A litre of Sunless by Jimmy Coco professional solution is rated at
approximately 28 full-body tans.** The solution is 10% DHA.

Twenty-eight is arithmetic you can check:

`1,000ml ÷ 28 tans = 35.7ml per full-body tan`

That is the dose the rating assumes — a little under 36ml on the client, once.
Not per coat, not per pass, and not including what you lose to the cup, the line
and the tent.

## What it costs you

`£60 ÷ 28 = £2.14 per tan`

On a £30 treatment, that is **7.1% of the ticket**. Worth holding on to, because
it is the fact that makes the rest of this article make sense.

## What actually moves your number

Six things, in rough order of how much difference they make:

**Coverage.** A full-body tan on a size 8 frame and a full-body tan on a size 20
frame are not the same volume of solution. This is the largest single variable
and nobody's rating can account for it.

**Technique.** Distance from the skin, speed of pass, overlap and how many passes
you make. A tight, efficient hand covers a body in materially less solution than
a heavy one, and this is the variable you can actually train.

**Equipment and setup.** Gun, needle, air pressure and pattern width. A pattern
that is too wide puts solution past the edge of the client.

**Priming and line loss.** Every prime, every purge, every colour change. Small
per session, real across a week.

**Cup residue.** What you cannot get out of the cup at the end of the day.

**Depth chasing.** Going back over an area to make it darker rather than
selecting the right depth to begin with. This is the most expensive habit on the
list and it does not reliably produce a darker result — depth comes from the
solution and the development, not from volume on the skin.

We have not found a published study putting numbers on any of those, and we are
not going to invent one. What follows is how to replace the estimate with your
own measurement.

## How to measure yours

One week. It is the most useful hour of admin in your tanning business.

1. **Start a fresh litre** and mark the date on it.
2. **Count every full-body tan** you do from it — a tally on the bottle works.
   Do not count touch-ups, half-body treatments or practice.
3. **Stop when the bottle is done**, not when it looks nearly done.
4. **Divide.** Tans ÷ 1 litre is your yield.
5. **Divide again.** £60 ÷ your yield is your true solution cost per tan.

Two conditions make the number honest: run it across a normal week rather than a
quiet one, and do not change how you spray while you are counting. A yield
measured on your best behaviour tells you what is possible, not what is
happening.

## What a tan of difference is worth

This is the part that surprises people.

At 12 tans a week — 624 a year — **each additional tan you get from a litre is
worth about £46 a year.**

| Your yield | Solution per tan | Cost per year at 624 tans |
|---|---|---|
| 26 | £2.31 | £1,440 |
| 27 | £2.22 | £1,387 |
| **28** | **£2.14** | **£1,337** |
| 29 | £2.07 | £1,291 |
| 30 | £2.00 | £1,248 |

The whole spread between a heavy hand and an efficient one — four tans a litre —
is about **£223 a year**. Real money, and worth having. But set it against the
things sitting next to it in the same business: three retail sales a week is
worth £1,404 a year, and £2 on your price is worth £1,229.

**Yield is worth improving. It is not worth optimising.** If measuring it turns
into changing supplier over 8p a tan, the tail is wagging the dog — and a
cheaper litre that produces one poor result costs more than a year of the
saving. The [full cost model](/articles/what-a-spray-tan-costs) sets out where
the money actually is.

## What it means for ordering

Once you have your own yield, reordering stops being a guess:

`litres per month = (tans per week × 52 ÷ 12) ÷ your yield`

At 12 tans a week and 28 to the litre, that is **1.9 litres a month**, or about
**22 litres a year**. At 20 tans a week it is 3.1 a month.

Order to your measured number, not the rated one, and keep a litre of headroom
for a busy week — running out mid-season costs a great deal more than a spare
bottle.

## What to do about it

1. **Measure one litre.** A tally on the bottle, one week.
2. **Recalculate your cost per tan** with your own number, not ours.
3. **Set your reorder point** from that figure rather than from when the shelf
   looks empty.
4. **If your yield is well below 28, look at pattern width and passes first** —
   they are the two variables you can change on Monday.
5. **Then stop thinking about it.** Solution is 7% of your ticket. The levers
   worth your attention are in the cost model.

## FAQ

**How many spray tans in a litre of solution?**
Approximately 28 full-body tans from a litre of Sunless by Jimmy Coco
professional solution, which works out at about 36ml per treatment. Your own
number will sit either side of that depending on coverage, technique and
equipment. Measure a full litre over a normal week to find yours.

**How much solution should a full-body spray tan use?**
Around 36ml, which is what 28 tans to a litre works out at. Using materially
more usually points to a pattern that is too wide, too much distance from the
skin or repeated passes over the same area — rather than to a better result.

**How much does the solution cost per spray tan?**
£2.14 at £60 a litre and 28 tans to the litre. On a £30 treatment that is 7.1%
of what the client pays you. Total consumables, including disposables, filters
and laundry, come to about £3.24 a tan.

**How do I work out my own tans per litre?**
Start a fresh litre, tally every full-body tan you do from it, stop when the
bottle is genuinely empty, and divide. Do it across a normal week and do not
change how you spray while you are counting. Then divide £60 by that number for
your true solution cost.

**Is it worth switching solution to get more tans per litre?**
Rarely. Each extra tan per litre is worth about £46 a year at 12 tans a week,
and the full realistic spread is around £223. One client lost to a result you
were not happy with costs more than that.

**How many litres should I order a month?**
Take your tans per week, multiply by 52, divide by 12, then divide by your
measured yield. At 12 tans a week and 28 to the litre that is 1.9 litres a
month. Keep a litre of headroom for a busy week.
