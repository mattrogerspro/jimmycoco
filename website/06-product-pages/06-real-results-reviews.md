# Product Page — Real Results and Reviews

## Purpose

This chapter provides credible proof that the product performs for real customers with different skin contexts, goals and experience levels.

## Real-results structure

Each featured result should include:

- Customer or model context
- Product used
- Selected shade or variant
- Number of coats
- Development time
- Lighting context
- Time since application
- Verification or disclosure status

Do not show anonymous before-and-after imagery without enough context to interpret it.

## Image treatment

- Match framing and lighting as closely as practical.
- Preserve natural skin texture.
- Avoid aggressive retouching and saturation.
- Do not alter the after image to exaggerate depth.
- Clearly label when images are customer-submitted.
- Ensure consent and usage rights are recorded.

## Proof hierarchy

1. Finished skin result
2. Customer context and application details
3. Concise quote
4. Verified-review status
5. Route to more reviews or similar results

The section should remain visual and easy to scan.

## Review filtering

Allow review exploration by useful concerns rather than only star rating:

- Fair or light skin
- Medium or deep skin
- First-time user
- Deep colour
- Natural fade
- Transfer concerns
- Event preparation
- Ease of application

Only expose filters when enough review volume exists to make them useful.

## Review card content

A useful review includes:

- Rating
- Review title
- Review body
- Verified purchase status
- Product variant
- Date
- Relevant customer attributes when voluntarily supplied
- Helpful-vote control where supported

Avoid filling the page with truncated generic praise.

## Rating summary

Show:

- Average rating
- Total verified-review count
- Rating distribution
- Common positive themes
- Common concerns when sufficiently evidenced

Do not hide lower ratings or manufacture summary claims.

## Objection resolution

Select a small number of representative stories that address genuine anxieties:

- “Will it look orange?”
- “Can a beginner apply it evenly?”
- “Does it transfer?”
- “How does it fade?”
- “Will the colour be deep enough?”

Never present a review as proof of a claim it does not support.

## Visual architecture

Use one dominant result story with a smaller supporting review stream. Avoid a generic wall of six equal testimonial cards.

## Accessibility

- Provide useful alt text for result images.
- Ensure filters and pagination are keyboard accessible.
- Announce updated review results.
- Do not encode rating only as star icons; include a text value.
- Keep customer-submitted image disclosures readable.

## Failure modes

Reject sections that:

- use stock models presented as customers;
- omit product and application metadata;
- show incompatible before-and-after lighting;
- hide negative reviews;
- use invented quotes;
- make star ratings the only proof;
- turn reviews into a visually repetitive carousel with no concern-based relevance.

## Success criteria

Visitors can find evidence from people with comparable goals, understand how the result was achieved and judge the product more confidently.
