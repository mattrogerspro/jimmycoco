# Legal, Service and Operational Copy

## Purpose

Define how Sunless writes transactional, legal, consent, delivery, account and support messages with the same care as commercial email.

## Principle

Operational copy must prioritise accuracy, action and reassurance over brand theatre.

## Service-message hierarchy

Every service message should answer:

1. What happened?
2. What does it mean for the customer?
3. What happens next?
4. Is any action required?
5. Where can the customer get help?

## Subject-line rules

Service subjects must be explicit.

Prefer:

- `Your Sunless order has shipped`
- `Action needed: confirm your delivery address`
- `Your refund has been processed`
- `A change to your VIP membership`

Avoid vague editorial subjects for important operational information.

## Order and delivery copy

Include only verified states. Distinguish clearly between:

- order received;
- payment authorised;
- payment captured;
- order processing;
- dispatched;
- delayed;
- delivered;
- partially fulfilled;
- cancelled;
- refunded.

Do not say an order is shipped before carrier handoff is confirmed.

Where timing is uncertain, use a range or explain the dependency rather than inventing certainty.

## Delay and failure copy

A useful delay message contains:

- acknowledgement;
- the affected order or item;
- current known state;
- next action by Sunless;
- realistic update timing;
- customer options where applicable;
- direct support route.

Avoid passive phrases that obscure responsibility.

Prefer:

> Your order has not left our warehouse on schedule. We are checking the affected item and will update you by tomorrow at 4 pm.

Avoid:

> Due to unforeseen circumstances, there may be a delay.

## Apology structure

A meaningful apology should:

1. name the problem;
2. acknowledge the impact;
3. state the corrective action;
4. give a clear next update or resolution;
5. avoid excuses.

Do not combine an apology with unrelated promotion.

## Returns and refunds

State:

- what has been received or approved;
- the amount and currency;
- original payment method where appropriate;
- expected processing time;
- any item or order references;
- what to do if the refund does not appear.

Never promise banking timelines outside operational knowledge.

## Consent and preference language

Consent copy must be specific about the type of communication and must not be hidden inside decorative language.

Preference and unsubscribe controls should be understandable and accessible.

Use separate concepts for:

- essential service messages;
- marketing messages;
- preference changes;
- programme communications;
- support replies.

Do not imply that unsubscribing from marketing prevents order or account messages.

## Terms and conditions

Terms must be:

- close enough to the relevant offer or action;
- legible;
- written in plain language where possible;
- consistent with website and checkout terms;
- reviewed for each market when required.

Critical conditions must not exist only behind a generic `Terms apply` link.

## Account and security copy

Security-related messages must be clear and non-alarming unless urgent action is genuinely required.

Include:

- the action or change;
- date and time where useful;
- device or location only when reliable and appropriate;
- what to do if the customer made the change;
- direct steps if they did not.

Never request passwords, full payment details or sensitive credentials by email.

## VIP and programme changes

When communicating tier, benefit or programme changes:

- use current verified status;
- state effective dates;
- explain retained and changed benefits;
- give review or appeal routes where applicable;
- avoid emotional pressure around downgrade or expiry;
- preserve required service information even when marketing consent is absent.

## Support handoff copy

The email should make ownership visible.

State:

- which team is handling the issue;
- what information has already been received;
- what additional information is needed;
- expected response timing;
- how replies are routed.

Do not make customers repeat information already present in the system.

## Legal footer copy

Footer content should include the approved sender identity, physical address, preference controls, unsubscribe route and any market-specific requirements.

Keep legal copy visually secondary but fully readable.

## QA checklist

Confirm:

- the state is technically accurate;
- dates, amounts, currency and references are correct;
- required action is unmistakable;
- timing is realistic;
- support ownership is clear;
- no promotional content conflicts with the service context;
- consent and unsubscribe language is correct;
- links point to the intended secure destinations;
- plain-text content preserves all essential meaning.
