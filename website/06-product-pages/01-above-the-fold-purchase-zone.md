# Product Page — Above-the-Fold Purchase Zone

## Role

This is the commercial centre of the product page. It must create desire and make the purchase path immediately obvious.

## Desktop architecture

Use a refined two-part composition on an invisible 12-column grid:

- Left 7 columns: gallery and product media
- Right 5 columns: product identity, suitability, selection and purchase

The layout may feel editorial, but the buying controls must remain unmistakable.

## Left gallery

The initial viewport should show one dominant image with supporting thumbnails or a restrained media rail. The first image must clearly show the exact product and packaging. Secondary imagery should demonstrate texture, scale, application and finished skin result.

Do not open with an abstract lifestyle image that hides the item being sold.

## Right purchase panel order

1. Product category or collection eyebrow
2. Product name
3. One-sentence result statement
4. Rating and verified-review count
5. Price, size and unit-price information where required
6. Suitability summary
7. Variant or shade selection
8. Stock status
9. Quantity only when genuinely useful
10. Add to Bag button
11. Payment, dispatch and returns reassurance
12. Shade-match rescue path

## Product statement pattern

Use specific outcome language:

> A buildable violet-based tanning mousse designed to create a deep, neutral-bronze result without excessive warmth.

Avoid:

> Your new secret to the ultimate glow.

## Suitability summary

Show a concise block before the purchase action:

- Best for: medium to deep results
- Undertone: violet / neutralising
- Development: factual product timing
- Finish: natural, satin or other verified finish
- Experience: beginner-friendly, intermediate or advanced

Every value must be supported by approved product data.

## Add to Bag

The primary button should be matte black, full width within the purchase panel and visually dominant after the product name and result statement.

Button states:

- Available: `ADD TO BAG — [PRICE]`
- Adding: `ADDING…`
- Added: `ADDED TO BAG`
- Unavailable selection: disabled with clear instruction
- Sold out: `NOTIFY ME WHEN AVAILABLE`

Never use a vague CTA such as “Continue” at this point.

## Reassurance beneath CTA

Keep concise and factual:

- Dispatch timing
- Delivery threshold or calculation
- Returns policy summary
- Secure payment methods

Do not create a row of oversized trust badges.

## Shade-match rescue

Visitors who are unsure should see a calm secondary route:

> Not sure this is your depth? **Check my match**

This should preserve the current product context and return the user to the page with a recommendation state.

## Sticky behaviour

On desktop, the purchase panel may remain sticky only while the primary gallery is in view. It must stop before editorial sections to prevent visual obstruction.

## Eye path

Product image → product name → visible result → rating → suitability → selection → price → Add to Bag → reassurance.

## Failure modes

Reject layouts that:

- hide the price below the fold;
- make Add to Bag secondary to editorial links;
- use tiny variant controls;
- separate the product image and purchase panel so widely they feel unrelated;
- show multiple equally dominant buttons;
- place essential suitability information only in accordions;
- use floating glass cards or marketplace-style clutter.

## Success test

Within five seconds, a new visitor must know what the product is, what it does, whether it may suit them, what it costs and how to buy it.
