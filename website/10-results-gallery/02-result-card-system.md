# 02 — Result Card System

## Purpose

Result cards are evidence summaries, not decorative image tiles. Each card must allow the visitor to judge relevance before opening the detail page.

## Card anatomy

1. Before-and-after image pair
2. Clear `Before` and `After` labels
3. Result depth label
4. Starting skin profile
5. Product name and shade
6. Coats and development time
7. Verification state where applicable
8. `View Result` action

## Image ratio

Use a consistent paired-image frame. Recommended desktop ratio: approximately 4:5 per image or one combined 8:5 comparison field.

Do not mix portrait, landscape and square cards arbitrarily within the same grid.

## Required metadata

Display a concise subset:

- Starting tone: Fair / Light / Medium / Tan / Deep
- Undertone where confidently known
- Result: Subtle / Sun-kissed / Golden / Deep bronze
- Product and selected shade
- Number of coats
- Development duration

Detailed application and capture information belongs on the result-detail page.

## Visual hierarchy

The outcome image is dominant. Metadata should be legible but secondary. Product information must not be reduced to tiny tertiary text because it is the bridge to purchase.

## Card interaction

- Entire image and title area may link to the detail page.
- Keep one visible text action: `View Result`.
- Hover may reveal a subtle second image state or raise metadata contrast.
- Do not use aggressive zoom, flip-card effects or autoplay video.
- Keyboard focus must be clearly visible.

## Verification labels

Approved labels include:

- Verified customer result
- Sunless studio result
- Campaign result
- Customer-submitted image

These labels describe provenance, not quality ranking.

## Shade-match relevance cue

For a visitor with an active recommendation, a card may show:

> Similar starting profile

or

> Uses your recommended shade

Only show these when the supporting data is present.

## Product link behaviour

The card itself should open the result story. A smaller product link may be included below the method metadata, but it must not compete with `View Result`.

## Mobile card

On mobile:

- one card per row;
- image comparison remains large enough to inspect;
- metadata wraps into two concise lines;
- result and product labels remain visible;
- no hover-dependent information.

## Failure modes

Reject card designs that:

- hide method data until hover;
- use vague labels such as `Amazing glow`;
- apply filters that make every result look identical;
- crop out the relevant skin area;
- mix inconsistent before-and-after framing;
- place promotional badges over the face or skin result;
- turn the grid into a product catalogue.

## Success criteria

A card succeeds when a visitor can answer `Is this result relevant to me?` without opening it.
