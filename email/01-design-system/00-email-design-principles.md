# Email Design Principles

## Purpose

Translate the Sunless visual identity into a channel constrained by inbox width, inconsistent client support and limited attention.

## Core principle

Email should feel like a concise editorial chapter from the Sunless website—not a compressed web page and not a generic promotional template.

## Design priorities

1. Immediate message clarity
2. One dominant action
3. Strong mobile reading
4. Authentic product and people imagery
5. Calm luxury and generous spacing
6. Email-client resilience
7. Accessible live text

## Visual character

- Warm ivory, linen, stone and restrained champagne tones
- Deep charcoal typography
- Matte-black primary CTAs
- Editorial serif headlines where technically safe
- Refined sans-serif body copy
- Natural skin and product photography
- Minimal borders and restrained surfaces

## Composition rules

- Use one central message per email.
- Build a clear opening scene, proof or education, then action.
- Prefer 3–5 meaningful modules over a long stack of interchangeable blocks.
- Vary module scale and density, but preserve one coherent reading path.
- Use images as editorial anchors, not decorative filler.

## Luxury restraint

Luxury in email means precision and editing. Avoid:

- excessive badges
- crowded offer bars
- multiple competing buttons
- decorative gradients
- heavy shadows
- glassmorphism
- long product grids
- constant urgency

## Content hierarchy

A typical hierarchy is:

1. Brand recognition
2. Relevant proposition
3. Product/result image
4. Short explanation or proof
5. Primary CTA
6. Supporting education or products
7. Quiet footer

## Live-text rule

Critical headlines, prices, benefits, CTAs, delivery information and legal copy should remain live HTML text whenever possible. Do not embed essential information into an image.

## Source-image integrity

Approved product, customer and celebrity photographs must be used as source assets. Do not generatively alter faces, bodies, packaging, skin results or product labels.

## Success criteria

An email succeeds visually when it is recognisably Sunless, readable in seconds, attractive with images disabled, strong on mobile and focused on one commercially meaningful next step.
