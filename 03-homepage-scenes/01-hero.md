# Scene 01 — Hero

## Role in the experience

The hero is the cinematic opening scene. It must communicate the brand, desired result, professional authority and next action within five seconds.

This is an evolution of the current hero, not a new concept.

## Commercial objective

Drive qualified visitors into the shade-matching journey while preserving a secondary route for visitors ready to shop bestsellers.

## User questions answered

- What is this brand?
- What outcome does it offer?
- Why should I trust it?
- What should I do next?

## Core message

> Red-carpet colour. Made effortless at home.

Alternative direction for testing:

> A red-carpet tan, matched to your undertone.

## Supporting copy

Use one concise statement connecting Jimmy’s authority to the product benefit:

> Professional self-tan developed by celebrity tanning artist Jimmy Coco, with undertone-aware, buildable formulas designed for natural-looking colour at home.

## CTA hierarchy

### Primary
**MATCH MY SHADE**

Supporting reassurance:

- Takes 60 seconds
- No email required
- Personal recommendation

Only include these statements if operationally accurate.

### Secondary
**Shop Best Sellers**

The secondary action should be outlined or text-led and visibly quieter.

## Composition

Preserve the current recognisable structure:

- Editorial copy anchored to the left third
- Large model occupying the visual centre
- Jimmy Coco visible in the right/background plane applying tan professionally
- Hero product bottle positioned as a premium foreground object
- Luxury tanning mitt included as a tactile product cue
- Integrated navigation above
- Authority strip transitioning into the next chapter

The composition should feel layered rather than divided into obvious columns.

## Architectural silhouette

The hero should be dense, cinematic and asymmetrical. It must contrast with the more contained recommendation engine below.

Suggested silhouette:

- Full-width photographic field
- Left copy block with controlled width
- Central human figure breaking the underlying grid
- Product render overlapping the model/background planes
- Lower trust band embedded into the scene transition

Do not place the model, Jimmy and product into separate cards.

## Eye-tracking path

The intended fixation order is:

1. Model’s face and skin result
2. Hero headline
3. Product bottle
4. Jimmy applying tan
5. Primary CTA
6. Trust proof
7. Secondary CTA

The primary CTA must not compete with the model’s face; it should become the strongest interface object after the emotional image has landed.

## Photography direction

- Cinematic natural daylight
- Warm but neutral skin colour
- No orange colour cast
- Soft directional window light
- Real skin texture retained
- Luxury backstage or private-suite atmosphere
- Controlled highlights on shoulders and face
- Matte, tactile fabrics
- No harsh beauty retouching
- No artificial HDR
- No nightclub glamour

Jimmy should appear genuinely engaged in professional application, not posed as a celebrity endorsement.

## Product rendering

- Packaging must match approved references exactly
- Correct bottle proportions
- Correct cap and pump geometry
- Legible hierarchy of the label, even if final text will be rebuilt later
- Soft grounded shadow
- No impossible floating angle
- Premium material response
- Product scale large enough to register immediately, but not larger than the human subject

## Colour environment

- Warm ivory base
- Cream and champagne textile tones
- Soft stone architectural surfaces
- Natural bronze skin tones
- Deep charcoal typography
- Matte black primary CTA

The hero must not become a monochrome beige wash. Use sufficient tonal separation between model, background, product and copy.

## Typography

- Elegant editorial serif headline
- Approximate desktop size: 64–72px
- Tight but breathable line spacing
- Maximum three lines
- Refined sans-serif supporting copy at approximately 17–19px
- Short line length for fast comprehension
- CTA typography clear and firm, not delicate

## Trust transition

Integrate three proof signals beneath or within the lower edge of the hero:

1. **4.8/5 from 1,642 verified customers**
2. **Featured in Vogue, Cosmopolitan and Harper’s Bazaar**
3. **Created by celebrity tanning artist Jimmy Coco**

These should feel like an elegant editorial colophon, not three detached floating cards.

## Conversion principles

- One dominant primary action
- Immediate effort reassurance
- Authority before product browsing
- Emotional result shown before technical explanation
- Secondary shopping route for high-intent visitors
- No discount-led interruption
- No pop-up visible in the concept

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Ensure headline and body copy maintain strong contrast over photography
- Use a controlled tonal panel or gradient only if required for legibility; it must be subtle and photographic, not decorative
- CTA target should support at least 44px height
- Do not place critical copy over complex skin or hair detail
- Navigation and CTA states must be rebuilt as live UI in production

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Replaces the current hero with a generic bottle-on-pedestal composition
- Makes Jimmy unrecognisable or anatomically incorrect
- Produces orange skin
- Distorts the product label or bottle
- Uses excessive floating objects
- Creates a three-column card layout
- Adds glassmorphism
- Makes the scene look like a hotel-spa stock photo
- Uses tiny copy
- Hides the primary CTA
- Places trust cards awkwardly over the hero
- Uses one flat beige tone with no depth

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- The brand is understood within five seconds
- The existing Sunless homepage remains recognisable
- The model, Jimmy and product form one coherent cinematic story
- The primary CTA is obvious
- The imagery creates desire without sacrificing credibility
- The scene feels premium, warm and professionally art-directed
- The lower trust transition naturally leads into the recommendation engine
