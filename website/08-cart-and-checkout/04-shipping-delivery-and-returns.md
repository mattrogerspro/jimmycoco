# Shipping, Delivery and Returns

## Purpose

Remove uncertainty about when the order will arrive, what delivery costs and what happens if the customer changes their mind or encounters a problem.

## Principles

- Communicate delivery information before payment.
- Use dates or date ranges where operationally reliable.
- Separate dispatch time from carrier transit time.
- Never imply guaranteed delivery unless it is contractually guaranteed.
- Keep returns language concise in the purchase flow and link to the full policy.

## Cart messaging

Show the current delivery status near the order summary:

- estimated delivery cost;
- free-delivery qualification;
- remaining threshold amount when relevant;
- delivery destination assumptions, such as UK mainland, when necessary.

Do not show a precise arrival date before the destination or service is known.

## Checkout delivery methods

Each method should include:

- service name;
- price;
- estimated delivery date or range;
- relevant cutoff or restriction;
- selected state;
- unavailable explanation where applicable.

Example:

> **Standard Delivery — £X**  
> Estimated arrival: Tuesday–Thursday

## Address effects

When the postcode or country changes available services, update the options clearly and preserve valid form input. Explain why a service disappears.

## Free-delivery threshold

The threshold must be based on the qualifying merchandise subtotal and update immediately when cart contents or promotions change.

Approved language:

> Add £X more to qualify for free standard delivery.

Avoid pressure language such as “Don’t miss out” or “Hurry.”

## Returns reassurance

Near checkout commitment, provide a concise factual summary such as:

> Returns accepted within the stated policy period on eligible unopened products.

The exact wording must reflect the approved legal policy. Hygiene exclusions, opened-product restrictions and international-return conditions must not be hidden.

## Delivery errors

When a delivery option cannot be calculated:

- preserve the address;
- explain the issue in plain language;
- provide a retry;
- provide a support route;
- never default silently to a more expensive method.

## Order confirmation

Repeat:

- delivery address;
- selected service;
- estimated arrival range;
- tracking expectations;
- contact route for corrections.

## Accessibility

Delivery-method controls require visible labels, sufficient hit areas and programmatically associated descriptions. Dynamic recalculation must be announced without moving focus unexpectedly.

## Success criteria

Before payment, the customer understands the available delivery service, expected cost and realistic arrival expectation, with no material surprise introduced after commitment.
