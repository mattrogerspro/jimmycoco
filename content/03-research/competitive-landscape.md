# Competitive landscape

**The finding: the UK category is empty.** This is the whole strategy. Everything
in `00-strategy/` follows from what's below.

Established across roughly 50 searches on operational spray tan questions a UK
salon owner would actually ask.

---

## Who actually ranks

### SalonGeek — a forum whose relevant threads run 2008–2018

Holds a **top-three position on roughly half of all operational queries**:

- tans per litre — **4 of the top 6 results**
- compressor choice — **5 of the top 8**
- COSHH · solution shelf life · patch test policy · bridal pricing · mobile
  profitability

It ranks because nothing else exists. Not because it's good, not because it's
current — because the space is vacant. A forum post from 2011 is the best
available answer to a question thousands of UK businesses ask every year.

That is the opportunity stated as plainly as it can be.

### Where SalonGeek doesn't rank, US brands do

Sjolie · Norvell · ArtesianTan · SprayTanClass — in dollars, under US
regulation, with US wage and insurance assumptions baked into every figure.
Useless to a UK reader, and actively misleading on anything regulatory.

### Sienna X — the only UK brand attempting professional content

Roughly **170–190 posts**, and most of it is consumer tan-tips relabelled for
therapists.

The flagship pricing article,
`sienna-x.co.uk/how-much-should-i-charge-for-a-spray-tan/`, is dated **October
2017**, runs about **450 words**, contains **one number**, and ends by asking
you to phone.

That page is the incumbent for the highest-commercial-intent query in the
category. It is beatable by a very large margin, by anyone willing to do the
arithmetic.

### Trade bodies are absent

**BABTAC's** most recent indexed asset on the subject is a **2016 PDF**.
**NHBF** has nothing spray-tan-specific. Neither is defending this ground.

### Templates are owned by Etsy

Consultation forms, consent forms, aftercare cards — sold by Etsy sellers.
**No free, GDPR-aware, UK-compliant forms library exists anywhere.** Not from a
brand, not from a trade body, not from an insurer.

### Every calculator is in dollars

- Sjolie's Glow & Grow — `sjoliespraytan.com/glow-and-grow/`
- `costpertan.com`

**No sterling equivalent exists for anything.** A free, ungated, £-denominated
spray tan profit calculator would be the only one.

---

## What formats actually win here

**Demonstrably wins**

- **Free, ungated calculators**
- **Downloadable templates**
- **Long-form pillars with real numbers.** The proof of concept: the one UK page
  carrying actual £ figures — `beautyschool.co.uk` — **ranks #1 for several
  queries**. Not because it's a strong domain. Because it has numbers in it.
- **Comparison tables**

**Demonstrably doesn't**

- **Short brand-tied listicles** — the Sienna X format
- **Gated content** — MineTan's registration wall is invisible to search *and*
  to AI retrieval. An asset behind a form is an asset that doesn't exist as far
  as either is concerned.

---

## White space, ranked by gap × value

1. **UK unit economics in £** — nothing exists
2. **UK licensing and compliance** — nothing current exists
3. **Insurance explained** — nothing exists
4. **Templates and forms** — owned by Etsy, not by any brand
5. **Seasonality — the UK tanning year** — **zero content anywhere**
6. **Retail attach** — nothing tanning-specific
7. **No-shows, for tanning specifically** — general salon content only, despite
   tanning having the worst rate of any category
8. **Inclusivity and darker skin tones** — badly served everywhere
9. **Neutral training buyer's guide** — everything is sold by the trainer

Pillars 1 and 2 launch first because they sit on gaps 1–4.

---

## What this means for us

We are not competing for these positions. We are the first entrant. The
practical consequence is that **quality bar is set by a 2011 forum post and a
2017 blog post** — which means the winning move is simply to do the work
properly, once, with sources.

The risk isn't a competitor. It's us publishing thin content and joining the
noise. `/articles` being live, indexed and empty is already a small version of
that problem.
