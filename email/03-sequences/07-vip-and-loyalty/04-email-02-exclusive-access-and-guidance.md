# Email 02 — Exclusive Access and Guidance

## Role

Deliver one genuinely valuable VIP benefit through relevant access, expert guidance or enhanced service.

This message must prove that VIP status changes the experience in a meaningful way. It must not be a generic campaign with a VIP label added to the subject line.

## Trigger

Send when an approved VIP benefit becomes available, such as:

- early access to a new product or collection;
- priority access to limited inventory;
- an expert-led shade or routine consultation;
- a private product education session;
- a verified invitation to an event;
- a service enhancement that is operationally available.

Do not send simply because the programme calendar contains an empty campaign slot.

## Primary objective

Help the customer use a real VIP benefit that is relevant to their history, preferences or current lifecycle stage.

## Message hierarchy

1. Clear explanation of the benefit
2. Why it may be relevant to this customer
3. Any real access window, limit or service condition
4. One primary action
5. A simple support route

## Access rules

Any exclusivity statement must be true and auditable.

Before send, confirm:

- who is eligible;
- when access starts and ends;
- whether inventory or appointment capacity is reserved;
- whether the benefit is market-specific;
- whether the customer has already used it;
- whether the destination page enforces the same eligibility rules;
- whether customer support can honour the promise.

Avoid vague language such as “just for you” when the same message is broadly available.

## Guidance-led route

When the benefit is expert guidance rather than product access, the email may offer:

- a personalised routine review;
- shade-match support;
- application troubleshooting;
- product-switch guidance;
- help preparing for a specific event or desired result.

Do not fabricate one-to-one availability. If support is asynchronous or group-based, state that accurately.

## Product-access route

When the benefit is early or priority access:

- show only approved products included in the access event;
- validate product, variant, price, currency and stock immediately before send;
- do not imply guaranteed availability unless inventory is truly reserved;
- state access timing plainly;
- use one direct CTA such as `Explore early access`.

## Subject-line territories

- Your VIP access is now open
- A first look, reserved for our VIP community
- Your next Sunless consultation is available
- Early access to the latest Sunless release

Avoid exaggerated exclusivity, false scarcity and status-heavy language.

## Visual structure

1. VIP programme identifier
2. Editorial benefit introduction
3. Dominant product, service or access module
4. Clear access details
5. Primary CTA
6. Support or preference link
7. Footer

The design should feel elevated through restraint, not through excessive gold, badges or ornamental decoration.

## Personalisation

Safe personalisation may use:

- verified VIP tier;
- known product or routine history;
- completed shade-match result;
- approved category preference;
- market and currency;
- prior benefit use.

Do not infer sensitive characteristics or use unverified behavioural assumptions.

## Required fallbacks

If the intended benefit becomes unavailable:

- replace it only with a genuinely equivalent approved benefit;
- otherwise delay or suppress the email;
- do not silently route VIP customers to a standard campaign experience.

## Exit and handoff

- Purchase transfers to post-purchase education.
- Cart creation transfers to cart abandonment when eligible.
- A support request transfers to service ownership.
- A shade-match completion may transfer to shade-match follow-up.
- Consent withdrawal suppresses all marketing immediately.

## Success criteria

Measure benefit use, qualified sessions, consultation starts, product engagement, purchase, service satisfaction and complaint rate. The email succeeds when it deepens usefulness and trust, not merely when it produces a click.
