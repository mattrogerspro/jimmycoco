# AI Production Principles

## Purpose

Define the operating principles for using AI within Sunless email production.

## Principle 1 — Approved truth comes first

AI may transform approved information into drafts, layouts, crops, prompts or variants. It may not become the source of product, customer, programme, offer, claim or policy truth.

## Principle 2 — Human accountability remains explicit

Every production asset and message must have a named human owner and approver. Approval cannot be inferred from an AI score or generated checklist.

## Principle 3 — Separate generation from delivery

AI tools must not send emails, modify consent, change eligibility, alter suppression or publish production templates directly. Generated output enters a controlled review and deployment workflow.

## Principle 4 — Preserve original assets

Approved product photography, packaging, logos, customer results and celebrity imagery must remain faithful to the source.

AI must not:

- change a person’s face, body, skin tone or identifying features;
- retouch or regenerate celebrity photography;
- alter product colour, packaging, typography or proportions;
- fabricate product combinations or shades;
- create a false before-and-after result;
- imply that generated imagery is documentary evidence.

## Principle 5 — Use AI where uncertainty is low

Appropriate uses include:

- drafting from structured briefs;
- controlled rewrites;
- summarising approved source material;
- generating non-documentary decorative backgrounds;
- producing layout concepts;
- adapting approved copy for length;
- auditing against defined rules;
- preparing alt-text drafts;
- generating test variants with one controlled change.

Higher-risk uses require stricter review or should not be used.

## Principle 6 — Label synthetic work internally

Every generated or materially AI-assisted asset must be identifiable in production records, even when no public label is required.

Record:

- model or tool;
- date;
- prompt version;
- source assets;
- transformations;
- reviewer;
- approval state;
- final export location.

## Principle 7 — Never hide missing information

When source data is incomplete, the output must state `[SOURCE REQUIRED]`, use a pre-approved fallback or stop.

AI must not guess:

- customer names;
- product variants;
- prices;
- stock;
- shade matches;
- offer dates;
- delivery times;
- review text;
- celebrity use;
- programme tier;
- consent or eligibility.

## Principle 8 — Optimise for customer trust

AI-assisted content must remain calm, legible, accurate and useful. Conversion does not justify pressure, false precision, invented intimacy or misleading visual proof.

## Principle 9 — Keep outputs reproducible

Prompts, inputs and versions must be stored so an approved output can be recreated or investigated.

## Principle 10 — Final-channel QA is mandatory

An image or copy draft that looks correct outside the email is not approved. It must be reviewed inside the actual responsive template, with final data, links, dark mode, mobile rendering, plain text and client constraints.

## Prohibited uses

AI must not autonomously:

- determine audience eligibility;
- override lifecycle precedence;
- bypass consent or suppression;
- invent or approve claims;
- produce synthetic celebrity endorsement;
- alter documentary customer-result imagery;
- send production messages;
- approve its own output;
- modify legal or policy language without review;
- create personalised content from inferred sensitive traits.

## Release standard

AI-assisted production is acceptable only when the final output is indistinguishable in quality, accuracy and accountability from carefully directed human production—and remains fully traceable.
