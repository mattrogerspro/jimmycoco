# Lifecycle Tone by Customer State

## Purpose

Define how the Sunless email voice changes according to customer intent, confidence, purchase state and service context while remaining recognisably one brand.

## Constant voice traits

Across every state, Sunless remains:

- calm;
- expert;
- precise;
- warm;
- restrained;
- respectful of customer choice.

Tone may adapt. Brand character must not fragment.

## Prospect and new subscriber

### Customer need

Orientation, relevance and a reason to trust the brand.

### Tone

Inviting, clear and lightly editorial.

### Copy behaviour

- introduce one useful idea at a time;
- explain what makes the method different;
- avoid assuming prior knowledge;
- offer guidance before pressure.

### Avoid

- excessive welcome enthusiasm;
- immediate discount dependency;
- long brand autobiography;
- unexplained tanning terminology.

## Shade-match participant

### Customer need

Confidence that the recommendation is understandable and suitable.

### Tone

Diagnostic, reassuring and transparent.

### Copy behaviour

- explain why the recommendation fits;
- distinguish primary and alternative options;
- acknowledge uncertainty honestly;
- connect the result to application guidance.

### Avoid

- fake scientific precision;
- absolute guarantees;
- implying one skin tone is more desirable than another;
- overwhelming the result with products.

## Browsing customer

### Customer need

Help deciding whether to continue without feeling watched.

### Tone

Relevant and low-pressure.

### Copy behaviour

- resume the product or category context;
- answer likely objections;
- add one useful proof or method detail;
- make returning effortless.

### Avoid

- `We noticed you…` surveillance language;
- repeated urgency;
- accusing the customer of abandoning anything.

## Basket customer

### Customer need

Clarity, reassurance and a simple route back.

### Tone

Direct, helpful and commercially focused.

### Copy behaviour

- identify the basket accurately;
- clarify shade, usage, delivery or returns where useful;
- preserve the selected variant;
- use one dominant return-to-basket CTA.

### Avoid

- guilt;
- invented scarcity;
- countdown pressure without a real expiry;
- unnecessary cross-sell before the basket is resolved.

## First-time purchaser

### Customer need

Reassurance that the order and application will go smoothly.

### Tone

Service-led, anticipatory and supportive.

### Copy behaviour

- confirm what happens next;
- provide timely preparation and application guidance;
- reduce avoidable mistakes;
- invite support without creating anxiety.

### Avoid

- premature review requests;
- immediate promotional overload;
- assuming the product has arrived or been used.

## Active customer

### Customer need

Useful continuity and increasing confidence.

### Tone

Familiar but not overly intimate.

### Copy behaviour

- build on known products and preferences;
- introduce compatible routine steps;
- recognise expertise gained through use;
- keep recommendations concise.

### Avoid

- repeating beginner education indefinitely;
- treating every purchase as a first purchase;
- overusing the customer’s name.

## Replenishment customer

### Customer need

A timely reminder without a false assumption that the product is empty.

### Tone

Practical, light and convenient.

### Copy behaviour

- frame timing as an estimate;
- reference the relevant product;
- provide direct reorder access;
- allow the customer to defer or ignore naturally.

### Avoid

- `You are running out` unless confirmed;
- false urgency;
- implying a fixed usage pattern.

## Lapsed customer

### Customer need

A credible reason to reconsider without being judged.

### Tone

Respectful, fresh and non-defensive.

### Copy behaviour

- acknowledge distance indirectly;
- lead with useful change, guidance or relevance;
- reduce friction to return;
- allow graceful disengagement.

### Avoid

- `We miss you` clichés as the entire proposition;
- guilt or disappointment;
- aggressive discount escalation;
- exposing lapse classifications.

## VIP customer

### Customer need

Recognition, reliable access and genuinely useful benefits.

### Tone

Considered, discreet and service-led.

### Copy behaviour

- communicate status accurately;
- prioritise access, expertise and utility;
- explain benefit terms clearly;
- recognise milestones without spend-shaming.

### Avoid

- excessive flattery;
- theatrical exclusivity;
- status pressure;
- unverified benefit or availability claims.

## Complaint, support or delivery-risk state

### Customer need

Resolution and confidence that the issue is being handled.

### Tone

Plain, accountable and calm.

### Copy behaviour

- state what is known;
- explain the next action and owner;
- provide realistic timing;
- give a direct support route.

### Avoid

- promotional content;
- defensive language;
- vague apologies without action;
- promises outside operational control.

## Tone conflict rule

The highest-priority active customer state owns the tone.

A delivery problem overrides promotional, replenishment, win-back and VIP language until resolved. Service accuracy always takes precedence over marketing continuity.
