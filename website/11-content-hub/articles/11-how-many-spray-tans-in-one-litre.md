# How Many Spray Tans Are in One Litre of Jimmy Coco Solution?

The Jimmy Coco one-litre professional solution is rated at **approximately 28 full-body tans** per bottle, which works out at about 36 ml per service.

That figure already allows for real salon use — gun priming, overspray, cup residue, coverage differences and artist technique — which is why it sits below a straight division of the bottle.

> Product figures come from Copy system › Professional Product Facts (`email/04-copy-system/14-professional-product-facts.md`). Do not restate them from another article.

## The simple calculation

A litre contains 1,000 ml.

$$
\text{Theoretical services} = \frac{1{,}000\text{ ml}}{\text{ml used per service}}
$$

| Average solution used | Theoretical services per litre |
|---:|---:|
| 30 ml | 33.3 |
| 32 ml | 31.25 |
| **36 ml** | **27.8 — the approved figure** |
| 40 ml | 25 |

The approved figure of approximately 28 therefore sits close to a 36 ml service. It accommodates normal service variation instead of pretending every millilitre reaches the client.

## Why “less than 35 ml” matters

Using extra solution does not automatically create a better or darker result. It can indicate:

- a spray pattern that is too wide or poorly controlled;
- excessive distance from the client;
- repeated passes over the same area;
- an incorrect gun setting;
- leakage or avoidable cup residue;
- an artist trying to create depth through quantity rather than solution choice.

Depth should begin with the correct Jimmy Coco solution:

- [Laguna](/products/laguna-ltr-professional-spray-light-medium) for Light/Medium;
- [Malibu](/products/malibu-1-ltr-professional-spray-medium-dark) for Medium/Dark;
- [Malibu](/products/laguna-ltr-professional-spray-light-medium-copy) for Dark/Extra Dark.

The artist then follows the approved technique and development plan.

Use the live trade price, not a number copied permanently into an evergreen article.

$$
\text{Solution cost per service} = \frac{\text{Net bottle cost}}{\text{Usable services per bottle}}

For illustration only, if a one-litre bottle costs £60 before any account-specific treatment:

## Track actual usage

Do not wait until a bottle is empty to discover that yield is low. Record:

- opening volume or bottle count;
- solution and batch;
- services completed;
- approximate ml used per service;
- artist and equipment setup;
- spills, priming or test sprays;
- remaining volume at stock count.

Review the rolling average by artist and solution. A single high-usage service may have a reasonable explanation; a consistent gap is a training or equipment signal.

## Protect the yield by storing solution correctly

Current Jimmy Coco professional directions state that the solution should be stored in a cool, dark environment away from elevated heat and sunlight, and should be at room temperature before use. Shake well before use.

Poor storage can turn an apparent cost saving into avoidable waste. Use first-in, first-out stock rotation and follow batch, opening and expiry controls required by the business and product.

## Do not reduce quality to hit a number

The figure of approximately 28 is a planning figure, not a quota that overrides complete, even coverage. Client size, service scope, equipment and technique affect use. The objective is controlled consistency inside approved directions.

If usage is above plan, diagnose the cause:

1. Check equipment and leaks.
2. Observe spray distance, overlap and speed.
3. Measure actual cup fill and residue.
4. Compare by artist and service type.
5. Confirm the chosen depth comes from the solution, not extra passes.
6. Retrain before cutting coverage.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 35 ml the exact amount for every client?

No. The current direction says approximately 28 full-body tans per litre, about 36 ml per service. Treat these as approved operating guidance, not a universal fixed dose.

### Why does 1,000 divided by 35 equal nearly 29, but the range starts at 25?

Real services include priming, overspray, residue and variation. The published range is designed for practical planning rather than perfect laboratory recovery.

### Should I use more Malibu to create an extra-dark result?

No. Malibu is already the Dark/Extra Dark option. Follow the approved application and development instructions instead of relying on oversaturation.

## Plan from evidence, not instinct

Start with approximately 28 full-body services per one-litre Jimmy Coco bottle, track actual ml use and investigate any consistent gap. The result is more predictable stock ordering, clearer service economics and more consistent technique.
