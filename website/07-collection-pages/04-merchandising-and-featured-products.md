# Merchandising and Featured Products

## Purpose

Merchandising controls which products receive attention and in what order. It must balance customer relevance, commercial priority, stock health and brand storytelling without disguising paid or arbitrary placement as personal recommendation.

## Governing rule

The first product row should help the greatest number of customers make progress. It should not simply display the highest-margin products.

## Recommended ordering logic

A sensible default order may consider:

1. Collection relevance
2. Customer suitability breadth
3. Best-selling performance
4. Review strength and volume
5. Stock availability
6. Seasonal relevance
7. Newness
8. Strategic commercial priority

The exact weighting should be documented and reviewable.

## Featured-product patterns

### Jimmy’s Pick
Use only where Jimmy has explicitly selected or endorsed the product for a stated reason.

Include a concise reason:

> Jimmy’s pick for controllable medium-to-deep colour.

### Best Seller
Use where supported by recent sales data and an agreed time window.

### New
Use for a defined launch period. Do not leave products marked New indefinitely.

### Award or press recognition
Use only with approved claim wording and evidence.

## Editorial interruption module

An editorial module may appear after the first or second product row. It should answer a buying question rather than act as decoration.

Approved purposes:

- explain mousse versus gradual lotion;
- show how development time changes the result;
- introduce Jimmy’s three-step method;
- show a verified real-skin result;
- guide first-time customers;
- connect preparation or finishing products to the routine.

Recommended composition:

- one dominant image or product grouping;
- one concise headline;
- 30–60 words of supporting copy;
- one clear link;
- visual contrast from the product grid without becoming a second hero.

## Product pinning

Pinned products must remain relevant to the active collection and filters. When a pinned product does not match a customer’s selected criteria, it should not be forced into the filtered results.

## Stock-aware merchandising

- Do not heavily promote products with unstable availability.
- Move sold-out products down unless waiting-list demand is strategically useful.
- Preserve product URLs and discovery for temporarily unavailable products.
- Clearly label low-stock or preorder states only when accurate.
- Provide a suitable alternative when a key product is unavailable.

## Bundles and routines

Bundles may appear within collections when they solve a clear need. Explain:

- what is included;
- the role of each product;
- who the routine suits;
- whether there is a genuine saving;
- whether products can be purchased separately.

Do not use oversized bundles to make entry into the brand feel expensive or confusing.

## Personalisation

Personalised ordering may use declared shade-match results, prior purchases or current browsing intent. It must remain understandable and reversible.

Preferred language:

> Recommended from your shade match.

Avoid opaque claims such as:

> Chosen by AI just for you.

## Promotional discipline

- Avoid multiple sale banners inside the grid.
- Do not attach a badge to every product.
- Keep promotional pricing transparent.
- Do not reorder products so aggressively that returning customers lose orientation.
- Preserve premium restraint during promotional periods.

## Measurement

Track:

- product-card click-through by position;
- filter usage;
- featured-module engagement;
- quick-add conversion;
- product-page conversion by entry position;
- out-of-stock exits;
- bundle attachment rate;
- shade-guidance usage.

Position alone should never be treated as proof that a product is genuinely preferred; exposure must be considered.

## Failure modes

Reject merchandising that:

- places irrelevant products at the top for margin alone;
- uses unsupported labels;
- interrupts the grid before customers see products;
- hides poor stock health;
- repeats the same editorial module on every collection;
- converts the page into a campaign collage;
- makes sponsored or manually promoted placement appear personalised.

## Success criteria

Merchandising succeeds when the range feels edited, customers quickly encounter appropriate products and commercial priorities remain honest and useful.
