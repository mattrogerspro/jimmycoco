# 02 — Questionnaire Structure and Question Design

## Governing rule

Every question must alter the recommendation, the selected variant, the application guidance or the confidence level. Remove questions that exist only to make the consultation feel longer or more sophisticated.

## Recommended sequence

### Question 1 — Desired result

Ask what the customer wants to achieve:

- Subtle warmth
- Natural sun-kissed colour
- Deeper bronze
- Event-ready intensity
- Not sure

Use calibrated result photography where approved assets exist. Do not use unrealistic orange-to-brown colour swatches.

### Question 2 — Natural skin depth

Use plain-language ranges supported by representative, consistently lit skin references. Include guidance that this is about natural, untanned skin.

Suggested answer structure:

- Very fair
- Fair to light
- Medium
- Tan to deep
- Deep
- Not sure

Exact ranges must be reviewed against the product system and inclusive asset library.

### Question 3 — Undertone confidence

Ask only if undertone materially changes product or shade guidance.

Possible answers:

- Cool or rosy
- Neutral
- Warm or golden
- Olive
- Not sure

Provide a brief help layer using practical cues rather than jewellery myths presented as fact.

### Question 4 — Application area

- Face
- Body
- Face and body

This determines product format, amount and routine rather than merely changing marketing copy.

### Question 5 — Desired speed and development style

- Gradual and highly controllable
- Develops over several hours
- Faster result
- No preference

Only present timings that match real products and approved instructions.

### Question 6 — Experience and control

- First time or cautious
- Comfortable with self-tan
- Experienced and seeking deeper colour

This can influence formula, depth, coat guidance and strength of application advice.

### Question 7 — Main concern

Allow one primary concern, with optional multi-select only if logic supports it:

- Looking orange
- Streaking or patchiness
- Dry or uneven fading
- Transfer
- Choosing too dark
- Not enough colour
- No major concern

Concern answers should modify reassurance and routine guidance, not manufacture unsupported product claims.

## Question design rules

- One clear decision per screen.
- Four to six answers is usually ideal.
- Answer labels must be understandable without tooltips.
- Use imagery only when it improves accuracy.
- Never rely on colour alone to communicate selection.
- Provide “Not sure” where ambiguity is normal.
- Do not request race, ethnicity, medical skin condition or other sensitive data unless legally and operationally justified; this system should not require it.
- Do not ask for email, date of birth or phone number as questionnaire inputs.

## Answer-card anatomy

Each answer card may include:

- short title;
- one supporting sentence where needed;
- accurate visual reference;
- clear selected state;
- accessible radio or checkbox semantics.

Avoid long paragraphs, decorative badges and ambiguous lifestyle imagery.

## Progress model

Use a simple label such as **“Question 3 of 6”** plus a restrained progress line. If branching changes the number of questions, use **“About halfway”** or another honest qualitative cue rather than a misleading fixed denominator.

## Back and change behaviour

Customers may move back freely. When an earlier answer invalidates a later branch, explain that the affected answer has been reset. Do not silently preserve incompatible data.

## Copy tone

Questions should sound calm and professional:

> What kind of result would you like?

Not:

> What’s your ultimate glow vibe?

## Validation

Selection should usually advance only after a deliberate Continue action, preventing accidental progression and supporting review. Auto-advance may be tested for simple binary questions but must not compromise accessibility.

## Success criteria

The sequence feels short, relevant and easy. Customers understand every answer option and can complete the journey even when unsure about skin depth or undertone.
