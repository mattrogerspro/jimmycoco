The five things that matter

  • They are a trade marketer, not a DTC brand. 215 of 219 ads send traffic to boots.com, superdrug.com or amazon.co.uk — the other four to TikTok Shop. Not one goes to their own site. They own no first-party customer.
  • Creator-whitelisted video is the whole engine. 79% video, overwhelmingly vertical UGC, much of it running from the creator's own page rather than the brand's.
  • One always-on catalogue layer underneath. A single dynamic (DCO) ad set has run continuously since 16 Dec 2025 — 47 instances, still live today.
  • Their calendar is the retailer's promo calendar, not their own. Headlines are literally "Buy one get one half price", "Save 1/3 at Superdrug".
  • Germany is a live test. A 31-day, four-creator burst in Jun–Jul 2026 racked up ~1.13m reach across four creatives — and EU law forced them to publish the targeting.

How to read this

  • No interest targeting at all. Country + age + gender, nothing else. They are handing audience-finding entirely to Meta's algorithm and letting creative do the targeting.
  • The hard gender filter is a choice, not a default. Meta's own guidance is to leave gender open; St. Moriz deliberately restricted to Women, which usually signals they tested open and it lost.
  • 18–34, not 18–44. They have written off the 35+ self-tan buyer entirely in this test.
  • Delivery skewed older than the target. 64% of disclosed reach landed in 25–34, not 18–24 — the algorithm found its cheapest delivery at the top of their age band.

Reasonable inference for their UK setup

The German test was almost certainly a clone of the UK playbook — same creative logic, same retailer routing, same creator format. So the UK setup is very likely:

  • United Kingdom, country level, no geo narrowing
  • Women, 18–34
  • Broad audience with Advantage+ expansion; no interest stacks
  • Advantage+ placements on default
  • Optimising for link clicks or landing-page views — see the next tab for why

Flagged as inference. The four German data points are observed fact; the UK mapping is a judgement call.

Germany is a beachhead, not a one-off Four creators, two retail chains (Müller and Rossmann), 31 days, 1.13m summed reach, all in German with proper Werbung/Anzeige disclosure in the copy. That is a funded market-entry test, not a spillover. Expect a bigger German push in summer 2027 — and note they picked the two drugstore chains that mirror Boots and Superdrug exactly.

What they optimise for

The Ad Library doesn't publish objectives or bid strategy. But when every ad routes to a third-party retailer, the set of things they can optimise for shrinks to almost nothing — and that constraint is the most exploitable thing about them.

The core deduction You cannot fire a Meta purchase pixel on boots.com. St. Moriz has no pixel, no Conversions API and no purchase event on any of its 219 destinations. Therefore they physically cannot run purchase-optimised campaigns. What's left is Link Clicks, Landing Page Views, ThruPlay or Reach.

What they are really buying

Read commercially rather than technically, the campaign is doing three jobs, in this order:

  • 1. Defending shelf space. Demonstrable digital support is what keeps a brand in Boots' and Superdrug's ranging decisions. Half of this spend is a listing-retention cost.
  • 2. Amplifying the retailer's promo. "Buy one get one half price", "Save 1/3" — the ad is the promo's megaphone, timed to the retailer's mechanic.
  • 3. Cheap reach among 18–34 women. Creator UGC at scale, letting the algorithm find volume.

Notably absent: acquiring a customer, capturing an email, building a repeat-purchase base, or defending a price point.

Copy is short — median body length is around 17 words, and 41% of creatives run under 15. Emojis in essentially every ad. No substantiation-heavy claims and no pricing in body copy; the offer always sits in the headline.

The one genuinely clever thing they do The headline carries the retailer and the commercial job, while the body carries the creator's voice with no sell at all. One real pair: headline "Shop at Boots 💖", body "My go-to body oil ✨" — that's the entire ad. Another: headline "Buy one get one half price 😍", body "My secret to bronzed, glowy skin ✨". The ad reads as content and converts as promo. That separation of jobs between headline and body is worth copying wholesale.

Your playbook

Eight moves for Sunless by Jimmy Coco, drawn from what the data actually supports. Ordered by how quickly they pay back.

Move 01 · Positioning

Take the premium lane while it's empty

Tan-Luxe — the only prestige self-tan advertiser in the set — has zero live ads. St. Moriz competes entirely on promo mechanics ("half price", "1/3 off"). Nobody in this category is currently buying Meta attention with a craft-and-expertise story.

Do: Lead with the professional method and Jimmy's credentials. Never match their discount language — your brand voice already rules it out, and the data says the discount lane is crowded and the expertise lane is vacant.
Move 02 · Targeting

Start where their disclosure points, then widen where they won't

Their tested setup is country + Women + 18–34, no interests. 64% of disclosed delivery landed in 25–34. But a premium price point and a professional-method story should sell older than a high-street drugstore mousse.

Do: Run UK · Women · broad, but set 25–54, not 18–34. Their disclosed age gate is tuned to mass-market economics. Test 35–54 as its own ad set — that's audience they have explicitly excluded and nobody in this set is bidding against you for it.
Move 03 · Measurement

Weaponise the one thing they don't have

219 ads, zero purchase events. St. Moriz optimises for clicks because clicks are all they can see. You sell direct — you can run purchase-optimised campaigns with Conversions API and let Meta's algorithm do work theirs never will.

Do: Get server-side CAPI live with purchase, add-to-cart and view-content events before spending meaningfully. Optimise for purchase from day one, not traffic. This single structural advantage is worth more than any creative idea on this page.
Move 04 · Creative

Copy their headline/body split, not their aesthetic

Their sharpest mechanic: the headline carries the commercial job ("Shop at Boots 💖") while the body carries a creator's unsold voice ("My go-to body oil ✨"). The ad reads as content and converts as commerce.

Do: Body copy under 15 words, first-person, no sell. Headline carries the specific action in your voice — "Book the Sunset spray", "Shop the Tinted Soufflé" — never "Learn More" or "Discover".
Move 05 · PRO channel

Run the Sienna X model, not the St. Moriz model, for salon acquisition

Sienna X Professional runs 132 ads from just 9 creatives, median 39-day flights, 28 live today, with LEARN_MORE and SIGN_UP CTAs to their own site. That is patient B2B lead-gen for salon accounts — exactly the shape of your PRO stockist ambition.

Do: A separate PRO campaign — small creative set, long flights, lead-gen objective, landing on your pro page. Different creative language entirely: margin, booth economics, client retention, treatment time. Not glow.
Move 06 · Calendar

Own January, and start summer in May

They launched nothing new between 16 Dec 2025 and 4 Feb 2026 — seven weeks dark. And 35% of their annual output landed in June–July, meaning auction prices in your category spike exactly then.

Do: Fund a January "reset" push into vacated auction space. Then front-run their summer — build audience and creative learnings across May so you're in optimised delivery by the time they flood June.
Move 07 · Creative volume

Match Coco & Eve's velocity, not St. Moriz's

St. Moriz: 34 creatives / 13 months. Coco & Eve: 87. The DTC brand can afford more because it can measure and kill fast. Once CAPI is live, creative volume becomes your compounding advantage.

Do: Target 6–8 new creative concepts a month, 2-week evaluation windows. You already have an AI generation pipeline in the repo — that's the cheapest route to the volume the winners run.
Move 08 · Where they're vulnerable

The gaps nobody in the category is covering

Across 1,249 ads from five brands, some obvious territory is untouched.

Unclaimed:
  • 35+ / mature skin. Explicitly age-gated out by St. Moriz. Zero competitor creative addresses it.
  • Event-driven urgency. Weddings, red carpet, holidays as a dated occasion — Jimmy's actual origin story, and nobody is running it.
  • Fixing a bad tan. Streaks, patches, orange tone. Their strongest educational creative (D JONES on face-to-body matching) was deployed 8 times, more than almost any other single video — yet they built only a handful of teaching ads in 13 months.
  • Salon-to-home bridge. "What the professional actually does differently." Sienna X gestures at it; nobody has owned it.
One caution before you spend Everything above is competitor intelligence, not a performance forecast. Ad Library shows what brands ran, never what worked — placement counts are a proxy for belief, not results. Treat these as hypotheses to test at small budget, not as a plan to fund at scale.

Method & caveats

How this was built

  • Meta Ad Library queried directly for page 234479433234119, active + inactive, UK and then all other markets.
  • 219 UK ad records and 16 German records extracted with full metadata: dates, platforms, display format, CTA type, destination URL, body copy, headline, collation count.
  • EU transparency panels opened individually on all four German creatives to read the disclosed location, age, gender and reach data.
  • Four comparator brands pulled the same way: Bondi Sands (378860292512613), Coco & Eve (1627356637522836), Tan-Luxe (1644750522420228), Sienna X Professional (882202738468251).
  • Data pulled 10 August 2026. Ad Library is a live surface — figures will drift.

What is observed vs. inferred

Observed fact — all ad counts, dates, formats, CTAs, destination domains, platforms, copy and creator pages, for St. Moriz and for all four comparator brands; the page like count and category; and the four German targeting disclosures with their reach and age figures.

Inferred, clearly flagged — the UK targeting setup (mapped from the German test), the campaign objective and bid strategy, the co-op funding interpretation of the Boots/Superdrug switch, and the creative-template groupings.

Not available at all — spend, impressions, CPM, CTR, ROAS, campaign structure, audience sizes, or any UK targeting parameters. The Ad Library does not publish commercial performance data for non-political ads, and the UK is outside the EU transparency regime.

Known limitations

  • "Uses" is a proxy, not a metric. It counts ad instances sharing one creative — a signal of how widely a creative was deployed, not how it performed.
  • The catalogue row bundles many rendered ads. All 47 DCO instances share one templated body ({{product.brand}}) in the underlying data, but each renders different live copy and imagery from the product feed. So "34 unique creatives" counts 33 fixed creatives plus one dynamic layer — the true rendered-creative count is higher.
  • Ads with no impressions are omitted by Meta, so very short or paused tests may be invisible.
  • Paid influencer content posted organically (not run as an ad) does not appear here. St. Moriz's real creator footprint is larger than 5 partners.
  • Google, TikTok, retail media and Amazon Ads are entirely outside this view. Four ads pointing at TikTok Shop hint at TikTok activity this teardown cannot see.
  • The German reach rows don't sum to the headline reach. Meta labels reach as estimated and rounds the breakdown independently; residuals run from −2,814 to +9,371 per ad. Treat the age split as directional, not exact.
  • Summed reach is not unique people. The 1.13m German figure adds four per-creative reach numbers whose audiences overlap. Unique reach was lower.
