# Email Frequency and Contact Policy

## Purpose

Protect long-term customer trust while maintaining a commercially useful contact rhythm.

## Governing principle

Sunless should communicate with relevance, not volume. Frequency should increase when customer intent is high and decrease when relevance is uncertain.

## Baseline policy

- Campaign calendar: normally 1–2 commercial sends per week.
- Educational/editorial sends: used to balance product-led messages.
- Automated flows: triggered by behaviour, purchase state or lifecycle need.
- Transactional messages: always sent when operationally required.

These are directional controls, not rigid quotas. Suppression and prioritisation rules take precedence.

## Priority hierarchy

When multiple messages are eligible, prioritise:

1. Transactional and service messages
2. Active checkout or cart recovery
3. Shade-match follow-up
4. Post-purchase education
5. Replenishment
6. Welcome flow
7. Browse recovery
8. General campaigns
9. Win-back

Lower-priority campaigns should be suppressed when a customer is inside a higher-intent flow.

## Contact pressure rules

- Avoid sending more than one non-transactional email within 16 hours unless a major launch or customer action justifies it.
- Suppress generic campaigns during the first 24 hours after purchase.
- Pause abandonment messaging immediately after purchase.
- Reduce promotional frequency after repeated non-engagement.
- Do not punish non-openers with escalating subject-line pressure.

## Engagement bands

### Highly engaged
Opened or clicked recently, browsed products, completed shade match or purchased.

Treatment: normal campaign cadence plus relevant lifecycle messages.

### Moderately engaged
Intermittent opens and limited site activity.

Treatment: fewer, stronger messages with clearer relevance.

### Low engagement
No meaningful engagement across an extended period.

Treatment: reduce cadence, test preference capture, then suppress before deliverability is damaged.

## Seasonal exceptions

Higher frequency may be appropriate around:

- major product launches
- Black Friday or Cyber Week
- holiday delivery deadlines
- event-season preparation
- limited stock or genuine replenishment urgency

Urgency must remain factual and restrained.

## Preference management

Allow customers to choose where operationally possible:

- product education
- offers and launches
- professional tips
- replenishment reminders
- reduced-frequency communication

## Prohibited behaviours

- Artificial countdowns that reset
- Daily discounting as the default
- Multiple same-day campaigns without clear reason
- Continued abandonment emails after purchase
- Repeated sending to persistently disengaged contacts
- Hidden or obstructive unsubscribe routes

## Success criteria

The contact policy succeeds when revenue per recipient, repeat purchase and customer trust improve without creating fatigue, complaints or deliverability decline.
