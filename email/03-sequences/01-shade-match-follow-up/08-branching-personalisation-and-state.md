# Branching, Personalisation and State

## Purpose

This document defines how the shade-match follow-up sequence responds to customer behaviour without becoming repetitive, inaccurate or intrusive.

## Core principle

Personalisation must be based only on verified customer inputs, current catalogue data and observed behaviour. Never infer complexion, ethnicity, age, gender, medical information or personal circumstances from weak signals.

## Required state

The sequence should retain:

- `shade_match_id`
- `contact_id`
- completion timestamp
- recommended product ID
- recommended variant ID
- recommendation reason codes
- desired result depth
- undertone answer, including `not_sure`
- preferred format
- development-time preference
- application-confidence answer
- product availability snapshot
- consent state
- current sequence step
- last message timestamp
- last meaningful action

## Primary branches

### A — Recommendation viewed, no product-page visit

Emphasise the recommendation and explain the reason in plain language.

### B — Product page viewed, no cart

Shift from general explanation to product-specific proof, application confidence and relevant results.

### C — Product added to cart

Exit this sequence and hand control to cart abandonment. Do not continue recommendation reminders in parallel.

### D — Purchase completed

Exit immediately and enrol in the correct post-purchase education flow based on purchased products.

### E — Shade match repeated

Treat the newest completed result as canonical. Cancel queued messages that reference the superseded recommendation.

### F — Recommended item unavailable

Pause product-specific sends. Recalculate an approved alternative or send a neutral message inviting the customer to review their updated recommendation.

## Behavioural state transitions

```text
completed_match
  -> result_viewed
  -> product_viewed
  -> cart_started
  -> purchased
```

Alternative exits:

```text
completed_match -> unsubscribed
completed_match -> consent_withdrawn
completed_match -> recommendation_superseded
completed_match -> product_unavailable
completed_match -> sequence_expired
```

## Personalisation hierarchy

Use the most meaningful verified data first:

1. Exact recommended product and variant
2. Desired finish or depth
3. Preferred application format
4. Development-time preference
5. Stated concerns or confidence level
6. Generic brand guidance

Missing values must gracefully fall back to broader copy rather than showing blank or invented fields.

## Dynamic copy rules

Approved patterns:

- “You told us you wanted a natural medium result.”
- “Your match was selected for a buildable finish and your preferred development time.”
- “Because you were unsure about undertone, we prioritised the most forgiving option.”

Prohibited patterns:

- “Our AI analysed your skin.”
- “We know your exact skin chemistry.”
- “This is guaranteed to suit you.”
- Any confidence percentage that is not generated by a documented and validated model

## Frequency controls

- Never send more than one shade-match follow-up in a 24-hour period.
- Suppress against active cart abandonment, post-purchase and customer-service conversations.
- Apply global marketing frequency limits.
- Pause when the customer replies and route the conversation for support handling.

## State freshness

Before every send, revalidate:

- consent
- suppression status
- purchase status
- cart status
- recommendation version
- product availability
- price
- destination URL

Messages must not rely on a stale snapshot created at sequence entry.

## Expiry

Recommendation-led messaging should expire after 30 days unless the customer re-engages or repeats the shade match. After expiry, use broader education or invite the customer to retake the match.

## Acceptance criteria

The branching system succeeds when:

- no customer receives conflicting flows;
- every product reference is current;
- purchase stops the sequence immediately;
- repeat shade matches replace old recommendations;
- uncertainty is acknowledged without fabricated precision;
- personalisation remains useful rather than invasive.
