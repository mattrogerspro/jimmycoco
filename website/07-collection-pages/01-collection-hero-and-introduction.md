# Collection Hero and Introduction

## Role

The collection introduction frames the category, reassures the customer that they are in the right place and explains the most important distinction before the product grid begins.

It is not a homepage hero and must not delay access to products.

## Content hierarchy

1. Breadcrumbs
2. Collection title
3. One concise benefit-led statement
4. Optional supporting sentence
5. Optional guided-selection CTA
6. Optional restrained editorial image

## Copy principles

Lead with the customer outcome rather than internal product taxonomy.

Prefer:

> Build natural-looking colour at your own pace.

Over:

> Explore our innovative range of premium tanning solutions.

Supporting copy should clarify the category:

> Choose from express mousses, gradual formulas and targeted face products, each designed to create controllable colour without guesswork.

Keep the visible introduction to approximately 30–55 words. Additional educational copy belongs below the product grid.

## Layout options

### Option A — Text-led introduction
Best for utility-led collections such as Shop All, Accessories or Search Results.

- Left-aligned title and copy
- Strong negative space
- No decorative image required
- Product controls immediately below

### Option B — Editorial split introduction
Best for core categories such as Self Tan, Gradual Tan or Face.

- Copy occupying four to five columns
- One editorial image occupying six to seven columns
- Image height controlled so products remain visible within or immediately after the first viewport
- No oversized campaign typography

### Option C — Result-led introduction
Best for concern or outcome collections.

- Realistic skin-result image
- Concise outcome statement
- Clear explanation of who the collection suits
- Direct guided-selection link

## Photography direction

- Real skin texture
- Natural neutral lighting
- Accurate, non-orange colour
- Editorial cropping rather than catalogue posing
- Warm ivory, stone and champagne environment
- Products used only where they add orientation
- No generic beach, nightclub or holiday clichés

## Guided-selection CTA

Use one quiet but visible route such as:

- Find My Formula
- Match My Shade
- Help Me Choose

The wording should match the actual flow. Do not promise personalisation if the experience is only a generic filter.

**Intro:** Prepare, apply and perfect your tan with tools designed for a smoother, more even finish.

## Failure modes

Reject designs that:

- use a full-screen campaign hero;
- push the first product row far below the fold;
- repeat the homepage hero composition;
- use vague luxury language without helping selection;
- make the image more prominent than the category purpose;
- add multiple competing CTAs;
- use tiny centred copy with excessive empty space.

## Success criteria

The introduction succeeds when the customer understands the category and sees the beginning of the shopping experience without feeling delayed.
