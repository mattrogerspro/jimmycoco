## 2. Engagement didn't decline — the audience got colder

Sends split cleanly into two eras:

| Period | Typical send size | Open rate | £/recipient |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan–Apr | 5,000–8,500 | 60–78% | £0.024–0.171 |
| May–Jul | 18,000–32,000 | 16–35% | £0.010–0.066 |

The content didn't get worse. The list got bigger and the sends got broader. Every metric that depends on *who* received the email moved against you at the same time. This is the same dynamic driving the Gmail placement problem behind the re-engagement project.

## 4. Editorial vs discount — they optimise different things

| Type | Example | AOV | Orders |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Editorial | July 24 — The Summer Edit | **£38.59** | 31 |
| Sitewide discount | Summer Flash Sale, 25% off | £33.30 | 55 |
| Aggressive discount | Payday sale, 25% off | £24.87 | 40 |

**Discount buys volume and costs you basket size. Editorial does the reverse.**

Since you want to lift order value *and* total revenue, the answer isn't one or the other — it's alternating them deliberately, and stopping the discount emails from cannibalising the editorial ones. Right now the calendar mixes them without a pattern.

## 5. The Pro / salon paradox — biggest untapped opportunity

| Audience | CTOR | AOV | Orders (whole year) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jimmy Coco PRO Salons | **7.06%** | £56.15 | 3 |
| Professional spray tan signup | 4.88% | **£68.18** | 6 |
| Consumer average | ~1.3% | ~£29–33 | — |

These are your **most engaged clickers by a factor of five, and your highest order values by a factor of two — and they almost never buy.** Nine orders across the year from two lists that click harder than anyone else.

They're clicking a consumer storefront with consumer pricing. There's no trade path: no wholesale pricing, no account application, no minimum-order incentive on the landing page. Every one of those clicks is a qualified B2B lead hitting a retail checkout.

Fixing this is likely worth more than any newsletter copy change.

## 6. Recommendations, ranked

**Do first — costs nothing, immediate effect**

1. **Stop sending to `Spa / Salons Reachout`.** 7,437 emails, **10.84% bounce**, zero revenue all year. It is actively damaging sender reputation, which suppresses inbox placement for every other send. Same for `JULY NEW PRO UK BUSINESS` (6.84% bounce, £0).
2. **Retire or merge the `Monthly Newsletter` list.** 13,480 sends, 15.7% open, £228. It drags aggregate engagement down for no return.
3. **Send the newsletter engaged-first.** Lead with Repeat Buyers, Buyers Club and Lapsing 60–90d — together roughly 25,400 profiles returning £0.10–0.20 each, versus £0.017 on the Monthly Newsletter list.

**Do next — structural**

4. **Give the Pro/salon audience its own track** with trade pricing and an account-application CTA. Highest CTOR and highest AOV in the account, converting at almost nothing.
5. **Alternate editorial and discount on a fixed rhythm** rather than ad hoc — editorial to protect AOV, discount to drive volume, and never two discounts back to back.
6. **Rebuild the payday campaign as a recurring monthly slot.** It was the single most efficient thing you sent all year and it hasn't been repeated since January.
7. **Use the £30 free-shipping threshold as an explicit AOV lever.** The Malibu Beach Duo at £28 sits £2 short — a "£2 from free delivery" nudge is a textbook basket-builder.

**Measurement**

8. Judge every send on **£/recipient**, not opens and not total revenue. Total revenue rewards sending to more people, which is exactly the behaviour that caused this year's decline.

## 7. What the Shopify data would add

Not answerable from Klaviyo alone:

- **True AOV and margin per product** — Klaviyo shows attributed revenue, not what you keep. A 25% discount on a low-margin line may be losing money at £24.87 AOV.
- **What genuinely sells together** — the current bundles were designed by eye. Real basket data would let you build bundles around actual co-purchase.
- **Repeat purchase interval** — the single most useful number for newsletter *timing*. Send the replenishment prompt at the median re-order gap.
- **Which products to feature** — whether the newsletter is featuring your actual bestsellers.
- **Attribution truth** — whether Klaviyo's £17,621.76 matches real orders, and how much of it email actually caused.
- **The two missing product URLs** for the A-List Glow Kit and A-List Essentials, still using `&item=` param workarounds in the v2 flash sale draft.
