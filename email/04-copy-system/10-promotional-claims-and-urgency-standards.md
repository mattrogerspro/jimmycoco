# Promotional Claims and Urgency Standards

## Purpose

Define how Sunless communicates offers, scarcity, deadlines, exclusivity and product benefits without weakening trust or premium positioning.

## Governing principle

Urgency must be factual, proportionate and useful. It must never be manufactured to force a decision.

## Approved urgency sources

Urgency may be used only when supported by a real operational condition, such as:

- a documented promotion end time;
- a verified product discontinuation;
- a limited production allocation;
- a confirmed event or dispatch cutoff;
- a benefit expiry;
- a stock state sourced from live inventory;
- a genuine application or delivery deadline.

The underlying condition must be recorded and reproducible.

## Prohibited urgency patterns

Do not use:

- evergreen countdown timers that restart;
- false low-stock messages;
- `act now` without a reason;
- repeated `last chance` emails after the stated deadline;
- vague claims such as `almost gone` without thresholds;
- pressure based on shame, fear or status loss;
- urgency in complaint, refund or unresolved service contexts.

## Deadline language

State the deadline precisely when it materially affects the customer.

Prefer:

- `Ends Sunday at 11:59 pm GMT.`
- `Order by 1 pm for the stated dispatch window.`
- `Your benefit is available until 30 September.`

Avoid:

- `Hurry.`
- `Don’t wait.`
- `Time is running out.`

A clear date is more credible than theatrical pressure.

## Scarcity language

Stock-based copy must use approved inventory thresholds and degrade safely when inventory data is stale.

Possible states:

- available;
- low stock under an approved threshold;
- unavailable;
- discontinued;
- expected back, with a verified date;
- no reliable state, in which case scarcity copy is omitted.

Never imply that a personalised shade has been reserved unless reservation functionality exists.

## Offer presentation

Every offer must state, where relevant:

- what the customer receives;
- qualifying products or order value;
- code requirements;
- start and end dates;
- market restrictions;
- exclusions;
- stacking rules;
- returns implications;
- whether stock is limited.

Terms should be easy to find and written in plain language.

## Discount hierarchy

Sunless should not default to discounting as the first answer to uncertainty.

Use value in this order where appropriate:

1. Guidance
2. Product suitability
3. Method and proof
4. Service and convenience
5. Relevant benefit
6. Financial incentive

Discounts should not train customers to wait for the next email.

## Claims standards

Claims must be:

- approved;
- specific;
- understandable;
- supported by suitable evidence;
- consistent across email, website, packaging and checkout;
- qualified where necessary.

Avoid turning subjective brand language into objective product guarantees.

Examples requiring evidence or qualification include:

- duration;
- transfer resistance;
- drying time;
- ingredient performance;
- suitability for sensitive skin;
- universal shade suitability;
- professional preference;
- celebrity use;
- comparative superiority.

## Celebrity and authority claims

Only use customer, professional or celebrity associations when the brand has approval for the exact use, context, market and duration.

Do not imply endorsement from image presence alone. Do not alter approved source imagery or add quotations that cannot be traced.

## Exclusivity language

Use `exclusive`, `private access`, `VIP only` or `early access` only when access controls genuinely enforce the distinction.

Prefer precise descriptions:

- `Available to VIP members 24 hours before public release.`
- `A benefit included with your current tier.`

Avoid decorative exclusivity that has no operational meaning.

## Price comparison

Reference prices, savings and percentage reductions must be calculated from approved current data and comply with applicable market rules.

Do not manually type savings into creative when they can be derived safely from product data.

## Frequency controls

Even truthful urgency becomes coercive when repeated excessively.

The orchestration layer should control:

- number of reminders;
- proximity to deadline;
- overlap with other sequences;
- recent customer engagement;
- customer service states;
- unsubscribe and complaint risk.

## QA checklist

Before approving urgency or promotional copy, confirm:

- the condition is real and documented;
- dates, timezone and market are correct;
- inventory data is current;
- terms match checkout behaviour;
- the claim is approved and evidenced;
- the message does not restart or contradict earlier urgency;
- the customer can understand the offer without the image;
- service or complaint states suppress the promotion;
- the tone remains consistent with a premium, trustworthy brand.
