# Scene 04 — Why Hollywood Looks Different

## Role in the experience

This chapter explains the professional mechanism behind the result. It converts aspiration into understanding by showing that Sunless is not merely a premium bottle, but a translated professional method.

It follows the shade recommendation engine, so the visitor has already received a product direction. This scene now answers the next question:

> Why should I believe this recommendation will look more natural than an ordinary self-tan?

## Commercial objective

Increase confidence in the recommended product by making Sunless's differentiation legible in seconds.

The chapter should:

- Explain why ordinary self-tan can appear flat, orange or overly heavy
- Connect Jimmy's expertise to clear product and application principles
- Reduce perceived risk before proof and product browsing
- Create a rational bridge between guided recommendation and real results
- Increase willingness to pay a premium

## User questions answered

- What makes this different from a typical self-tan?
- Why does undertone matter?
- Why is layering better than applying one heavy coat?
- What does Jimmy's professional experience change?
- Is the method understandable enough to use at home?

## Core message

> Hollywood colour looks different because it is matched, layered and controlled.

Alternative supporting line:

> Natural-looking colour is not about going darker. It is about choosing the right undertone and building depth with control.

## Strategic principles

The chapter explains three connected ideas:

1. **Undertone matching** — colour should work with the customer's natural skin tone rather than sit on top of it.
2. **Professional layering** — depth should be built gradually, allowing the result to remain believable.
3. **Buildable colour** — the customer should control intensity instead of committing to one fixed finish.

These ideas must feel like one system, not three unrelated feature cards.

## Architectural silhouette

This chapter should be bright, airy and typography-led. It must visually contrast with the denser recommendation engine before it and the photography-dominant Real Results scene after it.

Approved silhouette:

- Bright white or near-white full-width field
- Large amount of negative space above and below
- One editorial introduction occupying a narrow text column
- Three horizontally distributed explanation moments
- Minimal illustration or restrained photographic details
- No heavy card containers
- No full product grid

The section should feel like a calm explanatory spread in an expensive beauty magazine.

## Layout direction

Use an invisible 12-column grid.

Recommended desktop arrangement:

- Introductory eyebrow: columns 2–4
- Main headline: columns 2–8
- Supporting statement: columns 8–11, offset vertically
- Three principles: distributed across columns 2–11
- Use intentional blank columns between explanation groups

The three principles should not sit inside identical boxed cards. Separate them through spacing, typography, fine rules and illustration placement.

## Composition hierarchy

The intended reading order is:

1. Large chapter headline
2. Short explanatory statement
3. Undertone Matching
4. Professional Layering
5. Buildable Colour
6. Subtle link to Jimmy's method or real results

The eye should move calmly from left to right without encountering visual clutter.

## Illustration direction

Use refined, minimal line illustrations or close-up photographic details.

Approved illustration concepts:

- A subtle undertone spectrum or layered skin-tone arcs
- Transparent tonal layers building one over another
- A controlled gradient of depth across three restrained swatches
- A close-up macro of realistic skin with natural tonal variation

Illustrations should feel editorial and scientific without becoming clinical.

Avoid:

- Generic sparkle icons
- Beauty stars
- Droplets
- Shield symbols
- Cartoon faces
- Infographic-heavy diagrams
- Neon colour swatches

## Photography option

A restrained photographic alternative may be used instead of illustrations:

- One close crop of naturally lit skin
- One professional application detail
- One subtle product texture image

If photography is used, keep it subordinate to the typography. This chapter is primarily explanatory, not cinematic.

- Main typography: deep charcoal

- Fine rules: pale stone
- Accent: restrained muted bronze only where necessary

- Principle headings: 22–26px serif or confident sans-serif depending on established hierarchy
- Body copy: 17–18px refined sans-serif
- Eyebrow: 12–13px uppercase with controlled tracking
- Maximum body-copy line length: approximately 45–60 characters

Avoid tiny explanatory text. The chapter should feel generous and readable.

## Spacing and rhythm

Suggested internal pacing:

- Top padding: 180–220px
- Headline-to-supporting-copy relationship: 32–56px
- Intro-to-principles spacing: 100–140px
- Principle separation: 48–80px depending on width
- Bottom padding: 180–240px

The whitespace is part of the luxury signal.

## Conversion psychology

This chapter uses:

- **Cognitive fluency:** three simple principles instead of technical formulation language
- **Authority:** Jimmy's method explains the logic behind the recommendation
- **Risk reduction:** the user understands how orange or flat results are avoided
- **Processing ease:** short, mutually reinforcing concepts
- **Value framing:** the product feels like expertise translated into formula and method

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Ensure illustration meaning is not conveyed by colour alone
- Use strong contrast for explanatory text
- Preserve readable line lengths at desktop and mobile
- On mobile, stack the three principles with generous separation rather than compressing them into a carousel
- Maintain semantic heading order
- Decorative illustrations should use empty alt text in production
- Informative diagrams require concise alt descriptions

## Common AI failure modes

Reject or regenerate if the model:

- Creates three generic ecommerce feature cards
- Uses identical white boxes with shadows
- Adds beauty clichés such as stars, glitter or droplets
- Makes the copy tiny
- Uses a beige background too similar to the previous scene
- Overcomplicates the explanation with scientific diagrams
- Adds invented claims or pseudo-technology
- Creates a busy infographic
- Repeats the same centered heading/card-row composition used elsewhere
- Makes the scene feel like a skincare clinic

## Success criteria

The scene succeeds when:

- The difference between Sunless and generic self-tan is understood in under ten seconds
- The chapter feels spacious and editorial rather than card-based
- The three principles are visually connected but individually memorable
- The visitor understands why Jimmy's expertise changes the result
- The section creates a calm bridge into Real Results
- The background and silhouette are clearly different from adjacent chapters
